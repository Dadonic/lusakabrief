Politics · By-elections

Three wards voted and the UPND took all three, eight contests never happened, and the President held his first rally since August: what Thursday's by-elections produced

Of the 14 by-elections the Electoral Commission scheduled for Thursday 5 November, eight could not be held after candidates withdrew, News Diggers reported; in three of the wards that did vote, Lwingishi in Mansa, Lubaleshi in Kanchibiya and Mbeta in Sioma, The Zambian Observer reported UPND wins by margins of 700, 1,960 and 163 votes. In Mandevu, where the parliamentary contest collapsed, President Hichilema held his first public rally since the general election and introduced the MP declared without a poll, the Zambia Daily Mail reported.

By Lusaka Brief Politics Desk • MANSA — 7 November 2026 • 3 min read

The by-election day that was to have included two parliamentary seats and a mayoralty ended up being decided in a handful of council wards. News Diggers reported on Friday 6 November that of the 14 by-elections the Electoral Commission of Zambia had set for Thursday 5 November, two parliamentary (Lumezi North and Mandevu), the Mbala mayoral contest and 12 council wards, eight could not be held: both parliamentary seats, the mayoralty and five wards, after candidate withdrawals left single candidates standing. In five wards, the paper said, only the UPND successfully filed nominations.

The results

The Zambian Observer reported three results on Friday. In Lwingishi ward in Mansa district, the UPND's James Mwandu polled 1,832 votes to 1,132 for Chanda Chimba of the National Reconciliation Party for Unity and Prosperity (NRPUP); an earlier Observer report, which spelt the winner's surname Mwando, put the total votes cast at 3,039 with 55 rejected ballots from 5,974 registered voters. In Lubaleshi ward in Kanchibiya district, Nelson Bwalya of the UPND took 2,327 votes against 367 for Katongo Lupupa of the NRP and 249 for Doris Chintu of Citizens First. In Mbeta ward in Sioma district, Situmbeko Yuyi of the UPND won 522 votes to 359 for the independent Mwanamwalye Mwanamwalye. The Observer's reports did not give turnout for the second and third wards or name the returning officers.

Thursday's ward results, as reported by The Zambian Observer

  • Lwingishi (Mansa): Mwandu, UPND, 1,832; Chimba, NRPUP, 1,132
  • Lubaleshi (Kanchibiya): Bwalya, UPND, 2,327; Lupupa, NRP, 367; Chintu, CF, 249
  • Mbeta (Sioma): Yuyi, UPND, 522; Mwanamwalye, independent, 359

ECZ Commissioner Zevyanji Sinkala, who toured six of the ten polling stations in Lwingishi, commended voters, candidates and the police for keeping the peace, News Diggers reported, and said that democracy demands that citizens be given the chance to vote for their leaders. The rest of the Commission's remarks were behind the paper's paywall.

Mandevu, without a ballot

In Mandevu, where Adrian Banda was declared MP on Thursday after three opposition candidates withdrew, the President held what the Zambia Daily Mail called his first public event since the general election. The paper reported that Mr Hichilema thanked voters for re-electing him and the UPND on 13 August, introduced Mr Banda, and promised action on the constituency's water supply, roads and security. He said water was a basic necessity needing urgent action, voiced concern about insecurity caused by people commonly called junkies, directed the security wings to help the young people involved, and linked skills training for them to the enhanced Constituency Development Fund.

On August 13, you elected me and the UPND. We are ready to work for you,
— President Hakainde Hichilema at a rally in Mandevu on 5 November, quoted by the Zambia Daily Mail

News Diggers reported from the same engagement that the President told the nominated MPs present that they held their seats because of the UPND government and Bill 7, and should get out into their communities and work, and that he urged Mr Banda to stay humble. Lusaka Brief reported on Friday that the Lusaka Province Minister, Obvious Mwaliteta, had denied warning the Tonse candidate off the Mandevu race and that a judicial review of the Commission's handling of the seat, brought by Kasonde Mwenda, was before the High Court; no ruling on that application was reported on the pages read for this edition.
