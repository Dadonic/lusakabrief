€60 million for Kapiri Mposhi–Ndola and the 1984 Railways Act to go: the budget opens the line to private trains, as Wonderful Group talks of US$1.2 billion for the southern line

The 2027 budget speech carried the first firm money for Zambia Railways in some time: an initial €60 million to rehabilitate the Kapiri Mposhi–Ndola section, reported by Zambia Monitor, with Zambia Railways to become an infrastructure manager and freight and passenger services opened to private operators. A day earlier, the Zambia Daily Mail reported that the Wonderful Group of Companies intends to put more than US$1.2 billion into rehabilitating the Livingstone–Chililabombwe line to move its cargo off the roads.

By Lusaka Brief Business Desk • LUSAKA — 7 November 2026 • 2 min read

Railways had a day on Friday 6 November. Zambia Monitor reported that the Minister of Finance and National Planning, Situmbeko Musokotwane, told Parliament in the budget speech that Zambia had secured an initial €60 million to rehabilitate the Kapiri Mposhi–Ndola section of the national railway, and that the government was engaging other partners to fund the remaining sections of the Zambia Railways network. The Monitor's report did not name the lender.

Infrastructure manager, private trains

The structural change matters as much as the money. According to the Monitor, Zambia Railways Limited is to become the railway infrastructure manager, with freight and passenger train services opened to private operators under an open access model, and the minister said the government would "repeal and replace the Railways Act Cap 453 of 1984" to improve operational efficiency and sustainable infrastructure financing. The speech, as reported, also placed the start of construction on the Lobito railway line in the fourth quarter of 2027, said rehabilitation of the TAZARA line was under way, and promised a dry port at Kapiri Mposhi. Mr Musokotwane tied all of it to exports: "The Grow Zambia Agenda must be led by expansion in exports."

The same report listed the road and air works the budget funds: 787 kilometres of roads to be built, upgraded or rehabilitated; works already begun on the Mutanda–Kisasa–Mwinilunga–Jimbe road and on the Kasempa turn-off to Kabompo via Mufumbwe; Mansa and Mfuwe airports to be completed in 2027; and continuing work at Nakonde airport. The minister said border operations were being modernised with the private sector: "Government, working with the private sector, is modernising border operations to speed up trade processing."

A private bid for the southern line

The Zambia Daily Mail reported on Friday morning, from Chilanga, that the Wonderful Group of Companies plans to invest slightly more than US$1.2 billion in rehabilitating the Livingstone–Chililabombwe railway line, to shift part of its cargo from road to rail. The company's chief executive disclosed the plan during a tour of the group's industrial park on Thursday 5 November by senior public servants led by the Deputy Secretary to the Cabinet for Finance and Economic Development, Siazongo Siakalenge. The Daily Mail said the group moves its products on about 1,000 trucks a day, and that Mr Siakalenge said the investment would take pressure off the road network.

Mr Siakalenge used the visit to make a point about the civil service. The Daily Mail reported that he cited the eight months Wonderful Group waited to be granted industrial park status as an example of how slow administrative decisions hold back investment, and said the tour would help permanent secretaries identify legislation that industry finds problematic.

Having appreciated what is happening on the ground, permanent secretaries will be well positioned to put in effort
— Siazongo Siakalenge, Deputy Secretary to the Cabinet for Finance and Economic Development, at the Wonderful Group industrial park on 5 November, quoted by the Zambia Daily Mail

Neither report gave a timetable for the Wonderful Group project, said whether it had been agreed with the government or Zambia Railways, or explained how a private rehabilitation of the Livingstone–Chililabombwe line would fit the open access model announced in the budget. No statement from Zambia Railways on either announcement was found on the pages read for this edition.

Sources: Zambia Monitor, “Zambia secures initial €60 million for railway rehabilitation” (6 Nov 2026); Zambia Daily Mail, “Wonderful to invest $1.2bn in railway line” (6 Nov 2026).
