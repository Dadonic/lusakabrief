Two per cent of everything: Cabinet moves the NHIMA deduction from basic to gross pay from January 2027, as the Treasury takes on the scheme's K1.7 billion debt and dialysis bill

Health Minister Roma Chilengi said on ZNBC's main news on Thursday 12 November that Cabinet has approved calculating the 2% National Health Insurance contribution on total salary rather than basic pay, part of a rescue of a scheme he said was set up on flawed financial grounds. The government will absorb K1.7 billion NHIMA owes hospitals and suppliers, pay directly for kidney dialysis that has cost the fund about K400 million a year, and contribute for elderly people who do not pay in. The changes take effect with the 2027 budget in January.

By Lusaka Brief Health Desk • LUSAKA — 15 November 2026 • 2 min read

Workers will see a larger National Health Insurance deduction on their payslips from January 2027. Health Minister Professor Roma Chilengi, speaking on ZNBC TV1's main news on Thursday 12 November, said Cabinet had approved a transition so that the 2% contribution to the National Health Insurance Management Authority is worked out on gross pay rather than basic pay. News Diggers and the Zambian Observer, the latter carrying a ZANIS report, covered the announcement.

The Zambian Observer quoted the minister's summary: "Workers will now pay two percent of their total salary, not just two percent of basic pay." Neither outlet reported any change to the employer's share.

A rescue, not just a rate change

Chilengi framed the move as part of a wider effort to stop NHIMA sinking under debt. News Diggers reported him saying the government had stepped in because the scheme was established on flawed financial grounds and could not meet its obligations. The Zambian Observer listed the other measures Cabinet approved: the government will take over the K1.7 billion that NHIMA owes to hospitals and health suppliers; it will begin paying contributions on behalf of elderly citizens who do not contribute; part of the Social Cash Transfer allocation will be used to buy health insurance for beneficiaries; and the State will pay directly for kidney dialysis, a service the Observer said has cost NHIMA about K400 million a year.

The measures start in January 2027, when the next national budget takes effect, the Observer reported. The same budget proposes raising VAT from 16% to 16.5%, a change the IMF's resident representative Reginald Darius told a ZIPAR panel on Thursday 12 November would raise revenue provided compliance and administration improve, News Diggers reported; the NHIMA change adds a second item to what wage earners will see change in January.

Retirements 'in the national interest'

News Diggers also reported a second, separate remark from the same broadcast: Chilengi said that health workers who had been retired in the national interest could have been politically inclined during the general election of 13 August. The outlet's visible text does not say how many workers were affected or what evidence the minister cited, and the remark has not been reported in detail elsewhere.

What Cabinet approved, as reported

  • 2% NHIMA contribution calculated on gross pay, not basic pay, from January 2027
  • Government takes over K1.7 billion owed by NHIMA to hospitals and suppliers
  • Government to pay contributions for elderly non-contributors
  • Part of Social Cash Transfer funds to buy health insurance for beneficiaries
  • Kidney dialysis, about K400 million a year, to be paid directly by the State

Sources: News Diggers, “Cabinet approves NHIMA deduction on gross pay” (13 Nov 2026); Zambian Observer, “Government to pay NHIMA's K1.7 billion debt” (13 Nov 2026); News Diggers, “VAT hike to boost revenue, but improving compliance is key – IMF” (14 Nov 2026).
