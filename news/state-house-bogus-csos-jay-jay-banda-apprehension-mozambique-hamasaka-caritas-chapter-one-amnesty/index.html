‘A fugitive does not acquire immunity by crossing a border’: State House answers the civil-society groups questioning how Jay Jay Banda was brought home

A coalition of civil-society organisations has asked the Zambian and Mozambican governments to account for the cross-border apprehension of former Petauke Central MP Emmanuel ‘Jay Jay’ Banda and to explain how he came to be in a critical condition at Maina Soko Military Hospital. State House, through Clayson Hamasaka, calls the statement a political booby trap and says only bogus organisations could question the arrest of a man who, it says, publicly called for the President’s assassination.

By Lusaka Brief Politics Desk • LUSAKA — 21 October 2026 • 3 min read

A week after Emmanuel ‘Jay Jay’ Banda was apprehended in Mozambique on 14 October — an arrest the Zambian authorities have confirmed, according to Zambia Monitor — the argument has moved from whether he was caught to how. A joint statement by a consortium of local and regional civil-society organisations and human-rights defenders, issued on Sunday 18 October, called on Zambia and Mozambique to account for the operation and said that whatever Mr Banda’s past conduct, both governments must still follow the law in his arrest and transfer, News Diggers reported. The signatories listed by News Diggers include the Advocates for Democratic Governance Foundation, Caritas Zambia, Chapter One Foundation, Free Press Initiative Zambia and the LCK Freedom Foundation; the Zambian Observer puts the number of organisations at 24, including groups from South Africa and Botswana.

The statement also asked the authorities to disclose how Mr Banda came to be in a critical condition at Maina Soko Military Hospital, pointing to the government’s duty to protect the life of anyone in its custody.

State House’s reply

State House chief communications specialist Clayson Hamasaka responded on Tuesday 20 October. According to the Zambian Observer, which carried the statement, he described the coalition’s position as a political booby trap designed to turn a fleeing suspect into a martyr, pointed to what he called a contradiction between not declaring the apprehension unlawful and asking the international community to condemn acts of lawlessness, and defended the operation as mutual legal assistance between sovereign states. The statement said Mr Banda is safe in custody, will receive medical care and access to counsel and family, and will be presented before a competent court.

A fugitive does not acquire immunity by crossing a border.
— State House statement issued by Clayson Hamasaka on Tuesday 20 October, as carried by the Zambian Observer

News Diggers reports Mr Hamasaka as saying that only bogus organisations could question the apprehension of a suspect who publicly called for the assassination of the Head of State, and that no genuine human-rights defender could excuse such incitement and remain credible. Home Affairs permanent secretary Dickson Matembo asked why the same organisations had not spoken out about Mr Banda’s alleged violations against the President. Two days earlier Mr Hamasaka had called Amnesty International hypocrites after it questioned the operation.

What Amnesty asked for

Amnesty International Africa said on 17 October, as reported by Zambia Monitor, that the authorities had confirmed Mr Banda was arrested in Mozambique on 14 October by a joint task force. It asked for a qualified health professional to assess him, for confidential access to a lawyer and his family, and for the legal basis of his detention to be disclosed. It said no one should be transferred to a country where they face a real risk of serious rights violations, and that the authorities should either release him or establish lawful grounds before a competent court.

Background

Mr Banda was arrested in June 2024 and charged with aggravated robbery and attempted murder, according to Lusaka Times. On 4 August 2024 he escaped from Chipata General Hospital, where he was being treated under guard, and the Speaker declared his seat vacant in November 2024 after he ignored a summons to appear. The Zambian Observer reports that the government offered a K2 million bounty. Opposition figures said on 16 October that he had been seized at a hideout in Mozambique; opposition-aligned reports that he had been shot were not confirmed, Lusaka Times noted. He has not been convicted of any offence, and the charges against him are untested in court.
