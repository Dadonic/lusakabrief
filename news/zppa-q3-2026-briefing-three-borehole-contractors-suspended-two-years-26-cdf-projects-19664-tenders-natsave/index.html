Three borehole contractors barred for two years, 26 CDF projects faulted and a NATSAVE server tender sent back: the ZPPA's third-quarter report card

At its third-quarter briefing in Lusaka on Monday 16 November, the Zambia Public Procurement Authority said it had suspended Capricon Innovation, Nkanmu Investments and Totten Infrastructure from public tenders for two years after they failed to complete borehole work for the Ministry of Water Development and Sanitation. Director General Gloria Ngoma also reported irregular contract extensions and documentation lapses across 26 CDF projects in 11 councils, 19,664 tenders on the e-procurement system, and 18 bidder appeals, one of them at NATSAVE.

By Lusaka Brief Business Desk • LUSAKA — 18 November 2026 • 2 min read

The procurement regulator's quarterly accounting of what went wrong, and what it did about it, arrived on Monday 16 November. Gloria Ngoma, Director General of the Zambia Public Procurement Authority, told a media briefing in Lusaka that the authority had carried out five investigations in the third quarter of 2026, two of them into underperformance of contractual obligations and one into outright non-performance, News Diggers reported on Tuesday.

The most concrete outcome was the suspension of three firms from public procurement for two years: Capricon Innovation Limited, Nkanmu Investments Limited and Totten Infrastructure Limited. All three had been contracted by the Ministry of Water Development and Sanitation to drill and equip boreholes, and the authority opened its inquiry after a complaint from the ministry that the rehabilitation work had not been completed. News Diggers' report does not give the number of boreholes, the value of the contracts or their locations, and no response from the companies was reported.

The CDF findings

Separately, the authority monitored 26 Constituency Development Fund projects in 11 local authorities, spanning schools, clinics, water, sanitation and other public works, Zambia Monitor reported. It assessed how contracts were being implemented, the progress and quality of the works and whether procurement rules were being followed. The weaknesses it lists are irregular contract extensions and variations, inadequate contract documentation, and lapses in the handling of performance and advance payment securities, together with construction defects. The findings have gone to the procuring entities and to the Ministries of Local Government and Rural Development and of Infrastructure, Housing and Urban Development for remedial action. No contractor or council is named in the report.

Third quarter in figures

  • 5 investigations; 3 contractors suspended for two years
  • 26 CDF projects monitored in 11 local authorities
  • 19,664 tenders published by 659 procuring entities, up from 12,736 by 415 in the second quarter
  • 240 procurement officers and 185 suppliers trained on the e-GP system by 30 September
  • 18 bidder appeals, 17 concluded

The system and the appeals

Use of the electronic government procurement system, which Section 16(1) of the Public Procurement Act of 2020 makes compulsory, grew sharply: 19,664 tenders were published by 659 procuring entities in the quarter, against 12,736 by 415 entities in the previous one, a rise the authority attributes to enhancements to the system and a migration to new equipment. It says it has trained 240 officers from procuring entities and 185 suppliers, and continues to run fortnightly training sessions.

The authority also heard 18 appeals from bidders, concluding 17 by 30 September. In one, a bidder challenged the National Savings and Credit Bank over a tender to supply and configure IBM servers, arguing that the winning supplier was not an authorised IBM warranty service provider. The authority found that it was not, upheld that ground and directed NATSAVE to re-evaluate the tender, though it declined to cancel the award outright. The winning supplier was not named.

The briefing continues the regulator's practice of using public sanction, rather than only private correspondence, to deal with contractors who take public money and do not deliver.
