'Zambia does not belong to an individual': the ten candidates Hichilema beat in August sign one statement against removing presidential term limits

Harry Kalaba, Fred M'membe, Kelvin Bwalya Fube and seven other candidates from the 13 August election issued a joint statement in Lusaka on Thursday 12 November rejecting a proposal, attributed to the nominated youth MP Mwato Kabaso, to abolish presidential term limits. They cite the one-party years, Frederick Chiluba's failed third-term bid in 2001 and the 2024 ruling on Edgar Lungu's eligibility, and call on MPs of every party to defend the two-term rule. Zambia Monitor, the Daily Nation and the Zambian Observer carried the statement.

By Lusaka Brief Politics Desk • LUSAKA — 13 November 2026 • 3 min read

Ten of the people who stood against President Hakainde Hichilema in the general election of 13 August 2026 have put their names to a single statement opposing any move to remove the limit of two presidential terms. The statement, dated Thursday 12 November and issued in Lusaka, was carried in full by the Zambian Observer and reported by Zambia Monitor and the Daily Nation. It responds to a proposal that the three outlets attribute to Mwato Kabaso, a nominated Member of Parliament representing the youth.

The signatories, as listed on the statement, are Kelvin Bwalya Fube of Zambia Must Prosper, Given Chansa of the Movement for Economic Emancipation, Harry Kalaba of Citizens First, the independent Given Katuta, Howard Kunda of the Zambia Wake Up Party, Fred M'membe of the Socialist Party, Brian Mushimba of the Organised People's Party, Ackim Njobvu of the Democratic Union, Richwell Siamunene of the New Focus Party and Richard Silumbe of the Leadership Movement. Zambia Monitor gives two of the party names differently, listing Chansa under the Revamp Party and Njobvu under the Democratic Party.

The case the ten make

The statement's central line, quoted by all three outlets, is that "Zambia does not belong to an individual, a political party or an administration." Zambia Monitor adds the candidates' description of the presidency as an office of service rather than an inheritance. The Daily Nation reports their warning that abolishing the limit could undermine Zambia's democracy and concentrate political power excessively.

According to the text published by the Zambian Observer, the candidates describe the Constitution as a social contract rather than the property of whoever holds office, and reject the argument that Zambia is short of capable leaders, pointing to the growth in educated professionals since independence. They recall the one-party period from 1973 to 1991, arguing that concentrated power damaged the economy and institutions, while acknowledging that commodity shocks also played a part. They cite the resistance to Frederick Chiluba's bid for a third term in 2001 as a democratic precedent, and the December 2024 court ruling that former President Edgar Lungu was not eligible to stand again as an example of the limit being enforced through the courts.

On the cost of elections, an argument sometimes made for longer or fewer terms, the statement accepts that polls are expensive but says the remedy is better electoral administration and public financial management, not fewer elections. The ten also say they are concerned that the proposal came from a young legislator, and urge young people not to confuse political ambition with democratic progress.

The six-point declaration, as published

  • Reject any proposal to abolish presidential term limits
  • Call on MPs of all parties to defend the principle of democratic succession
  • Ask young people, churches, civil society, traditional leaders and professional bodies to join the debate responsibly
  • Call for stronger independence for the Electoral Commission, the Judiciary, Parliament and other constitutional bodies
  • Urge the government to concentrate on jobs, education, health, infrastructure and poverty reduction
  • Encourage national dialogue and respect for differing views

What is not in the reports

None of the three reports records a response from Kabaso, from the UPND or from State House to the statement, and none says that any bill or motion to amend the term-limit provision has been tabled in the National Assembly. Zambia Monitor describes the Kabaso proposal as "reported", and the joint statement itself treats it as a proposal rather than a draft law. The candidates' statement is therefore a pre-emptive position, taken before any formal process has begun.

The ten were on opposite sides of each other in August, and the statement is the broadest common position the losing field has taken since the election. It is addressed as much to MPs as to the public: the second of its six points asks members of every party, including the governing one, to defend the succession rules they were elected under.

Confirmed vs. alleged

Confirmed: That a joint statement dated 12 November 2026 opposing the removal of presidential term limits was issued in Lusaka in the names of ten candidates from the August election, and that it responds to a proposal attributed to nominated MP Mwato Kabaso: reported by Zambia Monitor, the Daily Nation and the Zambian Observer, the last of which published the full text.

Alleged or unverified: Whether any formal amendment has been drafted or tabled; what Kabaso proposed in his own words and whether he has responded; the government's position; and the exact party names of two signatories, which Zambia Monitor and the published statement give differently.

Sources: Zambia Monitor, “'Zambia doesn't belong to an individual,' 10 party chieftains oppose proposed removal of term limits” (12 Nov 2026); The Zambian Observer, “Joint press statement by the ten presidential candidates who participated in the 13th August 2026 general elections on the proposed removal of presidential term limits” (12 Nov 2026); Daily Nation, “Ten presidential candidates reject calls to remove term limits” (12 Nov 2026).
