Courts · Treason case Developing

'The State does not want it heard': a defence lawyer says the prosecution fears the 18 treason accused would be freed if their bail plea is argued, before Tuesday's ruling

Milner Katolo, one of the lawyers for Brian Mundubile, Makebi Zulu and 16 others, says the State has openly admitted it does not want their constitutional bail application heard, and that the substantive hearing will start at once if the court overrules the State's objection on Tuesday 10 November. The State argued on Thursday that the application was wrongly before the court. In Solwezi, the Subordinate Court has sent the separate sedition case to Lusaka, and Zulu has asked to represent himself.

By Lusaka Brief Courts Desk • LUSAKA — 8 November 2026 • 3 min read

The lawyers for the 18 people held on treason and terrorism-related charges, among them the 2026 presidential candidate Brian Mundubile and his running mate Makebi Zulu, say the State is trying to stop their bail application from being heard at all. News Diggers reported on Saturday 7 November that Milner Katolo, one of the defence lawyers, said the State had openly admitted that it did not want the constitutional bail application for the 18 heard, and that its fear was that the court would release them if it did. The Lusaka High Court is due to rule on Tuesday 10 November on whether it has jurisdiction to hear the application; Katolo said that if the State's preliminary objection is overruled the substantive bail hearing will proceed immediately, according to the Diggers report. The State raised the preliminary issue on Thursday 5 November, arguing that the application was wrongly before the court. The Diggers report is behind a paywall and the State's reasoning was not visible in the part published openly, so its full argument is not set out here.

The applicants have been brought to court from Mukobeko Maximum Security Correctional Facility for each sitting, Zambia Monitor reported on 29 October, when a three-judge panel of Derrick Mulenga, Davies Chibwili and Ireen Wishimanga warned that there would be no further adjournments after the Attorney General, Mulilo Kabesha, asked for more time to study the applicants' 32-paragraph affidavit and skeleton arguments. At that hearing Katolo argued that paragraph 59 of the National Prosecutions Authority's own manual recognises constitutional bail where trial proceedings are delayed, and raised concerns about the health of one of the applicants, who he said had missed appointments with a specialist abroad, according to the Monitor.

Solwezi sends the sedition case to Lusaka

The separate sedition case against Mundubile and Zulu is moving as well. Zambia Monitor reported on Friday 6 November that the Subordinate Court in Solwezi had ruled that Lusaka is the proper territorial forum, after examining sections 69, 70 and 72 of the Criminal Procedure Code and rejecting the argument that a livestream accessible across the country was enough to found jurisdiction in Solwezi. The court said that the mere assertion that the livestream was accessible nationwide did not, without more, establish the necessary connection, and declined to hear the matter there. The Monitor also reported that Zulu, a lawyer and vice-president of the Tonse Pamodzi Alliance, has asked the Lusaka High Court for leave to conduct his own case in the constitutional bail application, drawing a comparison in the report with the 1975 case of Shamwana v The People.

All 18 remain accused persons who have not been tried, and the bail application is about their detention pending trial, not about the charges themselves.

Confirmed vs. alleged

Confirmed: The jurisdiction ruling is set for Tuesday 10 November and the State raised a preliminary objection on Thursday 5 November (News Diggers). The three-judge panel, the 18 applicants held at Mukobeko and the Attorney General's earlier request for time (Zambia Monitor, 29 October). The Solwezi Subordinate Court's ruling that the sedition case belongs in Lusaka, and Zulu's request to represent himself (Zambia Monitor, 6 November).

Alleged or unverified: Katolo's characterisation of the State's position, that it does not want the application heard and fears the accused would be released, is the defence's account; the State's full argument was not visible in the report read and no response from the National Prosecutions Authority or the Attorney General to Katolo's remarks had been reported by Sunday morning. No date has been reported for the sedition case to begin in Lusaka.

Sources: News Diggers, “State doesn't want Mundubile's bail application heard – Lawyer” (7 Nov 2026); Zambia Monitor, “Mundubile, running mate to face sedition trial in Lusaka; Zulu seeks to represent self” (6 Nov 2026); Zambia Monitor, “Court rules no further adjournments in Mundubile, Zulu bail hearing” (29 Oct 2026).
