Kabulonga operation: former minister Kafwaya killed, opposition figures detained

A security operation at a Lusaka property days after the vote left former minister Mutotwe Kafwaya dead in what police described as an exchange of fire, and led to the detention of senior opposition figures — including, reportedly, Brian Mundubile found at the scene.

By Lusaka Brief Newsroom • 21 August 2026 • 6 min read

Archive note: Lusaka Brief launched in September 2026. This entry was added retrospectively so the record of a significant national moment is preserved. It is based on reporting and official statements that were publicly available at the time.

The days after the election turned violent in Lusaka's Kabulonga area, where a security operation on a property left former minister Mutotwe Kafwaya dead. Police said he was fatally wounded by law enforcement officers during what officials described as an exchange of fire.

Government said eleven people were held in the operation, described as a raid on a suspected militia, and opposition figures confirmed that Tonse Alliance leader Brian Mundubile was among those found at the scene. Police later summoned Mundubile for questioning.

The opposition has contested the official account, describing the operation as a raid on its officials days after a disputed election. The Tonse Alliance had claimed victory during the count, which the ECZ suspended before completing the declaration — a sequence that poisoned the atmosphere in which the operation took place.

What is confirmed: Kafwaya died during the operation; police acknowledge officers fired the fatal shots; eleven people were detained; Mundubile was summoned by police. What is contested: the characterisation of those at the property as a militia, and the necessity of the force used.

The killing of a former cabinet minister in a post-election security operation is without recent precedent in Zambia and has drawn international concern. Calls for an independent investigation have come from opposition parties, civil society and sections of the church. This archive entry will be updated if official inquiries produce findings.
