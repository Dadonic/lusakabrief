Health · Cholera

Fifty-six deaths and 2,658 suspected cases: cholera has added a thousand cases since late October, as the WHO offers help on water and the water minister calls in the NGOs

News Diggers reports Zambia National Public Health Institute figures of 98 new suspected cases and one death in 24 hours, taking the outbreak to 2,658 suspected cases, 351 confirmed and 56 deaths, with 138 people in hospital; on 28 October the toll stood at 1,622 cases and 30 deaths. WHO representative Dr Clement Lasuba told the Local Government ministry on Wednesday that most of Zambia's diseases are preventable and need more than one ministry; Elisha Matambo told the 65-member NGO WASH Forum that universal access needs all hands.

By Lusaka Brief Health Desk • LUSAKA — 13 November 2026 • 2 min read

Zambia's cholera outbreak has now killed 56 people, according to Zambia National Public Health Institute figures reported by News Diggers on Thursday 12 November. The institute recorded 98 new suspected cases and one death in the previous 24 hours, with four cases laboratory-confirmed, taking the cumulative count to 2,658 suspected cases, 351 confirmed and 56 deaths. Some 138 patients were in hospital and 2,464 had recovered and been discharged.

The figures mark a sharp acceleration. On 30 October, News Diggers reported that the Health Minister, Professor Roma Chilengi, had told Parliament of 1,622 cases and 30 deaths as of 28 October, across 20 districts in six provinces, with Lusaka Province accounting for 1,090 cases and 16 of the deaths occurring in communities before patients reached a health facility. In a little over two weeks the case count has risen by more than a thousand and the death toll has almost doubled.

News Diggers also reports that the Copperbelt provincial health director, Dr Charles Mwinuna, has disputed reports that two people died of suspected cholera in Mpongwe, saying the province had recorded only three cholera cases. None of the reports gives a district breakdown of the latest national figures.

Beyond the Ministry of Health

The World Health Organization's representative in Zambia, Dr Clement Lasuba, paid a courtesy call on the Ministry of Local Government on Wednesday 11 November and said the WHO was ready to work with the government to strengthen water and sanitation interventions against cholera, News Diggers reports. Most diseases in Zambia are preventable, he said, and dealing with them needs many stakeholders, because health challenges begin at household and community level before they become district, provincial and national concerns.

When we think of health primarily, we think it is the responsibility of the Ministry of Health.
— Dr Clement Lasuba, WHO Representative to Zambia, at the Ministry of Local Government on Wednesday 11 November, as reported by News Diggers

On the same day, the Times of Zambia reports, the Minister of Water Development and Sanitation, Elisha Matambo, received the national coordinator of the NGO WASH Forum, which has 65 member organisations working in all ten provinces, at his Lusaka office. He said partnerships with civil society were key to expanding access to water, sanitation and hygiene services, and that universal access requires the "collective efforts of Government, cooperating partners, civil society organisations", together with the private sector and communities.

Taken together, the three reports show the response widening from treatment centres to the ministries that control water supply, drainage and local markets, the ground on which cholera is won or lost. What none of them yet gives is a fresh count of affected districts, or a date by which the institute expects the daily case numbers to turn.

Sources: News Diggers, “Cholera death toll rises to 56” (12 Nov 2026); News Diggers, “WHO pledges support towards cholera eradication” (12 Nov 2026); Times of Zambia, “Government calls for stronger partnerships to improve WASH services” (12 Nov 2026); News Diggers, “MoH records 1,622 cholera cases, 30 deaths” (30 Oct 2026).
