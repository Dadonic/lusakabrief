Kampala falls flat: the Queens lead early, lose 2-1 at Nakivubo — and the Olympic road now runs through Ndola on Tuesday

The Copper Queens took an early lead at Hamz Stadium on Thursday but ended the night beaten 2-1 by Uganda's Crested Cranes in the first leg of their 2028 Olympic qualifier. The return leg is at Levy Mwanawasa Stadium in Ndola on Tuesday, 13 October — Zambia must win it to keep Los Angeles in view.

By Mwelwa Phiri • KAMPALA — 9 October 2026 • 4 min read

The redemption tour has taken a detour through the hardest away day in the region. The Copper Queens were beaten 2-1 by Uganda's Crested Cranes at Hamz Stadium, Nakivubo, on Thursday night, in the first leg of their second-round qualifier for the 2028 Los Angeles Olympic Games — a result that flips the tie on its head after Zambia struck first inside the opening six minutes.

The early goal seemed to vindicate a confident week. Captain Barbra Banda had declared that qualifying was "not negotiable"; interim coach Charles Haalubono, in his first competitive match since taking over after the WAFCON disappointment, had promised Zambia would put the ball down and play. Uganda, under a coach appointed only days before — the former Ethiopia handler Asefa Firew Hailegebreal — instead produced the comeback the home crowd demanded, turning the match around before the final whistle. The details of the decisive goals were still being collated by the Zambian camp's own channels at press time; what is certain is the scoreline and what it obliges.

The obligation is simple: win on Tuesday. The return leg is at Levy Mwanawasa Stadium in Ndola on 13 October, where Zambia must overturn a one-goal deficit against a side that has historically been awkward for them — the Crested Cranes have never lost this fixture, including a 5-0 friendly win in 2022 that still stings in the camp's memory. The winner on aggregate advances to face Ghana or Mauritius, two steps from the Olympic tournament, where Africa has two places in the sixteen-team field.

The frame around Tuesday is heavier than one tie. A generation that carried Zambian women's football to a World Cup spent 2026 learning that reputation wins nothing — a failed WAFCON campaign, a dismissed coach, an interim bench. Ndola now offers the fastest possible reply: ninety minutes at home, a one-goal hill, and a crowd that has waited months for something to roar about. Banda's word was non-negotiable. Tuesday is where it gets enforced.
