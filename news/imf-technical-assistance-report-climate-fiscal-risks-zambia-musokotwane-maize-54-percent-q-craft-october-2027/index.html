A 54 per cent maize collapse and 23-hour blackouts, written into the books: the IMF credits Zambia with budgeting for climate risk and sets an October 2027 deadline

An IMF technical assistance report on climate change and fiscal risks, dated October 2026 and publicised by the Treasury on Monday 16 November, says Zambia has made notable progress in writing climate shocks into public financial management. It records that the 2023–24 drought cut maize output by 54 per cent and pushed load-shedding to 23 hours a day, and asks for a quantified climate fiscal risk assessment by October 2027 and coverage of floods and storms by October 2028.

By Lusaka Brief Business Desk • LUSAKA — 17 November 2026 • 2 min read

The International Monetary Fund has given Zambia credit for the way it now accounts for drought, and set it two deadlines. Its technical assistance report, 'Zambia – Climate Change and Fiscal Risks', dated October 2026 and publicised by the Ministry of Finance and National Planning on Monday 16 November, says the country has made notable progress in integrating climate risks into public financial management, with better transparency, coordination between institutions and capacity to assess what climate shocks do to the economy. The Zambia Monitor and the Zambian Observer both reported the document and the minister's statement on Monday evening.

The report's starting point is the 2023–24 drought. It records that maize production fell by 54 per cent and that load-shedding reached 23 hours a day, and it puts drought-related spending at about 1 per cent of GDP in 2024 and 0.3 per cent in 2025. With hydropower making up about 80 per cent of installed generating capacity, it says, agriculture and mining remain exposed to prolonged dry spells, though it acknowledges the government's efforts to add solar and wind capacity.

What has been built, and what is asked

The IMF singles out the Fiscal Risk Statement, first published with the 2025 budget, as a major step in transparency, and notes that the 2026 statement added a climate fiscal risk register covering agriculture, electricity generation, mining, infrastructure and public revenue and spending. During a mission to Lusaka from 24 June to 3 July 2026, Fund staff worked with Zambian officials on a tool known as Q-CRAFT, the Quantitative Climate Risk Assessment Fiscal Tool, which models how climate scenarios would affect growth, revenue, expenditure and public debt.

The recommendations are dated. Zambia should publish its first quantified climate-related fiscal risk assessment by October 2027, and by October 2028 should strengthen its drought analysis and extend the assessment to floods and storms. The report also asks for a better risk register, clearer institutional responsibilities, stronger coordination between agencies and the alignment of adaptation investment with long-term financing.

Finance and National Planning Minister Situmbeko Musokotwane tied the report to the 2027 budget. "Our transition from economic stabilisation to sustained growth demands stronger protection against climate shocks," he said in a statement on Monday, adding that "climate resilience is therefore integral to fiscal resilience and central to the 2027 National Budget theme", which is 'Grow Zambia: From Stabilisation to Unlocking Opportunities, Creating Jobs and Improving Livelihoods for All'. Neither report quoted IMF staff directly, and the report is a technical assistance document rather than a programme review.

Sources: Zambia Monitor, “IMF hails Zambia's progress in managing climate-related fiscal risks” (16 Nov 2026); Zambian Observer, “IMF RECOGNIZES ZAMBIA'S PROGRESS IN PROTECTING THE ECONOMY AGAINST CLIMATE SHOCKS” (16 Nov 2026).
