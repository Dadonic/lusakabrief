A former ambassador to Switzerland is charged with treason: police say Martha Mwitumwa, 59, plotted with persons unknown between November 2025 and 23 October 2026

The Zambia Police Service said on Monday 9 November that it had arrested and charged Martha Lungu Mwitumwa, who represented Zambia in Geneva from 2019, with treason under Section 43(1) of the Penal Code. She is in custody awaiting a court appearance. The charge is an allegation; she has not entered a plea and no response from her or a lawyer has been reported. She becomes the latest person charged with treason since the August election, after the 18 accused in the Mundubile case.

By Lusaka Brief Courts Desk • LUSAKA — 10 November 2026 • 4 min read

A retired diplomat who spent two years as Zambia's ambassador in Switzerland has been charged with treason. Police spokesperson Chipo Kaitisha said in a statement issued in Lusaka on Monday 9 November that Martha Lungu Mwitumwa, 59, had been formally arrested and charged with the offence under Section 43(1) of the Penal Code, Chapter 87 of the Laws of Zambia, Zambia Monitor, News Diggers and The Zambian Observer reported. All three outlets carried the same account of the police statement.

According to that statement, as reported by the three outlets, the alleged offence took place in Lusaka District between November 2025 and 23 October 2026. Police say Ms Mwitumwa acted jointly with other people whose identities are not known and prepared, or endeavoured to carry out, an unlawful plan to usurp the executive power of the State. The statement gave no detail of what the plan is alleged to have involved, named no co-accused and did not say when she was arrested.

The suspect is currently detained in police custody and is awaiting appearance before the courts of law.
— Chipo Kaitisha, Zambia Police Service spokesperson, in a statement issued in Lusaka on Monday 9 November and quoted by The Zambian Observer

Who she is

Ms Mwitumwa was Zambia's Permanent Representative to the United Nations Office at Geneva and ambassador to Switzerland from 2019 to 2021, Zambia Monitor reported. The Observer, which carried a Kalemba report, said she took up the Geneva posting in May 2019 after more than thirty years in government service, with accreditation that also covered international organisations in Vienna, and that her earlier posts included Director for State Functions, Protocol and Public Relations at Cabinet Office, Director for Gender in Development at the Ministry of Gender from 2012 to 2017, and before that a district administrative officer and then district commissioner between 2009 and 2012. She began her career in teaching.

Zambia Monitor added one piece of recent history: in August 2026, during the election period, police detained Ms Mwitumwa and a campaign blogger at a campaign media centre. The government said at the time that the two had been taken in to assist with investigations, and both were later released. None of Monday's reports said whether that episode is connected to the new charge.

Where the charge sits

Treason charges have been brought repeatedly since the August general election. Human Rights Watch said in a report published on 1 November that 18 people had been charged with treason in the post-election period, two of whom also face sedition charges; the best known are the opposition presidential candidate Brian Mundubile and his running mate Makebi Zulu, two of 18 applicants held at Mukobeko whose constitutional bail application before a three-judge panel of the Lusaka High Court was listed for a final hearing on 5 November after repeated adjournments, with a ruling to follow, Zambia Monitor reported last month. The same report documented 71 cases of what it called arbitrary arrest or prolonged detention; the government has rejected figures of that kind before, and Zambia Monitor reported in September that the Foreign Minister, Mulambo Haimbe, had said official records did not support the detention figures cited by the European Parliament and that the courts, not the executive, decide bail and trial dates.

The government's broader position was restated on Friday 6 November by the Home Affairs Minister, Dr Clement Andeleki, who warned that the state would not tolerate anyone trying to throw the country into disarray and said Zambia was not a banana republic, News Diggers reported. The Tonse-Pamodzi Alliance's acting secretary general, Muhabi Lungu, answered on Monday that the government should not strip citizens of their rights while claiming to enforce the law, and said the alliance had acted within the Constitution. Neither statement referred to Ms Mwitumwa.

Nothing in Monday's reporting records a response from Ms Mwitumwa, her family or a lawyer, and no court date had been published by Monday night. If her case follows the path of the 18 earlier accused, the first contested question will be bail: their application, made to the High Court, was adjourned repeatedly through October and is still awaiting a ruling.

What the police statement says, and what it does not

  • Charge — treason, Section 43(1) of the Penal Code, Chapter 87.
  • Period alleged — November 2025 to 23 October 2026, in Lusaka District.
  • Alleged conduct — preparing or endeavouring, jointly with persons unknown, to usurp the executive power of the State by unlawful means.
  • Not stated — the date of arrest, what the plan is alleged to have been, the identity of any co-accused, or when she will appear in court.

Confirmed vs. alleged

Confirmed: That the police have charged Martha Lungu Mwitumwa, 59, with treason under Section 43(1), that the alleged period is November 2025 to 23 October 2026, that she is in custody awaiting a court appearance, and that she served as ambassador in Geneva from 2019 — all from the police statement as reported by Zambia Monitor, News Diggers and The Zambian Observer, and from the career details in the Observer.

Alleged or unverified: Everything about the alleged plot itself: what it involved, who else was supposedly party to it and what evidence the police hold. Whether the August detention is connected. Ms Mwitumwa's response. She is presumed innocent; a charge is an allegation that the State must prove in court.
