Economy · 2027 Budget

'Not solely on how much revenue it generates': the UNDP asks the Treasury to shield poor households from the 2027 budget's taxes, as farmers decry the mobile money levy

At the launch of a 2027 Budget Highlights Report in Lusaka on Monday 16 November, UNDP resident representative James Wakiaga urged government to keep VAT zero-rating on basic foods and to cushion low-income households against higher VAT, a doubled mobile money levy and new tariffs. The ZNFU says it received the budget with 'heightened anxiety'; News Diggers has set out the levy bands and a passport fee rising to K1,000.

By Lusaka Brief Business Desk • LUSAKA — 17 November 2026 • 3 min read

The United Nations Development Programme has asked the government to think again about the revenue measures in the 2027 budget that fall most heavily on ordinary households. Launching a 2027 Budget Highlights Report in Lusaka on Monday 16 November, UNDP resident representative James Wakiaga said the budget marked a shift from stabilisation to growth, but that its tax measures needed careful implementation so as not to add to the financial pressure on low-income families and small businesses. The Zambia Monitor reported him listing the measures of concern as the increase in value added tax, the higher mobile money levy, new tariffs on selected essential commodities and the reduction of tax relief on energy-saving appliances.

"Fiscal policy should not be assessed solely on how much revenue it generates," Wakiaga said, arguing that it should also be judged by its effect on poverty, inequality and welfare. He asked the Treasury to keep basic foods zero-rated for VAT and to adopt targeted safeguards, including adjustments to social cash transfers, and said growth "should generate private sector investment, decent jobs for young people". He credited Zambia with restoring macroeconomic stability and dealing with unsustainable external debt, and said living standards were the next test. At the same event Chipengo Zulu, director of corporate affairs at Zambian Breweries, said excise duties on plastic bottles, bottle caps and industrial sugar would raise production costs and prices and weaken local manufacturers against imports.

What the levy would cost

News Diggers set out the mobile money proposal on Sunday 15 November: the levy would double in every band, so that a transaction of up to K150 would attract K0.64 instead of K0.32, and a transfer of between K5,000 and K10,000 would attract K16 instead of K8. The same report says an ordinary 32-page passport would cost K1,000 in 2027 instead of K666.68, with a new two-day premium service at K5,000; the fee was last revised in August 2008 and government attributes the increase to higher production costs. The Mobile Money Association of Zambia had already objected on 7 November, its president Chris Sichende telling the Zambia Monitor the higher levy could "erode the gains made so far in terms of financial inclusion expansion" and asking for wider consultation.

The farmers' union has made the same point. Harad Lungu, executive director of the Zambia National Farmers' Union, told the 2027 National Budget Symposium last week that "the Union has received the proposed 2027 national budget with caution and heightened anxiety", and said the levy increase would hurt farmers and discourage the use of digital financial services, News Diggers reported. The Civil Society for Poverty Reduction, in the same report, opposed turning the Farmer Input Support Programme into a loan scheme.

The kwacha

The debate comes with the currency under mild pressure. The Zambia Monitor reported on Monday that the kwacha closed the week to Friday 13 November just below K20.50 to the US dollar, a weekly loss of 0.67 per cent and the largest since the week ending 16 October, as demand for foreign exchange outweighed supply and the dollar held near its strongest level since May 2025 on expectations of tighter United States monetary policy. Neither report carried a government response to the UNDP's recommendations, and the levy and fee changes remain proposals for 2027 rather than measures in force.
