The cushion expires on Tuesday: government still undecided on fuel-tax relief as the October price review looms

Since April, suspended excise duty and zero-rated VAT have quietly subsidised every litre sold in Zambia — at a cost of roughly $200 million a quarter in forgone revenue. The relief lapses on 30 September, and as of this weekend, Lusaka had not said whether it will be renewed.

By Natasha Banda • LUSAKA — 27 September 2026 • 5 min read

Three days before the deadline, the answer is still no answer. The government says it has not yet decided whether to extend the temporary suspension of fuel taxes — the excise-duty holiday and VAT zero-rating on petrol and diesel that have padded every pump price in Zambia since April — whose current mandate expires on Tuesday, 30 September.

The measure's mechanics are simple, and so is its cost. Introduced at the start of April as a three-month shield against the global energy shock that followed the outbreak of war in the Gulf and the closure of the Strait of Hormuz, the relief was renewed for a further 90 days from 1 July through statutory instruments gazetted in June. The government estimated in April that the first quarter of relief alone would cost it about $200 million in forgone revenue. It has been renewed once. Whether it is renewed twice is, as of this weekend, an open question — one officials say remains under consideration.

What expiry would mean at the pump is arithmetic, not speculation. The Energy Regulation Board's September review held national uniform prices unchanged — petrol at K25.29 per litre, diesel at K26.86, kerosene at K27.02, Jet A-1 at K28.71 — and said stability was achieved partly because of the tax measures, alongside the regulator's price-smoothing mechanism. Strip the tax lines back into the build-up and, all else equal, the October review — due within hours of the waiver's lapse — would face upward pressure that smoothing alone may not absorb.

The timing is awkward in both directions. The case for letting the relief lapse: inflation is inside the Bank of Zambia's 6–8 per cent band and easing — 6.1 per cent in September — the kwacha has been steadier around K19 to the dollar than in the spring panic, and diesel at K26.86 is already 21 per cent below May's K33.99 peak. The Treasury, mid-IMF-programme and mid-budget-cycle, could use $200 million a quarter. The case for extending: transport costs feed directly into food prices, the recovery's 7 per cent growth has not yet reached the cost of living for most households, and a pump-price jump in the run-up to the festive season would hand the opposition a talking point that writes itself.

There is a third option the ministry has used before: extend quietly, by statutory instrument, a day or two before expiry, and let the ERB's October statement absorb the news. The precedent of 1 July — an extension announced at the eleventh hour — suggests the decision, whenever it lands, will come late and whole. Watch the gazette before you watch the pump.
