Brooms before breakfast: 150 commandos clear the stretch from the Universal Church to the flyover, and the council says more than 360 illegal stalls at Kamwala are gone

The Special Forces Day clean-up announced last week went ahead in Kamwala on Monday 9 November, led by Brigadier General Moses Shapwaya, The Zambian Observer reported. Lusaka City Council said in a statement the same day that it had demolished over 360 makeshift stalls at the Kamwala flyover after notices and reminders to their owners, and that the railway line had been secured, News Diggers reported. The Observer said traders had dismantled many of their own structures before the soldiers arrived.

By Lusaka Brief Politics Desk • LUSAKA — 10 November 2026 • 2 min read

The operation that the Special Forces commander promised the Mayor of Lusaka last week was carried out on Monday morning, 9 November. About 150 commandos of the Zambia Army Special Forces, working with enforcement teams from Lusaka City Council, cleared rubbish, blocked drains and makeshift structures from the stretch of Kamwala between the Universal Church and the FINDECO flyover, The Zambian Observer reported. Brigadier General Moses Shapwaya, the Special Forces Commander, led the exercise, which marked the unit's day and its theme for this year of elite service and community work.

The council's account, issued in a statement on Monday and reported by News Diggers on Tuesday 10 November, put a figure on what was removed. Lusaka City Council said it had demolished more than 360 illegal makeshift stalls put up at the Kamwala Flyover Bridge, that the demolition followed earlier notices and several reminders to the owners to take their structures down, and that the railway line through the area had also been secured. The Diggers report said the army's clean-up and the council's demolition were separate parts of the same day's work.

How it went

The Observer described a largely cooperative morning. Traders had been warned in advance, it said, and many pulled down their own shacks before the commandos arrived; the soldiers then split into squads with brooms, rakes and wheelbarrows. The paper quoted an unnamed local coordinator thanking traders for their compliance. It reported that the area known as the Blue Tents market was open and clean by the end, but gave no figure for the waste removed or the cost of the operation, and did not describe any confrontation.

The plan had been announced on Thursday 5 November, when Brigadier General Shapwaya called on the Mayor, Chitambala Mwewa, and offered 150 personnel for the Monday, the Observer reported at the time. The Mayor said the help would speed up the council's cleanliness drive and reinforce its campaign against disorder in the city. The Observer's report of Monday's operation noted, without naming anyone, that some political observers had questioned whether elite defence units should be used for municipal tasks.

The question that follows every clearance is whether the traders return. Neither Monday's reports nor the council's statement, as reported, said where the owners of the 360 stalls are expected to trade from now, or whether any of them will be allocated spaces elsewhere.
