A K93 million KCM deal, 40% of the referees' arrears paid and Asanović's debt stretched to 2028: the Times of Zambia sets out FAZ's own account of 18 months of balance-sheet repair

Eighteen months after Keith Mweemba's executive took office, a Times of Zambia feature lays out what the association says it has done about money: a five-year sponsorship with Konkola Copper Mines, a committee tasked with raising US$30 million in two years, a referees' debt being cleared in instalments and separations from two former coaches without severance. The figures are FAZ's.

By Lusaka Brief Sports Desk • LUSAKA — 11 November 2026 • 2 min read

The Football Association of Zambia has signed an extended five-year sponsorship with Konkola Copper Mines worth K93 million covering men's and women's competitions, on top of a K10.8 million KCM deal for the Copper Queens, the Times of Zambia reported on Tuesday 10 November in a feature on the association's finances since Keith Mweemba's executive committee took office in May 2025. The larger deal is expected to fund new cup competitions, including an FA Cup, the paper reported. The feature draws largely on Mr Mweemba's own account, and the figures in it are the association's rather than independently audited ones.

A Commercial and Marketing Advisory Committee has been given the job of mobilising about US$30 million over two years for football development, the Times reported. On the other side of the ledger, FAZ acknowledged in April 2026 a debt of about K7 million to lower-division referees; it says it has now cleared 40% of those arrears and expects to pay the remaining 60% before the new season starts.

We inherited outstanding financial obligations to referees
— Keith Mweemba, FAZ president, as quoted by the Times of Zambia on Tuesday 10 November

The coaches

Three former coaches' contracts account for much of the inherited liability. The obligations to the former Chipolopolo coach Avram Grant were resolved through a mutual separation with no severance payment, the Times reported, and about five months of salary arrears owed to the former Copper Queens coach Nora Häuptle were addressed, leading to what the paper called a mutually separated arrangement. The debt to Aljoša Asanović, another former Chipolopolo coach, has been restructured into a repayment plan that runs to 2028.

Mr Mweemba framed the aim as building an association that can generate sustainable revenue rather than relying on limited traditional funding, and said its success should not be measured by the number of announcements it makes, the Times reported. The feature appeared two days after President Hichilema, hosting the under-15 world champions at State House on Sunday 8 November, promised to invite the mining houses to a meeting to raise money for FAZ and to work with the association to dismantle a legacy debt that Open Zambia put at more than K140 million; he asked Mr Mweemba to put the association's concerns in writing and urged it to avoid new borrowing.

FAZ's figures, as reported by the Times of Zambia

  • K93 million — five-year KCM sponsorship for men's and women's competitions.
  • K10.8 million — KCM deal for the Copper Queens.
  • US$30 million — two-year fundraising target for the new advisory committee.
  • K7 million — referees' arrears acknowledged in April 2026; 40% paid.
  • 2028 — end of the repayment plan for the Asanović debt.

Sources: Times of Zambia, “Off-the-pitch success by FAZ” (10 Nov 2026); Open Zambia, “President Hichilema turns to mining companies to help clear FAZ's K140 million debt” (9 Nov 2026).
