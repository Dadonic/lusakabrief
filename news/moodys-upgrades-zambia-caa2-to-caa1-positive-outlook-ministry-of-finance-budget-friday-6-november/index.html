One notch up, three days before the budget: Moody's lifts Zambia from Caa2 to Caa1 with a positive outlook, and the Treasury says the rating is a means, not an end

Moody's Ratings raised Zambia's sovereign credit rating to Caa1 on Friday 30 October and kept the outlook positive, the third agency to move the country up since the 2020 default. The Ministry of Finance and National Planning welcomed the decision on Monday 2 November, crediting debt restructuring, fiscal consolidation and a steadier economy, and warning that the rating must be turned into investment and jobs. Situmbeko Musokotwane presents the 2027 budget on Friday 6 November, a week later than first planned.

By Lusaka Brief Business Desk • LUSAKA — 3 November 2026 • 3 min read

Zambia's credit standing improved by one step on Friday 30 October, when Moody's Ratings raised the government's sovereign rating from Caa2 to Caa1 and kept its outlook at positive. The Times of Zambia, Zambia Monitor and the Zambian Observer all reported the decision on Monday 2 November, the day the Ministry of Finance and National Planning issued a statement welcoming it. Caa1 remains far down the agency's scale, but the direction matters: a positive outlook signals that a further upgrade is possible if the fiscal and economic reforms behind this one are sustained.

The Ministry's statement, as reported by Zambia Monitor and the Observer, attributed the upgrade to progress on three fronts, the restructuring of the debt on which Zambia defaulted in 2020, fiscal consolidation and macroeconomic stabilisation, and noted that S&P Global Ratings and Fitch Ratings had already moved the country up for similar reasons. Moody's, in the Observer's account, pointed to a substantial and sustained improvement in the trajectory of government debt.

What the Treasury says it expects

The Ministry listed the benefits it hopes will follow: stronger investor confidence, lower borrowing costs over time, more foreign direct investment and support for a stable exchange rate. It also set a limit on the celebration. Zambia Monitor quoted the statement as saying the rating should not be treated as an end in itself but as an opportunity to attract productive investment and raise living standards. The priority under President Hakainde Hichilema's Grow Zambia Agenda, the Ministry said, is to convert stabilisation into higher investment, production, exports and employment.

The upgrade arrives three weeks after the staff-level agreement reached with the International Monetary Fund on Friday 9 October for a proposed 36-month Extended Credit Facility worth US$1.472 billion, which still needs the approval of the IMF Executive Board. The Zambian Observer and Zambia Monitor both placed the Moody's decision in that sequence.

A budget pushed back a week

The rating lands in budget week. Finance and National Planning Minister Situmbeko Musokotwane is to present the 2027 National Budget to Parliament on Friday 6 November. News Diggers reported on Wednesday 28 October that Vice-President Mutale Nalumango had told the House the presentation was moving from Friday 30 October to 6 November because work on the budget was still under way, and that the Speaker, Nelly Mutti, would then suspend business for ten working days so that sector budgets could be analysed, with the House returning on Tuesday 24 November.

The postponement was announced as the National Assembly approved the restructuring of government ministries that took the number of portfolios to 30. Shiwang'andu MP Stephen Kampyongo, in an interview carried by News Diggers on Thursday 29 October, said the delay would leave lawmakers limited time to scrutinise the spending plans before approving them, and that a larger set of ministries sat uneasily with the government's earlier promise of a lean administration, while allowing that the delay was understandable given the restructuring.

The sequence

  • 9 October — IMF staff-level agreement on a proposed US$1.472 billion, 36-month Extended Credit Facility, subject to Board approval
  • 28 October — Parliament approves the restructuring to 30 ministries; budget presentation moved to 6 November
  • 30 October — Moody's upgrades Zambia from Caa2 to Caa1, outlook positive
  • 2 November — Ministry of Finance and National Planning statement welcomes the upgrade
  • 6 November — 2027 National Budget presented; House then suspends for ten working days, resuming 24 November

Confirmed vs. alleged

Confirmed: The upgrade from Caa2 to Caa1 with a positive outlook, dated Friday 30 October, and the Ministry of Finance and National Planning statement of Monday 2 November are reported by the Times of Zambia, Zambia Monitor and the Zambian Observer. The 9 October IMF staff-level agreement and its US$1.472 billion value are as reported by Zambia Monitor and the Observer. The budget date of Friday 6 November is reported by the Times of Zambia and Zambia Monitor; the reasons for the postponement are as reported by News Diggers.

Alleged or unverified: This edition relies on press reports of the Ministry's statement rather than the full Moody's rating action, so the agency's complete reasoning, and any conditions it attached to the positive outlook, are not set out here. The effect on Zambia's borrowing costs is an expectation, not yet a measured outcome. What the 2027 budget will contain is unknown until Friday.
