Seven percent bigger: Treasury tables a K271.9 billion spending plan for 2027 — and a copper ladder that must finally reach a million tonnes

Total expenditure is projected at K271.9 billion next year, revenue at K218.6 billion and borrowing at K44.9 billion, the finance ministry told public hearings on Monday. Growth this year is seen at 5.3 percent — below the 6.4 percent promised a year ago — and copper output must climb from 890,346 tonnes to 1.08 million in 2027.

By Natasha Banda • LUSAKA — 7 October 2026 • 5 min read

The 2027 budget has a silhouette, and it is seven percent larger than this year's. The Ministry of Finance and National Planning told public hearings on Monday — convened ahead of the formal budget presentation — that total expenditure next year is projected at K271.9 billion (about US$13.8 billion), with revenue expected to rise about six percent to K218.6 billion.

The gap will be financed the way Zambian gaps are: borrowing of K44.9 billion, of which K37.3 billion is domestic and K7.7 billion external. That is up from K34.5 billion of borrowing this year — a near thirty percent jump in the state's appetite that will compete with the private sector for the same kwacha pool, at a time when the currency has given back roughly three percent over the past month to trade near K19.7 to the dollar.

Inside the framework is a confession. Economic growth this year is now expected at 5.3 percent — respectable, but short of the 6.4 percent projected when the 2026 budget was presented a year ago. And the copper ledger, on which so much of the Grow Zambia Agenda rests, records 890,346 tonnes for 2025 against a target of one million. The ministry's ladder out of that miss: 1.08 million tonnes in 2027, 1.25 million in 2028 and 1.40 million in 2029. Each rung assumes mine expansions already announced actually reach production — from Konkola's revival to the newer Solwezi projects — on schedule, with power to match.

The hearings now move through the country, and the submissions will say much about the cost-of-living politics of a bigger state: civil society will argue for social protection lines that survive contact with the drought forecast; business will argue that a K44.9 billion borrowing plan cannot crowd out credit just as the corridor investments need financing. The formal speech belongs to Finance Minister Situmbeko Musokotwane. The arithmetic, as of Monday, belongs to everyone.
