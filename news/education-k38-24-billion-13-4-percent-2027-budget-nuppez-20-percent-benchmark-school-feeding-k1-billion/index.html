K38.24 billion and still short of a fifth: a teachers' union says education's 13.4% of the 2027 budget is not enough, as News Diggers asks whether pupils are learning

The National Union of Public and Private Educators of Zambia welcomed the rise in the education allocation from K33.04 billion to K38.24 billion but said its 13.4% share of the K284.8 billion budget falls below the 20% international benchmark, News Diggers reported on Sunday 8 November. On the published figures, debt service will take K67.6 billion. School feeding is to rise from K523 million to K1 billion.

By Lusaka Brief Business Desk • LUSAKA — 9 November 2026 • 3 min read

A complaint about the spending side of the 2027 budget has come from the classroom. The National Union of Public and Private Educators of Zambia (NUPPEZ) said in a statement on Saturday 7 November that the 13.4% of the budget going to education is below the 20% it described as the internationally recognised threshold, News Diggers reported on Sunday 8 November. The union's executive president, Charity Kampamba, began by welcoming the increase: Finance Minister Dr Situmbeko Musokotwane proposed K38.24 billion for the sector on Friday 6 November, up from K33.04 billion in the 2026 budget.

The arithmetic is straightforward. K38.24 billion is 13.4% of the K284.8 billion total, and the rise on last year is K5.2 billion, a little under 16%. A fifth of the 2027 budget would be about K57 billion, so the distance between the allocation and the union's benchmark is in the region of K18.7 billion. Those last three figures are this desk's calculations from the published numbers, not the union's.

News Diggers took up the theme in its editorial on Monday 9 November, under the headline "Govt must make K38.2bn count in classrooms". Free education has brought millions of children through the school gate, the paper wrote, and the question now is whether they are learning. It reported that Ms Kampamba had said the allocation was insufficient for the sector's growing demands and that the union had questioned the absence of a provision it expected to see; the paper's account of that point is behind its paywall.

What else the education line sits beside

Other readings of the same budget help place the number. Education and health together receive K68.5 billion, about 24% of proposed spending, and funding for school feeding nearly doubles from K523 million to K1 billion, according to economist Lubinda Haabazoka's analysis as reported by Zambia Monitor on 7 November. He argued that the feeding money could ease the burden on families and give local farmers who supply schools a reliable market. Health's share is K30.3 billion, according to an analysis by The People's Brief published in The Zambian Observer on Sunday 8 November.

The same analysis gives the comparison that frames the union's complaint: debt service is put at K67.6 billion, about 23.7% of the budget, which is nearly as much as education and health together and about K29 billion more than education alone. Domestic revenue is expected to cover K233.7 billion, or 82.1% of the total, up from 58.7% in 2021, it said. The government's argument, in the minister's words quoted there, is that "there are no donors to pay, we must pay ourselves".

Neither the union's statement as reported nor the editorial sets out how a 20% share would be financed, and none of the reports read for this edition records a reply from the Ministry of Finance or the Ministry of Education. The People's Brief argued that Parliament should scrutinise the proposals before they take effect on 1 January 2027.

The education number in context

  • Education, 2027: K38.24 billion, 13.4% of K284.8 billion (News Diggers)
  • Education, 2026: K33.04 billion (News Diggers)
  • NUPPEZ benchmark: 20% of the budget, about K57 billion on the 2027 total (calculation)
  • Health, 2027: K30.3 billion (The Zambian Observer); education and health together K68.5 billion (Zambia Monitor)
  • School feeding: K523 million to K1 billion (Zambia Monitor)
  • Debt service: K67.6 billion, about 23.7% (The Zambian Observer)
