'Housekeeper and personal assistant': former ambassador Martha Mwitumwa petitions the High Court over her treason arrest and an August 'abduction', saying she was held four days

Martha Lungu Mwitumwa, 59, formerly ambassador to Switzerland, has asked the Lusaka High Court to order her immediate release, arguing that her treason arrest this month and what she calls an abduction in August breached her constitutional rights, News Diggers and the Zambian Observer report. The petition names the Attorney General and says she was held four days in August and questioned about Brian Mundubile, for whom she says she worked only as housekeeper and personal assistant. Police charged her on 9 November with plotting to usurp executive power.

By Lusaka Brief Courts Desk • LUSAKA — 19 November 2026 • 2 min read

A former ambassador charged with treason has turned to the High Court to challenge not only her arrest this month but an episode in August that her family at the time described as an abduction. Martha Lungu Mwitumwa, who served as Zambia's ambassador to Switzerland and permanent representative to the United Nations offices in Geneva and Vienna, has petitioned the Lusaka High Court for her immediate release, the Zambian Observer reported on Tuesday 17 November and News Diggers on Wednesday 18 November. The petition names the Attorney General as respondent.

According to News Diggers' report, Mwitumwa says she was held for four days in August and questioned about her ties to Brian Mundubile, the NRPUP presidential candidate who is himself detained on treason charges. The paper reports that she explained she was his housekeeper and personal assistant. The Zambian Observer's account is the same: her association with Mundubile, she says, was limited to those two roles, and she argues that both the August detention and her November arrest violated her constitutional rights.

The charge

The Zambia Police Service announced her arrest on Monday 9 November. In a statement issued by its spokesperson, Chipo Kaitisha, and reported by News Diggers that evening, the police said they had formally arrested and charged Mwitumwa, 59, with treason under section 43(1) of the Penal Code, alleging that between November 2025 and 23 October 2026, in Lusaka district, she acted with unidentified people to prepare an unlawful plan aimed at usurping the executive power of the State. No further detail of the alleged plan has been reported. She is one of several people arrested on treason-related allegations since the August election.

What happened in August

The August episode entered the public record through an open letter from Mwitumwa's husband to the Inspector General of Police, Graphel Musamba, published by the Zambian Observer on 10 August. The letter said that on the morning of Sunday 9 August Mwitumwa and a companion, Hanel Katontoka, left home for a church service in Libala, that Mwitumwa made a distress call saying three unmarked white vehicles were following her near the InterContinental Hotel, that shots were fired and the car was forced to stop, and that police stations across Lusaka had no record of either woman in custody. The family described both women as members of the NRPUP campaign team and said it suspected political involvement. None of those claims has been tested in court, and no police account of the August events has been reported.

Confirmed vs. alleged

Confirmed: Mwitumwa was arrested and charged with treason on 9 November, per the police statement reported by News Diggers. She has petitioned the Lusaka High Court for release, naming the Attorney General, per News Diggers and the Zambian Observer. Her stated relationship with Mundubile is that of housekeeper and personal assistant.

Alleged or unverified: Her account of a four-day detention and questioning in August, and her family's description of an abduction by unmarked vehicles, are allegations in a petition and an open letter; the State's response has not been reported. The substance of the alleged plan is not public. Charged is not guilty: she is presumed innocent.
