From an operating loss to K6.3 billion after tax: Kapala hails ZESCO's turnaround on his first visit as Electricity Minister, and the utility's own report trims his K6.5 billion

Peter Kapala, the first holder of the new Electricity portfolio, visited ZESCO's head office on Wednesday 11 November and praised what a ministry statement called a K6.5 billion profit last year, crediting government reforms. ZESCO's 2025 Integrated Report records a group profit after tax of K6.3 billion on revenue of K32.7 billion, up from K30.8 billion, and an operating profit of K11.2 billion against an operating loss of K8.7 billion in 2024. Customers have grown from about 1.2 million in 2022 to about 1.5 million.

By Lusaka Brief Business Desk • LUSAKA — 13 November 2026 • 2 min read

The Minister of Electricity, Peter Kapala, used a familiarisation visit to ZESCO's head office on Wednesday 11 November to congratulate the utility on its return to profit. News Diggers, citing a statement from his ministry, reports that Kapala commended ZESCO for declaring a K6.5 billion profit last year after years of financial difficulty, and attributed the improvement to the government's policies and reforms in the electricity sector.

Green Building Africa, reporting the same visit, quotes Kapala calling the result "an encouraging improvement in the Corporation's financial performance". He met the ZESCO board, led by its chairperson Vickson Ncube, and the executive team under managing director Justin Loongo. Ncube said the utility had aligned its priorities with the Grow Zambia Agenda and its target of 10,000 megawatts of installed capacity by 2031; Loongo credited the better numbers to an expanded generation portfolio and cited the country's 1,000 MW solar milestone. ZESCO's customer base, the outlet reports, has grown from about 1.2 million in 2022 to about 1.5 million in 2026. Sitali Liyoko, the director for transmission, operations and trade, presented the roadmap to 10,000 MW.

What the accounts say

ZESCO's own 2025 Integrated Report, covering the year to 31 December 2025, gives a slightly different headline figure from the one in the ministry's statement. The finance director's performance overview records a group profit after tax of K6.3 billion. Group revenue rose 6 per cent, from K30.8 billion in 2024 to K32.7 billion, and operating profit was K11.2 billion against an operating loss of K8.7 billion the year before. The report describes 2024 as a loss-making year without stating the net figure.

The K200 million gap between the minister's K6.5 billion and the report's K6.3 billion is not explained in either account; the report's figure is for the group, and the ministry statement does not say whether it refers to the company or the group, or to profit before or after tax. Both numbers point the same way, and the operating line is the more telling: a swing of almost K20 billion in a single year.

The ministry and the utility

Kapala is the first holder of the newly established Ministry of Electricity, and Ncube congratulated him on the appointment, Green Building Africa reports. The visit was, in the utility's own framing as reported by Green Building Africa, a chance to brief the new minister on performance and to discuss expanding access and reliability for households, businesses and industry. The profit is being presented by the government as evidence that its reforms worked, and by ZESCO as the product of more generation. Neither report addresses the question that customers will ask first: whether a profitable utility will mean fewer load-shedding hours.

Sources: News Diggers, “Govt hails ZESCO's K6.5bn profit turnaround” (12 Nov 2026); Green Building Africa, “ZESCO returns to profit as Zambia's new Electricity Minister welcomes improved financial performance” (12 Nov 2026); ZESCO Limited, “ZESCO Integrated Report 2025” (2026).
