Fuel prices hold for September — and it's the suspended taxes doing the work

Petrol stays at K25.29 and diesel at K26.86 despite Middle East pressure on oil prices. The ERB's build-up shows why: excise duty suspended, VAT zero-rated, and a smoothing mechanism absorbing the shock.

By Lusaka Brief Newsroom • 1 September 2026 • 4 min read

The Energy Regulation Board has kept pump prices unchanged for September: petrol at K25.29, diesel at K26.86, kerosene at K27.02 and Jet A-1 at K28.71 per litre. ERB board chairperson James Banda said international prices came under upward pressure from Middle East geopolitical tensions and exchange-rate movements — but the board held the line.

The mechanism matters more than the headline. Excise duty on fuel remains suspended and VAT zero-rated — both appear as dashes, not numbers, on the ERB's September price build-up — and the regulator's price-smoothing mechanism absorbed the rest.

The year's real story

Diesel hit K33.99 in May after a global oil shock, and has fallen every month since until this hold. September's price is 21% below that peak — and Zambia now has the cheapest diesel among the region's tracked markets, at roughly US$1.41 a litre against South Africa's US$1.81 wholesale.

September 2026 pump prices

  • Petrol: K25.29 (unchanged)
  • Diesel: K26.86 (unchanged)
  • Kerosene: K27.02 · Jet A-1: K28.71
  • Uniform nationally — same price in Nakonde as in Lusaka
