Solar rows and a copper shaft: Britain's Africa trade commissioner ends a five-day Zambia pitch today

Alastair Long's Zambia programme closes on Friday after a week that took the UK Trade Commissioner for Africa from a GreenCo solar site to First Quantum's Trident mine — with the Green Growth Compact and British International Investment's strategy doing the framing, and a Terzeon reception doing the networking.

By Natasha Banda • LUSAKA — 2 October 2026 • 4 min read

The most consequential British visit to Zambia this year ends not with a signing ceremony but with a departure lounge. Alastair Long, the UK Trade Commissioner for Africa, completes a five-day Zambia programme on Friday — a week that began with Monday's arrival courtesies and a reception hosted by the British High Commission at Terzeon, and then went deliberately to the two places London wants its Zambia story told from: a solar generation site in the GreenCo portfolio, and First Quantum Minerals' Trident copper mine in North-Western Province.

The itinerary is the message. The solar leg aligns the visit with the UK–Zambia Green Growth Compact, the framework under which Britain has promised to back Zambia's energy transition with investment rather than communiqués; the Trident leg takes the commissioner inside the single largest British-linked investment story in the country, at a moment when copper's price and Zambia's output targets are both pointing the same direction. Between the two sits British International Investment, whose Africa strategy — patient capital into power, infrastructure and growth-stage firms — is the instrument London reaches for when a visit is meant to outlast its own headlines.

ZNBC's coverage of the week has tracked the official framing: green growth, mutual prosperity, and a relationship being retooled from aid arithmetic to investment arithmetic. What the week has not produced is a headline figure — no round billion to match the Abu Dhabi courtship that dominated the same news cycle. The commissioner's office would call that the point: trade diplomacy measured in site visits and pipelines rather than signing ceremonies. The test, as with every courtship this season, is the follow-through — and on that, the Green Growth Compact's delivery ledger is the document to watch when the next budget is laid.

Sources: ZNBC coverage of the UK Trade Commissioner's visit and the Green Growth Compact (Sep–Oct 2026); British High Commission Lusaka programme announcements (Sep 2026); GreenCo and First Quantum Minerals public materials on the sites visited.
