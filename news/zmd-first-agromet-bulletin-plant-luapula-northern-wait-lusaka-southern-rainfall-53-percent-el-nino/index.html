Plant in Kawambwa, wait in Lusaka: the Met Department's first bulletin of the season clears ten northern districts to sow, tells 42 to hold off and warns of a short southern season

The Zambia Meteorological Department's first agrometeorological bulletin of 2026/27, covering 13 to 26 November and reported by the Zambia Monitor, puts the country at a yellow 'Get Ready' level. Rain since 1 October stands at 125% of the long-term average in Northern Province and 110% in Luapula, but only 63% in Southern, 53% in Lusaka and 52% in Central. Ten Luapula, Northern and North-Western districts may plant now; all of Lusaka and Southern and most of Central and Eastern are told to wait, with little rain expected before 26 November.

By Lusaka Brief Business Desk • LUSAKA — 16 November 2026 • 3 min read

Zambia's rainy season has started, but only in the north, and the Meteorological Department's first planting advisory of the year tells most of the country to keep its seed in the bag. The Zambia Monitor reported on Sunday 15 November on the department's first Agrometeorological Bulletin of the 2026/27 season, which covers the fortnight from 13 to 26 November and places the country at a yellow advisory level, labelled Get Ready. The El Niño warning behind it has been building since October, when News Diggers reported the department urging farmers to spread their risk across drought-tolerant crops.

The bulletin's rainfall figures, as reported by the Monitor, show how uneven the start has been. Measured against the 1991 to 2020 average, rain since 1 October has reached 125% of normal in Northern Province and 110% in Luapula, but 72% on the Copperbelt, 63% in Southern, 53% in Lusaka and 52% in Central. Soils are dry almost everywhere: as of 10 November only six northern districts had moist soil, and seven Copperbelt districts had received between 9 and 13 millimetres in the first ten days of the month.

Three tests before planting

The department's rule, according to the Monitor's account, is that a farmer should plant only when three conditions are met at once: at least 40 millimetres of rain over the preceding one to two weeks, soil moisture to a depth of about 15 centimetres, and a two-week forecast with no dry spell longer than ten days. On that basis ten districts are cleared to plant now or with the next rain: Chiengi, Chipili, Kawambwa, Mwansabombwe and Nchelenge in Luapula; Kaputa, Lunte and Mporokoso in Northern; and Ikelenge and Mwinilunga in North-Western. Sixteen more, including Kasama, Mansa, Mbala and Kasempa, should plant after the next good rain. The seven Copperbelt districts of Chililabombwe, Chingola, Kitwe, Luanshya, Masaiti, Mufulira and Ndola are told to wait for two good rains, with a planting window expected around 20 to 26 November.

At the other end of the scale, 42 districts are simply told to wait: all of Lusaka and Southern provinces, most of Central and Eastern, and Mwandi and Sesheke in Western, where little rain is expected through 26 November. Kabwe, Chama, Chasefu and Mongu are told to hold off because a dry spell is likely. The bulletin also addresses the 15 districts, mostly in Southern and Lusaka, that had a false start in early October: farmers who planted then should not replant after the next isolated shower but wait until their district is marked Plant.

A season that may be short

The longer-range warning is the one that matters for the maize crop. The Monitor reported the department saying that the El Niño-influenced season may last 90 days or less in Southern and Lusaka provinces and in the valley areas, and recommending drought-tolerant crops such as sorghum, pearl millet and cowpeas, with maize confined to a small area and to extra-early varieties, planted in two or three batches about a week apart. Afternoon temperatures of 39 to 41 degrees Celsius are forecast in the Zambezi and Luangwa valleys and around 38 degrees across the south, with Southern, Lusaka, Western and Central provinces running 3 to 5 degrees above normal, which raises evaporation, bushfire risk and heat stress.

That advice echoes what the department's senior meteorologist Peggy Thole told News Diggers in late October, when a 14-day outlook for 22 October to 4 November warned of temperatures of 41 to 44 degrees. "They (farmers) should also look at planting various crops, not just relying on one crop," she said then, adding that "some of those crops should be drought-tolerant crops". State House's statement on Sunday on the term-limit debate listed El Niño and food security among the President's immediate priorities. The department's own website had not yet posted the new bulletin by Monday morning; the most recent document listed there dates from March 2026.
