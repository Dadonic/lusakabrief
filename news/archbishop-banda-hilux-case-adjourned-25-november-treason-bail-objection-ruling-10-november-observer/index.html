Two more dates: Archbishop Banda's Hilux case goes to 25 November while the DPP answers a defence application, and the treason bail panel is reported to rule on 10 November

The Archdiocese of Lusaka said on Friday that the case against Archbishop Alick Banda, charged with possessing a Toyota Hilux suspected of being unlawfully obtained, has been adjourned to Wednesday 25 November, with the Director of Public Prosecutions to respond to a defence application and the defence to reply by 18 November, ACI Africa and The Zambian Observer reported. The Observer also reported that the High Court panel hearing the bail petition of Brian Mundubile, Makebi Zulu and 16 others will rule on the State's objection on 10 November.

By Lusaka Brief Courts Desk • LUSAKA — 7 November 2026 • 3 min read

Neither of the two Lusaka cases that had Friday 6 November in their diaries produced a decision. The trial of Archbishop Alick Banda, which had already been put off on Tuesday when the prosecution did not appear, now has a date three weeks away, and the bail petition of the 18 treason accused has, according to one outlet, a ruling date on a point of procedure.

The Archbishop

ACI Africa reported on Friday that the Archdiocese of Lusaka said in a statement that the case "has been adjourned to Wednesday, 25th November 2026". The prosecution, led by the Director of Public Prosecutions, is to respond to an application filed by the defence, and the defence is to make its subsequent response by 18 November. The Zambian Observer carried the same dates and the same sequence. Neither report described what the defence application asks for or named the magistrate.

The charge, as ACI Africa set it out, is possession of a blue Toyota Hilux, registration ALF 7734, suspected of being unlawfully obtained, between 22 April 2021 and 27 December 2023. The Drug Enforcement Commission says the vehicle belonged to the Zambia Revenue Authority and is part of a wider investigation into about 22 ZRA vehicles allegedly disposed of improperly. The Archbishop has pleaded not guilty. He was charged on 28 September and granted bail of K80,000 in his own recognisance with three working sureties of K50,000 each, surrendered his travel documents and spent one night at Chimbokaila before the bail process was completed; the case was mentioned on 14 October, and the 3 November hearing was postponed because the prosecution team did not appear.

The treason bail petition

The Zambian Observer reported on Friday that the three-judge Lusaka High Court panel hearing the constitutional bail application of the NRPUP presidential candidate Brian Mundubile, his running mate Makebi Zulu and 16 co-accused adjourned the matter to 10 November, when it will rule on the State's preliminary objection that the application should be dismissed on procedural grounds. In the Observer's account, lead defence counsel Sakwiba Sikota argued that holding the accused without trial breached their rights to liberty and the presumption of innocence and called it "an illegal prolonged detention", while the State maintained that the offences are non-bailable and a grave matter of national security. If the objection fails, the panel would hear the bail application on its merits; if it succeeds, the application is dismissed without a hearing, and the defence has said it would challenge that outcome.

That account carries the byline of Anthony Mukwita and reads as commentary rather than a court report, and no other outlet read for this edition had reported the 10 November date; it is treated as unverified below. What the established record shows is that the panel of Judges Derrick Mulenga, Davies Chibwili and Ireen Wishimanga allowed the State on Thursday to raise its objection and adjourned to Friday morning, as Lusaka Brief reported yesterday; that the State's affidavit, reported by Zambia Monitor on 29 October, argues the two leaders are a flight risk with networks that could interfere with witnesses; and that Mr Zulu, as Zambia Monitor reported on Friday, has asked the court to let him conduct his own case, a step the Monitor likened to Shamwana v The People. The 18 have been in custody since August on treason and terrorism-related charges, which they deny.

Confirmed vs. alleged

Confirmed: Archbishop Banda's case is adjourned to 25 November, with the DPP to respond to a defence application and the defence to reply by 18 November (Archdiocese of Lusaka statement, reported by ACI Africa and The Zambian Observer). The charge, the plea of not guilty and the bail terms (ACI Africa). Makebi Zulu's application to represent himself (Zambia Monitor).

Alleged or unverified: The 10 November ruling date on the State's preliminary objection in the treason bail petition rests on a single Zambian Observer column; no court reporter's account of Friday's sitting was found. The nature of the defence application in the Archbishop's case. Whether the accused in the treason matter were brought to court on Friday.
