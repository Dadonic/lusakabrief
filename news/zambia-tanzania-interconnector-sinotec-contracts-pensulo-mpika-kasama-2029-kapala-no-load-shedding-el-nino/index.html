Two contracts signed, 386 kilometres of line, power by 2029: Kapala tells Parliament the Tanzania interconnector is under way, and promises no load shedding, El Niño or not

The Minister of Electricity, Peter Kapala, told the National Assembly on Thursday 5 November that Zambia's 613-kilometre share of the Zambia–Tanzania–Kenya interconnector will be commissioned in the second quarter of 2029 and will let Zambia trade up to 500 megawatts. Ecofin Agency reported that the first two of five packages, the Pensulo–Mpika and Mpika–Kasama lines, were awarded to China's Sinotec consortium on 19 October. News Diggers reported his assurance that there will be no load shedding whatever the coming rains bring.

By Lusaka Brief Business Desk • LUSAKA — 7 November 2026 • 2 min read

The transmission line that is meant to join southern Africa's power pool to east Africa's has moved from memoranda to contractors. In a ministerial statement to Parliament on Thursday 5 November, reported by Zambia Monitor, the Minister of Electricity, Peter Kapala, said Zambia's portion of the Zambia–Tanzania Interconnector, about 613 kilometres of line and associated works from Pensulo in Serenje district through Mpika and Kasama to Nakonde, is expected to be completed within 24 months and commissioned in the second quarter of 2029.

Five packages, two awarded

Ecofin Agency, reporting on Friday 6 November, said the minister told Parliament that the first two construction contracts, for the Pensulo–Mpika and Mpika–Kasama lines, were awarded on 19 October to China's Sinotec consortium, which began detailed site surveys on 2 November. Together they cover 386 kilometres of 330-kilovolt line. The remaining packages are the Kasama–Nakonde lines, a new substation at Nakonde, for which procurement is expected to conclude by March 2027, and voltage-support equipment at the Pensulo and Nakonde substations. ZESCO is implementing the project.

The money is in place, in grants rather than loans. Zambia Monitor and Ecofin both reported US$292 million secured for the Zambian section: US$245 million from the World Bank's International Development Association, US$30 million from the European Union and US$17 million from the United Kingdom, under a grant facility agreement signed on 29 January 2025 that became effective on 25 April 2025. The wider corridor, about 2,300 kilometres linking Zambia, Tanzania and Kenya under a tripartite memorandum signed in December 2014, is estimated at US$1.2 billion, with each country financing and building the section in its own territory.

The Zambia-Tanzania Interconnector is a strategic project to the Grow Zambia Agenda
— Peter Kapala, Minister of Electricity, in a ministerial statement to the National Assembly on 5 November, quoted by Zambia Monitor

Mr Kapala said the line would allow Zambia to trade up to 500 megawatts and help make the country a regional power-trading hub, and he asked MPs and stakeholders to support the government in implementing it.

No load shedding, he says

News Diggers led its report on the same statement with a different line: that with or without El Niño there will be no load shedding. The paper reported that Mr Kapala assured citizens there would be no power rationing despite the anticipated El Niño pattern, and that the interconnector was part of the government's effort to strengthen supply. Ecofin noted the background to that promise: ZESCO's 2025 integrated report puts generation at 8,739 gigawatt-hours in 2025, up from 7,469 in 2024, after the 2024 drought cut reservoir levels and brought shortages and load shedding, while export revenue fell to K5 billion in 2025 from K7.2 billion the year before. The Diggers report was partly behind a paywall; the figures on which the no-load-shedding assurance rests were not on the part of the page that could be read.

Sources: Zambia Monitor, “Zambia-Tanzania power interconnector to be commissioned by 2029, Parliament told” (5 Nov 2026); Ecofin Agency, “Zambia Awards First Contracts for 500MW Tanzania Power Interconnector” (6 Nov 2026); News Diggers, “With or without El Niño, there'll be no load shedding – Kapala” (6 Nov 2026).
