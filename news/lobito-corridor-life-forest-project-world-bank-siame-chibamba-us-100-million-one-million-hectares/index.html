A million hectares and more than US$100 million: the government asks the World Bank to hurry the Lobito Corridor forest project, with first activities pencilled in for January

The Minister of Green Economy and Environment, Gilbert Siame, urged a World Bank delegation in Lusaka on Thursday 5 November to speed up the design of the Lobito Corridor Integrated Forest Economy (LIFE) Project so that an agreement can be signed, Zambia Monitor reported. News Diggers reported a day earlier that the ministry's Permanent Secretary, Douty Chibamba, put the cost of the one-million-hectare plantation scheme at more than US$100 million and said preliminary work should start in January 2027.

By Lusaka Brief Business Desk • LUSAKA — 7 November 2026 • 2 min read

The forestry project that the government wants to run along the Lobito Corridor now has a name, a rough price and a target date, but not yet an agreement. Zambia Monitor reported on Friday 6 November that the Minister of Green Economy and Environment, Gilbert Siame, met a World Bank Group delegation at his office in Lusaka on Thursday and asked the Bank to accelerate the design of the Lobito Corridor Integrated Forest Economy (LIFE) Project.

We need to accelerate the design and outline of the LIFE Project so that we can conclude the project agreement
— Gilbert Siame, Minister of Green Economy and Environment, meeting a World Bank delegation in Lusaka on 5 November, quoted by Zambia Monitor

What the project is

As the Monitor described it, LIFE is envisaged as a phased, private sector-led scheme to build a sustainable forestry economy: plantations, outgrower arrangements, domestic processing and market development, with stronger forest management alongside. The delegation was led by the World Bank's country manager for Zambia, Martine Valcin, who said a recent joint exploratory mission to the Copperbelt had given the government and the Bank "a clearer understanding of how they can collaborate". The Bank's lead natural resources specialist for eastern and southern Africa, Laurent Debroux, said several companies visited on that mission were already planning to expand their plantations and invest in value addition, and a colleague, Andrew Zakharenka, said the delegation would meet the Forestry Department the following week to agree the project design before the agreement is finalised.

The price and the date

News Diggers reported from Ndola on Thursday 5 November that the government needs more than US$100 million to establish a one-million-hectare forestry project along the corridor in partnership with the World Bank. The ministry's Permanent Secretary, Douty Chibamba, speaking on Wednesday during a visit to ZAFFICO's operations, where a delegation assessed the company's forestry and timber-processing capacity, said resources were being mobilised from various contributors and that preliminary activities were expected to begin in January 2027. He said the government wanted rural communities along the corridor to benefit directly. The rest of the Diggers report, including Mr Chibamba's remarks on ZAFFICO's difficulties, was behind a paywall.

The Monitor's report also recorded a related launch: the Miombo Thrive movement, launched on Friday at the Pamodzi Hotel in Lusaka, which aims to turn landscape restoration into livelihoods. SNV's country director for Zambia and Zimbabwe, Lucy Fulgence, said the movement "seeks to change how landscape challenges are addressed by connecting conservation with livelihoods". The miombo woodlands, the Monitor noted, cover an estimated 2.7 million square kilometres and support more than 300 million people.

Neither report gave the financing split between the government, the Bank and private investors, the location of the first plantations, or the terms on which communities would take part. The budget speech on Friday placed the start of construction on the Lobito railway itself in the fourth quarter of 2027.
