Six days without a court date: Lima MP Kelvin Kaunda applies for bail from Mwembeshi after a transfer his lawyer says he was not told about

Kelvin Kaunda, the NRPUP MP for Lima, was arrested on 15 October on a charge of assault occasioning actual bodily harm over an incident during the August campaign. He has applied for bail, saying he has been held without being taken before a court; the Correctional Service confirms he has been moved from Lubwa police station to Mwembeshi remand, where his lawyer says he was refused access. His election petition hearing was adjourned on Tuesday because he was not in court.

By Lusaka Brief Courts Desk • LUSAKA — 21 October 2026 • 2 min read

The Member of Parliament for Lima, Kelvin Kaunda, has been in custody since Thursday 15 October on a single charge of assault occasioning actual bodily harm, and on Wednesday 21 October News Diggers reported that he had been moved from Lubwa police post to Mwembeshi, where his lawyer, Emmanuel Kaluba, said he was denied access to his client. Zambia Correctional Service spokesman Superintendent Cornelius Mwanza confirmed the transfer, telling the paper: “He is at Mwembeshi Remand.” Mr Kaluba said the police had moved his client to an undisclosed location without informing him.

The charge, under section 248 of the Penal Code, arises from a complaint that a 26-year-old man from Lusaka’s Soweto area was assaulted with stones and fists on Zingalume Road during campaign roadshows at about 19:30 on 7 August, suffering a deep cut to the forehead and a swollen lip, police spokesperson Chipo Kaitisha said in a statement reported by Zambia Monitor on 16 October. Mr Kaunda, 44, of Roma, was described as being in custody awaiting court proceedings. He has not entered a plea and the allegation has not been tested.

The bail application

Zambia Monitor reported on Wednesday that Mr Kaunda has applied for bail pending trial. In an affidavit he says he has been held at Lubwa since 15 October without being brought before a court. His lawyers argue that the offence is bailable, that he has a fixed address in Lusaka and is not a flight risk, that he can provide traceable sureties and will not interfere with witnesses, and that the Criminal Procedure Code requires an arrested person to be taken to court within 24 hours or considered for bond where that is not practicable. The outlet does not name the court hearing the application or report any police response to the custody claims.

The arrest came shortly after the Lusaka High Court adjourned an election petition brought by UPND candidate Lee Mukupa, who alleges electoral malpractice in a contest Mr Kaunda won by 22,760 votes to 8,041, Zambia Monitor reported; the petition hearing was adjourned on Tuesday because Mr Kaunda was not present in court.

Questions about the length of detention

The detention has drawn public comment. Lusaka businessman Chomba Kaoma said on 19 October, as reported by the Zambian Observer, that fellow MPs had submitted sureties and documents for police bond and that the law provides for 48 hours, accusing officers of disregarding the President’s guidance on detentions. Those are his assertions; the police had not responded to them in the reports reviewed.

Confirmed vs. alleged

Confirmed: Mr Kaunda was arrested on 15 October and charged with assault occasioning actual bodily harm (police statement via Zambia Monitor). The Correctional Service confirms he is at Mwembeshi remand (News Diggers). He has filed a bail application (Zambia Monitor). His election petition was adjourned on Tuesday in his absence.

Alleged or unverified: Whether and when he will appear in court on the charge; the reason for the transfer to Mwembeshi; the lawyer’s account of being refused access; and the claim that sureties were lodged and ignored. The assault allegation itself is untested.
