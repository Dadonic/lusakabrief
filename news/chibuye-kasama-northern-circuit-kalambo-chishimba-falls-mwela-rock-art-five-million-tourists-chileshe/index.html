Kalambo, Chishimba and the Mwela rock art: the Culture minister visits Kasama and says the Northern Circuit needs roads, marketing and money to help reach five million visitors

Christopher Chibuye paid a courtesy call on Northern Province Minister Wedden Chileshe on Tuesday 10 November to see the state of the province's heritage sites. He called for investment in heritage infrastructure and better access roads, and said the Northern Circuit could contribute to the national target of five million tourist arrivals, Zambia Monitor reports.

By Lusaka Brief Culture Desk • KASAMA — 11 November 2026 • 2 min read

The Minister of Culture and Arts, Christopher Chibuye, visited Kasama on Tuesday 10 November and said Northern Province's heritage sites needed investment in infrastructure, better roads and stronger marketing if they were to draw the visitors and investors the government wants, Zambia Monitor reported. He was shown the condition of the province's sites during a courtesy call on the Northern Province Minister, Wedden Chileshe, whose office arranged the visit to set out the challenges to their development.

The sites he named were Kalambo Falls, Chishimba Falls and the Mwela Rock Art Sites, along with the province's traditional ceremonies and other heritage assets. Mr Chibuye said the Northern Circuit could help the country reach its target of five million tourist arrivals, but that doing so would need informed interventions and stronger collaboration between government and other stakeholders to promote the attractions, improve infrastructure and make the area attractive to investors.

Culture defines our nation and must be preserved and promoted through the arts.
— Christopher Chibuye, Minister of Culture and Arts, in Kasama on Tuesday 10 November, as quoted by Zambia Monitor

What the province said

Mr Chileshe said the province was ready to work with the ministry and to help the sector contribute to the Grow Zambia Agenda, and both ministers said tourism development would support jobs and local economic activity, Zambia Monitor reported.

No figures were given for what the heritage investment would cost, which sites would be prioritised, or when any road works might begin; the report records a statement of need rather than a programme. Nor did either minister say which agency, the Culture ministry, the National Heritage Conservation Commission or the road authorities, would be responsible for the works the minister called for.

Mr Chibuye's case, as Zambia Monitor reported it, was that the sites already exist and already draw ceremonies and visitors; what they lack is the road, the signage and the promotion that would turn them into a circuit. He asked for joint efforts by government and other stakeholders to promote the attractions, improve infrastructure and create an environment that investors would find attractive, and said that informed interventions and stronger collaboration would be critical to positioning the Northern Circuit for the five-million target.

Sources: Zambia Monitor, “Culture minister, Chibuye, wants investment in heritage infrastructure” (10 Nov 2026).
