'Not to proceed': police cite intelligence in telling two consortiums to call off their 25 November term-limit marches, as Andeleki says there is nothing to protest

In a statement on Tuesday 17 November, police spokesperson Chipo Kaitisha said the service had reviewed notifications from two groups planning processions in Lusaka on Wednesday 25 November over presidential term limits and was advising both not to go ahead, citing intelligence gathered and the prevailing national security situation. One march was to defend the two-term limit, the other to call for its removal. Home Affairs Minister Clement Andeleki told News Diggers he expects no one to protest, because the President is not seeking a third term.

By Lusaka Brief Politics Desk • LUSAKA — 18 November 2026 • 4 min read

A week before two rival processions were due to converge on Parliament, the Zambia Police Service has asked both to stand down. In a statement dated Tuesday 17 November and signed by its Public Relations Officer, Chipo Kaitisha, the service said it had received notifications of intention to hold peaceful processions and public assemblies on the question of the presidential term limit, all planned for Wednesday 25 November. After what it called a careful review of the notifications from the two consortiums, and in the light of intelligence gathered and what it described as the prevailing national security situation, it said it wished to advise the organisers not to proceed. The full text was carried by the Zambian Observer on Tuesday evening; News Diggers reported the same statement that night, describing it as a refusal to authorise the civil society procession.

The statement does not name either group, publish the intelligence it relies on, or set out any legal consequence for marching regardless. Nor does it offer an alternative date. It simply urges the organisers of the planned processions and assemblies not to go ahead.

Who had notified the police

The first notification came from a consortium of civil society organisations that wrote to the police on 4 November about a procession in defence of the two-term limit in Article 106 of the Constitution. News Diggers lists Chapter One Foundation, Caritas Zambia, Transparency International Zambia, the Alliance for Community Action, ActionAid, the Zambia Council for Social Development and the Council of Churches in Zambia among its members. The consortium's case, as reported by the paper, is that the two-term limit is an important safeguard against the concentration of power in one individual.

The second came from the Zambia Youth Organisation Consortium, which wants the limit removed. Its vice chairperson, Boas Kanyama, told reporters at the weekend that 63,151 young people from all ten provinces were expected to walk from Northmead bus station to Parliament Buildings on the same day, that the police had been formally notified, and that around 500 officers were expected to provide security, the Zambian Observer reported on Monday 16 November. Kanyama insisted the consortium was not a political party and that the march was about citizens exercising a democratic right rather than about a third term for anyone in particular.

This is not about the third term, it's about Zambians exercising their democratic right
— Boas Kanyama, vice chairperson, Zambia Youth Organisation Consortium, as reported by the Zambian Observer on 16 November

The minister's view

On Wednesday morning News Diggers carried the response of Home Affairs Minister Clement Andeleki. He said he did not expect anyone to protest on 25 November, whether for or against removing the limit, because there was no need: President Hakainde Hichilema, he said, is not seeking a third term and is concentrating on development in his new five-year mandate. He urged civil society organisations and concerned citizens to raise their concerns through government offices, saying established procedures exist for proposing changes to the law.

That echoes the line State House has taken since the petitions appeared. Its chief communications specialist, Clayson Hamasaka, is quoted by the Zambian Observer asking why the head of State should be dragged into a campaign that, in his words, has nothing to do with him. Earlier in the month the Information Minister, Elvis Nkandu, advised the civil society groups to reconsider their march on the ground that the President had made no formal move on term limits; Transparency International Zambia's executive director, Maurice Nyambe, replied that it was not the minister's or the government's role to dictate how citizens express themselves, and asked why a youth procession supporting removal had been allowed to reach Parliament while the civil society groups were being told to stay home, the Daily Revelation reported.

What happens next

None of the pages read for this edition carried a response from either consortium to Tuesday's statement, so it is not yet known whether the civil society groups will march anyway, seek a court order, or accept the advice, nor whether the youth consortium will. The Law Association of Zambia has said it is prepared to go to court to defend the two-term limit, and the petitions seeking its removal remain before the National Assembly. The police advice adds a public-order question to a constitutional one, and it will be tested on the morning of 25 November.

Confirmed vs. alleged

Confirmed: The Zambia Police Service issued a statement on 17 November, signed by Public Relations Officer Chipo Kaitisha, advising two consortiums not to proceed with processions and assemblies planned for 25 November over the presidential term limit, citing intelligence gathered and the prevailing national security situation (Zambian Observer, News Diggers). A civil society consortium notified the police on 4 November of a procession to defend the limit (News Diggers). The Zambia Youth Organisation Consortium said it had notified the police of a march from Northmead to Parliament on the same day (Zambian Observer). Home Affairs Minister Clement Andeleki said he expects no protests because the President is not seeking a third term (News Diggers).

Alleged or unverified: What the intelligence consists of; whether either consortium will march despite the advice or challenge it in court; the youth consortium's figure of 63,151 marchers, which is its own estimate; and whether the police will treat a march that goes ahead as unlawful.
