'Verification and recruitment can run together': Tonse says freezing teacher hiring in 2027 contradicts what the UPND once demanded of the PF; the Treasury says count first

Muhabi Lungu, acting secretary general of the Tonse-Pamodzi Alliance, told Capital FM that the decision not to recruit teachers next year is a missed chance to bring the pupil-teacher ratio towards 1:25, that the UPND criticised the PF for exactly this, and that education's 13% of the 2027 budget falls short of the 20% benchmark. The Finance Minister said on ZNBC on Sunday that there is no plan to recruit in 2027 until deployment irregularities are resolved and the real staffing gaps are known.

By Lusaka Brief Politics Desk • LUSAKA — 12 November 2026 • 2 min read

The opposition Tonse-Pamodzi Alliance has made the suspension of teacher recruitment its first line of attack on the 2027 budget. Its acting secretary general, Muhabi Lungu, speaking on Capital FM's Conversations with HOPE programme, called the decision a missed opportunity to address teacher shortages and improve the pupil-teacher ratio, The Zambian Observer reported on Wednesday 11 November. He said Zambia should be aiming for a ratio of between 1:25 and 1:30, and that classes of 100 or 150 pupils to a teacher undermine any teaching at all.

Mr Lungu's political point was that the UPND had attacked the Patriotic Front government over difficulties in recruiting teachers and was now taking the same approach itself. His practical point was that the government's stated reason for the freeze, a verification exercise, does not require one: in his words, as reported by the Observer, the government can conduct its verification exercise while continuing to recruit teachers. He questioned the roughly 13% of the 2027 budget allocated to education against the internationally advocated 20%, warned that growth in enrolment under free education must be matched by quality teaching, and said the suspension would slow youth employment in education and health. The alliance reserved a full assessment of the budget's third part pending technical analysis, saying it did not want to make uninformed pronouncements on matters that needed detailed economic analysis.

The government's reason

The policy itself was set out by the Minister of Finance and National Planning, Situmbeko Musokotwane, on ZNBC's Sunday Interview, News Diggers reported on Tuesday. Asked whether more teachers would be recruited in 2027, he answered plainly that there was no plan to do so next year, and tied the decision to irregularities in teacher deployment and to establishing the actual staffing gaps in schools before hiring again. In the same interview he said the government wanted to improve domestic tax collection and that Zambians might have been spoilt by years of donor support.

No, for next year there is no plan to recruit teachers.
— Situmbeko Musokotwane, Minister of Finance and National Planning, on ZNBC's Sunday Interview on 8 November, as quoted by News Diggers

The two positions are not quite opposites. The Tonse argument is that redistribution and recruitment are not alternatives and can run in parallel; the Treasury's, as the minister put it on Sunday, is that it cannot know how many teachers to hire until it knows where the existing ones are and why deployment went wrong.

Neither report records a response from the Ministry of Education to Mr Lungu's remarks, and the education vote is still before the House as part of the 2027 estimates.

Sources: The Zambian Observer, “Tonse Alliance questions teacher recruitment suspension” (11 Nov 2026); News Diggers, “We've no plans to recruit teachers next year – Govt” (10 Nov 2026).
