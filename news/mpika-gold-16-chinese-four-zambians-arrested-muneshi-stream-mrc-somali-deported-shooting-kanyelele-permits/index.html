Twenty arrests at a suspended gold site, a deportation after a shooting and 15 held over permits: the State moves on Mpika's informal gold rush

The Minerals Regulation Commission said on Sunday 15 November that 16 Chinese nationals and four Zambians had been arrested at Muneshi Stream in Chief Mukungule's area of Mpika, where it says mining resumed two months after it suspended the site and the stream is suspected to have been polluted and blocked. Separately, the Immigration Department deported a Somali national on Friday 13 November over a shooting at a gold plant in Kanyelele, and held ten Chinese and five Tanzanians for allegedly working without permits.

By Lusaka Brief Business Desk • MPIKA — 16 November 2026 • 3 min read

Three arms of the State acted against Mpika's informal gold trade in the space of a weekend. The Minerals Regulation Commission (MRC) announced on Sunday 15 November that 16 Chinese nationals and four Zambians had been arrested for allegedly mining and processing gold without licences at Muneshi Stream, in Chief Mukungule's area of Mpika District, Muchinga Province. The announcement was reported by the Zambia Monitor and, via Kalemba, by the Zambian Observer. News Diggers reported the same day that the Immigration Department had deported a Somali man over a shooting at a gold processing plant elsewhere in the district and detained 15 foreign workers at two mines.

The MRC's statement, issued in Lusaka and attributed by the Zambia Monitor to Malfred Moolela, its Manager for Minerals Security and Protection, said the arrests were made with the Zambia Environmental Management Agency and state security wings. The alleged offences are mining and processing gold without the required licences or authorisations, suspected pollution of the stream with unidentified substances, and obstruction of its natural flow, which the Commission says has limited water access for communities downstream. According to Moolela, the Commission had suspended mining and processing at the same site about two months earlier, and the activity had carried on regardless. The Zambian Observer reported the Commission warning that it would take decisive action against individuals and companies that break mining and environmental law. Neither report says what charges, if any, have been laid, or whether the 20 remain in custody.

A shooting at Kanyelele

The Immigration Department's action concerns a different site. In a statement reported by News Diggers, the department's chief public relations officer, Namati Nshinka, said a 50-year-old Somali national had been deported on Friday 13 November through Kenneth Kaunda International Airport for discharging a firearm during a dispute at Empiric Trading Limited, a gold processing facility in the Kanyelele area of Mpika. In the same statement the department said ten Chinese and five Tanzanian nationals had been apprehended for allegedly working without valid employment permits at two mines, in Shiwang'andu and Mpika districts respectively. Lusaka Brief is not naming the deported man, who has not been charged before a court; the rest of the statement is behind the paper's paywall.

Formalisation and enforcement, side by side

The arrests follow a stakeholder meeting in Mpika on which the Zambia Monitor reported on Thursday 12 November, where the Ministry of Mines and Minerals Development and the MRC began formalising artisanal mining at Kamambwe, Kanyelele and Danger Hill. In a speech read for him by Moolela, the MRC's Director General, Tom Hara, said informal operators at the identified sites had been told to leave so that the sites could be demarcated and tenements allocated to eligible artisanal miners, and he cited Section 20(5) of the Minerals Regulation Act No. 14 of 2024, which reserves artisanal mining rights for Zambian citizens subject to the Commission's approval. Muchinga's deputy permanent secretary, Grant Chisapa, warned at the meeting about mercury use and the risk to water sources and to the North Luangwa ecosystem, while Chief Chikwanda of Mpika welcomed the consultation of local miners before tenements are allocated.

Confirmed vs. alleged

Confirmed: The MRC announced the arrest of 16 Chinese nationals and four Zambians at Muneshi Stream (Zambia Monitor; Zambian Observer via Kalemba). The Immigration Department announced the deportation of a Somali national and the apprehension of ten Chinese and five Tanzanian nationals (News Diggers). The formalisation exercise was reported by the Zambia Monitor on 12 November.

Alleged or unverified: The allegations of unlicensed mining and pollution are the Commission's; none of the 20 has been reported as charged, and no response from them or from the companies involved has been published. The quantity of gold or equipment seized, if any, has not been reported.

Sources: Zambia Monitor, “16 Chinese nationals, four Zambians arrested over illegal gold mining in Mpika” (15 Nov 2026); Zambian Observer, “16 Chinese, 4 Zambians arrested for illegal gold mining in Mpika” (15 Nov 2026); News Diggers, “Immigration deports Somali national over shooting at Mpika gold plant” (15 Nov 2026); Zambia Monitor, “Mines Ministry begins formalising Mpika mining operations, warns illegal operators” (12 Nov 2026).
