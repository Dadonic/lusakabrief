Health · The US compact Developing

Two readings of one signature: the health pact's fine print — US$975 million of Zambian money, a mineral clause decoupled — and M'membe's demand to see all of it

Forty-eight hours after the US-Zambia health compact was signed at the Ministry of Finance, its architecture is clearer: Washington intends more than US$1.5 billion over five years, Zambia pledges US$975 million in new domestic health spending, co-financing fell from US$243 million to US$154 million, and the minerals question has been formally decoupled — though not settled. Fred M'membe calls the deal padded chains and wants the full text published; the minister says the public already won the argument that mattered.

By Mwelwa Phiri • LUSAKA — 10 October 2026 • 6 min read

The ink dried on Thursday; the arithmetic arrived on Friday. The bilateral health memorandum signed at the Ministry of Finance by Finance Minister Situmbeko Musokotwane and US Chargé d'Affaires Mich Coker — with Health Minister Professor Roma Chilengi applauding — commits Washington, in the US Mission's formulation, to “intend to provide more than US$1.5 billion over the next five years” for HIV/AIDS, tuberculosis, malaria, maternal and child health, disease surveillance and outbreak preparedness. The Zambian side of the ledger is now public too: a pledge to increase domestic health expenditure by US$975 million over the same five years, a civil-service takeover of frontline health workers currently paid by Washington, and a handover of commodity procurement to Lusaka.

What changed between the leaked draft and the signed text is substantial, and the activists who fought it are saying so. The signed compact carries US$504 million more than the previous draft, cuts Zambia's co-financing requirement from US$243 million to US$154 million, and drops the compulsory 25-year sharing of pathogen information. “An inspiring, coordinated campaign of advocacy, pressure and outcry from Washington to Lusaka secured meaningful changes in this deal,” said Asia Russell of Health GAP — while warning that weaknesses remain. The comparison doing quiet work in Lusaka's favour is Harare: Zimbabwe failed to renegotiate its own compact and lost the aid entirely, with the consequences already visible in its malaria, TB and HIV programmes. In 2024, 84 percent of Zambia's HIV programme was PEPFAR-funded and 1.3 million Zambians were on antiretrovirals; that is the exposure this signature manages.

Then there is the metal. Foreign Affairs Minister Mulambo Haimbe told reporters at the signing that Lusaka had insisted the health pact and a proposed critical-minerals agreement be decoupled — “and this has been done” — while confirming the minerals agreement is still being negotiated. Decoupling answers the March leak, in which a State Department memo contemplated taking support away from Zambia “on a massive scale” to secure mining access; it does not answer what the minerals text will say when it arrives.

Fred M'membe is unmoved. The Socialist Party president says the removal of the specimen and data clauses does not free Zambia from dependency: “Renegotiating the terms of our dependency does not make us free… the Government is celebrating that the chains have been padded rather than broken.” His statement makes three demands — publication of the complete, unedited MoU; vigilance against health aid becoming a Trojan horse for mineral access; and a financing model built on recovered mining tax revenue rather than what he calls an “ad-hoc band-aid” tied to Washington's political weather. Professor Chilengi's office has not committed to publishing the full text; the minister's position all week has been that the contentious matters were the ones the public raised, and that the public won. The document, when or if it appears, will settle which reading is right.

Confirmed vs. alleged

Confirmed: The signing on 8 October and the principals present; the US intention of more than US$1.5 billion over five years (US Mission); Zambia's US$975 million domestic-spending pledge, the workforce and procurement takeovers, and the draft-to-signature changes in funding, co-financing and pathogen clauses (Health Policy Watch; Health GAP); Haimbe's decoupling statement and the continuing minerals negotiation; M'membe's statement and demands; the 2024 PEPFAR share and treatment numbers.

Alleged or unverified: That the compact conceals structural alignments harmful to Zambia — asserted by the Socialist Party, denied in substance by the Ministry of Health, and unverifiable until the full MoU is published; the ultimate terms of the separate critical-minerals agreement, which is still under negotiation.
