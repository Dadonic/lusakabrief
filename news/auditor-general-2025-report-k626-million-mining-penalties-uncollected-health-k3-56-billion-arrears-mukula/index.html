K626 million in mining penalties never collected, K3.56 billion owed by the Ministry of Health: the Auditor-General’s account of 2025

The Auditor-General’s report on the accounts for the year to 31 December 2025, released by Acting Auditor-General Patricia Malaila, finds that the Ministry of Mines failed to collect K626.64 million in penalties from licence holders, that the Ministry of Health carries K3.56 billion in unpaid obligations some dating to 2014, and that only 27 of 5,595 confiscated mukula logs could be found. Unresolved findings fell from 33 to 27.

By Lusaka Brief Business Desk • LUSAKA — 21 October 2026 • 2 min read

The Office of the Auditor-General has published its report on the accounts of the Republic for the financial year ended 31 December 2025, and the headline figures are about money owed rather than money stolen. According to Zambia Monitor, Lusaka Times and News Diggers, the Ministry of Mines and Minerals Development failed to collect K626.64 million in penalties due from mining licence holders, and a further K30.48 million in annual area charges remained outstanding. The Department of Immigration had not remitted US$433,820 collected on the Treasury’s behalf, and the Zambia Revenue Authority could not account for K41.97 million in customs duties and K9.03 million in temporary imports because supporting documents were missing.

Acting Auditor-General Patricia Malaila said the weaknesses had caused revenue losses and inefficient use of public resources and had reduced the effectiveness of service delivery, Lusaka Times reported.

Health arrears and payroll

The largest single figure sits at the Ministry of Health, which the report says has outstanding obligations of K3.56 billion covering employee-related liabilities and unpaid suppliers’ bills, some dating back to 2014.

The continued growth of these obligations places considerable pressure on public finances
— Acting Auditor-General Patricia Malaila, in the report as quoted by Zambia Monitor on 19 October

The report warns that the arrears expose the Treasury to penalties, interest and litigation, and notes that Mbala General Hospital was still paying to transport oxygen from other towns. At the Ministry of Education, 125 officers who were paid salaries totalling K1.23 million did not appear on staff returns and were not recognised at their reported stations — an irregularity the report says raises concerns about the integrity of payroll controls. The ministry was also cited for not finishing the printing and distribution of learning materials for the new curriculum.

Farm inputs, land and logs

Under the Farmer Input Support Programme, auditors found irregularities exceeding K72 million involving inactive and unapproved beneficiaries and people drawing on more than one government support scheme. The Ministry of Lands could not assess ground rent on 74,840 properties because its land records are incomplete, and the Ministry of Water Development and Sanitation had not secured title deeds for 663 government properties. At the Ministry of Green Economy and Environment, auditors recorded 5,595 confiscated mukula logs but could physically verify only 27, leaving 5,568 unaccounted for.

Ms Malaila said the number of unresolved audit findings had fallen from 33 in 2024 to 27, which she attributed to engagement with controlling officers, while noting that significant gaps remain. She urged controlling officers to implement the report’s recommendations without delay, strengthen internal controls, improve revenue collection, tighten procurement and payroll management, safeguard public assets and recover funds where applicable. None of the ministries named had responded publicly in the reports reviewed.
