'I will invite all mining firms': Hichilema hosts the under-15 champions at State House and promises to help FAZ clear a legacy debt whose size is reported two ways

The President told the squad that won Tier B of the FIFA U-15 World Cup in Baku that he would convene the mining houses to raise money for the Football Association of Zambia and help dismantle its old debt, on condition that it stops living beyond its means, ZamFoot reported on Sunday 8 November. FAZ president Keith Mweemba put the debt at not less than K40 million; the same report elsewhere says more than K140 million.

By Lusaka Brief Sports Desk • LUSAKA — 9 November 2026 • 3 min read

The boys who won in Baku have had their reception, and the Football Association of Zambia has come away from it with a presidential promise. President Hakainde Hichilema hosted the men's under-15 national team at State House after their victory in Tier B of the FIFA U-15 World Cup, where they beat Portugal 5–2 in the final under coach Brian Kunsanama, and told the association he would bring the mining companies together to raise money for it, ZamFoot reported on Sunday 8 November in an account credited to FAZ Media. Daily Revelation's Monday edition, describing the same meet-and-greet ceremony, places it a day before publication, on Sunday.

I will invite all mining firms to a meeting to help raise funds for FAZ.
— President Hakainde Hichilema, at the State House reception for the under-15 national team, as reported by ZamFoot on Sunday 8 November

The promise answered a request. FAZ president Keith Mweemba used his speech to raise what he called one of the association's lingering headaches, its legacy debt, reminding the President that the matter had first been put to him in December 2024 when he hosted the under-17 boys' and girls' teams at the Taj Pamodzi Hotel. Some progress had followed the President's directive then to restructure the debt, Mr Mweemba said, but the association remained heavily indebted. He attributed the debt to the cost of running several national teams against the income FAZ generates, and asked the government to extend its support to the junior sides when they play in COSAFA, CAF and FIFA competitions, citing an under-17 women's team that has reached three consecutive World Cups at great cost to the association.

K40 million, or K140 million

How large the debt is depends on which line of the report is read. Mr Mweemba is quoted as saying: "The legacy debt of FAZ currently stands at not less than ZMW40 million." The article's own summary, a few paragraphs away, says the debt "stands over K140 million". The report does not reconcile the two, and no other figure was found for this edition. One of them is presumably a slip; which one cannot be established from the published account.

The President attached a condition. He said the government and the association would work together to dismantle the debt and that he had asked Mr Mweemba to write to him formally about it, describing the problem as one that "is creating problems for the development of football". But he said FAZ must manage its resources more prudently once the debt is gone, must not take on new borrowing by living beyond its means, and should spend on young players and not on consumption. He also addressed what he suggested had been an occasional rivalry between the association and the State, saying "government is never a competitor to its own FAZ" and reminding his audience that he is its patron.

The team captain, Simon Nyirenda, thanked the President for the reception and promised that the players would keep working hard, ZamFoot reported. Daily Revelation's account of the same ceremony led on a different passage, in which the President told what he called negative Zambians to turn over a new leaf and look at the good things in the country.

No date was given for the meeting with the mining houses, no sum was mentioned as a target, and the report records no reward for the players themselves. Whether the companies will be asked for sponsorship, a levy or a one-off contribution was not explained.

What was said at State House, as reported

  • The President will invite all mining firms to a meeting to raise funds for FAZ (ZamFoot)
  • FAZ is to write to the President formally about its legacy debt
  • Debt: "not less than ZMW40 million" in Mr Mweemba's quoted words; "over K140 million" elsewhere in the same report
  • Condition: more prudent management and no new debt once the old one is cleared
  • FAZ's request: government support for junior national teams in COSAFA, CAF and FIFA competitions

Sources: ZamFoot, “TIER B U15 FIFA WORLD CHAMPIONS GET PRESIDENTIAL TREAT…Hichilema Offers to Restructure Historic FAZ debt” (8 Nov 2026); Daily Revelation, “I want to say to a negative Zambian, for once turn a leaf and look at good things – Hichilema” (9 Nov 2026).
