'There are no perfect democracies': the EU and Zambia hold the dialogue Lusaka asked for in September, and Haimbe asks to comment on the observers' report before it is published

At the second EU–Zambia Extraordinary Partnership Dialogue in Lusaka on Monday 9 November, the EU's Africa director, Francesca Di Mauro, said electoral grievances should be settled through fair and impartial courts and offered support for a national process of dialogue and reconciliation. The Foreign Minister, Mulambo Haimbe, asked for the government to be allowed to comment on the Election Observation Mission's findings before its final report is published. Two days earlier the government spokesman had told the mission to leave criminal cases alone.

By Lusaka Brief Politics Desk • LUSAKA — 10 November 2026 • 3 min read

Zambia and the European Union sat down in Lusaka on Monday 9 November for a meeting that the government itself had asked for. The second EU–Zambia Extraordinary Partnership Dialogue was requested by Lusaka to discuss what Zambia Monitor described as post-election challenges and a European Parliament resolution on the country's electoral environment. Francesca Di Mauro, Director for Africa at the European External Action Service, led the EU side; the Minister of Foreign Affairs and International Cooperation, Mulambo Haimbe, led Zambia's.

The EU's message, as reported by both Zambia Monitor and News Diggers, was that grievances arising from the August general election should be dealt with transparently, through fair and impartial judicial processes, in full respect of the constitutional rights and duties of citizens and institutions. News Diggers quoted the EU's position that there are no perfect democracies, and that the Union stands ready to support a national process for dialogue and reconciliation "in full respect of Zambia's ownership, institutions and democratic processes". Dr Di Mauro congratulated President Hakainde Hichilema on his re-election and welcomed the new Cabinet and Parliament, while saying the EU remained concerned about electoral governance, civic space and the political environment, including access to justice and fundamental freedoms, Zambia Monitor reported.

We welcome this opportunity to engage openly with the Government on electoral governance.
— Francesca Di Mauro, Director for Africa at the European External Action Service, at the dialogue in Lusaka on Monday 9 November, quoted by Zambia Monitor

What was agreed

Both sides agreed to work towards implementing the recommendations of the EU Election Observation Mission, including those that need legislation or administrative change, ahead of the 2031 general election, with the EU offering technical support, Zambia Monitor reported. Mr Haimbe welcomed the mission's findings as material that could strengthen electoral administration, institutional capacity, transparency and public confidence, and asked that the government be given a chance to formally consider and comment on them before the final report is made public. He also said the partnership should cover regional peace and security, and reaffirmed Zambia's commitment to multilateralism.

We believe that such engagement would contribute to a balanced appreciation of the electoral environment.
— Mulambo Haimbe, Minister of Foreign Affairs and International Cooperation, at the dialogue on Monday 9 November, quoted by Zambia Monitor

The road to Monday's meeting runs through September. The European Parliament adopted a resolution on Zambia's post-election situation on 17 September; three days later Mr Haimbe called it premature, because the observers' final report had not yet been published, and said Zambia had not been consulted. In the same statement he asked the EU delegation in Lusaka to convene a formal partnership dialogue with the European Commission and the External Action Service, Zambia Monitor reported at the time. Monday's meeting is the result.

The mission and the Mundubile trial

The meeting came two days after a sharper exchange about the mission's remit. The EU observers said on Thursday 5 November that they would closely follow the trial of Brian Mundubile and Makebi Zulu, and the Chief Observer, Michael McNamara, said the case had what he called an undoubtedly political dimension given that the two men are the most popular opposition leaders, News Diggers reported. Elvis Nkandu, the Chief Government Spokesperson, replied in an interview on Friday 6 November that he did not understand why an election observation mission would take an interest in a trial that had nothing to do with elections, and that the mission should concentrate on election-related matters rather than criminal cases. Neither Monday's reports nor the EU's statement, as reported, mentioned the trial by name.
