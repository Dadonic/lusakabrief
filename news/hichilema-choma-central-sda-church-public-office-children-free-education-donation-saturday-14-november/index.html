'Especially our children': in Choma Central SDA, Hichilema tells MPs and councillors that public office exists to improve lives, and leaves a gift the papers count differently

On the second day of his Southern Province visit, President Hichilema and the First Lady attended a service at Choma Central Seventh-day Adventist Church on Saturday 14 November. He told office-holders to make sure children get the benefit of free education, school feeding and the scrapping of exam fees, and said leaders should read the church's messages carefully. The Times of Zambia reports a K100,000 gift to the congregation; News Diggers puts the First Family's contribution to the building project at K200,000.

By Lusaka Brief Politics Desk • CHOMA — 16 November 2026 • 2 min read

A day after his rally in Choma, President Hakainde Hichilema spent Saturday 14 November in church in the same town, and used the pulpit to address the politicians in the pews. At Choma Central Seventh-day Adventist Church, with First Lady Mutinta Hichilema beside him, he said that those who hold public office sought it in order to work for the people and improve their lives, and that children in particular must feel the benefit. The service was reported by the Times of Zambia and by News Diggers.

According to the Times, the President said the government is committed to prioritising the welfare and education of children across the country, and that public leaders must ensure children benefit from free education, the school feeding programme and the cancellation of examination fees. "Those of us in public office must continue working tirelessly for our people, especially our children," he said. He addressed members of Parliament, mayors, council chairpersons and councillors directly, asking them to stay committed to serving the people and to make sure government policies change the lives of ordinary citizens.

Reading between the lines

News Diggers reported the President saying that office-holders should discern and correctly interpret the messages they hear from the church so that they can serve citizens diligently, and the Times reported him praising the congregation's message as holding lessons for people in leadership. He urged leaders to understand the environment in which they operate and to read between the lines before taking decisions. He opened, in News Diggers' account, by saying he did not have much to say beyond a few things.

The two papers agree on the message and differ on the money. The Times of Zambia reported that the President donated K100,000 to the congregation. News Diggers reported that the President and the First Family had donated K200,000 towards the church's building project. No State House figure had been published by Monday morning, so both are given here as reported.

The service followed a community engagement in Choma on Friday 13 November at which, News Diggers reported, the President dismissed talk that the UPND was losing popularity by pointing to its clean sweep of the 14 seats contested in the 5 November by-elections, promised a dual carriageway from Lusaka to Southern Province on the model of the Lusaka–Ndola road, and warned teachers and nurses who refuse rural postings that they risk their jobs. It preceded by a day the State House statement in which his spokesman said he would not be drawn into the argument over presidential term limits because his focus is on delivering development. The church address, with its emphasis on free education and school feeding, was in the same register: a government listing what it says it has done for ordinary families.
