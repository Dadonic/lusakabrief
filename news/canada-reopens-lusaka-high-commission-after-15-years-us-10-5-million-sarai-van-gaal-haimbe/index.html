Back after 15 years: Canada reopens its High Commission in Lusaka with US$10.5 million in new support and a cooperation agreement that names critical minerals first

Canada's Secretary of State for International Development, Randeep Sarai, opened the mission on Monday 9 November alongside Mulambo Haimbe and Situmbeko Musokotwane, announcing US$10.5 million for growth, women's empowerment, energy access and institutions. Zambia had been handled from Dar es Salaam for more than a decade; Corry Van Gaal is the new High Commissioner.

By Lusaka Brief Politics Desk • LUSAKA — 11 November 2026 • 2 min read

Canada formally reopened its High Commission in Lusaka on Monday 9 November, ending a period of roughly 15 years in which its relations with Zambia were run from its mission in Dar es Salaam, Zambia Monitor, the Voice of Nigeria and The Zambian Observer reported. The opening was performed by Randeep Sarai, Canada's Secretary of State for International Development, with the Foreign Affairs and International Cooperation Minister, Mulambo Haimbe, and the Finance and National Planning Minister, Dr Situmbeko Musokotwane, among the Zambian officials present, Zambia Monitor reported.

Mr Sarai used the occasion to announce US$10.5 million in new Canadian support for Zambia, aimed at economic growth, women's empowerment, energy access and the strengthening of institutions, according to Zambia Monitor. The two governments also signed an Agreement on Economic and Development Cooperation covering trade and investment, critical minerals, private-sector development, regional security and inclusive growth.

The opening of Canada's High Commission in Lusaka marks a new chapter in the relationship between our two countries
— Randeep Sarai, Canada's Secretary of State for International Development, at the opening in Lusaka on Monday 9 November, as quoted by Zambia Monitor

The money already here

The scale of Canadian interest in Zambia is mostly in the mines. The Voice of Nigeria, carrying an APA report, put Canadian mining assets in Zambia at about US$12 billion in 2024, enough to make Zambia the fifth-largest destination for Canadian mining investment abroad; Mr Haimbe, as reported by The Zambian Observer from a Muvi TV report, gave a figure of more than US$12.9 billion. Both reports put bilateral trade at US$97 million in 2025, a modest figure that the Zambian side says it wants to double within five years. The sectors named for new activity were mining, renewable energy, fibre processing and small business.

Mr Sarai said Canada had provided more than US$210 million in international assistance to Zambia since 2020, Zambia Monitor reported; the APA report gives the same sum but dates it from 2021. Zambia Monitor also recalled the Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney's recent announcement of a planned US$50 million loan from FinDev Canada to Zanaco, intended for women-owned businesses, agribusinesses and small and medium-sized enterprises, and noted the two countries' long joint work on ending child, early and forced marriage, including co-sponsoring the first UN General Assembly resolution on the subject in 2013.

The new High Commissioner, Corry Van Gaal, has more than 20 years in international affairs, including senior posts at Global Affairs Canada, work with the World Bank's Global Agriculture and Food Security Program and postings across Africa and South America, The Zambian Observer reported. She described the reopening as a chance to deepen economic collaboration and open new areas of partnership, according to the APA report, while Mr Haimbe said a resident Canadian mission would strengthen economic relations and support Zambia's efforts to attract investment.

Canada and Zambia, by the numbers

  • US$10.5 million — new support announced on Monday (Zambia Monitor).
  • US$210 million — Canadian assistance since 2020 (Mr Sarai) or 2021 (APA).
  • US$12 billion to US$12.9 billion — Canadian mining assets in Zambia, depending on the report.
  • US$97 million — bilateral trade in 2025, which Lusaka wants to double in five years.
  • US$50 million — planned FinDev Canada loan to Zanaco announced by Prime Minister Carney.

Sources: Zambia Monitor, “Canada announces $10.5m support, inaugurates High Commission in Zambia” (10 Nov 2026); Voice of Nigeria, “Canada Reopens High Commission in Zambia after 15-Year Closure” (10 Nov 2026); The Zambian Observer, “Canada has reopened its High Commission in Lusaka after more than 15 years” (10 Nov 2026).
