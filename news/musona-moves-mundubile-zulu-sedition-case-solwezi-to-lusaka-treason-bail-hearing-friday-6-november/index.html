Courts · Sedition and bail Developing

Back to Lusaka: Judge Musona moves the Mundubile–Zulu sedition case out of Solwezi, while the treason bail hearing for 18 accused is put back to Friday 6 November

Of the two decisions expected on Thursday 5 November in the cases of Brian Mundubile and Makebi Zulu, one arrived and one did not. The Zambian Observer reports that High Court Judge Edward Musona, sitting in Solwezi, ordered the sedition case transferred to a subordinate court in Lusaka, where the two men live and were arrested. In Lusaka, the three-judge panel hearing their constitutional bail petition allowed the State to raise a fresh jurisdictional objection and adjourned to 09:00 on Friday.

By Lusaka Brief Courts Desk • SOLWEZI — 6 November 2026 • 4 min read

Thursday 5 November was meant to settle two questions for Brian Mundubile and Makebi Zulu: where they would answer sedition charges, and whether they and sixteen co-accused in a separate treason case would be released on constitutional bail. By the evening the first had an answer and the second had a new date.

The venue question

The background was set out on Wednesday 4 November by the Times of Zambia and News Diggers. Lusaka High Court Judge Edward Musona, sitting in Solwezi, had heard a challenge to the authority of the Solwezi Magistrates' Court to try the two men, and the State's advocate had told the court the ruling would come at 14:00 on Thursday. News Diggers reported that the charges arise from Facebook posts of 14 August 2026, the day after the general election, in which Mr Mundubile allegedly said his party was leading according to its parallel vote tabulation and called on the Electoral Commission to resume counting, and that the defence argued the alleged offences were committed in Lusaka, where both men were at the time. The Times quoted defence lawyer Chifumu Banda welcoming the timetable: "This is good because we now know when the pending issue of jurisdiction will finally be determined by the court."

The Zambian Observer, citing court records, reported on Thursday that Justice Musona ordered the case moved to a subordinate court in Lusaka and granted leave to appeal. According to the Observer's account of the order, the judge relied on Section 80 of the Criminal Procedure Code and on three findings: that the two men reside in Lusaka, "a fact which was not disputed"; that they were arrested and have been held in custody in Lusaka; and that Lusaka is the more convenient and appropriate forum. A separate Observer report said the court found the Solwezi Subordinate Court "was not the correct and appropriate forum". The two men face two counts of seditious practices under Section 57 of the Penal Code. The Observer said they had been travelling about 440 kilometres from Kabwe, where they are held, for each Solwezi hearing.

The respected Judge Musona's decision to move the case to a Lusaka subordinate court in my view is good for the State (DPP & AG).
— Sakwiba Sikota, lead defence counsel for Brian Mundubile, in remarks reported by The Zambian Observer on 5 November

Mr Sikota argued, in the same report, that the State would no longer have to move lawyers and witnesses to Solwezi for every sitting, and asked that the two accused also be brought to Lusaka, which he said made "logistical, security and financial sense". No date for the first Lusaka hearing was given.

The bail hearing

The constitutional bail petition by Mr Mundubile, Mr Zulu and sixteen others is before a three-judge panel of the Lusaka High Court which Zambia Monitor identified on 29 October as Judges Derrick Mulenga, Davies Chibwili and Ireen Wishimanga. That day the panel fixed 09:00 on 5 November and warned, in the words of defence lawyer Milner Katolo quoted by the Monitor, that "this is the last adjournment". The Observer reported that when the matter was called on Thursday, under a heavy police presence around the court, the Acting Chief State Advocate, Chibesa Mulonda, argued that constitutional bail could not be pursued through a constitutional petition and that the applicants should seek bail in the criminal proceedings. Mr Katolo objected that the Director of Public Prosecutions was barred from raising the point after failing to file an answer to the petition. The court dismissed that objection, allowed the State to raise its jurisdictional challenge once it had filed a response, and adjourned to 09:00 on Friday 6 November.

The Observer also reported that Mr Zulu told the court he would represent himself and declined an adjournment to prepare, citing the 1975 case of Shamwana v The People, in which the accused likewise acted for himself. The eighteen have been in custody since 14 August and face treason and terrorism-related charges which carry life imprisonment on conviction. The State's affidavit opposing bail, reported by Zambia Monitor on 29 October and sworn by Senior Superintendent Donald Mulimba, argues that the two leaders are a flight risk, that their networks create a risk of interference with witnesses, and that "the balancing of interests test favours the State". Mr Mundubile has denied that his party had a militia and, according to the Monitor, has said it "did not support violence or attempts to overthrow the constitutional order".

Confirmed vs. alleged

Confirmed: Judge Musona ordered the sedition case transferred from Solwezi to a subordinate court in Lusaka and granted leave to appeal (The Zambian Observer, citing court records; the ruling was scheduled for 5 November according to the Times of Zambia and News Diggers). The bail hearing was adjourned to 09:00 on Friday 6 November after the court allowed the State to raise a jurisdictional objection (The Zambian Observer). The panel had set 5 November as a final date (Zambia Monitor).

Alleged or unverified: The date of the first Lusaka sitting of the sedition case, and whether the accused will be moved from Kabwe. Whether the State will appeal the transfer. The outcome of the jurisdictional argument on bail, which the court had not decided at the time of writing. No outlet other than The Zambian Observer had published a full account of Thursday's rulings in the pages read for this edition.
