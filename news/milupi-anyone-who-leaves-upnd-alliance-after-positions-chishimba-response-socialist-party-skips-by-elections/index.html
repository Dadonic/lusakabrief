'Sacrifice is met with insinuation': Saviour Chishimba answers Charles Milupi's jibe at alliance partners who leave, as the Socialist Party explains why it is skipping by-elections

Charles Milupi, who chairs the UPND Alliance Council of Presidents, said that anyone leaving the alliance now that the President has finished his appointments would show they joined only for a position; only two alliance leaders, Milupi and Felix Mutati, are in Cabinet. UPP president Saviour Chishimba replied that he was one of six people at the alliance's founding meeting in 2017 and that Milupi has been persistently inaccessible. Separately, the Socialist Party says it is skipping by-elections to rebuild after a poor August.

By Lusaka Brief Politics Desk • LUSAKA — 8 November 2026 • 2 min read

Charles Milupi, Minister of Transport and Logistics and chairperson of the UPND Alliance Council of Presidents, said in an interview on Wednesday that any member who leaves the alliance now, after President Hakainde Hichilema has completed his appointments, would be showing that they had joined only to seek a position, News Diggers reported on Saturday 7 November. The President recently appointed 30 Cabinet ministers and 10 provincial ministers, and only two members of the Council of Presidents were among them: Milupi himself, and the MDC leader Felix Mutati, who kept his post as Minister of Technology and Science, according to the Diggers report, which The Zambian Observer republished.

The reply came within hours. In a statement published by The Zambian Observer late on Saturday under the heading that political alliances cannot flourish where consultation is absent and sacrifice is met with insinuation, Saviour Chishimba, president of the United Progressive People, objected to the remarks "if these remarks have indeed been accurately attributed". He said he was one of six people at the alliance's inaugural meeting at Community House in 2017, alongside Hichilema, Geoffrey Bwalya Mwamba, the late Mike Mulongoti, Milupi and Nevers Mumba; that Milupi had been persistently inaccessible to him as alliance chairperson, with calls and messages unanswered, a complaint he said he raised at the official opening of Parliament; and that he and others had carried heavy personal costs in the 2026 campaign from which they were still recovering. He called for an alliance chairperson who is accessible and whose public statements reinforce the dignity of members. The statement is Chishimba's own account; no response from Milupi had been reported by Sunday morning.

Hon. Milupi has remained persistently inaccessible to me in his capacity as Alliance Chairperson.
— Saviour Chishimba, president of the United Progressive People, in a statement published by The Zambian Observer on Saturday 7 November

The Socialist Party sits out

On the other side of the aisle, the Socialist Party has explained why it has not fielded a single candidate in the by-elections held since August. Its general secretary, Cosmas Musumali, told News Diggers in an interview published on Saturday that the party is concentrating on rebuilding its structures, that by-elections are expensive and the party has had to ask whether contesting them is economically viable given how weak those structures are, and that the decision came out of a review the party began immediately after what he called its very poor performance in the August general election.

The ruling party, meanwhile, spent the week answering its foreign critic. Gertrude Imenda, a UPND proportional-representation MP, said in an interview on Wednesday that the Patriotic Front administration had been more dictatorial than the present government, told the former Botswana president Ian Khama to attend to his own country's affairs, and said politicians should not escape punishment for offences they have committed, News Diggers reported on Saturday. Her remarks followed a statement on Monday by the Tonse Alliance chairman, Sakwiba Sikota, that Khama's description of Hichilema as Africa's newest dictator reflected growing concern about democracy and human rights in Zambia, according to the same report.
