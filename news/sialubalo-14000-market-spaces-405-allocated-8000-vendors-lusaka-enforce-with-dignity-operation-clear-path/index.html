Fourteen thousand stalls, 405 allocated: Sialubalo tells councils to move street vendors with dignity and hand out market spaces fairly, a month into Operation Clear Path

Lusaka City Council has more than 14,000 trading spaces in 34 markets available for immediate allocation but has handed out only 405 of them, the Minister of Local Government and Rural Development, Gift Sialubalo, told Parliament in a ministerial statement on Tuesday 3 November. With about 8,000 registered street vendors in the city, he said the arithmetic allows every one of them a stall, and told councils that enforcement must be lawful and humane and that allocation must be transparent.

By Lusaka Brief Business Desk • LUSAKA — 6 November 2026 • 2 min read

A month after Lusaka City Council began clearing traders from the capital's streets, the minister responsible has told Parliament that the problem is no longer a shortage of market stalls but the way they are handed out. The Zambia Daily Mail reported on Wednesday 4 November, and News Diggers on Thursday, that the Minister of Local Government and Rural Development, Gift Sialubalo, had given a ministerial statement on street-vending control and the allocation of trading spaces on Tuesday 3 November.

His figures, as reported by both outlets, were these: Lusaka has 34 markets with more than 14,000 trading spaces available for immediate allocation; only 405 have so far been allocated; and the city has roughly 8,000 registered street vendors, all of whom could be accommodated in the space available. News Diggers added that Operation Clear Path, the council's campaign to remove vendors from the central business district, was launched on 2 October, and that the council has introduced regular patrols to stop traders returning.

Allocation, not just removal

The Daily Mail reported that Mr Sialubalo cited allegations of unfair allocation, multiple occupation of trading spaces by single traders, unauthorised subletting and demands for unofficial payments, and that he urged local authorities to allocate stalls "in a transparent, fair and accountable manner". He said removing traders without offering alternative spaces might not solve street vending. News Diggers reported that he asked councils to inform traders of the spaces available to them.

'Lawfully, professionally, consistently and humanely'

On enforcement, the minister's language was pointed. The Daily Mail quoted him saying it should be carried out "lawfully, professionally, consistently and humanely"; News Diggers headlined the same message as an instruction to evict vendors with dignity and to ensure enforcement officers treat them accordingly. The newspaper's report quotes him beginning a passage on the capital's role as "the country's principal commercial" centre, but the rest sits behind its paywall.

The statement is the clearest official account yet of the gap between the council's clearance campaign and the alternative it is supposed to offer. Lusaka Brief has reported since early October on the dawn operations, the fines handed down by a fast-track court and the second phase of the operation in Katondo Street; the minister's numbers suggest that, five weeks in, about one in twenty of the city's registered vendors has been given a market space.

Sources: Zambia Daily Mail, “Sialubalo calls for fair stall allocation” (4 Nov 2026); News Diggers, “Evict street vendors with dignity, minister urges councils” (5 Nov 2026).
