A flash disk, five paragraphs and a second objection: the treason bail hearing stalls again, and the court rules on Friday whether the raid video stays on the record

A day after losing its first preliminary objection, the State raised another at the Lusaka High Court on Wednesday 11 November, asking the three-judge panel to strike five paragraphs and a video recording from the affidavit filed by Brian Mundubile, Makebi Zulu and 16 co-accused. The defence says the footage shows the raid on Zulu's home and answers the State's flight-risk claim. The court adjourned to 11:00 on Friday 13 November to rule, then hear the bail plea. In the sedition case, The Zambian Observer reported that the two dismissed their lawyers.

By Lusaka Brief Courts Desk • LUSAKA — 12 November 2026 • 5 min read

The constitutional bail application by the defeated presidential candidate Brian Mundubile, his running mate Makebi Zulu and 16 other people charged with treason was supposed to be argued on Wednesday 11 November. Instead the day was spent on a new preliminary objection. The Director of Public Prosecutions, Gilbert Phiri, asked the Lusaka High Court to strike out five paragraphs and a video recording from the applicants' affidavit in reply, Zambia Monitor reported on Thursday morning, and the three-judge panel adjourned to 11:00 on Friday 13 November to rule on that request and, if it can, to hear the bail application itself.

The objection came a day after the same panel, Justices Derrick Mulenga, Davies Chibwili and Ireen Wishimanga, dismissed the State's first preliminary objection, which had argued that the application was begun in the wrong form. News Diggers, which carried the defence lawyer Sakwiba Sikota's account on Thursday, reported that the State's new position is that the applicants raised fresh issues in their affidavit in reply on which it had not been allowed to respond.

What is on the disk

According to The Zambian Observer, which carried a TV Yatu report on Wednesday afternoon, the footage shows a shoot-out during a raid on Mr Zulu's residence, and the defence filed it to answer the State's argument that the accused are a flight risk because they allegedly evaded arrest, including by seeking shelter at United Nations premises. The defence's position, as Mr Sikota put it, is that the applicants went to the UN because they feared for their lives, and that this should not count against them in a bail decision. He said the substantive hearing could only start once the preliminary issues were disposed of.

It seems they [State] are very uncomfortable with the flash disk.
— Sakwiba Sikota, lawyer for the applicants, speaking outside court on Wednesday 11 November, as quoted by The Zambian Observer

Zambia Monitor set out the legal arguments in more detail. The State questioned the authenticity, origin and integrity of the recording and cited Section 9 of the Electronic Communications and Transactions Act, together with the case of OTK Limited v Amanita Zambia Limited and Others, to argue that electronic evidence must be authenticated before it is admitted. The applicants answered that the disputed paragraphs respond directly to the State's flight-risk claim and that doubts about authenticity go to the weight of the evidence, not to whether it can be received. Mr Zulu, who is representing himself as the second applicant, argued that the affidavit had been properly sworn before a Commissioner for Oaths, that the State's demands belong to oral testimony at trial rather than affidavit proceedings, and that a flash disk is a document under the Evidence Act. The panel observed that the matter is a constitutional case with criminal implications before adjourning.

The Observer's earlier report on Wednesday said the hearing had been listed for 09:00 and that the Attorney General, Mulilo Kabesha, had asked for an adjournment because of another court commitment. The 18 applicants have been in custody since 14 August over alleged treason, sedition and terrorism-related offences, the Observer reported; they have sued Mr Kabesha and Mr Phiri, arguing that their continued detention breaches their constitutional rights, and are asking for unconditional release or bail on reasonable conditions.

The sedition case, and the lawyers

Mr Mundubile and Mr Zulu were also due before the Lusaka Magistrate's Court on Wednesday in the seditious practices case transferred from Solwezi. The Daily Nation reported that proceedings did not start in the morning because the two accused were required at the High Court, and that the matter was pushed to 12:00. The Zambian Observer reported on Wednesday evening that the two men had dismissed their lawyers in that case and asked for ten minutes before taking plea; its short report, built around a Zambian Watch video, did not say why the lawyers were let go or who will now represent them, and no other outlet read for this edition had confirmed the detail by Thursday morning. The sedition case alleges that the two declared themselves winners of the August election before the Electoral Commission of Zambia announced the result; that is the State's allegation, and the case has not been tried. Their bail application in the sedition case was adjourned on Monday to Monday 16 November, the Observer reported on Wednesday morning.

The Observer also carried a statement on Wednesday by the governance activist Laura Miti, who placed the detentions in a line that runs from the 1993 Zero Option arrests through Kenneth Kaunda's 1997 detention to Hakainde Hichilema's own treason arrest in 2017, and said the State should either bring substantive charges to trial or release the accused. The State's position in court, as reported by the Observer, is that the detentions are lawful and that the accused are flight risks.

What happens on Friday

  • 11:00, Lusaka High Court — ruling on the State's application to strike five paragraphs and the video from the affidavit in reply.
  • If the objection fails or is disposed of — the constitutional bail application can finally be argued.
  • Monday 16 November, Lusaka Magistrate's Court — the adjourned bail hearing in the sedition case.

Confirmed vs. alleged

Confirmed: That the State raised a second preliminary objection on Wednesday 11 November seeking to strike five paragraphs and a video from the applicants' affidavit in reply, that the applicants opposed it, and that the court adjourned to 11:00 on Friday 13 November to rule and then hear the bail application — from Zambia Monitor, News Diggers and The Zambian Observer. That the sedition proceedings were pushed to midday on Wednesday — from the Daily Nation.

Alleged or unverified: What the video actually shows: the defence's description of a raid and shoot-out at Mr Zulu's residence has not been tested, and the State disputes its authenticity. The report that the two dismissed their lawyers in the sedition case rests on The Zambian Observer alone, and the reasons are not known. All 18 are presumed innocent; treason and sedition are allegations the State must prove.
