Mandevu has an MP without a ballot: Adrian Banda is declared elected, a minister denies pushing out the Tonse candidate, and the ECZ says it counts votes, not makes them

The UPND's Adrian Banda was declared the winner of the Mandevu parliamentary by-election on Thursday 5 November without a vote being cast, The Zambian Observer reports, a week after his three opponents withdrew. Lusaka Province Minister Obvious Mwaliteta has denied Tonse Alliance claims that he warned the FDD's Daniel Chisenga he would 'get into trouble', and ECZ commissioner McDonald Chipenzi told agents in Sioma that the Commission counts votes and does not manufacture them.

By Lusaka Brief Politics Desk • LUSAKA — 6 November 2026 • 3 min read

Mandevu's parliamentary by-election ended on Thursday 5 November the way the Electoral Commission of Zambia said it would: with a declaration and no poll. The Zambian Observer reported celebrations in the constituency as Adrian Banda of the United Party for National Development was declared the winner, the only candidate left after the Forum for Democracy and Development's Daniel Chisenga, the Leadership Movement's Ntazana Musukuma Mutungwa and the independent Daniel Daka pulled out. The Observer's short report did not name the declaring officer.

The legal route had been laid out a week earlier. Zambia Monitor reported on Friday 30 October that the Commission's Chief Electoral Officer, Brown Kasaro, had issued a public notice citing Regulations 20(3) and 52(2) of the Electoral Process (General) Regulations of 2016, after receiving three withdrawal letters dated 28 October, and had said Mr Banda, "being the only validly nominated candidate, will be declared duly elected Member of Parliament". The Zambia Daily Mail repeated on Thursday morning that the seat would be filled without a poll. Mr Banda stood in Mandevu as an independent in August; the Observer reported on 1 October, carrying a UPND media release, that he had rejoined the ruling party, saying, "I have rejoined the UPND because it is a winning team."

'I cannot entice people to withdraw'

The withdrawals remain contested. News Diggers reported on Thursday that the Tonse Alliance's secretary general, Muhabi Lungu, had claimed Lusaka Province Minister Obvious Mwaliteta telephoned Mr Chisenga and warned him he would "get into trouble". Mr Mwaliteta, in an interview on Wednesday 4 November, denied it. "I cannot entice people to withdraw," he said, describing himself as a politician who believes in democracy and saying he lacked the capacity to do what Mr Lungu alleged. He told Mr Lungu to put his own house in order and said, according to the newspaper, that the alliance had lacked the money to back its candidate. The rest of his remarks sit behind the newspaper's paywall, and Mr Chisenga's own account of why he withdrew has not appeared in the reports read for this edition.

The President in Mandevu

The Zambia Daily Mail reported on Thursday morning that President Hakainde Hichilema was expected in Mandevu that day, at the Mutambe Community grounds, to meet residents and take their questions, following a State House announcement which the paper said had drawn more than 13,000 comments within three hours. The Zambian Observer had reported on Wednesday that the President framed the visit as an unscripted engagement, telling residents, "bring your neighbours, bring your questions and let us talk about what matters to you", and that fuel prices, inflation and jobs were expected to dominate. No account of what was said at the meeting was available in the pages read for this edition.

Six wards vote

Thursday was also polling day in six council wards. Ahead of the Mbeta ward poll in Sioma, Western Province, ECZ commissioner McDonald Chipenzi told a stakeholders' briefing on Tuesday 3 November, reported by the Zambia Daily Mail and News Diggers, that parties should recruit honest polling agents for every station rather than blame the Commission afterwards. "Our role as a commission is to count votes, and not to manufacture votes. We don't manufacture votes. We only want counts," he said, according to the Daily Mail. News Diggers quoted him saying preparations had concluded and "we're ready for the polls". Results from the six wards had not been published in the sources read at the time of writing.
