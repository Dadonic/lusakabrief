'Must immediately release the eighteen': Amnesty International asks Hichilema to free the treason detainees, ahead of High Court rulings on their liberty on 20 and 24 November

In an Urgent Action dated Tuesday 17 November, Amnesty International asked President Hakainde Hichilema to release Brian Mundubile, Makebi Zulu and 16 others detained since the August election, saying they were held 16 days without charge, denied timely access to courts, lawyers and family, and allegedly tortured. Reported by Zambia Monitor and the Zambian Observer on Wednesday, the appeal lands as the High Court prepares rulings on 20 and 24 November. Spokesperson Elvis Nkandu told News Diggers the government does not control judges.

By Lusaka Brief Politics Desk • LUSAKA — 19 November 2026 • 4 min read

Amnesty International has added its name to the calls for the release of the 18 men charged with treason after Zambia's 13 August general election. In an Urgent Action dated Tuesday 17 November, numbered UA 121/26 and indexed AFR 63/1686/2026, Amnesty International asked President Hakainde Hichilema to free the detainees at once, Zambia Monitor reported on Wednesday 18 November. The Zambian Observer carried the same appeal on Wednesday evening.

The document names the 18; the two reports list six of them: Ezra Tolopa, Dickson Tembo, Victor Muzyamba, Harry Valden Findlay, Brian Mundubile and Makebi Zulu. Amnesty describes the 18 as affiliated to the National Reconciliation Party for Unity and Prosperity (NRPUP) and the Tonse-Pamodzi Alliance, and says they are being held for exercising their rights to take part in public affairs and to voice opposing political views. Zambia Monitor's account of the document says the detainees were held without charge, denied timely access to the courts, to lawyers and to their families, and allegedly tortured.

Amnesty's timeline

The Urgent Action, as summarised by Zambia Monitor, sets out a sequence of events. On 1 August, Tolopa, Tembo and Muzyamba were arrested at Kenneth Kaunda International Airport and, Amnesty alleges, tortured, including by waterboarding. On 9 August four men were charged with a terrorism-related offence and moved to Mwembeshi Correctional Facility. On 14 August, the day after the vote, security forces raided a property linked to Mundubile and arrested him and others; Amnesty alleges that Mutotwe Kafwaya, who was at the property, was fatally shot. Those held were, in Amnesty's account, kept for 16 days without charge. On 29 August the 18 were charged with treason, and on 30 August they were moved to Mukobeko Maximum Correctional Facility, where they remain.

Amnesty's position, as both outlets report it, is that national security cannot justify arbitrary detention or restrictions on freedom of expression and association, and that the authorities' legitimate power to investigate and prosecute genuine offences has to be used within Zambia's international obligations. The Zambian Observer quotes the document as saying the authorities "must immediately release the eighteen individuals". Neither report carried a response from the government to the appeal.

What the government has said

The government's general position was set out this week by Chief Government Spokesperson Elvis Nkandu. In an interview reported by News Diggers on Wednesday, Nkandu responded to Economic Front leader Wynter Kabimba, who had warned that the ruling party would suffer in the long run if the State failed to secure convictions or dropped the treason case. Nkandu said the government was law-abiding and did not control judges, and that the UPND would stay in power because of its works rather than out of sympathy.

Calls for release have also come from inside Zambian politics. Former minister George Chulumanda, responding to the police advice against the 25 November term-limit processions, called for Mundubile and Zulu to be freed and urged Zambians to defend the Constitution through peaceful action, the Zambian Observer reported on Wednesday.

The church and the courts

The Council of Churches in Zambia has meanwhile sought to correct the narrative about its role on the day of the arrests. Its General Secretary, Rev Canon James Phiri, told a caller on Radio Christian Voice's Chat Back programme that the CCZ had been engaged as a neutral party to pass Mundubile and Zulu to their lawyers at the Cathedral of the Holy Cross, not to hand them to the State, and that church leaders only noticed police officers as the two were being escorted outside, the Zambian Observer reported, citing KBN TV. Phiri also said the current presidential term limit was appropriate.

The appeal arrives at a decisive moment for the detainees' lawyers. The Lusaka High Court is due to rule on Friday 20 November on a preliminary issue raised by the State in the separate bail application brought by Mundubile and Zulu on seditious practices charges, and on Tuesday 24 November on the constitutional bail application filed by all 18, News Diggers reported on Tuesday 17 November. Defence lawyer Sakwiba Sikota, the paper reported, described the security arrangements around the treason case as "over-dramatised".

Key dates

  • 1 August — three men arrested at Kenneth Kaunda International Airport, per Amnesty
  • 14 August — raid on a property linked to Mundubile; arrests
  • 29 August — 18 charged with treason; moved to Mukobeko the next day
  • 17 November — Amnesty International Urgent Action issued
  • 20 November — High Court ruling on the State's preliminary issue in the sedition bail case
  • 24 November — High Court ruling on constitutional bail for the 18

Confirmed vs. alleged

Confirmed: Amnesty International issued an Urgent Action dated 17 November calling for the release of the 18, as reported by Zambia Monitor and the Zambian Observer. The 18 were charged with treason on 29 August and are held at Mukobeko. The High Court has fixed 20 and 24 November for its rulings, per News Diggers. The government's spokesperson says it does not control judges, per News Diggers.

Alleged or unverified: The allegations of torture, including waterboarding, and the circumstances of Mutotwe Kafwaya's death are Amnesty's account; neither report includes a government or police answer to them. No response from the government to the Urgent Action itself had been reported by Wednesday night. Charged is not guilty: all 18 are presumed innocent.
