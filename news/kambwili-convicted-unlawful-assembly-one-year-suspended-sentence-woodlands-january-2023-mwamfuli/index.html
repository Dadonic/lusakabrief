Guilty, but not going to prison: Chishimba Kambwili is convicted of unlawful assembly over a 2023 gathering at his gate and given a one-year suspended sentence

Lusaka resident magistrate Kawama Mwamfuli found on Monday 9 November that a crowd of 15 to 20 youths who sang at the former minister's Woodlands home on 26 January 2023 had turned a lawful gathering into an unlawful one, and that his role in facilitating it made him liable even though he had told them to stop. The sentence is suspended; The Zambian Observer reported that the magistrate cited his health. No appeal has been reported.

By Lusaka Brief Courts Desk • LUSAKA — 10 November 2026 • 2 min read

Chishimba Kambwili, the former Information Minister and former Member of Parliament for Roan, was convicted of unlawful assembly in the Lusaka Magistrates' Court on Monday 9 November and sentenced to one year in prison, suspended. Resident Magistrate Kawama Mwamfuli found that the prosecution had proved the charge beyond reasonable doubt, Zambia Monitor reported. The two outlets that covered the judgment differ on one detail: Zambia Monitor said the charge was brought under Section 75 of the Penal Code, The Zambian Observer under Section 74.

The case concerned a gathering at Mr Kambwili's home in Woodlands on 26 January 2023. According to the judgment as reported by Zambia Monitor, between 15 and 20 youths came onto the premises carrying placards and sang a song containing tribal sentiments. Mr Kambwili's media team filmed the scene and the footage was later posted on his Facebook page. The court accepted that he had cautioned the young people against the song and against hate speech, and that he did not join in the singing, but held that by facilitating the gathering he was criminally liable for what it became.

Lawful at the gate, unlawful inside

The magistrate's reasoning, as Zambia Monitor set it out, turned on two points. First, the gathering had started lawfully because the police had been notified in advance, but it became unlawful through the conduct of the participants, which the court found was likely to provoke a breach of the peace. Second, the fact that it took place at a private residence did not put it outside the law on unlawful assembly. Senior Public Prosecutor Lewis Loti of the National Prosecution Authority appeared for the State.

The defence had argued throughout, The Zambian Observer reported, that Mr Kambwili was simply receiving visitors who had come to apologise for earlier political slights, while the State characterised the crowd as an unauthorised assembly aimed at disturbing public peace. The Observer, which described the crowd as disgruntled youths singing about the cost of living, said the magistrate decided against a custodial sentence on account of Mr Kambwili's health; the paper noted that he had often needed help from prison officers to get into court. Under the terms of the judgment as the Observer described them, he will not serve the sentence unless he commits a similar offence within a period the reports do not specify.

Mr Kambwili made no statement to reporters as he left the court, the Observer said, and neither outlet reported whether he intends to appeal.

Confirmed vs. alleged

Confirmed: The conviction, the suspended one-year sentence, the magistrate's name, the date and place of the gathering, and the court's reasoning on why a notified gathering became unlawful, all from Zambia Monitor and The Zambian Observer.

Alleged or unverified: The exact section of the Penal Code (the two reports differ), the length of the suspension period, and whether an appeal will be lodged.
