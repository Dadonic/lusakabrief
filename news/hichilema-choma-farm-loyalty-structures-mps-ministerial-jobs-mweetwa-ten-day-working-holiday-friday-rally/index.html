'A true leader should never take people for granted': from his Choma farm, Hichilema tells MPs who wanted ministries to be grateful for their seats and the party to stay united

Speaking to supporters who gathered at his farm in Choma on Tuesday 10 November, at the start of a ten-day working holiday in Southern Province, the President told elected leaders and appointees to remain loyal and respectful to the UPND structures that built the party, and told MPs unhappy at being passed over for Cabinet to appreciate that voters elected them, citing the Choma MP Cornelius Mweetwa's decade in the seat. A public thank-you rally in Choma follows on Friday.

By Lusaka Brief Politics Desk • CHOMA — 12 November 2026 • 2 min read

President Hakainde Hichilema used a ten-day working holiday in Southern Province to speak to his own party. Addressing residents and supporters who had gathered to welcome him at his farm in Choma District on Tuesday 10 November, he told UPND elected leaders and ministerial appointees to remain loyal and respectful to the party structures, and said that holding public office should not make anyone forget the people who built the party, the Times of Zambia reported on Wednesday.

News Diggers reported the sharper edge of the same remarks. The President said elected leaders should not take people for granted, because people have their own views, feelings and opinions, and he told MPs unhappy at not having been named ministers to appreciate that voters had chosen them. He pointed to the Choma MP, Cornelius Mweetwa, who has held the seat for more than ten years, as his example. He also asked UPND members to mobilise support more actively and to publicise the government's achievements, saying the party had learned lessons from the August election campaign.

a true leader should never take people for granted because they have their own views, feelings and opinions
— President Hakainde Hichilema, speaking in Choma District on Tuesday 10 November, as quoted by News Diggers

What else he said

The Times of Zambia reported that the President asked party members in the province to keep their structures functional and united, and announced a major public engagement in Choma on Friday to thank citizens for renewing the UPND's mandate. He said his main concern was the welfare of Zambians, especially children and orphans and their access to a good education, and that the public university planned for Choma would serve the whole country rather than Southern Province alone; every province, he said, deserved one. The visit includes public engagements and tours of development projects, the Times reported.

News Diggers framed the reference to Mr Mweetwa as a message to MPs who had hoped for ministerial jobs after the August election. Neither outlet reported a response from Mr Mweetwa or any other MP, and neither carried an opposition reaction.

The President's Southern Province programme runs for ten days, the Times of Zambia reported, with Friday's Choma rally the first fixed public event.

Sources: Times of Zambia, “'Remain loyal, respectful,' HH tells leaders” (11 Nov 2026); News Diggers, “Be grateful you're an MP, stop complaining about ministerial jobs, HH tells Mweetwa” (11 Nov 2026).
