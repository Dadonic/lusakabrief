'Not conditional on any commercial agreements': US Democrats plan probes into Africa minerals deals from January, with the reported threat to Zambia's HIV aid at the centre

Democrats preparing to take control of the US House of Representatives in January say they will open formal investigations into the Trump administration's critical-minerals agreements in Africa, Semafor reported, in a story that reached Zambian readers through the Zambian Observer on Wednesday 18 November. Among their grievances is a March report that the State Department threatened to withhold HIV assistance to push Zambia into a minerals deal. A State Department spokesperson said US health assistance is not conditional on any commercial agreement.

By Lusaka Brief Health Desk • WASHINGTON — 19 November 2026 • 2 min read

The question of whether American health money was used as a bargaining chip in talks over Zambia's minerals is about to get a formal hearing in Washington. Democratic members of the US House of Representatives, who take the majority in January, intend to open investigations into the Trump administration's critical-minerals agreements across Africa, Semafor reported on Monday 16 November in a story syndicated by Yahoo News. The Zambian Observer carried the report on Wednesday 18 November, framing it around what it called the Zambia health aid controversy.

Representative Jonathan Jackson, a Democrat from Illinois, told Semafor that the deals had been struck in near-total secrecy and that his party now had a mandate to open formal investigations and commission public reports mapping who was receiving them. Jackson, Representative Linda Sánchez and Representative Jared Huffman, both of California, wrote to four federal agencies in August; Jackson said the administration's reply dodged almost all of their questions, and Sánchez called it little more than something artificial intelligence would write, adding that oversight changes in January.

The Zambia allegation

The Zambian strand dates from March 2026, when the New York Times reported that the State Department had threatened to withhold HIV aid to press Zambia into a minerals agreement. Representative Jimmy Panetta, also of California, who has pressed Secretary of State Marco Rubio for details of new global health agreements signed with 36 countries, told Semafor that using lifesaving aid to extract unrelated economic concessions was what Russia, Iran or China had traditionally done. The Semafor report gives no figures for the aid at stake and names no Zambian officials or companies.

The administration's answer

The State Department rejected the premise. A spokesperson told Semafor that "our health assistance is not conditional on any commercial agreements", and that the health pacts contained nothing related to critical minerals. White House spokeswoman Lauren Bis said the President was willing to work with anyone to deliver for Americans, and that Democrats pursuing what she called sham investigations would be working against the American people.

For Zambia the stakes sit on both sides of the ledger: the HIV assistance the March report said was at risk, and the copper and cobalt that the Observer's report describes as the object of Washington's effort to expand access to Africa's strategic minerals. No Zambian government response to the planned probes had been reported by Wednesday night, and neither article identifies which agreement with Zambia, if any, would be examined first.

Sources: Semafor (via Yahoo News), “Democrats plan probes into Africa minerals deals” (16 Nov 2026); The Zambian Observer, “US Democrats to investigate Trump's African minerals deals amid Zambia health aid controversy” (18 Nov 2026).
