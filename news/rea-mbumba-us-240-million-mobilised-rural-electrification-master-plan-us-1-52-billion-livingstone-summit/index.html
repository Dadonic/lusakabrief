US$240 million of US$1.52 billion: the Rural Electrification Authority tells the Livingstone summit how far it has got with the master plan for universal access by 2030

Zambia has so far mobilised US$240 million from the government and the World Bank towards the US$1.52 billion its updated Rural Electrification Master Plan says is needed to reach universal electricity access by 2030, the Rural Electrification Authority's chief executive, Alex Mbumba, told a panel at the Zimbabwe-Zambia Energy Projects Summit in Livingstone, according to News Diggers. The plan, launched in April, relies on solar mini-grids and home systems as well as grid extension.

By Lusaka Brief Business Desk • LIVINGSTONE — 6 November 2026 • 2 min read

The third Zimbabwe-Zambia Energy Projects Summit in Livingstone, which Lusaka Brief reported on Thursday as it opened, has produced a figure for how far Zambia's rural electrification plan has got. News Diggers reported on Thursday 5 November that the Rural Electrification Authority's Chief Executive Officer, Alex Mbumba, told a panel discussion entitled "Rural Power Play: Turning Access into Productive Use" that US$240 million had been mobilised from the government and the World Bank towards the plan.

That is against a total requirement which Mr Mbumba put at US$1.52 billion. "If you read the Electrification Master Plan, you will see that it provides an envelope of US$1.52 billion required," he said, in the part of his remarks published before the newspaper's paywall. He said the plan aims to deliver connections through grid extension, solar mini-grids and solar home systems, and would bridge the gap between the private and public sectors. The rest of his contribution was not available.

The plan behind the number

The updated Rural Electrification Master Plan was launched in April at the Mulungushi International Conference Centre by the Minister of Energy, Makozo Chikote, the trade publication SolarQuarter reported on 15 April 2026. Its target is universal electricity access by 2030, in line with Vision 2030, and its cost was put at about US$1.52 billion, the same figure Mr Mbumba quoted in Livingstone. SolarQuarter reported that the minister described the plan as "bankable", that it replaces scattered projects with a coordinated programme meant to avoid duplication, that the Authority is shifting its focus to "last mile" connections for remote communities, and that a digital platform for the public to track progress was promised by the end of 2026.

On the summit's own figures, as Lusaka Brief reported on Thursday, Zimbabwe's ZESA counted 635 megawatts of generation under construction while ZESCO described a market opening to private producers. Mr Mbumba's numbers show the scale of the rural task on the Zambian side: with roughly a sixth of the money in hand and a little over four years to the 2030 target, the remainder depends on the public-private partnerships the plan was built to attract. Neither report gives a connection count achieved so far under the updated plan.

Sources: News Diggers, “We've mobilised $240M for the Rural Electrification Master Plan – REA” (5 Nov 2026); SolarQuarter, “Zambia Launches Updated Rural Electrification Master Plan To Achieve Universal Power Access By 2030” (15 Apr 2026).
