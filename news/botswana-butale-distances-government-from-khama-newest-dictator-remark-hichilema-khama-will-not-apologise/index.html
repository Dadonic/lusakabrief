'Excellent diplomatic relations': Botswana's foreign minister distances his government from Ian Khama's attack on Hichilema, as Khama says he will not apologise

Dr Phenyo Butale, Botswana's Minister of International Relations, has said that the former president's description of Hakainde Hichilema as 'Africa's newest dictator' is not his government's position, and has defended Gaborone's attendance at the September inauguration, The Zambian Observer reported on Sunday 8 November. Mr Khama told the Botswana Gazette last week that he stands by his allegations. Neither report records a formal exchange between the two governments.

By Lusaka Brief Politics Desk • LUSAKA — 9 November 2026 • 4 min read

Botswana's government has drawn a line between itself and its former president. Dr Phenyo Butale, the Minister of International Relations, has said that Ian Khama's description of President Hakainde Hichilema as "Africa's newest dictator" does not reflect official policy, and has defended the decision to attend Mr Hichilema's inauguration after the August general election, The Zambian Observer reported on Sunday 8 November. The Observer said the minister spoke in a telephone interview and to Botswana's The Voice newspaper; it did not say on which day.

We enjoy excellent diplomatic relations with Zambia, hence we attended the inauguration of President Hichilema.
— Dr Phenyo Butale, Botswana's Minister of International Relations, in a telephone interview quoted by The Zambian Observer on Sunday 8 November

The minister's case, as the Observer set it out, is about who speaks for a state. The opinions of an individual, he said, "should not be taken as representative of the policy or position of the sitting Government", whatever office that individual once held. Remarks by a former head of state can be mistaken for an official line, he said, and the government was alive to the possibility that this could affect "diplomatic confidence, complicate bilateral engagement" and damage Botswana's standing abroad. He added that Botswana had held no discussions with Zambia about the outcome of its election and that foreign policy is communicated through authorised government institutions. He did not dispute Mr Khama's right to speak: the Observer reported that he defended the former president's constitutional freedom to express an opinion while urging former officials to act responsibly on sensitive international matters.

How the row began

The remark that started it was made in Ntlo ya Dikgosi in Gaborone, where Mr Khama sits as Kgosi Khama IV, while he was questioning Dr Butale about Botswana's attendance at the inauguration, the Botswana Gazette reported on 4 November. The same paper had reported on 28 October that he used the chamber to challenge the UDC government over what the paper described as its reluctance to condemn alleged abuses in other African states, naming Tanzania, Uganda, Rwanda and Zambia, and that Dr Butale replied that Botswana works through regional and continental bodies and observes the principle of non-interference. President Duma Boko attended Mr Hichilema's second-term inauguration in Lusaka on 1 September, the Gazette reported on 30 September.

Mr Khama has not retreated. "Absolutely, I will not apologise," he told the Gazette in the report published on 4 November, in which he stood by allegations of political persecution and interference with electoral and judicial processes in Zambia, cited arrests, detentions and beatings, and said Zambians had sent him messages of thanks. In September he had told the same paper that his public criticism did not run parallel to Botswana's foreign policy and that no minister had called him to object.

The reaction in Lusaka

The Zambian response has come from several directions. The Gazette quoted State House Chief Communications Specialist Clayson Hamasaka as saying Zambians "can't believe those statements are attributed to a former President", reported that former Vice-President Enock Kavindele had urged Mr Khama to respect Zambia's sovereignty and produce evidence, and quoted nominated MP Judith Kabemba Nkonge calling the remarks "reckless and unbecoming of a former head of state". A youth advocate, Mukuka Kampamba, demanded a public apology within 24 hours and asked President Boko to step in, the paper said. UPND Deputy Secretary General Gertrude Imenda told News Diggers that Mr Khama should concentrate on his own country's affairs and that the Patriotic Front had been more dictatorial in office than the UPND, the paper reported on 7 November.

Not every Zambian voice has been critical of the former president. Tonse Alliance chairman Sakwiba Sikota said on Monday 2 November that the description reflected growing concern about democracy and human rights in Zambia, according to the same News Diggers report. Human Rights Watch said on 2 November that it had documented 14 apparent enforced disappearances in August and 71 arrests on charges it considered potentially politically motivated, Zambia Monitor reported; the Gazette notes that Mr Hichilema's government rejects allegations of electoral wrongdoing.

What Dr Butale's statement settles is narrow. He has said that Mr Khama's words are not Botswana's; he has not, in the remarks reported, said whether they are true. None of the reports read for this edition records a formal protest from Lusaka to Gaborone, a reply from Zambia's foreign ministry to the minister's remarks, or any step by Botswana's government to restrain the former president.

The sequence, as reported

  • 1 September: President Duma Boko attends Mr Hichilema's second-term inauguration in Lusaka (Botswana Gazette)
  • 30 September: Mr Khama says no minister has asked him to stop criticising African leaders (Botswana Gazette)
  • 28 October: the Gazette reports his challenge in Ntlo ya Dikgosi over Tanzania, Uganda, Rwanda and Zambia; Dr Butale cites non-interference
  • 4 November: "Absolutely, I will not apologise" (Botswana Gazette)
  • 8 November: The Zambian Observer reports Dr Butale's statement that the remarks are not government policy

Confirmed vs. alleged

Confirmed: Dr Butale is reported by The Zambian Observer to have said that Mr Khama's views are not Botswana's policy and that Botswana attended the inauguration because of its relations with Zambia. Mr Khama told the Botswana Gazette he will not apologise. State House, a former Vice-President, a nominated MP and the UPND's deputy secretary general have criticised his remarks, according to the Gazette and News Diggers.

Alleged or unverified: Mr Khama's allegations of persecution and electoral interference are his own and are rejected by the Zambian government. The Observer does not say when Dr Butale spoke, and its account has not been checked against The Voice's original report. Whether the two governments have communicated formally about the matter has not been reported.
