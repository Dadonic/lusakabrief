While Lusaka watched a courtroom, the President landed in Abu Dhabi — and Haimbe told the UN that Zambia is stable and open

Day one of the UAE working visit puts a bilateral meeting, MoUs and an investment forum on the President's schedule. In New York, the foreign minister has already delivered the companion message: constitutionalism intact, Human Rights Council candidacy live, and the Grow Zambia Agenda open for partners.

By Chanda Mwape • LUSAKA — 29 September 2026 • 4 min read

The presidency did not pause for the Archbishop's Monday. President Hakainde Hichilema arrived in Abu Dhabi at the start of a two-day working visit that, by the ministry's design, is about conversion — turning three years of warming relations with the Emirates into signed memoranda, an investment forum and progress toward a Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement. An official bilateral meeting with UAE President Sheikh Mohamed bin Zayed Al Nahyan anchors the visit, alongside the Zambia-UAE Business and Investment Forum, engagements with Emirati business leaders and a meeting with the Zambian community in the UAE. "The focus in Abu Dhabi is to attract investment and promote private sector-led growth," the President said on departure, framing the trip squarely inside the Grow Zambia Agenda: minerals and value addition, agriculture, energy, infrastructure, logistics, tourism and healthcare.

The relationship's plumbing is new enough to matter. Zambia opened its Abu Dhabi embassy in 2023; the UAE's first resident ambassador presented credentials in Lusaka only this month. What Lusaka wants — capital at a scale the domestic budget cannot produce — is precisely what Abu Dhabi deploys in exchange for minerals, food security and corridors. The ministry's pre-visit phrasing about "outstanding implementation gaps" is the honest line in the briefing pack: several announced commitments have yet to become projects, and this visit is partly an audit of that gap.

In New York, the companion track played on Monday. Foreign Affairs Minister Mulambo Haimbe delivered Zambia's national statement to the 81st UN General Assembly's General Debate, under the session's theme of restoring trust in a United Nations that delivers. The statement — delivered on the President's behalf — reaffirmed Zambia's commitment to constitutionalism, the rule of law and fundamental freedoms, and carried the delegation's live priority: Zambia's candidature for a Human Rights Council seat for the 2027–2029 term, with the vote set for 13 October. The minister had spent the days before the address on a parallel defensive line, publicly refuting claims of anti-Zambia protests at UN headquarters — a rebuttal aimed at social-media reports that the delegation judged damaging enough to answer.

Neither capital will mention the Archbishop. Both audiences will know. The government's week now runs on three screens at once — a courtroom in Lusaka, a palace in Abu Dhabi and a rostrum in New York — and the message on two of them is stability. The opposition's counter-message is that the third screen contradicts the other two. The Human Rights Council vote in a fortnight is where those two tellings meet an actual count.
