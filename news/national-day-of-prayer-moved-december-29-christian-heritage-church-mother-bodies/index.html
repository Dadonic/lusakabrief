The prayer day moves: church mother bodies merge 18 October with the Christian-nation commemoration — one national event, on 29 December

Zambia's National Day of Prayer, Fasting, Repentance and Reconciliation — a fixture of 18 October since 2015 — is being merged with the commemoration of the Christian-nation declaration into a single observance on 29 December 2026, under the theme 'National Day of Prayer and Christian Heritage'. The church mother bodies say consolidation enhances rather than diminishes the day; this year, the prayers are asked to happen in the pews, not the stadium.

By Lusaka Brief Culture Desk • LUSAKA — 10 October 2026 • 4 min read

Two dates on Zambia's spiritual calendar are becoming one. The church mother bodies — the Council of Churches in Zambia, the Evangelical Fellowship of Zambia, the Zambia Conference of Catholic Bishops, the Seventh-Day Adventist Church and the Independent Churches of Zambia among them — announced in Lusaka this week that, after consultations with the Government, the National Day of Prayer, Fasting, Repentance and Reconciliation will be merged with the commemoration of Zambia's declaration as a Christian nation into a single national event.

CCZ vice-president Bishop Juden Siachitema told the press briefing that 29 December 2026 will mark the first commemoration of the merged event, under the theme “National Day of Prayer and Christian Heritage”. The consolidation, he said, aims at “a stronger, more inclusive and sustainable national observance”, and does not diminish either element — prayer, repentance, thanksgiving and reconciliation remain the content. The Daily Nation reports the change follows President Hichilema's directive designating 29 December as National Prayer Day, replacing the 18 October observance that President Edgar Lungu introduced in 2015 and that has since rung the public-holiday bell each year — bars closed from six to six, stadiums full.

This year, notably, there will be no single large national gathering: the National Day of Prayer will still take place, but individual churches are encouraged to dedicate time to prayer during their regular Sunday services — a decentralised format the clergy framed as broadening participation rather than shrinking it. Bishop Siachitema was careful with the constitutional boundary, affirming “the importance of maintaining the distinction between the responsibilities of the Church and those of the Government” while defending the partnership itself.

The public argument has already begun, and it splits along a familiar seam: those who hear 29 December as the date of the 1991 declaration itself — the Christian-nation proclamation of the Chiluba era, which the merged event now formally commemorates — and those who ask what was wrong with 18 October, a day that had grown into one of the few genuinely cross-denominational fixtures of the republic's year. The mother bodies have given their answer; the first merged observance in December will show whether the country prays along.
