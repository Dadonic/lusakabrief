'You can't make law for one man': Kabimba joins the term-limits argument, Harrington says the Speaker should not preside, and Chapter One demands reasons for the march ban

Economic Front leader Wynter Kabimba told Prime Television that the law is not made for one man, the Zambian Observer reported on Thursday, as the Daily Nation carried William Harrington's argument that Speaker Nelly Mutti, who sat on the Mung'omba commission that rejected a third term, should recuse herself from any term-limits debate. Chapter One Foundation gave police until Thursday to give specific reasons for advising against the 25 November marches. The Observer also reports Saviour Chishimba's apology for campaigning for the UPND Alliance.

By Lusaka Brief Politics Desk • LUSAKA — 20 November 2026 • 4 min read

A week before two rival processions are meant to reach Parliament, the argument over presidential term limits turned on Thursday to questions of procedure: who should chair any debate in the National Assembly, and what reasons the police had for advising against the marches. The Zambian Observer's morning brief on Thursday 19 November reported that Wynter Kabimba, leader of the Economic Front, had warned on Prime Television's Matters Arising against changing constitutional rules for one individual. "You can't make law for one man and law is not made for good and bad," he said, in the Observer's account. The same brief, written by The People's Brief news desk and carried by the Observer, quoted a senior UPND official, speaking on condition of anonymity, as saying that even within the ruling party the removal of term limits is a contested matter.

The Daily Nation reported on Thursday evening, in an article for its subscribers, that William Harrington has argued that Nelly Mutti, the Speaker of the National Assembly, should recuse herself from presiding over any debate on the proposed changes. His reason, as the paper summarises it, is that Mutti served on the Mung'omba Constitutional Review Commission, which rejected a third term and the removal of the presidential limit, and that this raises questions about her presiding over proposals to alter the same clause. The Speaker's office has not been reported as responding. The full article is behind the paper's paywall.

The police letter and the deadline

The marches themselves remain in dispute. The police letter of Tuesday 17 November, addressed to Chapter One Foundation's executive director Josiah Kalala and signed by Lusaka Division Commanding Officer Ronald Zambo, cited intelligence information and the prevailing national security situation in advising organisers not to proceed on Wednesday 25 November, the Daily Revelation reported. According to the Observer, civil society groups led by Chapter One challenged the advice, asked for specific reasons and alternative arrangements, requested a written response by Thursday 19 November and said they might pursue legal remedies. The Observer's report did not say whether a reply had been received, and none had been published by Thursday evening in the outlets read for this story.

The Observer and the Daily Revelation both carried a reaction from George Chulumanda, a former minister and ambassador. "This is dictatorship in uniform," he said of the police decision. In the Daily Revelation's account he also asked, "What national security situation? The only security threat in Zambia today is a government that is afraid of term limits," called for the release of Mundubile, Zulu and other detainees he described as political, and urged Zambians to protest peacefully: "Let them arrest 10,000 of us peacefully. Let them fill the prisons with peaceful Zambians." Those are his assertions; neither outlet carried a police or government response to them.

The government's position, and the view from outside

The government's line has not changed. CAJ News Africa, in a report from Lusaka on Thursday, quoted Justice Minister Jack Mwiimbu telling a media conference: "The executive respects the independence of the legislature and will not involve itself in processes". State House, the Observer notes, says President Hakainde Hichilema has no involvement in the campaign to remove the limit; critics ask why he has not personally restated his earlier rejection of third-term politics. CAJ also quoted the Law Association of Zambia's president, Arnold Kaluba: "The association maintains its position on presidential term limits and reiterates that the Constitution is the supreme law of the Republic". The agency's own framing was sharper, describing the August election as widely discredited by observers and the push as dividing the country; it quoted the analyst Brian Polombwe as saying the bid "is pouring fresh fuel onto an already toxic political atmosphere". CAJ put the youth consortium's expected turnout on 25 November at more than 60,000, a figure it gave as "reportedly expected" rather than confirmed.

An apology from an August ally

In a separate development reported only by the Zambian Observer among the outlets read for this story, the opposition leader Saviour Chishimba, who signs his statements Yehudah Ben David, issued a public apology on Wednesday 18 November to supporters disappointed by his decision to join the UPND Alliance and campaign for President Hichilema in August. "To everyone who felt disregarded, disappointed or betrayed, I tender my most sincere and unreserved apology," he wrote, in the text the Observer published. He said the decision was his own political judgment, shaped by his detention without trial between 2016 and 2021 and a wish to preserve economic stabilisation after Zambia's debt default; that he was offered no money or post; and that he had once given up a parliamentary seat three weeks before mid-term gratuities fell due. "My decision involved no pecuniary transaction," he said. The Observer did not say who had alleged otherwise, and carried no response from the UPND.
