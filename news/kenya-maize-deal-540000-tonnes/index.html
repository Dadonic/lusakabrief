Kenya signs for 540,000 tonnes of Zambian maize — the record harvest finds its market

The Food Reserve Agency has signed a contract with Kenya's Baita Trading Company to export more than half a million metric tonnes of surplus maize, converting a record harvest into foreign exchange.

By Natasha Banda • 16 September 2026 • 4

Zambia and Kenya have concluded a deal for the export of Zambia's surplus maize, with the Food Reserve Agency signing a contract with Kenya's Baita Trading Company for 540,000 metric tonnes — one of the largest single grain export agreements in the country's history.

The deal is the commercial payoff of the historic harvest, forecast at nearly five million tonnes, that has underpinned the kwacha's strong run this year. Kenya, facing its own production shortfalls, gets supply security; Zambia converts surplus into export earnings at scale.

The domestic question

Export deals of this size always trigger the same scrutiny at home: will large outflows tighten local supply and push up mealie meal prices? The FRA's position is that the exportable surplus is calculated after national food security requirements are met. Traditional leaders, including Chief Nkweto, have separately cautioned farmers against selling their entire crop — a reminder that household-level food security is a different calculation from the national one.

"A surplus only exists when every Zambian household has eaten. The trucks can leave after that, not before."

Watch the execution: payment terms, delivery schedule, and whether the earnings show up in reserve data — the same reserves the central bank says are defending the kwacha.
