Sport · AFCON 2027 qualifiers

Chilufya scores, Girumugisha answers, Algeria pull three clear: a 1–1 draw before 44,000 in Ndola leaves Zambia second in Group I, with the return in Bujumbura on Tuesday

Edward Chilufya's 64th-minute finish from Albert Kangwanda's pass put Zambia ahead against Burundi at Levy Mwanawasa Stadium on Friday 13 November, but Jean Claude Girumugisha equalised nine minutes from time and the Chipolopolo found no winner in five added minutes. Algeria's 1–0 win over Togo in Tizi Ouzou on Saturday puts them on seven points to Zambia's four, with Togo on three and Burundi on two. George Lwandamina said his players failed to reproduce their training form; the squad landed in Bujumbura on Saturday for Tuesday's return.

By Lusaka Brief Sports Desk • NDOLA — 15 November 2026 • 2 min read

Zambia's Africa Cup of Nations qualifier against Burundi at Levy Mwanawasa Stadium in Ndola on Friday 13 November ended 1–1, a result that leaves the Chipolopolo second in Group I and three points behind Algeria after the weekend's other match. The gates had been opened to all fans after President Hichilema bought the tickets, and the Football Association of Zambia put the attendance at about 44,000, ZamFoot reported.

The first half was goalless and, in the accounts of ZamFoot and News Diggers, uncomfortable. Goalkeeper Willard Mwanza made two early saves, including from Jean Girumugisha, and News Diggers reported that Burundi wasted a one-on-one in the 38th minute. Albert Kangwanda's free kicks were saved or cleared. George Lwandamina sent on Eliya Mandanji for Derrick Bulaya at the interval, and in the 64th minute Edward Chilufya finished a move built through Kangwanda to put Zambia ahead. A triple substitution in the 77th minute brought on Gift Mphande, Wilson Chisala and Zephania Phiri for Kangwanda, Chilufya and Prince Mumba, but Girumugisha equalised with nine minutes of normal time left, and five added minutes produced no winner.

The team had been doing very well, training very well, but on the day of the game, they failed to replicate.
— George Lwandamina, Zambia coach, speaking after the match in Ndola on Friday 13 November, as reported by ZamFoot

'One mistake'

Lwandamina told reporters his side had made basic errors, even in ball control, which made the result more painful, and that the equaliser came from a single mistake after the midfield had begun to settle. He said fatigue and injury worries drove his substitutions, with players signalling they could not continue, and that forcing them on would have made the next match harder. FAZ president Keith Mweemba thanked the President for opening the gates and said the association hoped to make such support the norm, adding that he was confident the players would repay the fans in the away fixture.

The table after Saturday

Algeria beat Togo 1–0 at the Stade Hocine-Aït-Ahmed in Tizi Ouzou on Saturday 14 November, Ramy Bensebaini heading in Anis Hadj Moussa's cross in the 76th minute, Pan Africa Football reported. That leaves Algeria on seven points, Zambia on four, Togo on three and Burundi on two after three rounds. Zambia's results so far are a 2–0 home win over Togo, a 3–1 defeat in Algeria and Friday's draw.

The squad landed at Melchior Ndadaye International Airport in Bujumbura at 12:45 local time on Saturday and is based at the Hotel Club du Lac Tanganyika, ZamFoot reported from a FAZ Media release. The return against Burundi is on Tuesday 17 November; Togo host Algeria in Lomé the same day. The finals are in Kenya, Uganda and Tanzania in 2027.
