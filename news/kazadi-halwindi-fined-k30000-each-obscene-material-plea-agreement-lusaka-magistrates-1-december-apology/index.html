K30,000 each or nine months: David Kazadi and Mwaka Halwindi are convicted under a plea agreement in the obscene material case, as the producer apologises and cites counselling

Film producer David Kazadi, 34, and Mwaka Halwindi, 23, pleaded guilty under a plea agreement and were each fined K30,000, payable by Tuesday 1 December, with nine months' imprisonment in default, the Zambian Observer reported on Wednesday 18 November. The deal, struck under the Plea Negotiations and Agreements Act, was disclosed to Lusaka magistrate Kawama Mwamfuli on 10 November, Zambia Monitor reported. Kazadi said on Facebook that an apology alone could not undo what had happened and that he had undergone counselling, News Diggers reported.

By Lusaka Brief Courts Desk • LUSAKA — 19 November 2026 • 2 min read

A case that began with three not-guilty pleas ended on Wednesday 18 November with a negotiated settlement. At the Lusaka Magistrates' Court, film producer David Kazadi, 34, and Mwaka Halwindi, 23, pleaded guilty under a plea agreement to charges relating to the production, possession and circulation of obscene material, were convicted, and were each fined K30,000, the Zambian Observer reported. The fines are payable on or before Tuesday 1 December; in default, each faces nine months' imprisonment.

The State's allegation, as the Observer summarised it, was that between 1 January 2023 and 30 June 2025 Kazadi circulated videos involving Halwindi and shared other explicit material over WhatsApp. A third accused, Ruby Muzandu, 23, was charged alongside them; the Observer's report records no plea, conviction or sentence in her case.

How the deal was reached

The plea agreement was struck under the Plea Negotiations and Agreements Act No. 20 of 2010. Zambia Monitor reported on 10 November that when the matter came before Magistrate Kawama Mwamfuli for the start of trial that day, Senior Public Prosecutor Judith Mauka and Public Prosecutor Hope Mikwala disclosed that the State had reached an agreement with the three accused, but that it had not yet been served on the defence or filed. The magistrate ordered it served and filed by close of business and adjourned the case to 18 November. An earlier trial date in September had been lost because the magistrate was sitting on election petition tribunals, the Monitor reported.

The Monitor placed the deal in a wider context: the National Prosecution Authority, the Law Association of Zambia, the Legal Aid Board and the Judiciary have been promoting plea bargaining as an alternative to lengthy trials, visiting Chimbokaila and Mwembeshi correctional facilities to explain the process to remandees. The Act has been on the books since 2010 but is little used. Any agreement remains subject to the court, which must satisfy itself that the accused is pleading guilty to an offence the facts support and that the deal serves the interests of justice.

The apology

Ahead of the hearing, Kazadi addressed the public. In a statement on his Facebook page reported by News Diggers on Wednesday, he apologised over the controversy that led to the case, acknowledged that an apology alone could not undo what had happened, and said he had undergone counselling and was committed to becoming a better person.

Confirmed vs. alleged

Confirmed: Kazadi and Halwindi were convicted and fined K30,000 each under a plea agreement, payable by 1 December with nine months' imprisonment in default, per the Zambian Observer. The agreement was disclosed in court on 10 November before Magistrate Kawama Mwamfuli, per Zambia Monitor. Kazadi apologised on Facebook, per News Diggers.

Alleged or unverified: The outcome for Ruby Muzandu is not stated in the Observer's report. The terms of the agreement beyond the fines have not been published.
