The bill for the bumper harvest: Musokotwane asks Parliament for K19.9 billion more, three-quarters of it to pay farmers for maize the FRA did not plan to buy

Finance Minister Situmbeko Musokotwane presented Supplementary Estimates No. 2 of 2026 to the National Assembly on Thursday 22 October. Zambia Monitor and the Zambian Observer report that K14.7 billion of the K19.9 billion is owed to farmers who sold maize to the Food Reserve Agency, which bought 2.7 million tonnes against a target of 500,000, and that K18 billion of the total is to be raised by borrowing on the domestic market.

By Lusaka Brief Business Desk • LUSAKA — 23 October 2026 • 3 min read

Finance and National Planning Minister Situmbeko Musokotwane went to the National Assembly on Thursday 22 October with a request for K19.9 billion in additional spending authority for the current year. Zambia Monitor and the Zambian Observer both report that the Supplementary Estimates of Expenditure No. 2 of 2026 were tabled under Article 203 of the Constitution, which provides for extra appropriations when the money Parliament has already voted proves insufficient. The 2026 budget was approved in December 2025; the Observer notes that a first supplementary budget for the year went through in April.

The bulk of the new money is explained by one decision. According to both reports, the Food Reserve Agency bought 2.7 million tonnes of maize in 2026 against an original target of 500,000 tonnes. The minister told the House that the Government chose to use this year's bumper harvest to fill the strategic reserve ahead of a 2027 lean season that he linked to El Niño. Zambia Monitor quotes him as saying that "the FRA purchased 2.7 million metric tonnes of maize just this year alone".

Where the K19.9 billion goes

The Ministry of Agriculture takes K15.8 billion, or 79 per cent of the total. Of that, K14.7 billion is to pay farmers who delivered maize to the FRA this year, and K1.1 billion tops up the Farmer Input Support Programme for the 2026/2027 e-voucher season. The Ministry of Finance itself is allocated K1.3 billion, of which K1 billion remits 2026 Asycuda customs-processing fees to the Zambia Revenue Authority and K232 million brings additional World Bank grants onto the books for projects including the Catalysing Climate Risk Protection Shield for Zambian Smallholder Farmers, the Zambian Mineral Investments, Governance and Economic Sustainability project and the Irrigated and Sustainable Agriculture for Transformation project.

Education receives K540 million for the Higher Education Loans and Scholarship Board to cover shortfalls in tuition and student allowances this year; the Ministry of Defence gets K422.5 million for what the minister described as higher-than-expected operational costs of the security wings; and K300 million goes to the Contingency Vote for unforeseen spending in the rest of the financial year. Those itemised lines add up to roughly K18.4 billion; neither report sets out what the remaining K1.5 billion or so is for.

How it is to be paid for

  • K18 billion — additional borrowing on the domestic market, with details promised in a revised Annual Borrowing Plan
  • K1.3 billion — revenue collected above what was expected when the 2026 budget was approved
  • K510.8 million — additional funds from cooperating partners
  • K82.9 million — savings on external debt service
  • K27.3 million — unspent 2025 balances carried forward

What happens next

Speaker Nelly Mutti referred the estimates to the Expanded Planning and Budgeting Committee, which is to report back to the House; the Observer says no date was given. The spending is subject to Parliament's approval. Neither outlet carried a reaction from opposition MPs or civil-society budget watchers on the day.

The request landed on the same day that the Ministry of Finance published the minister's account of the IMF and World Bank annual meetings held from 12 to 18 October. Zambia Monitor reports that Dr Musokotwane said the World Bank had committed to support Zambia's 2027 fiscal programme, that the delegation had met the IMF's African Department director Zeine Zeidane after a staff-level agreement on a proposed three-year Extended Credit Facility of about US$1.5 billion still awaiting IMF management and Executive Board approval, and that the Government's targets for 2031 include 10 million tonnes of maize a year, 10,000 megawatts of generation capacity, five million tourist arrivals and three million tonnes of copper.

Our priority for the next five years is growth, growth, growth.
— Situmbeko Musokotwane, Minister of Finance and National Planning, in a ministry statement on the IMF and World Bank annual meetings, as reported by Zambia Monitor on Thursday 22 October

Confirmed vs. alleged

Confirmed: That a K19.9 billion second supplementary budget was presented to the National Assembly on Thursday 22 October; the headline allocations and the financing plan, including K18 billion in domestic borrowing; the FRA purchase of 2.7 million tonnes against a 500,000-tonne target; and the referral to the Expanded Planning and Budgeting Committee — all reported by both Zambia Monitor and the Zambian Observer.

Alleged or unverified: What the roughly K1.5 billion not itemised in either report covers; when the committee will report and when farmers will actually be paid; the terms and timing of the extra domestic borrowing, which await the revised Annual Borrowing Plan; and how the opposition and independent analysts will respond, since no reaction had been reported by Thursday evening.
