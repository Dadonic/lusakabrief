US$105 million through Zanaco for small firms, and a K5 billion State guarantee scheme in the 2027 Budget: Katotobwe asks that the money reach businesses that can use it

Four development lenders and Citi have put together a US$105 million facility to be lent through Zanaco to small and medium-sized enterprises, with Swedfund and FinDev Canada providing US$35 million each, Norfund US$25 million and Citi US$10 million, Zambia Monitor and the pan-African outlet Afrimag reported after the signing in Lusaka on Wednesday 18 November. Trade and Exports Minister Caroline Katotobwe used the occasion to point to the 2027 Budget's K5 billion Small-Business Guarantee Scheme and a planned development finance institution.

By Lusaka Brief Business Desk • LUSAKA — 20 November 2026 • 3 min read

Zambia's small businesses have a new line of credit and the promise of a State guarantee behind the next one. At a signing ceremony in Lusaka on Wednesday 18 November, Zanaco, Citi, FinDev Canada, Norfund and Swedfund concluded a US$105 million financing partnership for small and medium-sized enterprises, Zambia Monitor reported that evening. The money is split four ways: US$35 million each from Swedfund, Sweden's development finance institution, and FinDev Canada; US$25 million from Norway's Norfund; and US$10 million from Citi. It will be lent through Zanaco to eligible SMEs, with women-owned and women-led businesses and firms along the agricultural value chain among the stated targets.

Afrimag, a pan-African business outlet, reported the same facility on Wednesday as a syndicated loan led by Citibank and closed by Zanaco to widen access to credit for micro, small and medium-sized enterprises. Its account adds that Swedfund's US$35 million carries a European Union guarantee under the MSME Platform Plus programme, part of the EU's Global Gateway initiative, and that this is Swedfund's second commitment in Zambia. Afrimag quotes Siongo Kisoso, Swedfund's regional director for East Africa, as saying that the need for long-term capital in Zambia's MSME sector is significant and that the facility is meant to reach underserved communities. Lusaka Brief could not read Swedfund's own release, which Afrimag cites, and the EU guarantee is reported on Afrimag's account alone.

The minister's ask, and the Budget's promise

Caroline Katotobwe, the Minister of Trade and Exports, told the signing that the Government welcomed the partnership and asked Zanaco to make sure the money reached viable Zambian businesses, Zambia Monitor reported. She said the aim was capital structured so that firms could expand production, buy machinery, improve quality, add value and meet domestic and export orders. "Our expectation is that this financing will translate into tangible outcomes", she said. She also pointed to what the State itself is putting on the table: "The 2027 National Budget recognises this need through initiatives such as the K5 billion Small-Business Guarantee Scheme", she said, adding that the Government plans a development finance institution to improve access to affordable business finance.

A K5 billion guarantee for small firms is not an entirely new idea. In October 2025, Zambia Monitor reported that the Bank of Zambia Governor, Denny Kalyalya, had told the 2026 budget symposium that the central bank would launch a K5 billion fund in the first quarter of 2026 to provide credit guarantees to SMEs, that 15 fund managers had applied to run it, and that the bank was setting up a special-purpose vehicle to hold it. Wednesday's report does not say whether the Budget's Small-Business Guarantee Scheme is that vehicle under a new name or a separate instrument, nor whether the Bank of Zambia fund was launched on schedule; Lusaka Brief has not found a report that settles either point.

The Canadian High Commission in Zambia, Swedfund and Norfund reaffirmed their support for Zambian SMEs and women entrepreneurs at the signing, Zambia Monitor said, without giving further figures. Katotobwe encouraged the firms that borrow to invest in innovation and productivity so that they can compete regionally and internationally.

Who is lending what

  • Swedfund — US$35 million (with an EU guarantee under MSME Platform Plus, according to Afrimag)
  • FinDev Canada — US$35 million
  • Norfund — US$25 million
  • Citi — US$10 million, and lead arranger of the syndication
  • Zanaco — the lender to eligible SMEs, women-led firms and agricultural value-chain businesses

Sources: Zambia Monitor, “Zambian govt establishes K5 billion SME guarantee scheme” (18 Nov 2026); Afrimag, “Zambie | Inclusion financière : un facility de 105 millions de dollars pour débloquer le crédit aux MPME” (18 Nov 2026); Zambia Monitor, “Bank of Zambia to launch K5 billion SME credit guarantee fund in 2026” (5 Oct 2025).
