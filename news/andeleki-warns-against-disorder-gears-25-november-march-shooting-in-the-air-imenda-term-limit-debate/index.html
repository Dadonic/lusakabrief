Politics · Term limits

Andeleki warns that Zambia is no banana republic, a governance group calls the 25 November march 'shooting in the air', and a UPND official says the term-limit debate should go on

Dr Clement Andeleki said at a ministerial handover that the government would not tolerate anyone trying to throw Zambia into disarray and warned church leaders and politicians to stay within their mandate, News Diggers reported on Sunday 8 November. The paper set the remarks beside civil society's notice to the police of a march in defence of the two-term limit. Opinion on the march, and on the debate itself, is divided, including inside the ruling party.

By Lusaka Brief Politics Desk • LUSAKA — 9 November 2026 • 3 min read

The Minister of Home Affairs and Internal Security, Dr Clement Andeleki, has said the government will not tolerate anyone who tries to throw the country into disarray, and that Zambia is not a banana republic, News Diggers reported on Sunday 8 November. Speaking at a ministerial handover ceremony, he said the government had taken note of people agitating to cause chaos and gave an assurance that they would not succeed, the paper said. He also warned church leaders and politicians against operating outside their mandate.

News Diggers placed the warning beside a second fact: a consortium of civil society organisations has notified the Zambia Police Service of a peaceful procession planned for 25 November against calls to remove presidential term limits. The part of the report that was open to non-subscribers does not show the minister naming the march or its organisers, and it does not give the date or place of the ceremony. The connection between the two is the paper's, not, on the evidence available, the minister's.

Two views of the march

A separate News Diggers report on the same day showed how divided opinion outside government is. Luckson Lungu, board chairperson of the GEARS Initiative, a governance group, described the organisations planning the march as "shooting in the air". Governance activist Wisdom Muyunda took the opposite line on the substance, urging Members of Parliament to drop calls to lift the limit and saying the argument risks diverting the country from its economic priorities. The paper named the organisers as Chapter One Foundation, Transparency International Zambia, LCK Foundation and ActionAid, and recalled that a group of youths had earlier marched to Parliament to hand in a petition in favour of removal.

The ruling party is not speaking with one voice either. Gertrude Imenda, the UPND's deputy secretary general and an MP, said that because there are people on both sides the debate should continue, and that she has a position of her own which she is keeping to herself, Daily Revelation reported in its Monday 9 November edition under a headline quoting her as saying she had kept it "in my heart". Three days earlier The Zambian Observer reported UPND media director Mark Simuuwe as saying that most Zambians agree the limit should go and that only a few people, whom he associated with the opposition, object. The Observer noted that he cited no poll or survey for the claim.

Two terms are enough. Democracy must prevail. Our constitutional safeguards must remain intact.
— Dr Brian Mushimba, president of the Organized People's Party, in a statement carried by The Zambian Observer on 4 November

The visible part of the News Diggers report says that the government, through Chief Government Spokesperson Elvis Nkandu, had urged the organisers on the matter, but the sentence is cut off by the paper's paywall. What the reports read for this edition do not show is whether the police have replied to the notification, whether any conditions have been attached to it, and whom the minister had in mind when he spoke of church leaders acting outside their mandate.

Confirmed vs. alleged

Confirmed: Dr Andeleki made the remarks at a ministerial handover ceremony, according to News Diggers. Civil society groups have notified the police of a procession on 25 November, the same paper reports. GEARS has criticised the march; Ms Imenda and Mr Simuuwe of the UPND have made the statements attributed to them by Daily Revelation and The Zambian Observer.

Alleged or unverified: Whether the minister's warning was aimed at the march is not established: the full text of his remarks was behind a paywall. Mr Simuuwe's claim that a majority of Zambians support removing term limits is unsupported by any published survey. The police response to the notification has not been reported.
