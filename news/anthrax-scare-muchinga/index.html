Anthrax scare in Muchinga: what is confirmed, what is ruled out

Suspected anthrax has been recorded in Muchinga Province, prompting surveillance and public caution — while Lavushimanda district reports no cases in livestock. Here is what the evidence says so far.

By Lusaka Brief Newsroom • 15 September 2026 • 4

Health and veterinary authorities are responding to suspected anthrax cases recorded in Muchinga Province, with surveillance and public-health precautions activated in affected areas. Anthrax is a serious zoonotic disease — it moves between animals and humans — and suspected cases are treated with urgency even before laboratory confirmation.

At the same time, the Department of Fisheries and Livestock in Lavushimanda district says it has recorded no cases in livestock — a useful boundary on the picture, suggesting the concern is localised rather than province-wide.

What the public should know

Anthrax precautions

  • Do not slaughter, open, or eat animals found dead or suddenly sick.
  • Report sudden livestock deaths to veterinary officers immediately.
  • Anthrax is not spread person-to-person; risk comes from contact with infected animals or meat.
  • "Suspected" is not "confirmed" — laboratory results determine the formal status.

Zambia has managed anthrax outbreaks before, and the playbook is well established: surveillance, movement control where needed, and clear public communication. Lusaka Brief will update this report as laboratory results and official statements arrive.
