Back to their stations: the Ministry of Health sends 123 headquarters officers to their posts and orders a qualifications audit — as ZAMMSA starts hiring again

An internal assessment found 123 officers working at the Ministry of Health’s Lusaka headquarters under temporary arrangements, outside an approved establishment of 403 positions. Health Minister Roma Chilengi says they are being redirected to their substantive stations and that everyone left at headquarters will be assessed against professional benchmarks. ZAMMSA, whose entire workforce was dismissed, has advertised eight senior posts, and the Ministry says it is seeking cheaper financing for the sector.

By Lusaka Brief Health Desk • LUSAKA — 14 October 2026 • 2 min read

The Ministry of Health has begun what it calls an institutional clean-up of its Lusaka headquarters, sending 123 officers back to the stations where they are formally posted. An internal assessment found the officers had been working at headquarters under temporary administrative arrangements, outside the approved establishment, Zambia Monitor and News Diggers reported on Wednesday 14 October.

The 2021 establishment caps headquarters staffing at 403 positions, according to Zambia Monitor, and the 123 fell outside it. Health Minister Professor Roma Chilengi said in a statement on Tuesday 13 October that the officers were being returned to the stations where their pay points and original postings sit, and that the move was intended to strengthen delivery at frontline facilities and decentralised administrative centres.

An audit of everyone who remains

The redeployment is the first step. Professor Chilengi said the Ministry would now assess the qualifications and competencies of everyone holding one of the 403 headquarters positions; staff who lack the academic, professional or technical qualifications their roles require will be moved to operational stations that match their verified credentials.

Every position in the approved structure will be evaluated against established professional benchmarks and job descriptions.
— Professor Roma Chilengi, Minister of Health, in a statement on Tuesday 13 October, as reported by Zambia Monitor

The Ministry describes the exercise as part of wider reforms to enforce structural compliance and improve service delivery. No deadline for the qualifications assessment has been given, and neither report carries a response from health workers’ unions.

ZAMMSA: eight posts, from zero

The clean-up follows the Minister’s decision to dismiss the entire workforce of the Zambia Medicines and Medical Supplies Agency ‘in national interest’, citing years of repeated problems and the findings of a forensic audit commissioned by the Auditor General. ZAMMSA has now advertised eight vacancies — seven director-level positions and an executive secretary in the Director General’s office — seeking ‘suitably qualified and experienced Zambians’, News Diggers reported on Tuesday. Professor Chilengi has said the agency will need more than 400 employees to run its operations, so the eight posts are the start of a much longer rebuild.

The cost of borrowing for health

Money is the other front. At the third Health Sector Policy Meeting of the year, Dr George Sinyangwe, the Ministry’s Permanent Secretary for Donor Coordination, said the Government was exploring cheaper financing for the sector — lower-interest loans for institutions, debt-swap arrangements and a review of financing packages — noting that some entities faced borrowing costs of up to 30 percent against a prime lending rate of about 15 percent, Lusaka Times reported. Proposals for concessional loans and grants from multilateral development banks are being developed through the Health Impact Investment Platform with the World Health Organization. For the cooperating partners, Seth Broekman’s message, delivered through chairperson Anna Holmstrom, was that ‘Zambia must move beyond incremental improvements and implement transformative measures’ if universal health coverage is to be reached by 2030.
