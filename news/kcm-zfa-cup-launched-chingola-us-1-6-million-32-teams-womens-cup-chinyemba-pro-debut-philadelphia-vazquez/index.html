Sport · Football and boxing

A 32-team knockout and a first women's national cup, backed by KCM's US$1.6 million; and in Philadelphia tonight, Patrick Chinyemba turns professional against an unbeaten American

FAZ and Konkola Copper Mines launched the KCM–ZFA Men's and Women's Cups at KCM's headquarters in Chingola on Thursday 12 November: 32 teams from the Super League, the National League and the provinces over 31 matches in five rounds, and the first dedicated national cup for women, ZamFoot reports. KCM's deputy chief executive Satish Kumar put the company's 2026 commitment to Zambian football at US$1.6 million. In the United States, two-time Olympian Patrick 'Baddo' Chinyemba makes his professional debut on Friday 13 November against Santiago Vazquez.

By Lusaka Brief Sports Desk • CHINGOLA — 13 November 2026 • 2 min read

The Football Association of Zambia and Konkola Copper Mines have launched two new national knockout competitions, the KCM–ZFA Men's Cup and the KCM–ZFA Women's Cup, at a ceremony at KCM's headquarters in Chingola on Thursday 12 November, ZamFoot reports. The inaugural men's edition for 2026 will bring together 32 teams drawn from the MTN Super League, the National League and the provincial divisions, playing 31 matches over five rounds. The women's competition is described as the first dedicated national cup for women's football in Zambia.

FAZ president Keith Mweemba called the launch an important milestone and said the competitions were part of his plan to widen opportunities for clubs and to fill a gap in the women's domestic calendar. He acknowledged that the first edition would have to fit within the transitional football calendar, and said the aim was to make both tournaments permanent fixtures. Satish Kumar, KCM's deputy chief executive, said the company had committed US$1.6 million to Zambian football in 2026, covering its partnership with FAZ and support for the Chipolopolo and the Copper Queens, and described the cups as a grassroots investment that gives players in the lower divisions and community leagues a route upwards.

A debut in Philadelphia

Patrick "Baddo" Chinyemba, who boxed for Zambia at two Olympic Games, fights as a professional for the first time on Friday 13 November at the 2300 Arena in Philadelphia, News Diggers reports. He meets the undefeated American Santiago Vazquez over six rounds at bantamweight on Red Owl Boxing's BOXFEST XXII card. Chinyemba, who intends to use the Philly Shell defence associated with the city he is fighting in, closed his amateur career with a bronze medal for Zambia at the IBA World Boxing Championships in Dubai on 11 December 2025. "Two days to go until my professional debut," he said in remarks carried by News Diggers on Thursday.

The two stories sit at opposite ends of Zambian sport's funding problem. The KCM money is a mining company putting a sum it can name into a competition that gives clubs outside the top flight a reason to exist beyond their provincial leagues, and gives women's teams a national trophy to play for; whether the 32 teams can afford to travel for five rounds is the question the first edition will answer. Chinyemba's debut is a Zambian athlete taking his career to the market where the purses are, with no national structure behind him once the amateur medals are won. Both results, the cup draw and the fight, belong to the weekend's reporting.
