An uncle in WITCH and a 16-track answer: Sampa the Great's 'Nu Zamrock' takes the psychedelic rock of 1970s Zambia and rebuilds it with Mag44, Sia and a family interview

Sampa the Great released 'Nu Zamrock', her third studio album, on Friday 30 October through Loma Vista Recordings, Zambia Monitor reported on Tuesday. The 16 tracks, about 37 minutes, fold hip-hop, rock and soul into the Zamrock sound of the 1970s, and the record opens with an interview with her uncle George Kunda, known as Groovy Joe, a founding member of WITCH. Mag44 and Solomon Moyo produced with her and Cadenza; Sia, KAMAUU, Mwanjé and Nicole Gumbe are the guests. She plays two sold-out shows at Blue Note London on Wednesday.

By Lusaka Brief Culture Desk • LUSAKA — 4 November 2026 • 2 min read

The Zambian-Australian rapper and singer Sampa the Great has made the record her family history was waiting for. 'Nu Zamrock', released on Friday 30 October through Loma Vista Recordings and reported by Zambia Monitor on Tuesday 3 November, is her third studio album, after 'The Return' in 2019 and 'As Above, So Below' in 2022, and its 16 tracks, running about 37 minutes, set hip-hop, rock, soul and Zambian rhythm inside the sound of Zamrock, the psychedelic rock that Zambian bands made in the 1970s.

The album grew out of a discovery. While reconnecting with relatives in Zambia, Sampa learned that her uncle, George Kunda, known as Groovy Joe, was a founding member of WITCH, one of the bands that defined the movement. The record opens with an interview with him about the band's history, so that the genre's past introduces its present.

Who made it

Sampa executive-produced the album with Cadenza, and the Zambian producers Magnus 'Mag44' Mando and Solomon Moyo worked on it with them. The guests are Sia, on 'Supernova', KAMAUU, on 'Star', Mwanjé and Nicole Gumbe. Loma Vista's own description of the record is that it is fierce and uncompromising.

She previewed the material at festivals in Australia in October and plays a sold-out double show at Blue Note London on Wednesday 4 November, the first dates with the album out.

For Zambian listeners the interest is partly in the lineage. An album that puts a founding member of WITCH on its first track, made with producers from Lusaka and released on an American label, carries the sound of the 1970s to an audience that Zambia Monitor notes has already sold out two nights in London.

Sources: Zambia Monitor, “Sampa The Great releases 'Nu Zamrock' album inspired by family roots” (3 Nov 2026).
