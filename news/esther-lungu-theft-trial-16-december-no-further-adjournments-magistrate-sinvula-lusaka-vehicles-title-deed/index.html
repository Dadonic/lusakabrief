No more adjournments: a Lusaka magistrate sets 16 December for Esther Lungu's theft trial to resume, more than a year after it last moved and 17 months after her husband's death

Resident Magistrate Mbuvywana Sinvula told the Lusaka Magistrates' Court on Monday 16 November that the case against the former First Lady and four co-accused, charged with the theft of three vehicles and a certificate of title between August 2021 and September 2022, had gone more than a year without reaching continued trial and would not be adjourned again, Kalemba reported. The matter returns on Wednesday 16 December. Esther Lungu has not attended since former president Edgar Lungu died on 5 June 2025; none of the allegations has been tested at trial.

By Lusaka Brief Courts Desk • LUSAKA — 18 November 2026 • 3 min read

The theft case against former First Lady Esther Lungu, which has been stalled since her husband's death in June last year, has been given a date and a warning. At the Lusaka Magistrates' Court on Monday 16 November, Resident Magistrate Mbuvywana Sinvula directed that the matter proceed to continued trial on Wednesday 16 December and that no further adjournments or mentions be entertained, Kalemba reported in a story carried by the Zambian Observer. News Diggers reported the same adjournment to 16 December for continued trial on Tuesday.

According to Kalemba's account, the magistrate observed that more than a year had passed without the case progressing, and that the court had earlier allowed the accused time to mourn. Esther Lungu, 66, has not attended court since former president Edgar Lungu died in South Africa on 5 June 2025. The reports do not record what, if anything, her lawyers said on Monday about her absence, and no reason beyond the period of mourning is given.

The charges

Esther Lungu is jointly charged with James Phiri, 49, and Lee Chisulo, 31, both described as police officers, and with Kapambwe Lungu and Catherine Banda, who News Diggers identifies as a niece of the late president. The charges are theft of motor vehicles and theft of a certificate of title, and the prosecution alleges that the accused acted together in Lusaka between 9 August 2021 and 30 September 2022.

The particulars, as set out by Kalemba, are a Mitsubishi Canter, registration BAV 5282, valued at K320,000, belonging to Elizabeth Phiri; a Toyota Runnex, BLA 9772, and a Toyota Allex, BAV 3986, each valued at K110,000, belonging to Furhana Patel; and a certificate of title for Stand No. S/LUSAKA/0003/2977 in Libala, valued at K1,050,000, belonging to Phiri. News Diggers' report gives the first count in the same terms.

What is alleged

  • Count one: a Mitsubishi Canter, BAV 5282, valued at K320,000 (Elizabeth Phiri)
  • A Toyota Runnex, BLA 9772, and a Toyota Allex, BAV 3986, K110,000 each (Furhana Patel)
  • A certificate of title for a Libala stand valued at K1,050,000 (Elizabeth Phiri)
  • Period alleged: 9 August 2021 to 30 September 2022, Lusaka
  • Next date: continued trial, Wednesday 16 December 2026

What the ruling means

A direction that there be no further adjournments is a case-management order, not a finding on the evidence, and it binds the parties to be ready on 16 December. The reports read for this edition do not say how many prosecution witnesses have testified or remain, nor whether the defence has indicated any application. The accused are presumed innocent, and the allegations above are the prosecution's, yet to be tested in court.

Confirmed vs. alleged

Confirmed: The Lusaka Magistrates' Court adjourned the case to 16 December 2026 for continued trial (News Diggers, Kalemba via the Zambian Observer). Kalemba reports that Resident Magistrate Mbuvywana Sinvula directed that there be no further adjournments or mentions. The charges are theft of motor vehicles and a certificate of title; the co-accused are James Phiri, Lee Chisulo, Kapambwe Lungu and Catherine Banda (both outlets).

Alleged or unverified: The reasons for Esther Lungu's continued absence beyond the mourning period the court allowed; what her defence has said about it; the state of the prosecution's evidence; and whether the court will in fact refuse an adjournment if one is sought on 16 December. The spelling of one co-accused's first name differs between the two outlets (Kapambwe/Kapembwe).
