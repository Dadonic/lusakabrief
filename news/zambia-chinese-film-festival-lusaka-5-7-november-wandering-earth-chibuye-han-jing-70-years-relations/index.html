Three nights of Chinese cinema in Lusaka: The Wandering Earth opens a four-film festival that Beijing's embassy frames around 70 years of China–Africa relations

The Zambia Chinese Film Festival ran in Lusaka from 5 to 7 November with four films, opening with the science-fiction epic The Wandering Earth. China's ambassador, Han Jing, and the Minister of Culture and Arts, Chris Chibuye, spoke at the opening ceremony, according to a statement issued by the Chinese embassy through APO Group, the only account of the event found; the minister argued that cultural and creative industries can create jobs and asked for cooperation on infrastructure, skills and financing.

By Lusaka Brief Culture Desk • LUSAKA — 8 November 2026 • 2 min read

Lusaka has had three nights of Chinese cinema. The Zambia Chinese Film Festival opened on Wednesday 5 November and ran until Saturday 7 November with four films: the science-fiction epic The Wandering Earth, The Shadow's Edge, Dear You and the animated Boonie Bears: Future Reborn, according to a statement from the Embassy of the People's Republic of China in Zambia, distributed by APO Group and published by Pan African Visions on Friday 6 November. Guests at the opening ceremony watched The Wandering Earth after the speeches.

The ambassador, Han Jing, told the opening that the language of film crosses national borders and bridges cultural differences, and placed the festival within the 70th anniversary of diplomatic relations between China and African states and what Beijing has designated the 2026 China–Africa Year of People-to-People Exchanges, the statement said. He called for more regular cultural exchange and for partnership in all sectors in support of the Grow Zambia Agenda.

The minister's pitch

The Minister of Culture and Arts, Chris Chibuye, used the platform to make an economic case for culture. According to the embassy's account, he said films strengthen the friendship between the two countries, argued that developing cultural and creative industries can create jobs, stimulate business and open new avenues of growth, and asked for cooperation on local infrastructure, skills training and access to financing for the sector. The Deputy Secretary to the Cabinet, Siazongo Siakalenge, also attended, the statement said.

A caution on sourcing: the account above rests on a single statement issued by the Chinese embassy and republished by several syndication sites, and the embassy's text reports both speakers in indirect speech. No report of the festival by an independent Zambian outlet was found by Sunday morning, and the statement did not name the screening venues, say how many people attended, or say whether admission was free. Readers who went will know more about the rooms and the crowds than the embassy's text records.

Sources: Pan African Visions (APO Group / Embassy of China in Zambia), “Ambassador Han Jing Attends the Opening Ceremony of Zambia Chinese Film Festival” (6 Nov 2026).
