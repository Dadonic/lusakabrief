Six aboard, no survivors: the Marondera helicopter crash that killed Wicknell Chivayo — and the DNA wait now holding the region's attention

Zimbabwe police confirm six people were aboard the helicopter that went down at Kudenga Farm near Marondera on Wednesday evening; the civil aviation authority's preliminary account says the tail struck first. Identification of the victims awaits DNA analysis. Tributes have come from Presidents Mnangagwa and Ruto — and the story has been followed as closely in Lusaka as in Harare.

By Mwelwa Phiri • LUSAKA — 2 October 2026 • 5 min read

The flamboyance that made Wicknell Chivayo a fixture of southern African social media met its end in a field in Mashonaland East. The Zimbabwe Republic Police confirms that six people were aboard the helicopter that crashed at Kudenga Farm, in the Marondera area, at about 17:00 on Wednesday, and that there were no survivors. The family confirmed his death through his brother, Herbert Chivayo, speaking to the state broadcaster ZBC; the aircraft was reported to have been flying from the Gandami area of Chikomba when it came down.

The investigation's first public fragment is a trajectory. The Civil Aviation Authority of Zimbabwe's Cleopatra Mashababe gave a preliminary account indicating the helicopter's tail struck the ground first — a detail that will steer the technical inquiry toward what failed and in what sequence, without yet saying why. Formal identification of the six victims awaits DNA analysis, the police say, which means the official manifest of the flight remains unpublished even as the family has spoken. Until the laboratory answers, the confirmed facts are the site, the hour, the number aboard and the tail-first account; everything else is tribute or speculation.

The tributes arrived first and from the top. President Emmerson Mnangagwa mourned a son of Zimbabwe whose generosity, whatever its critics, was impossible to ignore; Kenya's President William Ruto added his voice, a measure of how far beyond Zimbabwe's borders the Chivayo spectacle travelled. In Zambia it travelled as far as anywhere: his visits to Lusaka made national news, his pledges and gifts were argued over on Zambian radio, and his name became shorthand here for a particular kind of moneyed, motorcaded proximity to power. The Zambian debate about him was always really a debate about ourselves — about what wealth owes the public, and what the public should ask of wealth.

That debate now pauses for the forensic sequence. DNA results, then the manifest, then the CAAZ preliminary report, then — if the evidence supports one — a fuller inquiry. The crash that killed the region's most famous gift-giver was, on the authority's early account, over before the aircraft knew it was crashing: a tail strike at dusk on a farm outside Marondera, six souls aboard, and a cross-border audience refreshing for answers that will come at laboratory pace, not social-media pace.

Confirmed vs. alleged

Confirmed: A helicopter crashed at Kudenga Farm near Marondera at about 17:00 on Wednesday, 30 September; the ZRP says six people were aboard and there were no survivors; the Chivayo family confirmed his death to ZBC; CAAZ has given a preliminary tail-strike account; identification awaits DNA analysis; Presidents Mnangagwa and Ruto have issued tributes.

Alleged / unverified: The identities of the other five victims (awaiting DNA and the official manifest); the flight's purpose and full routing; any claims about mechanical cause, weather or operator fault circulating online — none has been established by the investigating authorities.

Sources: Zimbabwe Republic Police statement via spokesperson Paul Nyathi (30 Sep 2026); ZBC interview with Herbert Chivayo (30 Sep 2026); Civil Aviation Authority of Zimbabwe via Cleopatra Mashababe (1 Oct 2026); Zimbabwe Ministry of Information via Nick Mangwana (30 Sep 2026); The Herald and NewsDay coverage (30 Sep–1 Oct 2026).
