Courts · Mundubile and Zulu Developing

09:30 at the High Court: Judge Geoffrey Mulenga rules today on whether Mundubile and Zulu's sedition bail plea can be heard in Lusaka, as Mpika North's MP keeps his seat

The Lusaka High Court is due at 09:30 this morning to rule on the Director of Public Prosecutions' objection that Brian Mundubile and Makebi Zulu's bail application in their sedition case is in the wrong court, the Daily Nation and the Zambian Observer reported on Thursday. A three-judge panel rules on Tuesday 24 November on constitutional bail for all 18 treason accused. Separately, the Daily Nation reports that Judge Catherine Lombe has upheld Francis Kapyanga's election in Mpika North, and that judgment is pending in the Lima petition.

By Lusaka Brief Courts Desk • LUSAKA — 20 November 2026 • 3 min read

Two rulings in five days will shape the next phase of the cases against Brian Mundubile, the runner-up in August's presidential election, and his running mate Makebi Zulu. The first comes this morning. The Daily Nation reported on Thursday afternoon that the Lusaka High Court is expected to rule today on the bail application the two men have brought in their seditious practices case before Judge Geoffrey Mulenga, and specifically on a preliminary objection by the Director of Public Prosecutions, Gilbert Phiri, that challenges whether the Lusaka High Court has jurisdiction to hear it. The Zambian Observer's morning news brief on Thursday gave the same date, 20 November, for the jurisdictional ruling.

The court reserved that ruling to Friday at 09:30 after hearing argument on Monday 16 November, Zambia Monitor reported that evening. The State's position, as that report describes it, is that the High Court's wide bail powers do not allow a party to bypass transfer procedures; that filing in Lusaka while the sedition matter was still before the Solwezi High Court amounted to a multiplicity of proceedings and an abuse of process; and that, now the case has been transferred to the Lusaka Subordinate Court, the two men should ask that court for bail first. The defence answered that section 123 of the Criminal Procedure Code gives the Lusaka High Court jurisdiction, that the Solwezi magistrate had declined jurisdiction and referred only the question of transfer upwards, and that no bail application had ever been filed in either Solwezi court.

The charges, and the second ruling

The sedition case rests on two counts of seditious practices under section 57 of the Penal Code, arising from Facebook posts and livestreams on 14 August 2026, the day after the general election. According to Zambia Monitor's 16 November report, the first count alleges that Mundubile called on the Electoral Commission of Zambia to announce the winner on the basis of parallel vote tabulation results; the second alleges that Zulu announced that Mundubile would address the nation at a scheduled time. Neither count has been tested at trial. Solwezi High Court Judge Edward Musona ordered the case moved to Lusaka on 5 November, Zambia Monitor reported, because both accused live in the capital and were arrested there.

The second ruling is on Tuesday 24 November, when a panel of three judges, Derrick Mulenga, Davies Chibwili and Ireen Wishimanga, decides the constitutional bail petition brought by Mundubile, Zulu and 16 others held at Mukobeko Maximum Security Correctional Facility in Kabwe since 14 August on treason and related allegations, News Diggers reported on Tuesday. The panel ruled earlier this month that the petition was properly before it, Zambia Monitor reported on 11 November, rejecting a State argument that it should have gone through the criminal registry. In the Diggers report, defence lawyer Sakwiba Sikota described the security arrangements around the treason case as over-dramatised, saying it involved three former commandos; the rest of his remarks sit behind that outlet's subscription.

Election petitions: Mpika North kept, Lima pending

The Daily Nation also reported on Thursday evening, in articles for its subscribers, on two parliamentary election petitions. High Court Judge Catherine Lombe declared Francis Robert Kapyanga the duly elected Member of Parliament for Mpika North, holding that the grounds raised to nullify his election in the 13 August poll did not stand. The identity of the petitioner and the grounds argued are not in the part of the report available to non-subscribers. In a second article, the paper reported that the Lusaka High Court is set to deliver judgment in the Lima constituency petition, in which the losing UPND candidate, Lee Mukupa, challenges the declaration of the NRPUP's Kelvin Kaunda as MP on grounds including alleged violence and intimidation. That petition is before Judge Derrick Mulenga of the Ndola High Court, sitting in Lusaka; the judgment date was not visible in the published extract.

Confirmed vs. alleged

Confirmed: That the Lusaka High Court is due to rule today, Friday 20 November, on the DPP's jurisdictional objection in the sedition bail application (Daily Nation; Zambian Observer; Zambia Monitor's 16 November report gives 09:30), and on constitutional bail for the 18 on Tuesday 24 November (News Diggers; Zambian Observer). That Judge Catherine Lombe upheld Francis Kapyanga's election in Mpika North is the Daily Nation's report.

Alleged or unverified: The outcome of either ruling. The grounds in the Mpika North petition and the identity of the petitioner, and the date of the Lima judgment, which are behind the Daily Nation's subscription. All charges against Mundubile, Zulu and their co-accused are allegations they deny and which no court has yet tried.
