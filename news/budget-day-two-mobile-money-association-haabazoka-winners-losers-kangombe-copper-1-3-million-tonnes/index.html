Mobile money firms want talks, an economist names the winners and losers, and a former MP does the copper arithmetic: the 2027 budget meets its first day of scrutiny

A day after Situmbeko Musokotwane presented a K284.8 billion budget, the Mobile Money Association of Zambia said the higher transaction levies were decided without proper consultation and could undo gains in financial inclusion. Economist Lubinda Haabazoka said Social Cash Transfer households and farmers gain while consumers and mobile money users pay, and former Kamfinsa MP Christopher Kang'ombe calculated that copper output growing at its current pace reaches 1.3 million tonnes by 2031, not the three million the government is targeting.

By Lusaka Brief Business Desk • LUSAKA — 8 November 2026 • 5 min read

A day after the Minister of Finance and National Planning, Situmbeko Musokotwane, set out a K284.8 billion budget for 2027, the argument moved from the headline totals to who carries them. The Mobile Money Association of Zambia said on Saturday 7 November that the government had decided to raise levies on mobile money transactions without adequately consulting the industry, Zambia Monitor reported. The association's president and secretary general, Chris Sichende, told the Monitor in an interview that stakeholders had asked for meaningful engagement before any change affecting the sector, that higher levies would raise transaction costs for the individuals and businesses that use mobile money most, and that the government should weigh the effect on users before taking a final decision. The budget proposes raising mobile money levies across all transaction bands, with the stated aims of broadening the tax base, improving equity and raising more domestic revenue, according to the same report.

This has the potential to erode the gains made so far in terms of financial inclusion expansion.
— Chris Sichende, president and secretary general of the Mobile Money Association of Zambia, in an interview published by Zambia Monitor on Saturday 7 November

Winners and losers

Economist Lubinda Haabazoka's reading of the budget, reported by Zambia Monitor on Saturday, puts households on the Social Cash Transfer and farmers among the likely winners and consumers and mobile money users among the likely losers. Social Cash Transfer funding rises to K10.4 billion and the number of beneficiary households is projected to grow from 1.5 million this year to more than 1.6 million in 2027; education and health together receive K68.5 billion, about 24% of spending; school feeding nearly doubles from K523 million to K1 billion; agriculture, fisheries and livestock receive K22.7 billion, of which K9.1 billion is for the Farmer Input Support Programme; roads receive K18.1 billion; and the Constituency Development Fund rises from K6.2 billion to about K9 billion, roughly K40 million a constituency. Haabazoka's caveat, as the Monitor reported it, is that the higher taxes and levies could raise living costs and offset part of those gains, so that what any household actually receives depends on the services it uses, the markets it reaches and the taxes it pays.

The consumer measures are now being itemised. Mpelembe Media reported on Saturday that the budget proposes a specific excise duty of K1 a kilogram on refined sugar, an excise of 80 ngwee on each plastic bottle, a rise in excise on single-use plastics to 200%, the end of VAT zero-rating for energy-saving appliances alongside the rise in VAT from 16% to 16.5% on 1 January 2027, and the new 39.5% PAYE band on monthly earnings above K37,000. Mpelembe described the mobile money levy as doubled but gave no rates, and it carried two different figures for the duty on imported ceramic tiles, 40% in one passage and a rise from 15% to 25% for tiles from outside SADC and COMESA in another, without reconciling them. It also reported that the Fight Inequality Alliance and the Jesuit Centre for Theological Reflection say the K50 monthly increase in Social Cash Transfer payments does not keep pace with inflation. An explainer published by The Zambian Observer on Saturday put the standard transfer at K400 to K450 a month and the payment for households with a person with a disability at K600 to K650, noted that FISP is to move towards an agricultural loan scheme from 2027, and put debt service at K67.6 billion, close to a quarter of the budget.

The copper arithmetic

The budget's growth story rests on mining, and that is where the sharpest arithmetic came from. Christopher Kang'ombe, the former Kamfinsa MP, wrote on his Facebook page, in remarks reported by Zambia Monitor on Saturday, that copper output is rising only about 4.5% a year, from 890,000 tonnes in 2025 to a projected 930,000 tonnes, and that at that pace Zambia reaches about 1.3 million tonnes by 2031, not the three million tonnes Musokotwane told Parliament remained within reach. Hitting the target, Kang'ombe said, would need average growth of about 26% a year from every producer, including NFCA. He went through them one by one: Mopani's Mufulira and Kitwe operations need big increases in 2027; Konkola Copper Mines is below expectations but could improve; First Quantum's Kansanshi and Trident lead but cannot close the gap alone; Barrick's Lumwana super pit should lift output though he doubted it would reach 26%; and Luanshya's contribution waits on the Shaft 28 project, while Lubambe lacks the material to make a difference.

Reuters, in a report carried by TimesLIVE on Saturday and by CNBC Africa on Friday, gave the government's side of the ledger: Musokotwane told Parliament on Friday 6 November that growth should stay around 5% in 2027, that the fiscal deficit is to fall to no more than 3% of GDP from 5.9% this year, and that copper output of 930,000 tonnes is expected in 2026, up from about 890,000 tonnes last year, under what he called a stable and predictable fiscal regime for mining companies. Reuters attributed this year's wider deficit to fuel tax cuts made to cushion households and businesses from the Iran war and to higher spending, including maize purchases for the national reserve, and noted that Zambia secured staff-level backing from the IMF in October for a new US$1.5 billion programme.

The measures under fire, as reported on Saturday

  • Mobile money: levies rise across all transaction bands; rates not itemised in the reports read (Zambia Monitor; Mpelembe)
  • Sugar: K1 per kilogram specific excise on refined sugar (Mpelembe)
  • Plastics: 80 ngwee per plastic bottle; single-use plastics excise to 200% (Mpelembe)
  • VAT: 16% to 16.5% from 1 January 2027; zero-rating ends for energy-saving appliances (Mpelembe)
  • PAYE: new 39.5% band above K37,000 a month (Zambia Monitor; Mpelembe; News Diggers)
  • Copper: 930,000 tonnes in 2026, 3 million targeted by 2031; Kang'ombe's projection 1.3 million (Zambia Monitor; Reuters)

Confirmed vs. alleged

Confirmed: The association's objection and Sichende's quoted words (Zambia Monitor). Haabazoka's allocations: K10.4 billion for Social Cash Transfer, K68.5 billion for education and health, K1 billion for school feeding, K22.7 billion for agriculture including K9.1 billion for FISP, K18.1 billion for roads, CDF to about K9 billion (Zambia Monitor; the CDF, education and agriculture figures also in News Diggers and The Zambian Observer). The 890,000 to 930,000-tonne copper figures, the 5.9% to 3% deficit path, growth around 5% and the US$1.5 billion IMF programme (Reuters via TimesLIVE and CNBC Africa; Zambia Monitor). The sugar, plastics and VAT details (Mpelembe Media).

Alleged or unverified: The actual mobile money levy rates and bands, which none of the reports read on Saturday itemised, and whether the levy is doubled as Mpelembe states. The duty on imported ceramic tiles, for which Mpelembe gives two conflicting figures. Kang'ombe's mine-by-mine assessment is his own analysis, posted on Facebook, and the government has not responded to it in any report found.
