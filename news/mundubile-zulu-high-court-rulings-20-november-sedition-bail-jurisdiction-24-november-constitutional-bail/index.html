Courts · Lead story Developing

Friday for jurisdiction, Tuesday for liberty: the High Court rules on 20 November whether it can hear Mundubile and Zulu's sedition bail plea, and on 24 November on bail for the 18

The Lusaka High Court heard the State's objection to the sedition bail application on Monday 16 November and reserved its ruling to Friday 20 November at 09:30. The Director of Public Prosecutions argued that filing in Lusaka while the case sat in Solwezi was an abuse of process; the defence relied on Section 123 of the Criminal Procedure Code. News Diggers reports that the separate constitutional bail application by all 18 treason accused will be decided on Tuesday 24 November, the day Mundubile and Zulu are also due to take plea before a magistrate.

By Lusaka Brief Courts Desk • LUSAKA — 17 November 2026 • 5 min read

Brian Mundubile and Makebi Zulu, who have been in custody since late August, now have two dates in the coming week on which judges will decide whether and how their applications for release can proceed. The first is Friday 20 November, when the Lusaka High Court rules on a preliminary objection by the State to their bail application in the seditious practices case. The second, reported by News Diggers on Tuesday 17 November, is Tuesday 24 November, when the same court is due to rule on the constitutional bail application brought by Mundubile, Zulu and 16 others held on treason-related allegations at Mukobeko Maximum Correctional Facility. That is also the day the two men are due to take plea on the sedition charges before Lusaka Resident Magistrate Webster Milumbe.

The hearing on Monday 16 November dealt only with the sedition matter. According to the Zambia Monitor, the State, led by Director of Public Prosecutions Gilbert Phiri, asked the court to throw out the bail application. ZNBC's report of the hearing, carried by the Zambian Observer, said the matter was before a three-judge panel led by Judge Mulenga and listed three questions the State wanted answered: whether the Lusaka High Court has jurisdiction when the case was previously before the Solwezi High Court; whether filing a bail application in Lusaka while the case was still in Solwezi amounted to a multiplicity of proceedings; and whether accused persons whose trial is before a Subordinate Court may seek bail from the High Court without first asking the trial court.

What each side argued

The Zambia Monitor reported the State's position as being that the High Court's bail powers do not allow parties to bypass the established procedures for moving cases between courts, and that the Lusaka filing, made while the case was still in Solwezi, "amounted to a multiplicity of proceedings and an abuse of court process". The State also argued that once the case had been transferred to the Lusaka Subordinate Court, any bail request should go there first.

The defence, whose lawyers the Monitor did not name, answered that Section 123 of the Criminal Procedure Code gives the Lusaka High Court jurisdiction to hear the application. They said the Solwezi Subordinate Court had declined jurisdiction, that the referral to the Solwezi High Court concerned only the transfer of the case, and that no bail application had been filed in either Solwezi court, so there was no duplication. The court reserved its ruling to Friday at 09:30 and adjourned the bail application itself until that ruling is given.

How the case reached Lusaka

The two men face two counts of seditious practices under Section 57 of the Penal Code, arising from Facebook livestreams on 14 August 2026, the day after the general election. The Monitor and the Times of Zambia summarise the counts the same way: Mundubile is alleged to have called on the Electoral Commission of Zambia to announce the winner on the basis of the party's parallel vote tabulation, and Zulu is alleged to have announced that Mundubile would address the nation at a set time. Both are lawyers; neither has yet entered a plea.

The case began in Solwezi, where the defence argued that the alleged offences were committed in Lusaka and that both men were arrested and live there. Principal Resident Magistrate Mambwe Mukonde referred the jurisdiction question to the High Court, and on 5 November Solwezi High Court Judge Edward Musona ordered the matter transferred to Lusaka. When it was called before Magistrate Milumbe on 11 November, the Times of Zambia reported, State Counsel Bonaventure Mutale, Chifumu Banda and Sakwiba Sikota asked to leave the courtroom because they had no instructions from their clients, and the magistrate adjourned plea to 24 November "in the interest of justice".

The treason matter and the political argument around it

The constitutional bail application is a separate proceeding in which all 18 people held over the alleged plot seek release pending trial. News Diggers reports that the ruling is set for 24 November and quotes Sakwiba Sikota describing the security arrangements around the case as "over-dramatised", contrasting it with the Captain Solo treason case, which he said involved more than 70 serving commandos, whereas this one involves three former commandos.

The approach of the rulings has also drawn political comment. Wynter Kabimba, the Economic Front president and a former justice minister, said on Prime Television's Matters Arising programme, in remarks reported by the Zambian Observer and News Diggers on Monday 16 November, that the governing UPND would suffer a backlash if the treason case ended in a nolle prosequi or without any conviction. He said the public expected at least some of the accused to be convicted, and asked how it could otherwise be persuaded of the seriousness of allegations over which the suspects had been held for so long. Neither report carried a response from the UPND or the government.

The dates

  • Friday 20 November, 09:30 — High Court ruling on the State's objection to the sedition bail application
  • Tuesday 24 November — High Court ruling on constitutional bail for the 18 treason accused (News Diggers)
  • Tuesday 24 November — Mundubile and Zulu due to take plea on the sedition charges before Magistrate Milumbe

Confirmed vs. alleged

Confirmed: The State's preliminary objection to the sedition bail application was heard on Monday 16 November and the ruling is reserved to Friday 20 November at 09:30 (Zambia Monitor; ZNBC via the Zambian Observer). The charges and the Solwezi-to-Lusaka transfer are as reported by the Zambia Monitor and the Times of Zambia. The 24 November plea date was set on 11 November (Times of Zambia).

Alleged or unverified: The 24 November date for the constitutional bail ruling rests on News Diggers' report of 17 November, which is behind a paywall and gives no further detail of Monday's proceedings in that matter. The names of the judges on the sedition bail panel, beyond the presiding Judge Mulenga, were not reported. All charges are allegations: no plea has been taken and nothing has been tested at trial.
