Courts · Sedition case Developing

The wait ends tomorrow: Solwezi rules on jurisdiction for Mundubile and Zulu — and the DPP has answered the critics

Principal Resident Magistrate Mambwe Mukonde will rule on Tuesday 13 October on whether her Solwezi court can hear the sedition case against Brian Mundubile and Makebi Zulu — and plea could follow. The State does not dispute that the alleged offence happened in Lusaka. Meanwhile, the DPP has a word for the delay critics: Shamwana took eleven months.

By Lusaka Brief Courts Desk • SOLWEZI — 12 October 2026 • 3 min read

One more night. Principal Resident Magistrate Mambwe Mukonde will on Tuesday 13 October rule on whether the Solwezi Magistrate's Court has jurisdiction to hear the sedition case against Brian Mundubile and Makebi Zulu — and, depending on the answer, plea could finally be taken. The ruling ends a procedural detour that began on 25 September, when the pair first appeared and the matter was adjourned because their lawyers were absent, and ran through last week's sessions, where the court declined to hear oral submissions, saying the issues had been adequately addressed in documents before it.

The defence argument is geography. Brian Mundubile, the losing 2026 presidential candidate, and his running mate Makebi Zulu face two counts of seditious practices contrary to Section 57, as read with Section 60, of the Penal Code — over Facebook livestreams in which Mundubile allegedly claimed victory on a parallel vote tabulation and called on the Electoral Commission of Zambia to resume counting, and in which Zulu allegedly said Mundubile would address the nation. They argue the alleged offences were committed in Lusaka, that trying them in North-Western Province breaches the Subordinate Courts Act and the Criminal Procedure Code, and that the venue blocks their witnesses and imposes financial hardship. On the location point, the State offered no resistance: Director of Public Prosecutions Gilbert Phiri told the court the prosecution did not dispute the warn-and-caution statements, which indicate the alleged offence was committed in Lusaka. The defence has also asked that constitutional questions be referred to the High Court — an application the State strongly opposes.

That's when the court will make its ruling on whether or not the court has got jurisdiction and whether or not this matter be referred to be heard in Lusaka.
— Sakwiba Sikota, lead counsel for the defence, after the hearing, quoted by Lusaka Times

The DPP's answer to the delay critics

The wait itself has become an argument. The church and international bodies have called for a speedy trial for Mundubile, Zulu and 16 others — arrested in August and charged with treason in a separate matter headed for the High Court — all in detention since. Answering on Wednesday at a graduation ceremony for a financial investigations and asset recovery pilot course, DPP Gilbert Phiri attributed delays to investigations and called it a "cliché" to assume a suspect must appear in court days after arrest regardless of complexity. His precedent: the 1985 Shamwana treason case, which he said took eleven months to commence.

What Tuesday settles is venue, not guilt. If the magistrate finds for the defence, the sedition file moves towards Lusaka; if she finds for the State, plea could be taken in Solwezi the same day. Either way the treason case waits its own turn — and the State's position, stated in open court, that it does not dispute where the alleged offence happened will follow it to whichever courtroom hears it.

The road to Tuesday

  • 25 September — first appearance; plea deferred, lawyers absent
  • 6 October — oral submissions declined; written submissions ordered
  • The State does not dispute the alleged offence occurred in Lusaka
  • 13 October — jurisdiction ruling; plea may follow
  • Separately: treason case, 18 accused, heads to the High Court

Confirmed vs. alleged

Confirmed: The jurisdiction ruling is set for Tuesday 13 October before Principal Resident Magistrate Mambwe Mukonde in Solwezi, per the Times of Zambia and Lusaka Times. The pair face two counts of seditious practices under Section 57 of the Penal Code; neither has taken plea. The State told the court it does not dispute that the warn-and-caution statements place the alleged offence in Lusaka. DPP Gilbert Phiri made his 'investigations take time' remarks on Wednesday 7 October, per News Diggers.

Alleged or unverified: Which way the magistrate will rule, whether plea will in fact be taken on Tuesday, and the fate of the defence's constitutional-referral request. The timing of the separate treason case is not on any published schedule.
