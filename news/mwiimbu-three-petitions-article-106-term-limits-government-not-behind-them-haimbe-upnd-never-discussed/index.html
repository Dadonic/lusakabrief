Three petitions and no sponsor: Mwiimbu says the government will not interfere with calls to remove term limits, and will not say whether Hichilema would sign one

Justice Minister Jack Mwiimbu told a Lusaka press briefing on Friday 13 November that three petitions asking Parliament to amend Article 106(3), the two-term limit, were initiated by citizens and are not sponsored by President Hichilema, the UPND or the government, which will leave the National Assembly to handle them. Asked whether the President would back an amendment if MPs passed one, he said he could not speak for him. UPND legal chairperson Mulambo Haimbe says the party has never discussed the matter.

By Lusaka Brief Politics Desk • LUSAKA — 15 November 2026 • 3 min read

The government has put a formal position on the record in the argument over presidential term limits, and it is one of distance. Justice Minister Jack Mwiimbu told journalists at a press briefing in Lusaka on Friday 13 November that three petitions have been submitted to the National Assembly seeking to amend Article 106(3) of the Constitution, the clause that limits a president to two terms, and that the executive will not interfere with how Parliament deals with them. The Times of Zambia, News Diggers and the Zambian Observer all reported the briefing.

According to the Times of Zambia, the petitions came from members of the public, political leaders and civil society organisations. Mwiimbu said the government "respects the constitutional right of citizens to petition the National Assembly", the paper reported, and that the Assembly is the institution mandated to consider what they raise. The Zambian Observer reported him citing Article 88(1), which gives citizens the right to petition Parliament, and saying that Parliament is independent of the executive.

Not ours, says the minister

The Zambian Observer reported that Mwiimbu categorically denied that President Hakainde Hichilema or the United Party for National Development was behind the petitions, and dismissed suggestions that the President was quietly encouraging a campaign. In the Observer's account he said the petitioners had acted on their own constitutional rights, and he urged leaders to stop misleading the public and to let lawful processes run. News Diggers reported him saying that any interference by the executive would itself be unconstitutional, that the government had no role in the petitions or the proposals, and that it would not be distracted from its Grow Zambia Agenda by political speculation.

The question that the briefing did not settle was the President's own view. Asked whether Hichilema would support an amendment if Parliament approved one, Mwiimbu declined to speculate and said he could not speak for the President, the Zambian Observer reported.

The party line, and the civil society warning

A day earlier, News Diggers carried an interview with Mulambo Haimbe, the UPND's deputy chairperson for legal affairs, who said that "at no point had the UPND, as a party, ever held discussions relating to the removal of presidential term limits". He was responding to Josiah Kalala, executive director of the Chapter One Foundation, who had said on Monday 9 November that civil society would consider legal action if there were attempts to remove the limit. Haimbe called it inappropriate to threaten litigation over a change the government had not proposed, and said citizens had a constitutional right to express views on possible amendments whether or not others agreed with them.

The petitions land in an argument that has been running for a week. On Thursday 12 November, as Zambia Monitor reported, ten of the candidates defeated by Hichilema in the election of 13 August 2026, among them Harry Kalaba, Fred M'membe and Kelvin Bwalya, issued a joint statement in Lusaka opposing any removal of the limit, declaring that "Zambia does not belong to an individual, a political party or an administration". Zambia Monitor attributes the proposal they were answering to Mwato Kabaso, a nominated Member of Parliament representing the youth. The Zambian Observer reported that opposition parties and civil society organisations fear the change would weaken safeguards against prolonged presidential rule.

Who has said what

  • Jack Mwiimbu, Justice Minister, Friday 13 November — three petitions are before the National Assembly; the government will not interfere and is not behind them; he cannot speak for the President on whether he would back an amendment
  • Mulambo Haimbe, UPND legal chairperson, Wednesday 11 November — the party has never discussed removing term limits
  • Josiah Kalala, Chapter One Foundation, Monday 9 November — civil society would consider legal action if an attempt were made
  • Ten defeated presidential candidates, Thursday 12 November — joint statement against removing the two-term limit

Confirmed vs. alleged

Confirmed: That three petitions seeking to amend Article 106(3) have been submitted to the National Assembly, and that the Justice Minister told a Lusaka press briefing on Friday 13 November that the government is not behind them and will not interfere, is reported by the Times of Zambia, News Diggers and the Zambian Observer. Haimbe's statement that the UPND has never discussed the matter is reported by News Diggers.

Alleged or unverified: Who exactly signed the three petitions and what they ask for in detail has not been published by the outlets cited. President Hichilema has not stated his own position on an amendment; his minister declined to speak for him. Whether the National Assembly will table the petitions, and on what timetable, is not yet known.
