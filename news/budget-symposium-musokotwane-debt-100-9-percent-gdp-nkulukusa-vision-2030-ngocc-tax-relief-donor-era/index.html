From 20.8% to 100.9% of GDP: Musokotwane makes the case for paying tax at the budget symposium, the Treasury says Vision 2030 is on track, and NGOCC says women are left behind

At the 2027 National Budget Symposium at Mulungushi on Monday 9 November, the Finance Minister traced the debt that rose from 20.8% of GDP in 2011 to 100.9% by the end of 2021 and defended the removal of subsidies, while the Secretary to the Treasury, Felix Nkulukusa, said GDP per capita should rise from US$1,318 to US$2,985 even if some mining targets slip. News Diggers reported the minister comparing tax to a funeral contribution, and the NGOCC said the budget's withdrawal of VAT relief on energy-saving appliances will hit female-headed households.

By Lusaka Brief Business Desk • LUSAKA — 10 November 2026 • 3 min read

Three days after presenting a K284.8 billion budget on Friday 6 November, Dr Situmbeko Musokotwane went back over why it looks the way it does. Speaking at the 2027 National Budget Symposium at the Mulungushi International Conference Centre on Monday 9 November, the Minister of Finance and National Planning said Zambia's economic priorities were shifting from stabilisation and debt recovery towards investment, productivity and expansion, and that the reforms of the past five years had laid the ground for that, Zambia Monitor reported.

The figures he gave were the ones he has used before, set out in order. Public debt stood at 20.8% of GDP in 2011 and 100.9% by the end of 2021, against a sub-Saharan African average he put at about 24%. By 2022 the country was spending roughly US$2 billion a year servicing debt, up from under US$90 million in earlier years, money that would otherwise have gone to social protection and investment. National poverty rose from 56.4% in 2015 to about 60% in 2022, with rural poverty near 78%. The response, he said, was fiscal stability, external debt restructuring and the removal of poorly targeted subsidies; the fuel subsidy, he argued, had given K1,500 a month to someone spending K3,000 on fuel and K50 to a villager paying K100 for a bus fare.

The Treasury's targets

Felix Nkulukusa, the Secretary to the Treasury, told the same symposium that Zambia remained on course for Vision 2030 even if some targets under the Grow Zambia Agenda take longer than planned. GDP per capita is projected to rise from US$1,318 in 2025 to US$2,985, against population growth he put at 3.4% by 2031; some mining targets could slip by one or two years because investment is being implemented more slowly than expected; and the aim is to bring the poverty headcount from 60% to below 20%, Zambia Monitor reported.

This means that we are on a good trajectory to achieving our Vision 2030.
— Felix Nkulukusa, Secretary to the Treasury, at the 2027 National Budget Symposium in Lusaka on Monday 9 November, quoted by Zambia Monitor

News Diggers reported the minister in blunter terms on Monday. Paying tax, he said, means losing money, in the way that contributing to a funeral at home does; the era of donors paying for services was over, and Zambians should be wary of anyone who promised a good life without saying how the economy would grow. He also said the country's maize stocks were enough to feed it until the 2028 harvest. The paper listed the measures the argument is meant to justify: VAT up from 16% to 16.5%, a 39.5% PAYE band on monthly income above K37,000, a five-point rise in corporate income tax on income currently taxed at 20% or less, and higher duty on ceramics.

The women's organisations' objection

Not everyone at the receiving end is persuaded. The NGOCC's executive director, Anne Anamela, said the budget leaves marginalised women and girls behind, News Diggers reported on Monday. Her specific complaint is the proposal to end the zero-rating of VAT on energy-saving appliances, which she said would raise living costs for female-headed households and women traders; she also noted that the Anti-GBV Fund remains unfunded and that the Gender Equity and Equality Commission is still not operating. She gave the budget credit for doubling the school feeding programme to K1 billion and for raising the number of households on social cash transfer to 1.6 million.

A third speech on Monday set the production side of the argument. In an address to the Third Zambia Commodities Market Outlook Conference in Lusaka, read for him by the Minister of Trade and Exports, Dr Caroline Kaunda Katotobe, President Hichilema said the next phase after stabilisation should be production, industrialisation, value addition and export-led growth, and set out targets of 10 million tonnes of maize, 3 million tonnes of soya beans, a million tonnes each of wheat and sugar, 10,000 megawatts of generation and 3 million tonnes of copper a year, Zambia Monitor reported. "Zambia is ready to produce more, add greater value and trade more competitively," the speech said.
