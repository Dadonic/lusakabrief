Up six places to 15th: the 2026 Ibrahim Index puts Zambia among Africa's ten most improved on governance, on data that stop before the August election

Zambia scored 58.4 out of 100 in the Mo Ibrahim Foundation's 2026 index, a gain of 5.1 points over 2016–2025 that lifted it from 21st to 15th of 54 countries, Zambia Monitor and the Daily Nation reported last week. Citizen participation rose more than anywhere else in Africa. Satisfaction with infrastructure fell by 30.4 points. The period measured ends in 2025, before the disputed aftermath of this year's vote.

By Lusaka Brief Politics Desk • LUSAKA — 9 November 2026 • 2 min read

Zambia has climbed six places in the continent's best-known governance ranking. The 2026 Ibrahim Index of African Governance, which the Mo Ibrahim Foundation launched on 31 October, places the country 15th of 54, up from 21st, with a score of 58.4 out of 100, Zambia Monitor reported on 5 November and the Daily Nation on 6 November. The score is 5.1 points higher than in 2016, which puts Zambia among the ten most improved countries in Africa over the decade the index covers.

Most of the gain is recent. Zambia Monitor reported that the pace of improvement quickened after 2021, with the overall score rising 6.4 points from that year, which on those figures means it had slipped slightly in the years before. The Daily Nation's summary made the same point, saying progress accelerated from 2021.

Where the points came from

The largest single movement was in citizen participation, up 15.5 points since 2016, the biggest rise recorded by any African country, according to Zambia Monitor, which said the index found the improvement "particularly evident following the August 2021 general election". The rights measure rose 9.5 points. On the indicator the index calls Absence of Civil Society Repression Zambia moved from 29th to 10th, and on Rights and Civil Liberties for Women it is ranked first on the continent.

Two economic measures also moved sharply. Rural market access improved by 45.3 points, the largest gain in Africa, and access to mobile money accounts by 47.5 points, Zambia Monitor reported. One indicator went the other way: citizens' satisfaction with infrastructure fell by 30.4 points over the period.

What the index does not yet measure

The dates matter for how the ranking is read at home. The improvement is measured over 2016 to 2025, so the index does not take in the general election of 13 August 2026 or what has followed it. Human Rights Watch said on 2 November that it had documented 14 apparent enforced disappearances in August, 61 cases of beatings or other violence during arrest or detention, and 71 arrests on charges it regarded as potentially politically motivated, Zambia Monitor reported. Eighteen people have been charged with treason, the same report said. The police have given their own account of the most serious incident, the raid of 14 August on opposition leader Brian Mundubile's Lusaka home, in which opposition members allege that former minister Mutotwe Kafwaya was killed by security forces: the Inspector General has said officers came under gunfire and returned fire.

Both things can be true: a decade's data showing wider participation and stronger rights scores, and a set of allegations from the past three months that the next edition of the index will have to weigh. Neither report recorded a government or opposition comment on the ranking, and neither gave the scores of other countries for comparison.

Zambia in the 2026 Ibrahim Index, as reported

  • Overall: 15th of 54, up from 21st; 58.4 out of 100; +5.1 points over 2016–2025 (Zambia Monitor; Daily Nation)
  • Citizen participation: +15.5 points, the largest gain in Africa
  • Rights: +9.5 points; Absence of Civil Society Repression: 29th to 10th
  • Rights and Civil Liberties for Women: 1st in Africa
  • Rural market access: +45.3 points; mobile money accounts: +47.5 points
  • Satisfaction with infrastructure: −30.4 points

Sources: Zambia Monitor, “Zambia among Africa's 10 most improved countries in governance, report says” (5 Nov 2026); Daily Nation Zambia, “Zambia among Africa's 10 most improved in governance” (6 Nov 2026); Zambia Monitor, “Human Rights Watch accuses Zambia of post-election crackdown on opposition” (2 Nov 2026).
