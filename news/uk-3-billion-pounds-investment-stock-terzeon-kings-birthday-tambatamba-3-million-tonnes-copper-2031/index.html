£3 billion in and more promised: at the King's birthday reception Britain's envoy pledges investment for Grow Zambia, and the mines minister asks for help towards 3 million tonnes

British High Commissioner Rebecca Terzeon told guests at the British Residence in Lusaka that UK investment stock in Zambia exceeds £3 billion, that the UK–Zambia Green Growth Compact has drawn £1.6 billion of UK-linked investment since 2021 and that £15 million for the Zambia–Tanzania interconnector helped unlock US$300 million from the World Bank and the EU. Mines Minister Brenda Tambatamba asked for British money in mining, energy and critical minerals, saying new discoveries, not just bigger mines, are needed to hit the 2031 copper target.

By Lusaka Brief Business Desk • LUSAKA — 15 November 2026 • 2 min read

The annual celebration of the Official Birthday of King Charles III at the British Residence in Lusaka doubled as an investment pitch from both sides. British High Commissioner Rebecca Terzeon said the United Kingdom's investment stock in Zambia now exceeds £3 billion, making it one of the country's largest investors, Zambia Monitor reported on Saturday 14 November. "The United Kingdom remains a committed and natural partner for Zambia," she said, echoing a description Zambia Monitor said President Hichilema himself had used.

Terzeon set out the figures Britain wants credit for. According to Zambia Monitor, the UK–Zambia Green Growth Compact has attracted an additional £1.6 billion in UK-linked investment since 2021; a £15 million British contribution to the Zambia–Tanzania power interconnector helped unlock a further US$300 million from the World Bank and the European Union; and British International Investment has backed small and medium-sized enterprises and acted as anchor investor in Zambia's first sustainability bond. She said the UK was ready to bring investment, expertise and partnerships behind the Grow Zambia agenda and was shifting from delivering services directly towards sharing expertise and strengthening Zambian institutions.

Growth with conditions attached

"Growth must be sustainable, resilient and inclusive," Terzeon said, linking that to reducing poverty and to resilience against shocks such as El Niño and Ebola. Zambia Monitor reported her saying prosperity rests on macroeconomic stability, effective institutions, accountable government, political pluralism, freedom of speech and the rule of law, and that Zambia has an important role in the mineral supply chains of the global energy transition.

The ask from the Zambian side

Mines and Minerals Development Minister Brenda Tambatamba spoke for the hosts' interest. News Diggers reported her asking for more British investment in mining, energy and critical minerals to help Zambia reach its goal of three million tonnes of copper a year by 2031. Getting there, she said, would need new mineral discoveries, greenfield projects, reliable electricity, stronger local suppliers and efficient regional trade routes. "Achieving this will require more than expansion of existing mines. It will require new discoveries," she said, in the words News Diggers quoted.

Neither outlet reported any new agreement signed at the reception, nor figures for the investment Tambatamba hopes to attract; the speeches set out positions rather than commitments. Zambia Monitor's account notes that the UK's description of itself as a natural partner matched language the President has used, and both outlets place the exchange within the government's Grow Zambia agenda and its 2031 copper target.

Sources: Zambia Monitor, “UK pledges more investment, partnerships to support Zambia's growth agenda” (14 Nov 2026); News Diggers, “Zambia seeks UK investment to hit 3 million tonne copper target” (14 Nov 2026).
