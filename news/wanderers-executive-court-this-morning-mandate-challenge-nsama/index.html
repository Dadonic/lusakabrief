Court at eight, training after: Mufulira Wanderers' executive answers a mandate challenge this morning

The executive committee of nine-time champions Mufulira Wanderers was due before the court at 08:00 hours on Wednesday, summoned by former physical trainer Davies Nsama and the Mufulira Wanderers Community Pressure Group in a challenge to its continued mandate. The club insists the plaintiffs are not paid-up members — and that football comes first.

By Mwelwa Phiri • MUFULIRA — 7 October 2026 • 4 min read

The most storied boardroom in Mufulira spent this morning in a courtroom. The executive committee of Mufulira Wanderers was due to appear before the court at 08:00 hours on Wednesday, answering a summons brought by Davies Nsama, a former physical trainer at the club, and the Mufulira Wanderers Community Pressure Group. Court documents filed in the action challenge the legality of the executive's continued mandate to run the club, with the plaintiffs contending its tenure has expired.

The club's defence begins before the merits. In its weekend statement, the board said that according to its records the people behind the court action are not paid-up members of the nine-time champions — a standing objection that goes to whether they may sue at all. The summons has been forwarded to the legal department of sponsors Mopani Copper Mines, and the club has pledged to comply with the process while noting that football governance disputes are ordinarily handled within football's own regulatory structures. "As a law-abiding institution, Mufulira Wanderers will honour that obligation," the board said.

The timing could hardly be less convenient. Mighty are five days into a transitional league season that opened with a 4-1 home defeat to Green Eagles at Shinde Stadium, and Red Arrows away waits on Saturday. The board's answer to the collision of calendars has been to repeat its Saturday line: the summons bars no official from football duty, and the focus remains on points.

By press time on Wednesday morning, no outcome from the 08:00 hearing had been reported; this story will be updated. What the court decides first is procedural — who may bring a members' club to book, and where. The larger question it leaves hanging over Kantanshi is older than any single committee: who, exactly, owns Mighty Mufulira Wanderers — the executive of record, the members who pay, or the community that fills Shinde?

Confirmed vs. disputed

Confirmed (by the club and the filing): The summons required the executive to appear at 08:00 on 7 October; the action was brought by Davies Nsama and the Community Pressure Group; the papers are with Mopani's legal department; the club undertook to comply.

Disputed or untested: The plaintiffs' claim that the executive's tenure has expired; the club's counter that the plaintiffs are not paid-up members; any effect on the committee's mandate — none of which the court had ruled on by press time.
