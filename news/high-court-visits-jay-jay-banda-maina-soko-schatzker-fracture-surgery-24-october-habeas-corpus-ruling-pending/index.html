The court goes to the patient: Judge Mulenga visits Jay Jay Banda at Maina Soko after a surgeon's report of a shattered shinbone, with the habeas corpus ruling still to come

After the State failed to bring former Petauke Central MP Emmanuel 'Jay Jay' Banda to court, High Court Judge Geoffrey Mulenga went to Maina Soko Military Medical Centre on Friday 13 November to see his condition for himself. Surgeon Dr Charles Mukunta's report says Banda had major surgery on 24 October to fix a severe fracture of the left shinbone near the knee with a plate and screws and must not bear weight on the leg. His mother, who brought the habeas corpus application, says she fears he will never walk again.

By Lusaka Brief Courts Desk • LUSAKA — 15 November 2026 • 3 min read

The habeas corpus proceedings over Emmanuel Jay Jay Banda moved from the courtroom to the hospital ward on Friday 13 November. News Diggers reported that High Court Judge Geoffrey Mulenga visited the Maina Soko Military Medical Centre in Lusaka to assess the health of the former Petauke Central Member of Parliament, after the State had failed to produce him for the hearing and his treating doctor had refused to let him be moved. The Zambian Observer reported that the Attorney General had applied for the court to move to the hospital, and that Banda's lawyer, Nkula Botha, accompanied the court.

The visit followed the medical report the judge had demanded earlier in the week. According to News Diggers, orthopaedic surgeon Dr Charles Mukunta reported that Banda remains admitted and must not put weight on his injured leg after major surgery to repair a severely fractured shinbone near the knee. The Zambian Observer gave the detail from the court documents: a Schatzker VI fracture of the left tibial plateau, with breaks to the tibia and fibula and damage to the knee joint, fixed on 24 October 2026 with a locking plate and screws. X-ray images and photographs of the surgical stitches were among the papers filed, the Observer reported.

Not a ruling

The Zambian Observer stressed that the visit was to establish Banda's condition independently and was not a decision on the habeas corpus application itself; it said a ruling is forthcoming but gave no date, and News Diggers' visible text gives none either. Banda, 41, was apprehended in Mozambique on 14 October 2026, according to the Observer, and his mother, Grace Nampokolwe Banda, filed the application on 30 October.

'My son will never walk again'

In a separate piece on Saturday the Zambian Observer carried an interview that Banda's mother gave to Zambian Watch after visiting him. "I saw him yesterday at Maina Soko Military Hospital, and I can tell you that my son will never walk again," she said, adding that he was in great pain and that his leg was terribly broken. She said she was relieved he was alive despite speculation on social media, and that she wished the situation that led to his detention had been handled differently. Her account of his prognosis is her own; the surgeon's report, as reported, describes post-operative recovery and a bar on bearing weight, not a permanent outcome.

The outlets cited do not describe the events behind Banda's injuries or the matters for which he is held, and no court has made findings on them. Earlier in the week the State had failed to produce him for the hearing; the judge responded by ordering the medical report that led to Friday's visit.

Confirmed vs. alleged

Confirmed: That Judge Mulenga visited Maina Soko on Friday 13 November, that Banda had surgery on 24 October for a severe fracture of the left shinbone and may not bear weight, and that the habeas corpus application was filed by his mother on 30 October, is reported by News Diggers and the Zambian Observer.

Alleged or unverified: The date of the ruling on the application has not been reported. The mother's statement that her son will never walk again is her own view and is not in the surgeon's report as reported. How Banda sustained the injury has not been set out by the outlets cited.
