'Happy heavenly 70th birthday, Dad': Tasila Lungu marks the day her father would have turned seventy, seventeen months after his death and with his remains still in South Africa

Edgar Chagwa Lungu, Zambia's sixth president, would have been 70 on Wednesday 11 November. His daughter, the former Chawama MP Tasila Lungu, wrote that his love and lessons were still with the family every day. The Zambian Observer noted that more than a year after his death in South Africa on 5 June 2025 his remains have not been buried; South Africa's Supreme Court of Appeal ruled in June that the family, not the Zambian government, decides where and how that happens.

By Lusaka Brief Culture Desk • LUSAKA — 12 November 2026 • 2 min read

Wednesday 11 November would have been the seventieth birthday of Edgar Chagwa Lungu, who was President of Zambia from 2015 to 2021 and died in South Africa on 5 June 2025. His daughter Tasila Lungu, the former Chawama MP, marked the day with a social media post in which she wrote that she missed him and that the love and lessons he gave her and her siblings were still with them every single day, The Zambian Observer reported. She signed off with a birthday wish to her father in heaven.

Happy heavenly 70th birthday, Dad.
— Tasila Lungu, in a social media post on Wednesday 11 November, as quoted by The Zambian Observer

The Observer's own note on the anniversary, published early on Wednesday, recorded the unresolved matter behind the tributes: more than a year after his death, the former president's remains are still in South Africa, and the question of his final resting place continues to draw public attention. The comments beneath both pieces, the Observer's readers arguing over the family's handling of the funeral, were a small sample of a dispute that has run since the week he died.

Where the burial stands

The legal position was settled, at least in South Africa, on 23 June 2026, when the Supreme Court of Appeal ruled that Mr Lungu's family should decide where and how he is buried, Reuters reported in a story carried by Polity. The judgment overturned a lower court's order that would have allowed the Zambian government to repatriate the body for a state funeral, finding that Zambia had not shown a legal right under South African law to override the family's wishes and that the evidence showed continuing negotiations rather than a concluded agreement. The government had wanted a burial at the site in Lusaka reserved for former presidents; the family preferred a private burial in South Africa and had said Mr Lungu would not have wanted his successor and long-time rival, President Hakainde Hichilema, at his funeral. South Africa's government said it would respect the family's wishes while regarding a state burial in Zambia as the best outcome.

Earlier in June, before that ruling, Ms Lungu had written that the family was still waiting for a chance to bury her father the way he wished and that the pain of his loss remained raw, News Diggers reported at the time. None of this week's reports says that a burial has since taken place or been scheduled, and neither the family nor the government was reported to have said anything new on the subject on Wednesday.

Mr Lungu led Zambia from 2015 to 2021 and died in South Africa, where he had gone for medical treatment, Reuters reported. His years in office are still argued over in Zambian politics as hotly as his burial, and Wednesday's birthday, marked by his daughter with a few lines online, was a reminder that the second argument has not ended.
