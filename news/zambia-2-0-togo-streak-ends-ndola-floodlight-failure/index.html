Two goals, one blackout and a weight lifted: Zambia beat Togo 2-0 and turn Group I into a contest

Albert Kangwanda scored inside three minutes, the floodlights failed for twenty, and Eliya Mandanji finished it in stoppage time. Zambia's first win of the campaign draws them level with Togo on three points — and turns November's double-header with Burundi into the decider it was always going to be.

By Mwelwa Phiri • NDOLA — 30 September 2026 • 4 min read

The drought ended before the rain delay — and in Ndola on Tuesday night, even the electricity got a mention in the match report. Zambia beat Togo 2-0 at Levy Mwanawasa Stadium, a first win of the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations qualifying campaign, delivered with an early goal, a twenty-minute blackout and a stoppage-time finish that let a tense stadium finally exhale.

Albert Kangwanda needed barely three minutes. A corner was worked short and swung in, Fashion Sakala rose to head it on, and Kangwanda arrived to finish — 1-0 before some of the crowd had settled. Then, mid-way through the first half, the stadium's power failed. Play stopped for about twenty minutes while the floodlights were coaxed back, the clock frozen at 25 minutes, and a nation's recent football history hung in the dark with it. When the lights returned, so did the pattern: Togo with the ball — 61 per cent of possession by the final count — Zambia with the chances. The visitors managed one shot on target all night; Zambia had four, and seven corners to Togo's five.

The second goal arrived where games like this are decided — in the fifth minute of stoppage time, on the counter. Matthews Banda played the pass forward and Eliya Mandanji, on as a substitute, broke into the area and drove it home. 2-0, full time, and a first competitive victory since the World Cup qualifying win of October last year. George Lwandamina, who had spent four days promising that Togo would not have it easy, was proved right in the most Zambian way possible: early noise, long anxiety, late relief.

The table is where the night's real value sits. In Bujumbura, Burundi held Algeria 2-2 — an own goal and a Jean Girumugisha strike for the hosts, Mohamed Belloumi and a second-half free-kick for the Desert Foxes — which leaves Group I after two matchdays reading: Algeria 4 points, Zambia 3 (goal difference 0), Togo 3 (goal difference −1), Burundi 1. A defeat would have left Zambia bottom and adrift; instead they are level with the side that arrived joint-top, with a November double-header against Burundi standing between them and a genuinely open group. Kings Kangwa's absence still hangs over the midfield — his CAF ban runs the whole window, and Charles Musonda's verdict that the suspension cost Zambia in Algeria will survive the week — but Friday's conquerors dropped points, and that was the night's other gift.

There is no need to pretend a qualifier heals anything beyond football. But a full Levy Mwanawasa, singing through a blackout and a goal drought at once, was its own small argument about the country's week. The lights failed and the match went on. Zambia, for once, did the same.
