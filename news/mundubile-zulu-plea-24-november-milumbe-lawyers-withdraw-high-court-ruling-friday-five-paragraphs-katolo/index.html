Courts · Sedition and treason Developing

A plea on 24 November and a ruling at 11:00 today: Mundubile and Zulu face the sedition charges without counsel, as the High Court rules on their account of the UN refuge

Lusaka Resident Magistrate Webster Milumbe refused an adjournment on Wednesday 11 November and ordered Brian Mundubile and Makebi Zulu to take plea on two counts of seditious practices; when Bonaventure Mutale, Sakwiba Sikota and Chifumu Banda said they had no instructions, he let them withdraw and set 24 November for a possible plea. In the treason bail case, the High Court rules on Friday 13 November at 11:00 on the State's bid to strike five paragraphs and a video from the applicants' affidavit, then hears the bail plea itself.

By Lusaka Brief Courts Desk • LUSAKA — 13 November 2026 • 3 min read

Brian Mundubile and Makebi Zulu will be asked to plead to two counts of seditious practices on Tuesday 24 November, after a hearing on Wednesday 11 November in which their lawyers withdrew and the two politicians, both lawyers by training, addressed the Lusaka Magistrates' Court themselves. Zambia Monitor, News Diggers and the Times of Zambia report the hearing before Resident Magistrate Webster Milumbe, in a case transferred to Lusaka from Solwezi.

According to Zambia Monitor, the magistrate initially insisted that the accused take plea and rejected a defence application for an adjournment, saying counsel had had enough time to obtain instructions. The Director of Public Prosecutions, Gilbert Phiri, opposed the adjournment, News Diggers reports, on the ground that the accused were not ready to plead. Senior counsel Bonaventure Mutale and Sakwiba Sikota told the court they could not proceed without instructions from their clients and asked to withdraw, which the magistrate allowed; the Times of Zambia also names Chifumu Banda among the lawyers who left.

Why the accused would not plead

Zulu told the court, as Zambia Monitor reports it, that the two had understood they were coming to Lusaka for High Court proceedings, not to take plea in the transferred matter, and that pleading before preliminary issues were resolved could undermine the applications they intend to make, including whether the charges disclose an offence at all. The accused said they had difficulty preparing from detention without phones or laptops. Zulu asked for 14 days; the magistrate granted seven and indicated 24 November as a possible plea date, adjourning, in the Times of Zambia's account, "in the interest of justice".

The Times of Zambia sets out the two counts: that Mundubile allegedly called on the Electoral Commission of Zambia to release results and declare the winner of the 13 August election on the strength of a parallel vote tabulation, and that Zulu allegedly announced on Facebook that Mundubile would address the nation at a scheduled time. A contributor's account in the Zambian Observer says both men deny the charges and that they invoked their right to counsel under Article 18 of the Constitution before the plea was deferred.

Five paragraphs and a flash disk

In the separate constitutional bail application by Mundubile, Zulu and 16 co-accused in the treason case, the Lusaka High Court has reserved its ruling on the State's objection to Friday 13 November at 11:00, with the substantive bail arguments to follow. The Zambian Observer, carrying a Kalemba report, says the State wants five paragraphs struck from the applicants' affidavit, paragraphs that explain why Mundubile and Zulu sought refuge at a United Nations facility before surrendering to police. Chief State Advocate Chali Hambayi argued the paragraphs raised matters not previously pleaded: "If we had not raised that objection, those matters would have gone on to the court without us having an opportunity to respond to them."

They did not expect them to just say we are not a flight risk, they needed to explain how they are not a flight risk.
— Milner Katolo, defence lawyer, speaking after the Lusaka High Court hearing on Wednesday 11 November, as reported by Kalemba via the Zambian Observer

Katolo argued that going to the UN facility after alleged shootings at the two men's homes and the death of Mutotwe Kafwaya was driven by fear for their lives and does not make them flight risks, and that the paragraphs are a clarification rather than new evidence. The State had itself cited the UN stay as a reason to oppose bail, he said. The applicants are asking for unconditional release or bail on reasonable conditions, arguing that prolonged detention without trial breaches their constitutional rights.

Confirmed vs. alleged

Confirmed: That the magistrate refused an adjournment, allowed the defence lawyers to withdraw and set 24 November for a possible plea (Zambia Monitor, News Diggers, Times of Zambia); that the High Court will rule at 11:00 on Friday 13 November on the State's objection to parts of the bail affidavit before hearing the bail application (Zambian Observer, carrying Kalemba).

Alleged or unverified: Whether Mundubile and Zulu will have new counsel by 24 November; how the High Court will rule on the five paragraphs and the video; and the outcome of the bail application itself. The charges are allegations; both men deny them and no plea has been taken.
