Courts · Habeas corpus Developing

Ordered to court at nine, not produced: the State says Jay Jay Banda's doctor will not let him leave Maina Soko, and the judge demands a medical report before Friday

Judge Geoffrey Mulenga had ordered the State to bring the former Petauke Central MP Emmanuel Jay Jay Banda before the Lusaka High Court at 09:00 on Wednesday 11 November to explain the circumstances of his detention. He was not brought. The Attorney General's office said the doctor treating him at Maina Soko Military Medical Centre had not authorised his movement, and the judge ordered a medical report. The court is expected to rule on Friday on whether the habeas corpus hearing moves to the hospital.

By Lusaka Brief Courts Desk • LUSAKA — 12 November 2026 • 2 min read

The habeas corpus application brought by the mother of Emmanuel Jay Jay Banda reached the Lusaka High Court on Wednesday 11 November without the man it concerns. Judge Geoffrey Mulenga had directed on Tuesday that the State produce the former Petauke Central MP at 09:00 the next morning to explain the circumstances of his apprehension and detention, News Diggers reported on Tuesday evening. On Wednesday the State did not do so, and the judge ordered the Attorney General, Mulilo Kabesha, to produce a medical report on Mr Banda's condition instead, News Diggers and The Zambian Observer reported.

The State's explanation, as reported by both outlets, is that the doctor attending to Mr Banda at Maina Soko Military Medical Centre had not authorised his movement. News Diggers, quoting a defence lawyer, reported that when the case came up in chambers Mr Kabesha applied for the proceedings to be held at Maina Soko itself, and that the court is expected to rule on Friday, after reading the medical report, on whether the hearing should move there. The Observer said the judge wanted the report in order to understand why Mr Banda remained absent.

A month without access

The application was filed on 30 October by Grace Nampokolwe Banda, who is asking for a writ of habeas corpus ad subjiciendum over her son's continued detention, News Diggers reported. In her affidavit she said she had made repeated attempts to establish his whereabouts and status without success, that her lawyers had been instructed to approach the Inspector General of Police, and that she believed he was being held at an undisclosed location, raising concerns about his welfare and the lawfulness of the detention.

The Daily Nation, previewing Wednesday's hearing, reported that Mr Banda was reportedly captured in Mozambique by State agents and had been at Maina Soko for more than a month, and that his mother told the court she had not had access to him since he was brought back to Zambia on 14 October and that the State had declined to disclose his whereabouts or condition. Police confirmed on 16 October that he had been apprehended and was in the country awaiting formal arrest and charging, News Diggers reported; none of this week's reports records a charge having been laid since.

A habeas corpus application asks a court to compel those holding a person to bring him before it and justify the detention. Wednesday's order does not decide the lawfulness of Mr Banda's detention; it only asks the State to show, in writing, why he cannot be brought to court. Friday's ruling will decide whether the court goes to him.

Confirmed vs. alleged

Confirmed: That the State did not produce Mr Banda on Wednesday 11 November, citing his doctor; that Judge Mulenga ordered a medical report; and that a ruling on holding the hearing at Maina Soko is expected on Friday — from News Diggers and The Zambian Observer. That the application was filed on 30 October by his mother — from News Diggers.

Alleged or unverified: Mr Banda's actual condition, the reason he is at a military hospital, where and how he was apprehended (the Daily Nation reports Mozambique), and whether any charge has been laid. The State has not been reported giving an account of the apprehension in court.
