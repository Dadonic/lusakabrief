'Vision is freedom': Hichilema launches HH Vision 100,000, a programme to give free spectacles to learners, soldiers and the elderly, saying six million Zambians need them

President Hakainde Hichilema told the Zambia Ophthalmological Society's annual conference, in a virtual address, that 100,000 pairs of spectacles have been procured for Zambians who cannot afford them. He said one in five schoolchildren struggles to see the blackboard and nearly one in three adults has poor vision, and framed the scheme as social justice rather than charity. It was piloted at three Lusaka secondary schools and at Mikango Barracks.

By Lusaka Brief Health Desk • LUSAKA — 8 November 2026 • 2 min read

President Hakainde Hichilema has launched a programme to distribute 100,000 pairs of spectacles free of charge to Zambians who cannot pay for them, the Zambia Daily Mail reported on Saturday 7 November. The scheme, called HH Vision 100,000, is aimed at learners, older people, military personnel, persons with albinism and other vulnerable groups with poor eyesight, according to the Daily Mail and The Zambian Observer. The President announced it in a virtual address to the annual conference of the Zambia Ophthalmological Society in Lusaka, the Daily Mail reported, citing ZNBC.

The figures the President used set out the scale of the problem as he sees it. More than six million Zambians need spectacles, he said; one in five schoolchildren struggles to see the blackboard, and nearly one in three adults has poor vision. He described the initiative as a matter of social justice rather than charity, and said that people who cannot pay will be able to collect free spectacles at their nearest health facility, with schoolchildren and military personnel expected to be among the first to benefit, The Zambian Observer reported.

This programme is about giving back sight, restoring dignity and opening the doors of opportunity for our people.
— President Hakainde Hichilema, in a virtual address to the Zambia Ophthalmological Society conference, as reported by the Zambia Daily Mail on Saturday 7 November

Where it was tried first

The programme was piloted in recent months at New Jack Secondary School in Chawama, Kabanana Secondary School in Mandevu and Chitanda Secondary School in Matero, and at a military base in Mikango, the Daily Mail reported. The Zambian Observer added Mikango Barracks, Mount Eugenia and the Shimabala area to the list of pilot sites. The President's argument for starting with learners and soldiers, in words the Daily Mail quoted, was that when learners see better they learn better, and when soldiers see better they serve better.

The launch lands in the week the government set out its 2027 spending plans. The budget presented on Friday 6 November allocates K30.3 billion to health and provides for the recruitment of 3,125 frontline health workers, according to News Diggers and Mpelembe Media. Neither of the reports on the spectacles programme said what the 100,000 pairs cost, who supplied them, how the first beneficiaries will be identified beyond the pilot sites, or over what period the distribution is to run.

HH Vision 100,000, as reported

  • 100,000 pairs of spectacles procured for people who cannot afford them (Zambia Daily Mail; The Zambian Observer)
  • Target groups: learners, older people, military personnel, persons with albinism and other vulnerable groups
  • Pilot sites: New Jack, Kabanana and Chitanda secondary schools in Lusaka; Mikango Barracks; Mount Eugenia; Shimabala
  • Access: free spectacles at the nearest health facility for those unable to pay (The Zambian Observer)
  • Not reported: cost, supplier, timetable and how eligibility will be assessed
