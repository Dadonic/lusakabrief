Algeria go three clear while Zambia fly south: the Chipolopolo are in Bujumbura for Tuesday's return against Burundi, with Kings Kangwa back and second place to defend

The senior side landed in Bujumbura at 12:45 on Saturday 14 November and are based at the Hotel Club du Lac Tanganyika ahead of Tuesday's Group I qualifier against Burundi. That evening Algeria beat Togo 1–0 in Tizi Ouzou through Ramy Bensebaini's 76th-minute header, moving to seven points; Zambia have four, Togo three and Burundi two. George Lwandamina says Kings Kangwa, back from a four-match suspension, is a player who can turn a game on his own.

By Lusaka Brief Sports Desk • BUJUMBURA — 16 November 2026 • 2 min read

The day after the 1–1 draw in Ndola, the Chipolopolo were in Burundi. FAZ Media, in a report carried by ZamFoot, said the squad landed at Melchior Ndadaye International Airport in Bujumbura at 12:45 local time on Saturday 14 November and settled into the Hotel Club du Lac Tanganyika, where they will prepare for Tuesday's Group I qualifier for the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations. The report did not give the kick-off time or the venue.

The group moved on without them on Saturday evening. Pan Africa Football reported that Algeria beat Togo 1–0 at the Stade Hocine-Aït-Ahmed in Tizi Ouzou, Ramy Bensebaini heading in Anis Hadj Moussa's cross in the 76th minute. That takes Algeria to seven points from three games, with Zambia on four, Togo on three and Burundi on two, according to the standings published by ZamFoot after the Ndola match and by Pan Africa Football after Saturday's game. Togo host Algeria in Lomé on Tuesday, the same day Zambia play in Bujumbura.

Kangwa back

The main change to George Lwandamina's options is the return of Kings Kangwa, who has completed the four-match ban he received for a red card in Zambia's 3–0 defeat by Morocco at the 2025 Africa Cup of Nations. ZamFoot quoted the coach after Friday's draw: "He's a seasoned player. I'm sure he's going to add value to the team," Lwandamina said of Kangwa, adding: "He's a player who can turn around the game individually. He has the brains." He also pointed to players who missed the first leg and said he expected them to make a difference in the return, without naming them.

Of the draw itself, in which Edward Chilufya scored in the second half before Jean Claude Girumugisha equalised for Burundi in the 82nd minute, Lwandamina was brief. "It was just a bad day. I'm sure the boys will rise," he told ZamFoot, saying he was disappointed to drop two points at home but confident of a response. The two points dropped in Ndola leave Zambia needing a result away to stay clear of Togo.

The arithmetic is simple enough. A win in Bujumbura would take Zambia to seven points and keep them level with Algeria if Algeria lose in Lomé, or three behind if Algeria win; a draw would leave Zambia on five, and a Togo win over Algeria in Lomé the same day would lift Togo to six and into second place. With the two sides meeting twice in four days, this is the half of the double-header that decides whether Friday's draw was a stumble or the start of a slide.
