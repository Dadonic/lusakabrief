Politics · Term limits

The lawyers come in: LAZ says it will litigate if need be to keep the two-term limit, as a youth consortium sets a march on Parliament for 25 November and M'membe cries foul

News Diggers reports that Law Association of Zambia president Arnold Kaluba has pledged to defend Article 106's two-term limit, by litigation if necessary, and welcomed the Justice Minister's assurance that the Executive will not interfere with the petitions before Parliament. The same day the Zambian Observer carried the Zambia Youth Organisation Consortium's plan for a march from Northmead to Parliament on 25 November, and Fred M'membe's reply calling it neither spontaneous nor organic.

By Lusaka Brief Politics Desk • LUSAKA — 17 November 2026 • 3 min read

The Law Association of Zambia has taken a position on presidential term limits, two weeks after its members and the Socialist Party asked it to. News Diggers reported on Tuesday 17 November that LAZ president Arnold Kaluba said the association would defend the two-term limit in Article 106 of the Constitution and would take legal action if necessary to stop any attempt to remove it. Kaluba urged members of Parliament to look beyond immediate political circumstances and to weigh the long-term effects of removing the limit on democracy, accountability and stability. He also welcomed Justice Minister Jack Mwiimbu's statement last week that the Executive will not interfere with Parliament's handling of the petitions seeking its removal. The Diggers report is behind a paywall, and the full text of the statement was not available.

The statement answers two requests made at the start of the month. On 2 November, the Zambia Monitor reported, Socialist Party president Fred M'membe wrote to LAZ asking it to convene an extraordinary general meeting on any amendment to Article 106 and to state its position within seven days. On 3 November, the Zambian Observer reported, three members, Linda Kasonde, the Chapter One Foundation executive director and a former LAZ president, Josiah Kalala and Henry Mulenga, lodged a formal requisition under the association's rules for an extraordinary meeting to adopt resolutions reaffirming a maximum of two five-year terms, opposing any extension and any application of a change to a sitting president, and authorising advocacy and litigation. The requisition recalled LAZ's part in the Oasis Forum that opposed a third term for Frederick Chiluba in 2001.

A march for removal, and its critics

On the other side of the argument, the Zambian Observer on Monday 16 November carried a report, credited to The People's Brief, that the Zambia Youth Organisation Consortium plans a march from Northmead bus station to the Parliament Buildings on Wednesday 25 November in support of removing the limit. The consortium's vice-chairperson, Boas Kanyama, said 63,151 young people from all ten provinces were expected, that the police had been formally notified and that about 500 officers were expected to provide security. The numbers are the consortium's own and no other outlet had reported the plan by Tuesday morning. "As a consortium, we will not be reduced to a political party," Kanyama was quoted as saying, adding that the march was about Zambians exercising a democratic right rather than about a third term.

The consortium is the body that presented a petition to Parliament on 28 October through nominated MP Francis Kope, according to the Zambia Monitor; it had first announced its request for a private member's motion on Article 106(3) in September. M'membe responded to the march announcement in an article published by the Zambian Observer the same evening. He called the plan "neither spontaneous nor organic" and a "calculated, state-sponsored assault on Zambia's constitutional order", asked who was funding it, and said young people should not be used as "cheap foot soldiers". He contrasted the police escort on offer with what he said were the difficulties opposition parties and civil society face in obtaining permits, repeated his call for a referendum on any change, and demanded that President Hakainde Hichilema "state clearly and publicly that he will neither seek nor accept a third term".

Where the government stands

The government's position has not changed. Mwiimbu has said the petitions are a matter for Parliament and that the Executive will not interfere. State House, through Chief Communications Specialist Clayson Hamasaka, has said that the President neither initiated nor influenced the campaign: "This has nothing to do with the head of State, so why should he be dragged into it?" Opposition leader Given Lubinda has called for a national referendum. Wynter Kabimba of the Economic Front, in remarks reported by News Diggers on Monday, said removing the limit would be a bad idea and could split the UPND itself.

What happens next depends on the National Assembly, which has yet to say how or when the petitions will be dealt with, and on LAZ, which has not said what form any litigation would take or at what stage it would be filed.
