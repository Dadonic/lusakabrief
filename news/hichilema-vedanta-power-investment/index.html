Hichilema presses Vedanta to fast-track power investment as copper ambitions outrun the grid

The president has urged Vedanta Resources to accelerate its power-generation plans, tying the miner's expansion to Zambia's electricity future — as fresh GreenCo funding signals the private build-out is moving.

By Natasha Banda • 15 September 2026 • 5

President Hakainde Hichilema has urged Vedanta Resources Holdings to accelerate its planned investment in power generation, directly tying one of the country's largest mining houses to the electricity build-out that Zambia's copper ambitions depend on.

The push follows the pattern we mapped in our grid analysis: tripling copper output needs roughly 2,000 new megawatts, and government is now visibly pressing every major power consumer to become a power producer. Mines are being told, politely and then less politely, that their expansion plans must come with generation attached.

The money is moving too

Africa GreenCo — the private power trader positioning itself between producers and the region's buyers — announced an additional US$11.5 million investment from PIDG and IFDK this week, while the Ministry of Mines took its critical-minerals pitch to investors, arguing the environment for extraction capital has never been more favourable.

"The electricity system is not adjacent to the minerals strategy — it is the minerals strategy. State House is now saying so out loud."

The metric that matters: megawatts financed and under construction, not megawatts announced. Lusaka Brief will keep the ledger.
