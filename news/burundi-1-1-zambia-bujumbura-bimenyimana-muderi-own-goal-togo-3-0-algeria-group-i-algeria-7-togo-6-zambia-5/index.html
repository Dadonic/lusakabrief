An own goal rescues a point in Bujumbura, but Togo's 3–0 over Algeria drops Zambia to third: Group I now reads Algeria 7, Togo 6, Zambia 5 with two rounds left

Burundi led through substitute Bonfils-Caleb Bimenyimana late in the second half at Stade Intwari on Tuesday 17 November, a goal Zambia argued was offside, before Akbar Muderi turned Zephania Phiri's cross into his own net in the 85th minute for 1–1. The point was worth less than it looked: at the Stade de Kégué, Togo beat Algeria 3–0 with all three goals before half-time, two of them from Thibault Klidjé, and leapfrogged Zambia into second place.

By Lusaka Brief Sports Desk • BUJUMBURA — 18 November 2026 • 2 min read

Zambia went to Bujumbura to protect second place in Group I and came home with a draw and third. The return match against Burundi at Stade Intwari on Tuesday 17 November finished 1–1, the same score as in Ndola four days earlier, and the result that mattered more came from Togo, where the hosts beat Algeria 3–0.

The first half was goalless. ZamFoot's report records Klings Kangwa, restored to the side in place of Derrick Bulaya in the only change from Ndola, going close from distance in the 20th minute, and Albert Kangwanda twice threatening, curling wide in the 54th minute and then blazing over from close range three minutes later after Mathews Banda had crossed. Zambia changed their forwards in the 66th minute, sending on Chanka Zimba and Joshua Mutale for Fashion Sakala and Edward Chilufya; Banda went off injured in the 72nd and was replaced by Zephania Phiri.

The goals

Burundi scored first, through substitute Bonfils-Caleb Bimenyimana, in the 78th minute according to ZamFoot and the 77th according to AfricaSoccer. The Zambian bench protested that the striker was offside, and ZamFoot's live commentary said the goal should not have stood. The equaliser came in the 85th minute: Phiri's cross was turned into his own net by Burundi defender Akbar Muderi. Neither outlet carried post-match comments from either coach.

The draw leaves Zambia on five points from four matches, with Burundi on three. What changed the table was Togo's result at the Stade de Kégué. Pan-Africa Football reports that Thibault Klidjé scored twice and Kennedy Boateng once, all before half-time, for Togo's first win over Algeria in the group after losing the reverse fixture 1–0. It was Algeria's first defeat of the campaign. After four rounds the standings are Algeria 7, Togo 6, Zambia 5, Burundi 3.

Group I after round four

  • Algeria 7 points
  • Togo 6
  • Zambia 5
  • Burundi 3
  • Burundi 1–1 Zambia (Bimenyimana; Muderi og 85); Togo 3–0 Algeria (Klidjé 2, Boateng)

What is left

Two rounds remain. The reports read for this edition do not give the dates of the final fixtures, and the Confederation of African Football's qualification rules for the 2027 tournament were not set out in them, so this page does not say how many teams from the group go through. What the table does say is that Zambia, who began the window second on three points, have taken two from six against the group's bottom side and are now the ones being chased rather than chasing. They will also do it without their captain: Fashion Sakala announced his international retirement within hours of the final whistle, as reported separately in this edition.
