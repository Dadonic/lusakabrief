'You are wasting your time': Kalaba takes the EU observers' report and tells them to find a way to enforce it, as the church monitors say their own findings match the mission's

The EU Election Observation Mission has begun handing its final report to the parties. Citizens First president Harry Kalaba received it in Lusaka and, Zambia Monitor reported on Friday 6 November, agreed with its findings on ZNBC airtime, the campaign timetable and police conduct. The Zambian Observer, citing Zambian Watch, reported that he also told the observers they were wasting their time without an enforcement mechanism. The Christian Churches Monitoring Group told News Diggers its conclusions were similar to the EU's.

By Lusaka Brief Politics Desk • LUSAKA — 7 November 2026 • 2 min read

Two days after the government said the EU observers could not see what the police could, the mission's final report on the 13 August 2026 general election is being delivered party by party, and the first opposition leader to receive it has accepted it with a condition. Zambia Monitor reported on Friday 6 November that the EU Election Observation Mission handed its report to Citizens First in Lusaka and that the party's president, Harry Kalaba, backed its findings and said the recommendations should be used to strengthen future elections.

What Kalaba agreed with

According to the Monitor, Mr Kalaba singled out the report's finding that ZNBC gave 96.2% of its television airtime to the ruling party, which he said undermined fairness; the ECZ campaign timetable, which the report said created unequal conditions and which he said should be reviewed; and the alleged police bias and cadreism that he said constrained opposition campaigning. He also pointed to the suspension of vote counting and the closure of courts during the election period: "Those incidents raised questions about transparency and access to justice during the election period," the Monitor quoted him as saying. Citizens First, the paper said, plans to engage stakeholders on implementing the recommendations, including turning ZNBC into a genuine public service broadcaster.

And what he said to the observers

The Zambian Observer carried a sharper version of the same meeting, crediting an interview with Zambian Watch. In that account Mr Kalaba told the mission that observers had been producing reports and recommendations on Zambia's elections since 1996 and that many of the same concerns persisted; that "election observation should not end with the publication of reports"; and that the observers "were wasting their time if they could not find practical ways of ensuring that their recommendations are enforced". The Observer's report quoted him warning that "without practical enforcement mechanisms, election observation reports risk becoming routine documents".

The church monitors

News Diggers reported on Friday that the Christian Churches Monitoring Group, which fielded its own observers, says its findings are similar to the EU's. The group's programme director, Peter Mwanangombe, told the paper in an interview on Wednesday that CCMG agreed with the EU's concerns about shrinking democratic space, restrictions on political activity and human rights around the election. The remainder of his remarks was behind a paywall.

The government's position, as News Diggers restated it, is that the EU does not have access to the intelligence, security information and law-enforcement investigations available to sovereign state institutions. Lusaka Brief reported on Wednesday 4 November that the final report made 13 recommendations and that the Chief Government Spokesperson had replied that observers cannot see what the police can. No government response to Mr Kalaba's or CCMG's remarks had been published on the pages read for this edition.
