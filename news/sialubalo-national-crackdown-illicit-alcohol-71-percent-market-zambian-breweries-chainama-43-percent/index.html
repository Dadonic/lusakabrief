Seventy-one per cent of the market: Sialubalo orders a national crackdown on illicit alcohol, as Zambian Breweries links 43 per cent of Chainama admissions to illicit drink

Local Government Minister Gift Sialubalo has directed enforcement agencies to step up inspections of unlicensed liquor outlets, dismantle illegal manufacturing and distribution networks and tighten border surveillance, telling a liquor enforcement conference in Lusaka on Wednesday 18 November that illicit alcohol is estimated at 71 per cent of Zambia's market, Zambia Monitor and the Daily Nation report. Zambian Breweries' managing director said 43 per cent of psychiatric admissions at Chainama Hills Hospital were linked to high-strength illicit alcohol.

By Lusaka Brief Health Desk • LUSAKA — 20 November 2026 • 2 min read

The Government has ordered a national crackdown on illicit alcohol, which it says makes up almost three-quarters of everything Zambians drink. Gift Sialubalo, the Minister of Local Government and Rural Development, issued the directive at the National Joint Stakeholders Liquor Enforcement Conference in Lusaka on Wednesday 18 November, Zambia Monitor reported that afternoon. He told enforcement agencies to step up inspections of unlicensed liquor outlets, dismantle illegal manufacturing and distribution networks and tighten surveillance at the borders. The Daily Nation reported the minister's pledge of tougher enforcement on Wednesday with the same estimate: that illicit alcohol accounts for 71 per cent of the country's market.

Sialubalo said the illicit trade threatens public health, undermines legitimate businesses and deprives the Treasury of tax revenue. The Government's plan, as both outlets describe it, is to strengthen liquor licensing, improve compliance monitoring and run joint enforcement operations, which the minister said would protect consumers, broaden the tax base, improve revenue collection and support the Grow Zambia Agenda. He said the fight required a coordinated response involving local authorities and other agencies. Neither report says who produced the 71 per cent estimate or how it was arrived at.

The industry's figures

The sharpest numbers of the day came from the industry. Thais Cavinatto, managing director of Zambian Breweries, told the conference that consumption of illicit alcohol had risen by more than 22 percentage points over the past five years, Zambia Monitor reported, and warned that alcohol made without quality controls or safety standards carries significant health risks. She cited a figure that 43 per cent of patients admitted to psychiatric units at Chainama Hills Hospital were there because of harmful consumption of high-strength illicit alcohol, and said the trade threatens jobs, discourages investment and denies the Government billions of kwacha in tax. Those figures are the company's; Zambia Monitor's report does not say where they come from, and Chainama Hills Hospital has not been reported as commenting.

Lusaka's Town Clerk, Oliver Muuka, urged manufacturers and distributors to comply with the law and with quality standards for producing and selling alcoholic drinks, describing compliance as both a legal and a corporate responsibility. The 71 per cent figure is an estimate, and neither report explains how it was reached. No trader, brewer of traditional spirits or consumer group was reported as responding to the directive on Wednesday.

Sources: Zambia Monitor, “Govt orders national crackdown on illicit alcohol trade” (18 Nov 2026); Daily Nation, “Illicit booze controls 71pc of country's market – Minister” (18 Nov 2026).
