'The signature begins the partnership, the results will give it meaning': Zambia and Malawi agree to stop testing the same goods twice, with a mutual recognition deal the next step

The Malawi Bureau of Standards and Zambia's three quality-infrastructure agencies signed a memorandum of understanding in Lusaka on Tuesday 17 November to cut the technical barriers that make traders certify products on both sides of the border. Trade Minister Caroline Katotobwe called it a milestone and asked for a results-oriented action plan; the Malawian side pressed for the mutual recognition arrangements that would let each country accept the other's test results. The World Bank-funded Southern Africa Trade and Connectivity Project backs the work.

By Lusaka Brief Business Desk • LUSAKA — 18 November 2026 • 2 min read

Zambia and Malawi have put their standards bodies to work on one of the quieter obstacles to trade between them: the need to have the same product tested, certified and measured twice, once in each country. At a ceremony in Lusaka on Tuesday 17 November the Malawi Bureau of Standards signed a memorandum of understanding with the Zambia Bureau of Standards, the Zambia Compulsory Standards Agency and the Zambia Metrology Agency, Zambia Monitor and the Malawi Broadcasting Corporation reported.

The agreement is meant to reduce what trade officials call technical barriers, the differences in standards, testing, certification and measurement rules that keep goods out of a market without any tariff being charged. The aim, as described by both outlets, is for products from either country to enter the other with fewer unnecessary restrictions while quality, safety and consumer protection are maintained. MBC says the document covers laboratory testing competence, metrological traceability and quality management systems, the three gaps identified in an analysis the two sides carried out on 19 December 2024.

What the minister asked for

Zambia's Minister of Trade and Exports, Caroline Katotobwe, described the agreement as an important milestone in trade relations between the two countries and said farmers, manufacturers, exporters and small businesses stand to gain from lower compliance costs and wider market access. She said duplicated testing and certification must be dealt with as the non-tariff barriers they are, asked the agencies for a results-oriented action plan that identifies priority products and sectors, and placed the deal in the context of integration under SADC, COMESA and the African Continental Free Trade Area, whose Annex 6 deals with technical barriers to trade.

Gerald Chizinga, executive director of the Zambia Compulsory Standards Agency, said an action plan would turn the agreement into specific activities with clear responsibilities, achievable targets and regular technical reviews. Humphrey Nkobeni, executive director of the Zambia Metrology Agency, said success would be visible in better services, clearer compliance guidance and stronger technical capacity.

The signature begins the partnership, the results will give it meaning,
— Humphrey Nkobeni, executive director, Zambia Metrology Agency, at the signing in Lusaka on 17 November, as reported by Zambia Monitor

Malawi's priority

For the Malawian side the next step is a mutual recognition arrangement, under which a product tested and certified in one country is accepted in the other without a second round. Zambia Monitor reports that the MBS director general, Symon Mandala, urged faster finalisation of those arrangements and said the cooperation should strengthen consumer protection and regional integration; MBC says his remarks were delivered on his behalf by Stephen Chalimba, the bureau's director of metrology services, who described the memorandum as the framework for building such an arrangement.

MBC adds that the initiative is supported by the World Bank-funded Southern Africa Trade and Connectivity Project, which works to lower trade costs along the Nacala and Beira corridors. Neither outlet gave a figure for the trade affected or a timetable for the mutual recognition arrangement; the agreement itself sets a direction rather than a deadline.

Sources: Zambia Monitor, “Zambia, Malawi sign pact, target technical barriers to cross-border trade” (17 Nov 2026); Malawi Broadcasting Corporation, “Malawi, Zambia sign standards agreement to ease cross-border trade” (17 Nov 2026).
