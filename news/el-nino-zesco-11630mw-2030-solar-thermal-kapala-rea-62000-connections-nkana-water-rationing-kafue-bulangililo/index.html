Planning for a dry season: ZESCO maps 11,630MW by 2030 with hydro down to a third, Kapala tells the REA to connect paid-up customers, and Nkana Water warns Kitwe of rationing

With an El Niño dry spell forecast for the 2026/27 rains, three strands of the power-and-water story came together this week. ZESCO presented targets to more than double installed capacity from 5,220MW to 11,630MW by 2030, with solar rising to 4,126MW; the Electricity Minister, on his first visit to the Rural Electrification Authority, said paid-up customers must be connected without delay; and Nkana Water cut output at Bulangililo by 18% because the Kafue River is falling.

By Lusaka Brief Business Desk • LUSAKA — 12 November 2026 • 3 min read

ZESCO says it is reshaping Zambia's generation mix so that another drought does not bring back the 20-hour power cuts of 2024. At a presentation reported by The Zambian Observer on Wednesday 11 November, the utility's Director of Transmission Operations and Trade, Lioko Sitali, set out targets to raise installed capacity from 5,220 megawatts today to 11,630MW by 2030. Solar would grow from a 2021 baseline of 88MW to 4,126MW, thermal from 515MW to 3,234MW, hydro from about 2,700MW to 3,970MW, and the first 200MW of wind would be added, with 100MW from biomass and other sources. Hydro's share of generation would fall from 82% in 2021 to about 34%, with solar at roughly 35%.

The Observer's account recalled how the last dry year unfolded: load shedding began at eight hours a day in March 2024, rose to twelve in May and eventually to between 19 and 20 hours, before new solar, thermal and independent projects allowed supply to stabilise in late 2025 and ZESCO to end nationwide load shedding in December 2025. The managing director, Justin Loongo, said the utility was assessing renewable technologies and financing models and that private participation and regional trading would be needed. The Electricity Minister, Peter Kapala, welcomed the projections and said he hoped the country would exceed the government's 10,000MW target and reach universal access, including rural areas, by 2030.

The last mile

Mr Kapala took the access argument to the Rural Electrification Authority on a familiarisation visit reported by the Daily Nation on Wednesday. He told the authority to speed up connections, above all for customers who have already paid, saying delays undermine the government's connection subsidy, and asked it to open offices in every province. ZESCO, he said, should concentrate on generation and transmission while the REA leads rural electrification. The REA's chief executive, Alex Mbumba, said the authority had made more than 62,000 new connections this year against a target of 100,000, hoped to pass 75,000 by the end of the year, and was expanding mini-grids and solar home systems.

Those who have already paid for the service should be connected without unnecessary delays.
— Peter Kapala, Minister of Electricity, during a familiarisation visit to the Rural Electrification Authority, as quoted by the Daily Nation

Water first

The drought risk is already showing in Kitwe's taps. News Diggers reported on Wednesday that Nkana Water and Sanitation Company may introduce rationing because the Kafue River, one of its main raw-water sources, is falling. The company presented a report on river levels to the Copperbelt Minister, Emmanuel Kasambo, on Monday, attributing the decline to El Niño, and said it had cut production at the Bulangililo treatment plant by 18%, from 95,000 to 78,000 cubic metres a day, to prevent cavitation in its pumps. Its corporate affairs manager, Mwaka Nakweti, said in a statement that rationing would be used to share what is available fairly. No rationing schedule had been published by Wednesday.

Zambia Monitor reported last week that the regional transmission line to Tanzania and Kenya, the other half of ZESCO's drought insurance, is due for commissioning on the Zambian side in the second quarter of 2029: 613km of line from Pensulo to Nakonde via Mpika and Kasama, financed by US$292 million in grants from the World Bank, the European Union and the United Kingdom, allowing up to 500MW to be traded.
