1,247 in Kitwe and 311 in Ndola: the Copperbelt minister orders 1,558 'excess' teachers out to rural schools, as Musokotwane says there will be no teacher recruitment in 2027

Emmanuel Kasambo says urban schools on the Copperbelt are carrying 1,558 more teachers than their establishments allow while rural classrooms go without, and has given provincial officials a week to complete the staffing count that will move them. On ZNBC's Sunday Interview the Finance Minister said the government would recruit no teachers next year until it knows where the real gaps are.

By Lusaka Brief Politics Desk • NDOLA — 11 November 2026 • 3 min read

The Copperbelt Province Minister, Emmanuel Kasambo, has ordered 1,558 teachers in Kitwe and Ndola to be moved to rural schools, saying they are surplus to the establishments of the urban schools where they draw their salaries while schools in outlying districts go short, The Zambian Observer reported on Tuesday 10 November. Speaking in Ndola after meeting representatives of teachers' unions, he gave the breakdown as 1,247 excess teachers in Kitwe, 699 of them in primary schools and 548 in secondary schools, and 311 in Ndola, 159 primary and 152 secondary, a figure he said would be revisited.

News Diggers reported on Monday that Mr Kasambo had first issued the directive at the Nsengele Kununka traditional ceremony of the Bulima people in Mpongwe District on Sunday 8 November, where he told the Provincial Permanent Secretary, the Provincial Education Officer and the District Education Board Secretaries that they had one week to complete a staffing assessment and provide the information needed for the exercise. Teachers who refuse a lawful transfer face disciplinary action, that report said; the Observer's account of his Ndola remarks was blunter, quoting him as saying that those who refuse rural postings will be dismissed.

Mr Kasambo also warned education board secretaries against facilitating transfers from rural to urban schools, saying some teachers arrive in town with letters from Lusaka after only a few months in a rural district, in breach of a Cabinet decision that requires four years of service before a transfer is considered. Officials who arrange such moves would face action, he said, according to The Zambian Observer.

'No plan to recruit teachers'

The provincial order lands in the same week as a national one. The Finance and National Planning Minister, Dr Situmbeko Musokotwane, told ZNBC's Sunday Interview that the government has no plans to recruit teachers in 2027, News Diggers, The Zambian Observer and the Times of Zambia all reported. His reason was the same as Mr Kasambo's: the government cannot know the true shortage until teachers are where their records say they are.

No, for next year there is no plan to recruit teachers.
— Dr Situmbeko Musokotwane, Minister of Finance and National Planning, on ZNBC's Sunday Interview, as quoted by News Diggers on Tuesday 10 November

Dr Musokotwane gave examples, as reported by the Times of Zambia: schools with an establishment of five or six teachers where only two or three report for duty, and schools with an establishment of six or seven that have 20 or 25. He called it a longstanding administrative problem, said teachers should return to their designated stations so that genuine gaps can be identified, and said deployment is one of the functions that ought to be decentralised to district level, The Zambian Observer reported.

None of Tuesday's reports carried a response from the teachers' unions to the Copperbelt directive or to the recruitment freeze, and neither minister said how long the counting exercise would take nationally or what would happen to teachers found to be at the wrong school outside the Copperbelt.

The Copperbelt numbers, as given by Mr Kasambo

  • Kitwe — 1,247 excess teachers: 699 primary, 548 secondary.
  • Ndola — 311 excess teachers: 159 primary, 152 secondary; to be revisited.
  • Deadline — one week for provincial and district officials to complete a staffing assessment (News Diggers, 9 November).
  • Transfer rule — four years' service before a transfer is considered, under a Cabinet decision he cited.
