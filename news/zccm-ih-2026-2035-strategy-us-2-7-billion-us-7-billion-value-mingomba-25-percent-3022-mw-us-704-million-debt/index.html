'A commitment, not a wish list': ZCCM-IH plans US$2.7 billion over ten years to become a US$7 billion company, with 3,022 MW of power, more of Mingomba and US$704 million of debt

At its Shareholder Day in Lusaka on Monday 16 November, the State mining investor unveiled a 2026–2035 plan to lift its value from about US$1.2 billion to US$7 billion and annual revenue from US$211 million to US$738 million. It intends to raise its Mingomba stake from 20 to 25 per cent, build a 3,022 MW generation portfolio, open gold and manganese buying centres and ring-fence exploration money. Funding is planned, not secured: US$704 million of debt, US$300 million of equity and US$1–1.7 billion from partners, after a K4.92 billion group loss in 2025.

By Lusaka Brief Business Desk • LUSAKA — 18 November 2026 • 4 min read

ZCCM Investments Holdings, the State's mining investment company, has set itself a decade-long target that would make it roughly six times more valuable than it is today. The 2026–2035 strategy, presented at the company's Shareholder Day in Lusaka on Monday 16 November by the Permanent Secretary in the Ministry of Mines and Minerals Development, Hapenga Kabeta, calls for US$2.7 billion of investment and a rise in company value from about US$1.2 billion to US$7 billion, Zambia Monitor reported on Tuesday. Annual revenue is to grow about three and a half times, from roughly US$211 million to US$738 million, and the company says it will pay a dividend every year of the plan, with US$2.45 billion in royalties and dividends expected over the period.

Chief executive Kakenenwa Muyangwa framed the plan as the second act after three years of rebuilding. He told the gathering that the company had shown it could deliver and that the next ten years were about scale, that Zambia must retain more of the value of the minerals it produces rather than export raw value and import finished value, and that the strategy was a commitment rather than a wish list.

We cannot continue to export raw value and import finished value.
— Kakenenwa Muyangwa, chief executive, ZCCM-IH, at the company's Shareholder Day in Lusaka on 16 November, as reported by Zambia Monitor

Four pillars, three phases

The money is to be spent in three stages: about US$750 million in a foundation phase to 2028, US$1.2 billion in a growth phase from 2029 to 2031, and US$736 million in an optimisation phase to 2035. Under what the company calls its 'earn' pillar it will lean on the Kansanshi royalty, agreed with First Quantum Minerals in 2023 and worth 3.1 per cent of the mine's revenue in place of dividends on a 20 per cent stake, which it expects to bring in more than US$990 million over the decade. It wants similar royalty arrangements with other investee companies, intends to market its share of their copper, and will raise its holding in the KoBold Metals-led Mingomba project from 20 to 25 per cent. Africa Briefing, citing Reuters, notes that Mingomba is expected to need more than US$2 billion and to start producing in the early 2030s, after the government's 2031 deadline for three million tonnes of national copper output.

The 'power' pillar is a 3,022 MW generation portfolio by 2035, overseen by a new energy holding company, including the Ever Great project, a large solar scheme and 312 MW of solar across constituencies under the Presidential Constituency Energy Initiative, alongside continued dividends from Copperbelt Energy Corporation and Maamba Energy. Under 'capture' the company will move into trading: gold and manganese buying centres with traceability systems first, then a stockyard and copper and energy trading, aiming by 2035 at a fifth of the country's formal gold trade, 35 per cent of its manganese trade and services for more than 30,000 artisanal miners, plus phosphate-to-fertiliser processing with Sino Great. 'Discover' is a ring-fenced, multi-year exploration budget with a five-gate decision framework and joint ventures to share risk.

The numbers

  • US$2.7 billion of investment, 2026–2035, in three phases (US$750m, US$1.2bn, US$736m)
  • Company value US$1.2bn to US$7bn; revenue US$211m to US$738m a year
  • Kansanshi royalty: more than US$990m expected over the decade
  • Mingomba stake 20% to 25%; power portfolio 3,022 MW by 2035
  • Planned funding: US$704.2m debt, US$300m equity, US$1–1.7bn from partners
  • 2025: group loss K4.92bn; investment income up 40% to K3.4bn; dividend K183m

Where the money comes from

The plan's own documents are candid that the financing is not yet in hand. Ecofin Agency, which read them, reports planned corporate borrowing of US$704.2 million, US$300 million of new equity and between US$1 billion and US$1.7 billion from joint-venture partners and development finance institutions, described by the company as planned sources rather than secured commitments, with ten-year cash inflows of US$6.04 billion against outflows of US$5.72 billion. Muyangwa told Reuters, according to Africa Briefing, that the company expects to borrow about US$700 million and that partners could put in up to US$1.7 billion.

The plan arrives after a difficult year in the accounts. ZCCM-IH's 2025 results showed a consolidated loss of K4.92 billion against a K39.85 billion profit in 2024, which the company attributes to the absence of the large one-off Mopani gain booked the year before, exchange-rate movements, changes in the value of its investments and a provision linked to the Trafigura arbitration; investment income nonetheless rose 40 per cent to K3.4 billion on the Kansanshi royalty and power dividends, and a K183 million dividend was paid. The company also plans a listing optimisation project to narrow the gap between its share price and net asset value, lift its free float towards 25 per cent and restore trading in London.

The strategy is pitched as a contribution to the Grow Zambia targets of three million tonnes of copper a year and 10,000 MW of generation by 2031. Kabeta, speaking separately to a Swedish delegation on Monday, said copper output this year is expected to exceed 900,000 tonnes, which the government describes as a record since independence, and that the local content law in force since January requires foreign suppliers to the mines to partner with Zambian firms, News Diggers reported.
