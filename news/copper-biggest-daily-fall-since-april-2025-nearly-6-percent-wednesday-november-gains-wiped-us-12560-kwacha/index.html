Copper's worst day since April 2025: a fall of nearly 6 per cent on Wednesday wipes out the metal's November gains and leaves the price close to where the year began

Copper lost close to 6 per cent on Wednesday 18 November, its steepest one-day fall since April 2025, before steadying on Thursday, according to bank commentary carried by Zambia Monitor and an analysis by the broker XTB. The metal is down roughly 12 to 13 per cent in November and trades near US$12,560 a tonne, about where it started 2026. For Zambia, whose kwacha slipped to just under K20.50 to the dollar last week, the question is whether September's record highs were the peak.

By Lusaka Brief Business Desk • LUSAKA — 20 November 2026 • 5 min read

Copper, Zambia's principal export and the metal to which its currency is most sensitive, had its worst day in more than 18 months on Wednesday 18 November. The price fell by close to 6 per cent before steadying, a move that Zambia Monitor, citing market commentary from Access Bank Group, described on Thursday as the biggest single-day drop since April 2025. The Polish broker XTB, in a note published on Thursday morning by its analyst Michał Jóźwiak, put the fall at nearly 6 per cent and called it the deepest one-day decline since April 2025, the month XTB associates with the US "Liberation Day" tariff shock.

The two accounts agree on the scale of the damage to November. Zambia Monitor's report puts the month's losses at about 12 per cent; XTB says the move since the start of the month has exceeded 13 per cent. Both say the price has given back most of what it gained earlier in the year. XTB quotes a price of about US$5.66 a pound, the lowest in seven months, and a price near US$12,560 a tonne, close to where copper began 2026. Zambia Monitor's report gives no dollar figure, but says the decline has pushed the price back towards the levels recorded at the beginning of the year.

Why it fell

Both commentaries trace the slide to the unwinding of a rally built on two expectations. The first was demand: copper had been bid up on forecasts of heavy buying for data centres, artificial-intelligence infrastructure and grid modernisation, and XTB says doubts about the pace of AI spending, together with regulatory worries after the Democratic victory in this month's US mid-term elections, have cooled that story. The second was tariffs. Zambia Monitor's account says speculation about US duties on refined copper had earlier pulled large volumes of metal into the United States, tightening supply elsewhere and helping to drive the rally to record highs in September; investors are now reassessing demand and reducing bullish positions.

A stronger dollar did the rest. Both accounts cite a repricing of the US Federal Reserve's likely path towards tighter policy, which lifted the dollar and made copper dearer for buyers outside the United States. XTB adds two data points from the week: Chinese industrial production data released on Monday 16 November showed growth of 4.8 per cent year on year, below expectations, while Chile's monthly copper output rose to 388,000 tonnes. Both reports also mention the investor Stanley Druckenmiller, a prominent advocate of the AI-driven copper thesis, who is reported to have turned bearish; XTB says he has disclosed a short position in the metal. Lusaka Brief has not seen that disclosure and reports it as the two outlets do.

The physical market tells a different story

Neither account treats the fall as a collapse in real demand. Zambia Monitor's report says physical market conditions remain relatively tight: inventories in China are described as critically low, and spot copper trades at a significant premium to futures. Among other base metals, aluminium and zinc recovered modestly while tin extended its losses, Zambia Monitor said. XTB's technical reading is that copper is deeply oversold, with its relative strength index below 23 and the price more than 10 per cent under its 50-session moving average, although the longer-term averages still point upwards. The broker's levels to watch are support around US$12,400 a tonne, a further target near US$11,680 if that breaks, and resistance around US$13,360.

What it means in Lusaka

The kwacha was already under pressure before the metal turned. Zambia Monitor reported on Monday 16 November that the currency weakened 0.67 per cent in the week to Friday 13 November, closing just below K20.50 to the dollar, its largest weekly decline since the week ending 16 October, as demand for foreign exchange continued to outweigh supply and the dollar traded close to its strongest level since May 2025. The same report said the market was watching for signs that the rise in US Treasury yields was near its limit, which could bring some consolidation in the dollar.

None of the reports read for this story gives a figure for what a lower copper price would mean for Zambia's export earnings, mining royalties or the 2027 Budget, and no statement from the Bank of Zambia, the Ministry of Finance or the Chamber of Mines on Wednesday's fall had been published by Thursday evening in the outlets Lusaka Brief reviewed. Those are the responses to watch, together with Friday's London close, which will show whether Thursday's stabilisation holds.

The numbers

  • Nearly 6 per cent — copper's fall on Wednesday 18 November, the largest one-day drop since April 2025 (Zambia Monitor, citing Access Bank Group; XTB)
  • About 12 to 13 per cent — the decline since the start of November (Zambia Monitor; XTB)
  • About US$12,560 a tonne and US$5.66 a pound — the price XTB quoted on Thursday, the pound price its lowest in seven months
  • Just under K20.50 — where the kwacha closed against the dollar on Friday 13 November, after a 0.67 per cent weekly loss (Zambia Monitor)
  • September 2026 — when copper reached record highs, according to Zambia Monitor's account of the rally

Confirmed vs. alleged

Confirmed: That copper fell by nearly 6 per cent on Wednesday 18 November, its largest one-day fall since April 2025, and has lost about 12 to 13 per cent in November, is reported by Zambia Monitor (citing Access Bank Group commentary) and by XTB. XTB's price levels of about US$12,560 a tonne and US$5.66 a pound are that broker's figures. The kwacha's close just under K20.50 on 13 November is Zambia Monitor's reporting.

Alleged or unverified: Stanley Druckenmiller's reported short position is second-hand in both accounts and Lusaka Brief has not seen the disclosure. The effect on Zambia's export receipts, royalties and the kwacha this week has not yet been reported by any outlet or official body. XTB's support and resistance levels are an analyst's opinion, not a forecast of where the price will go.
