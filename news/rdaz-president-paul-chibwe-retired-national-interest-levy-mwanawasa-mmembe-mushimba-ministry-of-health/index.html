'I have done nothing wrong apart from speaking for doctors': the RDAZ president is retired in the national interest, and two opposition leaders ask the Ministry of Health why

Dr Paul Chibwe, president of the Resident Doctors Association of Zambia and an orthopaedic and trauma surgeon at Levy Mwanawasa University Teaching Hospital, told News Diggers he was handed a letter retiring him early in the national interest without an explanation. The Socialist Party's Fred M'membe asked the Ministry of Health to reverse the decision, and the OPP's Brian Mushimba said the circumstances should be established. No reason from the ministry had been published on the pages read for this edition.

By Lusaka Brief Health Desk • LUSAKA — 7 November 2026 • 2 min read

The doctor who has spent the past year speaking for Zambia's unemployed and volunteer medical graduates is now out of a job himself. News Diggers reported on Friday 6 November that Dr Paul Chibwe, president of the Resident Doctors Association of Zambia (RDAZ) and an orthopaedics and trauma surgeon at Levy Mwanawasa University Teaching Hospital, had been retired in the national interest. He announced it in a Facebook post on Wednesday and told the paper in an interview on Thursday that he was simply handed a letter informing him of his early retirement, without a detailed explanation.

What he says

Dr Chibwe told News Diggers that he does not know why he was retired and that he has done nothing wrong apart from speaking for doctors. He said he was not aware of any wrongdoing on his part, and argued that if he had committed an offence, disciplinary action would have been taken against him. The rest of the interview was behind the paper's paywall; the visible portion did not name the appointing authority that signed the letter or the legal provision relied on.

Two opposition reactions

The Socialist Party president, Fred M'membe, in a statement published by The Zambian Observer on Friday, said the Ministry of Health under Professor Roma Chilengi had implemented what he called a sudden retirement in the national interest, and urged the ministry to reconsider. His statement said that using administrative retirement against the leadership of professional bodies "creates a chilling effect", described Dr Chibwe as part of "a vital tier of highly specialised young medical personnel that Zambia cannot afford to lose", and placed the decision alongside what it called the mass termination of 400 staff at the Zambia Medicines and Medical Supplies Agency and the redeployment of 123 Ministry of Health headquarters workers. Those two figures come from Mr M'membe's statement and were not reported separately on the pages read.

We urge the Ministry of Health to reconsider its decision regarding Dr. Chibwe.
— Fred M'membe, Socialist Party president, in a statement published by The Zambian Observer on 6 November

The Organised People's Party president, Brian Mushimba, said in remarks carried by the Observer that the circumstances behind the dismissal should be established, warned against retaliation for speaking out, and asked why trained doctors remain unemployed after the public investment in their education: "How can we invest in them so much and leave them on the streets?" He said his party had employed at least 20 unemployed doctors in its secretariat before the August election and that he had helped a former RDAZ president, Dr Brian Sampa, relocate to the United States after his dismissal.

The context is a long argument between the association and the ministry over recruitment. Lusaka Brief reported last month that RDAZ had asked that hiring under the US health pact begin with the doctors already volunteering inside the system, and Friday's budget speech, as reported by The Zambian Observer, provides for 3,125 health workers to be recruited in 2027. Neither the Ministry of Health nor the Health Services Commission had given a reason for Dr Chibwe's retirement on any page read for this edition, and his letter has not been published.
