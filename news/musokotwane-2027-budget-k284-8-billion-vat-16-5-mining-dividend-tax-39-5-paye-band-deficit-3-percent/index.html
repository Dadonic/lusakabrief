K284.8 billion and half a point on VAT: Musokotwane's 2027 budget taxes mining dividends at 15%, adds a 39.5% PAYE band and aims the deficit at 3% of GDP

The Minister of Finance and National Planning, Situmbeko Musokotwane, presented a K284.8 billion budget for 2027 to the National Assembly on Friday 6 November, the largest in Zambia's history and 28.4% of GDP. Domestic revenue is to cover K233.7 billion of it, borrowing K41.9 billion and grants K9.2 billion. VAT rises from 16% to 16.5%, a 15% withholding tax lands on mining company dividends, a new 39.5% PAYE band applies to monthly pay above K37,000, and debt service alone takes K67.6 billion.

By Lusaka Brief Business Desk • LUSAKA — 7 November 2026 • 5 min read

Zambia's 2027 budget is K284.8 billion, up from K253.1 billion this year and equal to 28.4% of GDP, and it is to be paid for largely by Zambians themselves: Zambia Monitor reported on Friday 6 November that the Minister of Finance and National Planning, Situmbeko Musokotwane, told the National Assembly that domestic revenue of K233.7 billion would cover 82.1% of spending, with K41.9 billion of domestic and external borrowing (14.7%) and K9.2 billion of grants making up the rest. The budget's theme is "Grow Zambia: From Stabilisation to Unlocking Opportunities, Creating Jobs and Improving Livelihoods for All", and the Monitor reported that the new tax measures are expected to raise K10.8 billion.

What changes on 1 January

The tax measures, all effective from 1 January 2027 according to Zambia Monitor, start with the headline consumption tax: value added tax rises from 16% to 16.5%. On income, a new 39.5% PAYE band applies to monthly earnings above K37,000. Mining companies, which have paid no withholding tax on dividends, will pay 15%, and a 5% export duty is introduced on zinc concentrates. In the other direction, the mineral royalty on gold that artisanal miners sell to approved aggregators falls from 6% to 3%, customs duty and VAT are suspended on imported raw materials for making sanitary products, and import duties rise on ceramic tiles, selected aluminium products and processed meat to shelter local producers. Briefs, a finance site reporting from Bloomberg's account of the speech, confirmed the VAT increase and the 15% mining dividend levy.

The Zambian Observer, in a first reaction by the Socialist Party president, Fred M'membe, listed further measures that the other pages read for this edition did not itemise: higher mobile money levies in every band, a new tax of K1 per kilogram on sugar, and higher passport and civil registration fees. Those three items rest on that single account and are noted as such below.

Where the money goes

The largest function is general public services at K90.5 billion, or 31.8% of the budget, which The Zambian Observer, carrying a Phoenix FM report, said includes K67.6 billion for debt service, K4.7 billion for dismantling arrears and K1.7 billion for digitalising government services. A second Observer report, on the reaction of the Bwana Mkubwa MP Warren Chisha Mwambazi, put education at K38.2 billion and health at K30.3 billion, with 3,125 health workers to be recruited and six universities to be built; K18.1 billion for building, upgrading and rehabilitating 787 kilometres of road; and the Constituency Development Fund rising from K6.2 billion to about K9 billion while the K40 million per constituency stays the same. The same report said the Farmer Input Support Programme becomes an agricultural loan scheme in 2027 and that a K5 billion loan guarantee scheme is proposed for small businesses. Mr M'membe's statement said the Social Cash Transfer rises by K50 to K450 a month.

The 2027 budget in figures

  • K284.8 billion total, 28.4% of GDP (K253.1 billion in 2026)
  • K233.7 billion domestic revenue, K41.9 billion borrowing, K9.2 billion grants
  • VAT 16% to 16.5%; new 39.5% PAYE band above K37,000 a month
  • 15% withholding tax on mining dividends; 5% export duty on zinc concentrates
  • K67.6 billion debt service; K38.2 billion education; K30.3 billion health
  • Deficit target 3% of GDP in 2027, from an expected 5.9% this year

The deficit and the copper behind it

Briefs reported, citing Bloomberg, that the 2026 deficit is now expected at 5.9% of GDP against a 2.1% target, that the 2027 target is 3%, and that the plan narrows it to 1.2% by 2029. The gap widened, in that account, because spending rose even as near-record copper prices lifted revenue: the government shielded households from the fuel price surge linked to the Iran war and, in an election year, bought maize from farmers at five times the volume first intended. Copper topped US$14,000 a tonne this year; output is projected at 930,000 tonnes in 2026, short of the one-million-tonne goal, with targets of more than one million tonnes in 2027 and 1.4 million in 2029. Briefs said meeting the deficit goal is "key to securing final approval" of the IMF-backed programme.

The Zambia Daily Mail, previewing the speech on Friday morning, set out the Grow Zambia targets the budget is meant to serve: three million tonnes of copper a year, ten million tonnes of maize, 10,000 megawatts of generation capacity and five million tourist arrivals by 2031. News Diggers reported the same morning that the budget came amid calls for jobs, tax relief and a lower cost of living, and that Parliament had just approved a K19.9 billion supplementary budget, about K14 billion of it to pay farmers owed for maize delivered to the Food Reserve Agency.

The first reactions

Mr Mwambazi, according to the Observer, welcomed the loan guarantee scheme as a route to entrepreneurship and jobs, and said the test was delivery.

The success of these commitments will depend on effective implementation, responsible use of public resources
— Warren Chisha Mwambazi, MP for Bwana Mkubwa, in remarks reported by The Zambian Observer on 6 November

Mr M'membe took the opposite view of the same numbers. He said that one kwacha in every four goes to debt, that the K67.6 billion for lenders is more than double the health allocation, and that VAT, the mobile money levies and the sugar tax fall on everyone regardless of income. He welcomed the mining dividend tax, but called it late.

This step is overdue, and it is small.
— Fred M'membe, Socialist Party president, in a statement published by The Zambian Observer on 6 November

His overall verdict was that the budget "serves the lenders and the investors first, and ordinary Zambians last". The government's case, as Zambia Monitor reported it, is that fiscal sustainability has been the UPND's strategic focus since it came to office and that the budget is built to accelerate development while keeping discipline. The Monitor quoted the opening of the minister's sentence on that point: "One of the UPND's strategic focus since coming into Government has been to strengthen fiscal sustainability".

Confirmed vs. alleged

Confirmed: The totals (K284.8 billion; K233.7 billion revenue; K41.9 billion borrowing; K9.2 billion grants), the VAT rise to 16.5%, the 15% withholding tax on mining dividends, the 39.5% PAYE band and the 1 January start date (Zambia Monitor; the totals and the two main tax changes also in The Zambian Observer and in Briefs citing Bloomberg). Debt service of K67.6 billion, education K38.2 billion and health K30.3 billion (two Zambian Observer reports). The 3% deficit target and the 5.9% expected outturn for 2026 (Briefs, citing Bloomberg).

Alleged or unverified: The mobile money levy increases, the K1 per kilogram sugar tax and higher passport and registration fees rest on Fred M'membe's statement as published by The Zambian Observer and were not itemised on any other page read for this edition. Growth and inflation targets, the full list of sector allocations and the financing split between domestic and external borrowing were not on the pages read; Lusaka Times, ZNBC and Mwebantu reports on the speech could not be opened. The budget speech itself had not been published on a government page found during this run.
