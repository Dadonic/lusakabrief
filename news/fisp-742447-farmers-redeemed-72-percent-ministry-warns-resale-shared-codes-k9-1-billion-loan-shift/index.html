Nearly three in four farmers have their inputs, and some are selling them: the ministry warns against resale and shared codes as the budget plans a move from FISP to farm loans

As of Friday 6 November, 742,447 farmers, or 72.47% of the 1,024,434 targeted, had redeemed their inputs under the 2026/27 Farmer Input Support Programme, the Ministry of Agriculture said on Saturday. It also said it had received reports from several districts of farmers selling redeemed inputs to agro-dealers and sharing their e-voucher codes with cooperative leaders, and warned both farmers and dealers of action. The 2027 budget gives FISP K9.1 billion within K22.7 billion for agriculture.

By Lusaka Brief Business Desk • LUSAKA — 8 November 2026 • 3 min read

The Ministry of Agriculture says 742,447 farmers had redeemed their inputs under the 2026/2027 Farmer Input Support Programme by Friday 6 November, 72.47% of the 1,024,434 beneficiaries targeted across the country, Zambia Monitor reported on Saturday 7 November. The figure came in a statement issued in Lusaka on Saturday by the ministry's principal public relations officer, Bhalewa Zyuulu, and it came with a complaint: the ministry has received reports from several districts that some farmers are selling the inputs they have just collected to agro-dealers and others, which Zyuulu said undermines the programme's purpose of raising production, productivity and national food security.

The statement, as the Monitor reported it, also described farmers sharing their e-voucher codes with cooperative leaders and intermediaries. Zyuulu advised farmers to protect their codes and personal identification details and to collect inputs individually from authorised agro-dealers, warning that anyone who hands a code to a third party risks unauthorised transactions and the loss of their entitlement. Accredited agro-dealers were told not to buy back, receive or facilitate the resale of farmers' inputs and to follow the programme's operational guidelines, and the ministry said anyone undermining the programme risked action under those guidelines.

How the season has run

The pace of redemption has picked up sharply since the season opened. At the start of September MUVI TV reported ministry figures of 270,523 farmers issued with redemption codes and 51,052 who had collected inputs, together with the same advice that codes should never be shared and that no agro-dealer may deny an eligible farmer access to inputs. Two months later the ministry's count stands at three quarters of the target, with the planting rains approaching.

What the budget does with FISP

The programme's future shape was set out in Friday's budget. The Minister of Finance and National Planning, Situmbeko Musokotwane, allocated K22.7 billion to agriculture, fisheries and livestock for 2027, of which K9.1 billion is for FISP and K100 million for beef compartments in support of a target of US$1 billion a year in beef exports, Zambia Monitor reported. He listed eight Grow Zambia Agenda targets that bear on the sector, among them 10 million tonnes of maize, three million tonnes of soya beans, one million tonnes each of wheat and sugar and the beef export goal, and announced a farm block development agency to coordinate infrastructure and investment, a review of agricultural land policy favouring land near water, roads, rail and power, six more mechanisation centres in each district by 2031 on top of the existing 125, irrigation of at least 500,000 hectares by 2031, and commencement orders in 2027 for the Food Reserve Agency Act, the Agricultural Marketing Act and the Agricultural Credit and Warehouse Receipts Act, all passed in 2026. An explainer in The Zambian Observer on Saturday reported that FISP itself is planned to shift towards an agricultural loan scheme beginning in 2027, and economist Lubinda Haabazoka counted farmers among the budget's likely winners, according to Zambia Monitor.
