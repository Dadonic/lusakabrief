Three o'clock in Kampala: the Copper Queens open their Olympic road against Uganda — with the captain's demand ringing in their ears

Kick-off is 15:00 CAT at Hamz Stadium, Nakivubo — after this edition went to press. Barbra Banda leads a 25-player squad including eight foreign-based professionals into the first leg of the 2028 Olympic qualifier against Uganda, with the return in Ndola on 13 October and Ghana or Mauritius waiting beyond.

By Mwelwa Phiri • KAMPALA — 8 October 2026 • 4 min read

The talking is done; the road to Los Angeles starts at three. The Copper Queens face Uganda's Crested Cranes at Hamz Stadium, Nakivubo, this afternoon — kick-off 15:00 hours Zambian time, 16:00 in Kampala — in the first leg of their second-round qualifier for the 2028 Olympic Games. This edition went to press before kick-off; the scoreline, when it exists, will lead tomorrow's sport pages.

The captain has already set the terms. "Qualifying is not negotiable," Barbra Banda declared ahead of the tie, a message her senior colleagues have echoed through the week in camp. Interim head coach Charles Haalubono — in charge of his first competitive fixture since taking over from Nora Häuptle after the disappointing WAFCON campaign — has a 25-player squad at his disposal, its spine flown in from abroad: Banda and Racheal Kundananji leading the attack, with Prisca Chilufya and Mercy Chipasula among eight foreign-based professionals reinforcing the seventeen home-based players.

The tie's architecture is unforgiving. Uganda at Nakivubo is one of the region's harder away days — a compact, physical side in front of a home crowd — and the return leg comes quickly, in Ndola on Tuesday, 13 October. The winner on aggregate advances to face Ghana or Mauritius in the next round of the African qualifying series, two steps from the Olympic tournament itself.

The frame around the fixture has not changed since this newspaper left the team's Kampala camp on Wednesday: this is a redemption tour. A generation that carried Zambian women's football to a World Cup and an Olympics spent 2026 learning that reputation wins nothing; a new technical bench has been given the Olympic pathway as its mandate. By this evening, there will be a first answer — and by Tuesday in Ndola, a verdict.
