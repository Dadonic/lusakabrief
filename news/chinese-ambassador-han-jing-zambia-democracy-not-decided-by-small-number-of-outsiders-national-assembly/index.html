'No universal, one-size-fits-all' model: China's ambassador says Zambia's democracy should not be judged by a small number of outsiders, days after the EU's election dialogue

Speaking at a handover of computers, televisions and office equipment to the Nelly Mutti Crèche at the National Assembly on Wednesday 11 November, Ambassador Han Jing said Zambia's democratic future was for Zambians to decide and that each country may shape democracy to its circumstances. He praised the expanded Parliament for widening representation. News Diggers and the Zambian Observer report the remarks, which land as the government seeks to respond to the EU observers' final report before it is published.

By Lusaka Brief Politics Desk • LUSAKA — 13 November 2026 • 2 min read

China's ambassador to Zambia, Han Jing, has said that the country's democratic future should be settled by its own people and not judged by what he called a small number of external actors. News Diggers reports that he made the remarks on Wednesday 11 November at a ceremony to hand over computers, televisions and other office equipment donated to the Nelly Mutti Crèche, items intended to improve efficiency at the National Assembly.

According to News Diggers, Han said there was no universal, one-size-fits-all model of democracy and that every country may shape its democratic path to fit its own circumstances. He also said the expanded composition of Parliament had strengthened the representation of groups that were previously under-represented. The Zambian Observer, reporting the same speech, adds that Han said Zambia's development should be understood in its own political, social and historical context, and frames his position as part of China's long-standing opposition to what it regards as external interference in other countries' political systems.

The audience he did not name

Neither report quotes Han naming any other country or organisation. The context is nonetheless clear from the week's news. On Monday 9 November the European External Action Service's director for Africa, Francesca Di Mauro, told the Zambia–EU Extraordinary Partnership Dialogue that no democracy is perfect but that electoral grievances should be handled "with transparency through a fair and impartial judicial process", and that the EU stood ready to support a national dialogue and reconciliation process, as News Diggers reported on 10 November. Foreign Affairs Minister Mulambo Haimbe asked at the same meeting for the government to be allowed to respond to the EU Election Observation Mission's final report before it is published.

The EU mission's chief observer, Michael McNamara, has also taken an interest in the court proceedings involving the opposition leaders Brian Mundubile and Makebi Zulu, News Diggers noted in an editorial on Thursday, which recorded the UPND elections chairperson Likando Mufalali's warning to the Europeans against protecting criminals. The editorial's own position, in its headline, was that the EU report may not be accurate but that its concerns should be heard.

Two partners, two readings

The sequence gives two of Zambia's major external partners visibly different readings of the August election within a single week: one urging transparent handling of grievances and offering to support reconciliation, the other saying the judgement belongs to Zambians alone. Han's remarks were made at a goodwill event rather than a press conference, and neither outlet reports a response from the government, the opposition or the EU delegation.
