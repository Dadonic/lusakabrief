Tomorrow he opens Parliament — but the loudest argument in the foyer is about a clause the President has not proposed

President Hichilema opens the First Session of the 14th National Assembly on Friday with a policy address — yet the week's loudest pre-speech row is over Article 106's two-term bar. A UPND MP says the limit can constitutionally be revisited and complains his remarks were clipped out of context; the opposition calls the trial balloon a confession. State House has repeatedly distanced itself from third-term talk.

By Chanda Mwape • LUSAKA — 8 October 2026 • 5 min read

The address has a timetable; the argument does not. President Hakainde Hichilema officially opens the First Session of the 14th National Assembly tomorrow, setting out the Government's policy direction for 2026–2027 before a House his party dominates. But the pre-speech week has been consumed by a question the speech is not expected to answer: whether Zambia's presidential term limit should be revisited — and what it means that the idea keeps surfacing from inside the ruling party itself.

The latest spark came from UPND backbencher Mark Simuuwe, who argued that the Constitution's amendment clauses — Articles 62 and 79 — leave the door open to revisiting the two-term bar, and then complained that video of his remarks had been clipped out of context. The constitutional position as it stands is blunt: Article 106 makes a person who has twice held office as President ineligible to contest again. Amending that provision is not a caucus decision; it is a supermajority exercise with referendum gravity. State House has said as much before — Chief Communications Specialist Clayson Hamasaka and senior UPND figures have repeatedly distanced the party from third-term speculation, insisting the President's focus is the mandate he won in August.

The opposition has chosen to treat the balloon as a confession. The DPP's Antonio Mwanza has framed the recurring talk as evidence the idea is alive in the ruling party regardless of the denials; one weekend columnist's advice was satirical and blunt — if that is the destination, make him King and spare the country the choreography. The civic coalition CAAC, through its chairperson Tembo, dismissed the expected legislative agenda as "brought in dead" unless it confronts the governance questions first: the detained opposition leaders — Given Lubinda's successors Makebi Zulu and Brian Mundubile among them — and the reconciliation the Catholic bishops have pressed for since the disputed election. Other voices, the Chiputa grouping among them, have made the release of the detainees the price of any serious national conversation.

Tomorrow's speech will be parsed on two levels: the programme it announces, and the silence it keeps. A President who wants the third-term story dead could kill it from the podium in one sentence. A President who says nothing about it will have said something anyway — and an opposition that campaigns through courtrooms and columns will spend the weekend writing the interpretation.
