0.2 seconds at 18:21: ZESCO explains the night Levy Mwanawasa went dark — and promises new lights

The blackout that stopped Zambia's 2-0 win over Togo for eighteen minutes lasted, on the utility's account, a fifth of a second. The floodlights simply could not forgive it: their design demands about twenty minutes to return to full brightness. ZESCO will now engage the Ministry of Youth, Sport and Arts on a redesign.

By Mwelwa Phiri • NDOLA — 1 October 2026 • 4 min read

The most scrutinised power cut in Zambian football history has been measured, and the measurement is almost comic: 0.2 seconds. In a statement issued the morning after Tuesday's 2-0 win over Togo, ZESCO acknowledged the power disturbance at Levy Mwanawasa Stadium, timed it at 18:21 hours, and gave its duration as a fifth of a second — a flicker the grid barely noticed. The stadium, however, noticed for eighteen minutes. "The design of the affected stadium floodlights requires approximately 20 minutes for them to return to full brightness after a power disturbance," the utility explained.

That sentence is the whole story of Tuesday's strange interlude. The floodlights' lamp technology — the metal-halide family that has lit stadiums for decades — extinguishes on even a momentary dip and then must cool and restrike before it will burn at full power again. Play stopped with Zambia a goal to the good, Albert Kangwanda's third-minute finish on the board and the clock frozen at 25 minutes; the crowd sang through the dark; and when full brightness returned, so did the pattern of the night, ending in Eliya Mandanji's stoppage-time second. The match report recorded a blackout of about twenty minutes. The engineering report records a fault of 0.2 seconds and a lighting system unable to absorb it.

ZESCO's remedy goes beyond an apology. "ZESCO will engage the Ministry of Youth, Sport and Arts to look into redesigning the stadium's lighting system to minimise the risk of similar disruptions during future matches," the utility said, and it congratulated the Chipolopolo on the victory. A redesign means modern instant-restrike or LED sports lighting — the standard at venues that cannot afford to lose twenty minutes to a flicker — and it now has a deadline no ministry will say aloud: November, when the AFCON qualifying campaign resumes and Levy Mwanawasa expects to host again.

The coach, for his part, is treating the football the way the utility is treating the lights: functional, but due an upgrade. George Lwandamina described the Togo win as important and promised improvements to come — a fair summary of a performance that produced two goals from four shots on target while ceding 61 per cent of possession. Zambia sit level with Togo on three points in Group I, with the November double-header against Burundi now the pivot of the campaign. The team found a way to play through the dark on Tuesday. The country's grid, its stadium and its supporters all know the same thing: you cannot keep asking them to.
