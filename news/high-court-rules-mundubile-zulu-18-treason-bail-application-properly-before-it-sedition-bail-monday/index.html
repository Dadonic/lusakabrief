'Properly before the court': the High Court rejects the State's objection and clears the way for the 18 treason accused to argue for bail, as the sedition bail plea slips to Monday

A three-judge panel of the Lusaka High Court ruled on Tuesday 10 November that the constitutional bail application by Brian Mundubile, Makebi Zulu and 16 co-accused was properly before it, rejecting the State's argument that it had been started in the wrong registry. Nobody has been released: the ruling only means the application will now be heard. A day earlier, the pair's other bail plea, in the sedition case, was adjourned again after the State said it had a preliminary issue to raise.

By Lusaka Brief Courts Desk • LUSAKA — 11 November 2026 • 4 min read

The constitutional bail application that has been adjourned, re-listed and argued over since October can now be heard. The Lusaka High Court ruled on Tuesday 10 November that the application by the defeated presidential candidate Brian Mundubile, his running mate Makebi Zulu and 16 other people charged with treason is properly before the court, Zambia Monitor reported on Wednesday morning. The Maravi Post, carrying a Muvi TV report, described the decision the same way: a procedural hurdle has been cleared and the substantive arguments on release can proceed.

The State had raised a preliminary objection, arguing that the application had been wrongly commenced as a civil petition rather than through the criminal registry and should be dismissed for want of jurisdiction, Zambia Monitor reported. The defence answered that the objection did not touch the central question, which is whether keeping the 18 in custody without trial is constitutional; their case rests on Articles 13 and 28 of the Constitution and asks for unconditional release or bail on reasonable conditions. The application names the Attorney General, Mulilo Kabesha, and the Director of Public Prosecutions, Gilbert Phiri, as respondents.

The panel hearing the treason matter is made up of Justices Derrick Mulenga, Davies Chibwili and Ireen Wishimanga, according to Zambia Monitor; The Zambian Observer listed the same three judges in its account of Tuesday's sitting, which it said resumed at midday under heavy security that at times kept relatives and journalists away from the building.

We continue praying for openness, fairness and justice to prevail
— Brian Mundubile, in a statement after the ruling, as quoted by Zambia Monitor on Wednesday 11 November

What the ruling does not do

Both reports are careful on this point. The court has not ordered anyone's release and has not yet heard the bail arguments themselves; the Maravi Post's report says plainly that the development should not be mistaken for a bail victory. The 18 remain at Mukobeko Maximum Security Prison in Kabwe, where they have been held since August on allegations of treason and of sedition and terrorism-related offences, all of which they deny. Zambia Monitor said Mr Zulu, a constitutional lawyer, has been granted leave to represent himself. Neither report gives a date for the substantive hearing.

The defence's argument, set out by Sakwiba Sikota SC before Tuesday's sitting and reported by The Zambian Observer, is that the accused have been held far beyond the 48 hours within which a detained person should be brought before a court, that prosecutors still describe their investigation as continuing, and that some of the co-accused have arrived in court hungry because of delays in transporting them from Kabwe. The Observer, which put Tuesday at day 74 since the arrests, said Mr Mundubile and Mr Zulu had spent Monday night in custody in Lusaka rather than making the roughly 150-kilometre return trip to Mukobeko.

The sedition bail plea waits for a Monday

The two men also face a separate case: two counts of seditious practices under section 57 of the Penal Code over Facebook livestreams on 14 August 2026, the day after the general election, in which prosecutors say they claimed that their parallel vote tabulation showed the NRPUP had won and demanded that the Electoral Commission of Zambia publish the results. That case was transferred from Solwezi to the Lusaka High Court by Judge Edward Musona on 5 November, the Times of Zambia reported.

Their bail application in that case was due to be heard on Monday 9 November but did not proceed. The State asked for an adjournment so that it could raise a preliminary issue, without saying what it was, and the court granted the request and adjourned the hearing to the following Monday, the Times of Zambia and News Diggers reported. The Zambian Observer, carrying a Phoenix FM report, said the panel, led by Judge Geoffrey Mulenga and sitting in chambers, gave the State until the next day to file its preliminary application. Mr Sikota complained that the State raises a preliminary issue every time the matter comes up, and said the bail application had been filed on 19 October and had still not been heard.

Two cases, two panels

  • Treason — 18 accused including Mr Mundubile, Mr Zulu and the former Anglican bishop Trevor Mwamba; constitutional bail application ruled properly before the court on Tuesday 10 November; panel Justices Derrick Mulenga, Davies Chibwili and Ireen Wishimanga.
  • Sedition — Mr Mundubile and Mr Zulu only; two counts under section 57; bail hearing adjourned on Monday 9 November to the following Monday; panel led by Judge Geoffrey Mulenga.
  • Custody — all 18 remain at Mukobeko Maximum Security Prison in Kabwe.

Confirmed vs. alleged

Confirmed: The High Court ruled on Tuesday 10 November that the constitutional bail application by the 18 treason accused is properly before it (Zambia Monitor; Maravi Post citing Muvi TV). No release has been ordered. The sedition bail hearing was adjourned on Monday 9 November to the following Monday after the State sought time to raise a preliminary issue (Times of Zambia; News Diggers; The Zambian Observer citing Phoenix FM).

Alleged or unverified: The date of the substantive constitutional bail hearing has not been reported. Outlets differ on when the 18 were taken into custody: Zambia Monitor says 14 August, The Zambian Observer says 27 August. One Zambian Observer report said the sedition matter had been set down for ruling on Friday 13 November rather than Monday; the Times of Zambia, News Diggers and the Observer's own Phoenix FM report all say Monday. The content of the State's preliminary issue in the sedition case has not been disclosed.
