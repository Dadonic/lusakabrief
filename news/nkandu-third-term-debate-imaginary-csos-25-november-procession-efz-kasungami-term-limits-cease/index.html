'An imaginary issue': the government asks civil society to rethink its 25 November term-limits march, as the Evangelical Fellowship demands that the debate end now

Twelve civil society organisations have notified the police of a procession on Wednesday 25 November in defence of the two-term presidential limit, and the Evangelical Fellowship of Zambia has called for all talk of removing the limit to cease immediately. The Chief Government Spokesperson, Elvis Nkandu, replied on Thursday that every Zambian may hold an opinion but that an opinion is not a government decision, and asked the organisers why they would march against something that is not happening.

By Lusaka Brief Politics Desk • LUSAKA — 6 November 2026 • 4 min read

The argument over whether Zambia's presidents should remain limited to two terms has reached the point where the government is being asked to stop something it says is not happening. News Diggers reported on Friday 6 November that the Chief Government Spokesperson, Elvis Nkandu, had urged a consortium of civil society organisations to reconsider a procession planned for Wednesday 25 November, and had asked why anyone would protest against what he called an imaginary issue. In the same week the Evangelical Fellowship of Zambia demanded that all discussion of removing presidential term limits stop at once.

Twelve organisations and a letter to the police

The procession was announced in a joint statement. Zambia Monitor reported on Thursday 5 November that the statement, headed "Joint CSO Statement on the Planned Peaceful Assembly in Defence of the Constitution and Presidential Term Limits", was signed by Josiah Kalala of the Chapter One Foundation, Leah Mitaba of the Zambia Council for Social Development and Maurice Nyambe of Transparency International Zambia, and that the organisers had notified the Zambia Police Service of a peaceful procession and public assembly on 25 November. News Diggers listed twelve member organisations, among them Caritas Zambia, ActionAid, the Council of Churches in Zambia, the Free Press Initiative, the Non-Governmental Gender Organisations' Coordinating Council, the Alliance for Community Action, the Advocates for Democratic Governance Foundation and the LCK Freedom Foundation. The Zambian Observer, which carried the announcement on Wednesday 4 November, said the statement had been issued on Tuesday 3 November.

What prompted it, according to both Zambia Monitor and the Observer, was a petition presented to the National Assembly on 27 October by a group of citizens asking, among other proposals, for presidential term limits to be abolished. The organisations said they disagreed with the petitioners but respected their right to be heard. They also pointed to last year's constitutional review process, in which, they said, the committees that consulted the public recommended keeping two terms of five years each.

If those voices mattered last year, they matter this year.
— Joint statement by Chapter One Foundation, the Zambia Council for Social Development and Transparency International Zambia, as reported by Zambia Monitor on 5 November

The Observer quoted the statement's description of term limits as "an important safeguard against excessive concentration of political power" and its view that "protecting the Constitution is a collective responsibility shared by all Zambians". News Diggers reported that the procession follows an earlier march by youth-led civil society groups on the same question.

The government's reply

Mr Nkandu's response came in an interview on Thursday 5 November, reported by News Diggers the next morning. He said that "every Zambian has every right, their freedoms to express their opinion" and that "not even government can gag such an opinion", but argued that the views being expressed amounted to opinion rather than policy. "So it's just an opinion which does not culminate into government doing" anything, he said, in the part of his remarks visible before the newspaper's paywall, and he asked the organisers to rethink the protest. The government has not, in any of the reports read for this edition, published a bill or formal proposal to change the two-term rule.

The churches weigh in

The Evangelical Fellowship of Zambia's intervention came through its Executive Director, Reverend Allan Kasungami, in a statement reported by The Zambian Observer on Thursday 5 November. He called the debate "unnecessary" and said it "risks plunging the country into political instability", described the constitution as "the cornerstone of Zambia's democracy" which "should not be altered for political expediency", and said that "all discussions regarding the removal of presidential term limits should cease immediately". He invoked the Oasis Declaration of 2001, when churches and civil society groups combined to oppose a third-term bid, and said the country should be concentrating on the cost of living, national healing after a divisive election, and public services. The Council of Churches in Zambia, a separate body, is among the organisers of the 25 November procession.

Key dates

  • 27 October — a citizens' petition to the National Assembly proposes abolishing presidential term limits, among other changes
  • 3 November — twelve civil society organisations issue a joint statement and notify the police of a procession
  • 5 November — the Evangelical Fellowship of Zambia demands the debate end; Elvis Nkandu calls the issue imaginary
  • 25 November — planned peaceful procession and public assembly

Confirmed vs. alleged

Confirmed: A consortium of civil society organisations has notified the police of a 25 November procession in defence of presidential term limits (News Diggers, Zambia Monitor, The Zambian Observer). The Evangelical Fellowship of Zambia has called for the debate to cease (The Zambian Observer). Elvis Nkandu has asked the organisers to reconsider and called the issue imaginary (News Diggers). A petition to abolish term limits was presented to the National Assembly on 27 October (Zambia Monitor, The Zambian Observer).

Alleged or unverified: None of the reports read names the petitioners of 27 October or says who, if anyone, in government or the ruling party supports their proposal. No bill or formal government proposal to amend the two-term rule has been published. The police response to the notification, and the route and assembly point of the procession, had not been reported at the time of writing.
