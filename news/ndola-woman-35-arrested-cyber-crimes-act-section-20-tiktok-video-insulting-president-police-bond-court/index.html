A TikTok video from September, a charge under the Cyber Crimes Act in November: Ndola police arrest a 35-year-old woman over remarks about the President and release her on bond

Police in Ndola say a 35-year-old woman from Twapia has been charged with using a computer system to commit an offence, under Section 20 of the Cyber Crimes Act No. 4 of 2025 read with Section 179 of the Penal Code, over a TikTok video posted on 9 September that they say contained insulting remarks about President Hichilema. She is on police bond and is yet to appear in court; Lusaka Brief is not naming her.

By Lusaka Brief Courts Desk • NDOLA — 17 November 2026 • 2 min read

A 35-year-old woman from Twapia township in Ndola has been arrested and charged over a TikTok video that police say insulted President Hakainde Hichilema. The arrest was announced by Police Public Relations Officer Chipo Kaitisha in a statement on Monday 16 November and reported by News Diggers and the Zambian Observer. Lusaka Brief does not name her: she has not yet appeared before a court.

The charge is the use of a computer or computer system to commit an offence, contrary to Section 20 of the Cyber Crimes Act No. 4 of 2025, read with Section 179 of the Penal Code. According to the police statement as carried by the Observer, the video was posted on 9 September 2026 between 18:00 and 22:00 from a TikTok account, and police allege that she acted jointly with other persons unknown. The statement said the video "contained insulting remarks directed at the Republican President, Mr Hakainde Hichilema", and that she had been "released on police bond and is expected to appear in court soon".

What is not yet known

No court date has been given, and nothing has been reported from the woman herself, her family or any lawyer. News Diggers' report is behind a paywall and the visible text ends before the facts of the case; the Observer carried the police statement in full. Neither report says what the video contained beyond the police description, or why the arrest came more than two months after the posting.

The next step is a first appearance before a magistrate in Ndola, where the charge would be read and a plea taken, and where any application to vary the bond or to challenge the charge would be made. Until then the public record consists of a single police statement: an allegation, the section of the law relied on, the date of the posting and the fact of release on bond. The woman is presumed innocent, and the question of what was said in the video, and whether it amounts to an offence under Section 20, is one for the court. Lusaka Brief will name her only if and when she is charged before a court and named by at least two established outlets.

Confirmed vs. alleged

Confirmed: The arrest, the age and township of the accused, the charge and the police bond are in the police statement as carried by News Diggers and the Zambian Observer.

Alleged or unverified: The content of the video beyond the police description; the identity of the 'other persons unknown'; the court date; the accused's response. The charge is an allegation.

Sources: News Diggers, “Ndola woman arrested for insulting HH on TikTok” (16 Nov 2026); Zambian Observer, “TikToker arrested for insulting President Hichilema!” (16 Nov 2026).
