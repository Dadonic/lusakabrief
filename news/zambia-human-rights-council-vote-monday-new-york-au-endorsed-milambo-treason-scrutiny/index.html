Monday in New York: Zambia takes its Human Rights Council bid to the vote — as its own rights record is argued over at home

The UN General Assembly elects members of the Human Rights Council for 2027–2029 on Monday, and Zambia — the African Union-endorsed candidate — has spent the autumn lobbying for the seat, with Permanent Representative Chola Milambo working the corridors of UNGA 81. The bid lands in the same week the Government publicly rejected UN experts' concerns over the treason and sedition cases against opposition figures, telling the world body to let the courts decide.

By Chanda Mwape • NEW YORK — 10 October 2026 • 4 min read

The campaign ends on Monday. The United Nations General Assembly votes on 13 October to seat the Human Rights Council's 2027–2029 class, and Zambia goes into the ballot as the African Union-endorsed candidate, having intensified its lobbying through the General Assembly's high-level week — including a reception on the margins of UNGA 81 hosted by the Permanent Mission, fronted by Ambassador Dr Chola Milambo. Fifteen candidates are contesting this year's election, which will be broadcast on UN Web TV.

Zambia's pitch, as made in New York, is the record Lusaka wants discussed: the abolition of the death penalty and the criminal defamation of the President, the peaceful August transfer of incumbency back to a second term, and the candidacy itself as a continuation of the democratic-restoration narrative the administration has sold since 2021. The election for the African seats is shaped by continental endorsement, and the AU's backing makes Zambia the frontrunner for its group.

The awkward companion to the bid is the record Lusaka does not want discussed. In the same week, the Government publicly pushed back against UN special rapporteurs' concerns over the treason and sedition charges facing opposition figures — Brian Mundubile and Makebi Zulu among them — with ZNBC reporting the official line that the UN experts' accounts are refuted and that the courts, not the council's mechanisms, should decide the validity of the charges. The sedition case returns to a Solwezi courtroom on Tuesday, one day after the New York vote; the treason matter has yet to reach a High Court plea. Voting members will hold both files in the same hand.
