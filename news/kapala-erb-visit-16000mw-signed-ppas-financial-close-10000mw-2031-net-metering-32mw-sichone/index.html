16,000 megawatts on paper, 4,722 on the grid: Kapala visits the ERB and calls in the developers whose signed power deals cannot find the money to build

The new Minister of Electricity, Peter Kapala, used a familiarisation visit to the Energy Regulation Board on Monday 9 November to ask why projects with signed power purchase agreements are not reaching financial close. The ERB's chairman put the signed pipeline at more than 16,000 MW; the national target is 10,000 MW of capacity by 2031, from 4,722 MW in October.

By Lusaka Brief Business Desk • LUSAKA — 11 November 2026 • 2 min read

Zambia has more than 16,000 megawatts of proposed generation capacity sitting in signed power purchase agreements, and the main reason most of it is not being built is that the developers cannot get their financing closed. That was the message the Energy Regulation Board's chairman, James Banda, gave the Minister of Electricity, Peter Kapala, during the minister's familiarisation visit to the regulator on Monday 9 November, The Zambian Observer reported on Tuesday 10 November and the Ecofin Agency reported on Wednesday.

Mr Kapala said he would convene a meeting with renewable-energy developers to identify solutions, and listed his priorities as getting signed agreements to financial close, finalising the framework for an Independent System Operator, speeding up electric-vehicle charging infrastructure and expanding transmission, the Observer reported. He said the government wants to turn investor interest into working plants and reach 10,000 MW of generation capacity by 2031, and expressed the hope that the Zambia–Tanzania interconnector would be completed in time to meet demand, particularly from the mines.

The gap between the pipeline and the plug

Installed capacity rose from 3,777 MW in 2024 to 4,722 MW in October 2026, according to Ministry of Energy figures cited by Ecofin, which means the 2031 target needs more than 5,000 MW of new capacity in five years. Mr Banda told the Observer that some developers were being asked by financiers for requirements already covered by the Ministry of Energy's investment framework, and urged sponsors to comply with the framework before submitting agreements for regulatory approval; he proposed closer coordination between the ministry, the ERB and project sponsors.

Ecofin set out the wider obstacles identified by others: doubts about whether ZESCO and other buyers can pay independent producers, exchange-rate movements and transmission constraints flagged by the International Energy Agency, and a 2025 World Bank analysis that said Zambia's fiscal position limited the sovereign guarantees it could offer private power projects. The government's responses, the agency reported, include a coordinated procurement framework approved in December 2025, new procurement, incentive and financing guidelines published in May 2026, and a renewable-energy liquidity mechanism, backed by the UK-funded Green Cities, Infrastructure and Energy Programme, to cushion projects against short-term payment problems.

The ERB's Director General, Elijah Sichone, told the minister that net metering, under which customers with their own solar panels feed power into the grid, had reached about 32 MW against a target of 100 MW, and called for a collective push to accelerate it, the Observer reported. Mr Banda said the Board supported the government's decision to create a dedicated Ministry of Electricity.

The numbers from the ERB visit

  • 16,000 MW+ — proposed capacity in projects with signed PPAs (James Banda, ERB).
  • 4,722 MW — installed capacity in October 2026, up from 3,777 MW in 2024 (Ministry of Energy, via Ecofin).
  • 10,000 MW — government target for 2031.
  • 32 MW of 100 MW — net metering achieved against target (Elijah Sichone, ERB).

Sources: The Zambian Observer, “MINISTER KAPALA MOVES TO ADDRESS INVESTOR BOTTLENECKS IN 10,000MW POWER GENERATION DRIVE” (10 Nov 2026); Ecofin Agency, “Zambia Plans Talks Over Financing Hurdles for 16,000MW Power Pipeline” (11 Nov 2026).
