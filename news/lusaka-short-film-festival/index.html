Short film, long shadow: Lusaka's festival wraps a breakout edition

The Lusaka Short Film Festival has concluded after screenings that drew new audiences to Zambian storytelling — another marker for a film scene building real momentum.

By Lusaka Brief Newsroom • 22 September 2026 • 3 min read

The Lusaka Short Film Festival has concluded its latest edition, closing a run of screenings that showcased Zambian short-form storytelling to growing audiences in the capital.

Short film is where a national cinema gets built: low budgets, sharp ideas, new directors learning their craft in ten-minute increments. This year's programme leaned into that — work from emerging Zambian filmmakers alongside regional selections, with audience discussions that stretched past the credits.

It arrives in a good season for Zambian culture generally — the Danny Kaya festival packed the Copperbelt earlier this month, the Kwacha Music Awards are set for November, and the 'Grow Zambia' agenda explicitly courts the creative economy as part of its five-million-tourist ambition.

The festival's challenge is the industry's: turning applause into infrastructure — distribution, funding and screens. But every sold-out screening makes the argument louder.
