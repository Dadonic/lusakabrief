150 Special Forces personnel and a clean-up in Kamwala: the unit marks its anniversary with the mayor's campaign on Monday, and two opposition leaders say it is the council's job

The Zambia Special Forces are to provide 150 personnel on Monday 9 November to clean the stretch between the Universal Church and the FINDECO flyover, The Zambian Observer reports, as part of the unit's annual day. Mayor Chitambala Mwewa welcomed the help. Socialist Party president Fred M'membe and Democratic Union president Ackim Njobvu have objected to elite soldiers doing municipal work. The deployment is reported by one outlet.

By Lusaka Brief Politics Desk • LUSAKA — 9 November 2026 • 3 min read

Lusaka's clean-up campaign is due to get an unusual workforce on Monday 9 November. The Zambia Special Forces will provide 150 personnel to clean the area between the Universal Church and the FINDECO flyover bridge in Kamwala, The Zambian Observer reported on 6 November in an account credited to Zambian Watch. The unit's commander, Brigadier General Moses Shapwaya, announced the exercise during a courtesy call on the Mayor of Lusaka, Chitambala Mwewa, the report said. No second outlet's account of the announcement was found for this edition, and whether the exercise has gone ahead was not known at the time of writing.

The explanation given is the calendar. The unit marks Special Forces Day every year on 9 November, and this year's observance is built around cleanliness, the Observer reported, under the theme "43 Years of Elite Service: Defending the Nation, Serving the Community." General Shapwaya said the exercise was meant to support the mayor's efforts. Mr Mwewa thanked the unit, said its help would speed up the work and reinforce the campaign against disorder in the city, and said the council was ready to work with it on what he calls the Make Lusaka Clean agenda. He added that the offer showed most residents were happy with the council's efforts, according to the report.

The objections

Two opposition leaders have criticised the plan. Ackim Njobvu, president of the Democratic Union, said highly trained soldiers should not be given work other institutions could do, and suggested the Zambia National Service or correctional facilities instead, the Observer reported on 6 November.

Our country is losing direction. We are abusing our men in uniform. They deserve RESPECT.
— Ackim Njobvu, president of the Democratic Union, in remarks carried by The Zambian Observer on 6 November

The Socialist Party went further in a statement signed by its president, Fred M'membe, and published by the same outlet on 7 November. The party said it fully supports the goal of a clean and orderly capital, but questioned the use of specialised combat units for routine sanitation, calling it an institutional overstep that undermines civilian governance. "A clean Lusaka cannot be sustained through sporadic military interventions or public relations exercises," the statement said. If the council cannot keep the city clean, it argued, that is a failure of local government funding and administration, and the answer is to resource the council properly and pay its workers fairly.

A campaign with a history

The exercise is the latest turn in a drive that began soon after Mr Mwewa's election. Zambia Monitor reported on 23 August that the new mayor had launched a campaign called "Throw It in the Bin" and was touring the city with a loudhailer urging residents, traders and motorists to stop littering, and on 12 September that the removal of street vendors from parts of the city had begun, with the council expected to keep moving traders to designated markets. Both reports recorded strong approval on social media.

What separates the two positions is not whether Kamwala should be clean. The mayor and the commander present a one-day anniversary gesture of community service; the critics see a precedent for using the military in civil administration. The reports do not say who is meeting the cost of the exercise, whether council workers are taking part alongside the soldiers, or whether the Ministry of Defence approved it. The Observer recorded no reply from the council or the government to the criticism.

Confirmed vs. alleged

Confirmed: The Socialist Party and the Democratic Union have publicly criticised a plan to use Special Forces personnel in a Lusaka clean-up; their statements were published by The Zambian Observer. The mayor's clean-up and vendor-removal campaigns since August are reported by Zambia Monitor.

Alleged or unverified: The details of the deployment, including the number of personnel, the area and the date, come from one outlet, The Zambian Observer, crediting Zambian Watch. Whether the exercise took place as announced on Monday 9 November was not known when this edition was compiled.
