'No cousin, no brother': Hichilema swears in his second-term cabinet with a warning

President Hakainde Hichilema has sworn in the first batch of his new cabinet at State House, telling ministers that delivery — not relationships — will decide who keeps their seat.

By Lusaka Brief Newsroom • 17 September 2026 • 5

President Hakainde Hichilema on Monday swore in the first members of his second-term cabinet at State House, delivering the message that has since defined the week in Zambian politics: "No cousin, no brother" — appointments are for delivery, not for loyalty or family ties.

The swearing-in capped days of intense speculation. Stakeholders across the political spectrum had pressed the president for an inclusive cabinet, while Economic Front leader Wynter Kabimba publicly appealed for an "action-oriented" team rather than a rewards list. The political column of the week was written by events inside the opposition too: former vice-president Nevers Mumba's resignation added to the churn of a system resetting after the 13 August vote.

Why the message matters

Zambia's second-term governments have historically drifted. By framing appointments around competence from day one — and saying so in language every market trader can repeat — Hichilema is setting a public benchmark he can be measured against.

"No cousin, no brother. You are here to work for the Zambian people."

Attention now shifts to the remaining portfolios and to the hundred-day plans each ministry will be expected to defend. Lusaka Brief will track appointments, and more importantly, delivery.
