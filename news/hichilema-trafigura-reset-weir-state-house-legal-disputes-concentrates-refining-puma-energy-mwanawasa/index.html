'Business should not be done through the courts': Hichilema and Trafigura's chairman agree a reset, with legal disputes to settle and concentrate exports in the President's sights

President Hichilema received a delegation led by Trafigura's chairman Jeremy Weir at State House on Monday 9 November and said afterwards that the two sides had agreed to reset their relationship. News Diggers reports that he asked for outstanding legal disputes to be resolved and for Zambia to stop exporting mineral concentrates. His former policy adviser Chipokota Mwanawasa was recently appointed the trader's Country Executive Director for Zambia.

By Lusaka Brief Business Desk • LUSAKA — 11 November 2026 • 2 min read

President Hakainde Hichilema met a delegation from the commodity trader Trafigura, led by its chairman Jeremy Weir, at State House on Monday 9 November, and said afterwards that the government and the company had agreed to reset their relationship, Open Zambia, The Zambian Observer and News Diggers reported. In a statement addressed to citizens after the meeting, reported by Open Zambia and the Observer, the President thanked Trafigura for its long-standing interest in Zambia and for the jobs it supports, including through Puma Energy and Servara, and said the aim was a partnership that delivers for Zambians.

News Diggers reported two further points from the meeting. The President called for the outstanding legal disputes between the government and Trafigura to be resolved, saying business should not be conducted through the courts, and he said Zambia should stop exporting mineral concentrates and invest instead in refining capacity. Neither the Open Zambia nor the Observer account mentions the disputes or the concentrates, and none of the three reports describes what the disputes are about.

We look forward to a partnership that delivers more investment, more jobs and more opportunity for Zambian businesses
— President Hakainde Hichilema, in a statement after meeting the Trafigura delegation at State House on Monday 9 November, as quoted by Open Zambia and The Zambian Observer

Where the trader fits

Trafigura buys and finances copper, moves fuel and builds the logistics that connect producers to markets, and the President's statement mapped those three strengths onto three Grow Zambia Agenda goals: three million tonnes of copper a year by 2031, reliable and affordable energy, and links to regional and global markets, Open Zambia reported. He suggested the company could play a direct part in all three.

The meeting follows Trafigura's recent appointment of Chipokota Mwanawasa, until recently Policy Adviser to the President, as its Country Executive Director for Zambia, a move News Diggers noted in its report, which describes the company as a multinational that sources, stores, blends and transports raw materials and energy products.

What the reset means in practice has not been spelt out. No agreement, investment figure or timetable was announced in any of the three reports, and Trafigura's own account of the meeting was not reported. The two concrete tests will be whether the disputes the President referred to are settled and whether the company's copper business in Zambia moves towards refined metal rather than concentrate, as he asked.

Sources: News Diggers, “HH seeks fresh start with Trafigura” (10 Nov 2026); Open Zambia, “President Hichilema agrees to reset Zambia's relationship with Trafigura” (9 Nov 2026); The Zambian Observer, “PRESIDENT HICHILEMA AGREES TO RESET ZAMBIA'S RELATIONSHIP WITH TRAFIGURA” (10 Nov 2026).
