'Why was the group supporting removal allowed to march?': civil society says it will go ahead on 25 November, as a UPND member abroad tells his party to end the term-limit debate

Transparency International Zambia, the Chapter One Foundation and the LCK Foundation say the government is being inconsistent in discouraging their 25 November procession in defence of presidential term limits after a petition to abolish the limits was presented to the National Assembly on 27 October and its supporters were allowed to march. The government's spokesman has called the debate imaginary. From the United States, a UPND member's Facebook live video urging the President to end the debate has been widely shared.

By Lusaka Brief Politics Desk • LUSAKA — 8 November 2026 • 3 min read

The civil society coalition planning a procession in defence of presidential term limits says it will not be talked out of it. News Diggers reported on Saturday 7 November that Transparency International Zambia has accused the government of inconsistency for discouraging the 25 November march while having allowed young people who support removing the limits to march to Parliament. Josiah Kalala, executive director of the Chapter One Foundation, said the organisations would demonstrate peacefully and within the law, and Linda Kasonde, executive director of the LCK Foundation, said the UPND government appeared to be deliberately preventing public gatherings, which she said undermines democracy, according to the Diggers report.

TI-Z's executive director, Maurice Nyambe, made the same point a day earlier. Daily Revelation Zambia reported on Friday 6 November that Nyambe said the advice from the Chief Government Spokesperson, Elvis Nkandu, to reconsider the march conflicted with the freedoms of expression and assembly in the Constitution, that it was not for a minister or the government to dictate how citizens express themselves, and that the coalition had given formal notice to the Zambia Police Service and expected it to act professionally.

Why was the group supporting the removal of term limits allowed to march while CSOs are being advised not to?
— Maurice Nyambe, executive director of Transparency International Zambia, as quoted by Daily Revelation Zambia on Friday 6 November

The government's answer

Nkandu's position, as The Zambian Observer reported it on Friday, is that the third-term debate is an imaginary issue: neither President Hichilema nor the UPND's supreme organ has signalled any intention to extend term limits, free expression applies equally to supporters and opponents of constitutional change, and the President is focused on jobs, the cost of living and electricity generation. The Observer named the coalition as the Chapter One Foundation, Transparency International Zambia, Caritas Zambia, the Council of Churches in Zambia, the LCK Foundation and the NGO Coordinating Council, and said the trigger was a petition presented to the National Assembly on 27 October calling for the abolition of presidential term limits. In their joint statement of 3 November, reported by the Observer on 4 November, the groups said they disagreed with the petitioners but respected their right to speak, recalled the country's resistance to a third presidential term in 2001, and pointed out that the 2025 constitutional review consultations had recommended keeping the current limits.

A warning from inside the party

The debate is also dividing the ruling party's own supporters. The Zambian Observer reported on Saturday that Akashambatwa Masheke, a UPND member based in the United States, had urged the President in a widely shared Facebook live video to shut down the term-limit debate so the government could concentrate on the Grow Zambia Agenda. He warned that if the limits were removed and Hichilema then lost in 2031 the consequences would fall on the Zambezi region and on the President's family, said the party's parliamentary majority should not tempt it to amend the Constitution, cited the failed third-term bids of Frederick Chiluba and Edgar Lungu, and named the party's media director, Mark Simuuwe, among those whose conduct he said risked damaging the President's legacy. Hichilema was sworn in for a second and final term on 1 September, which under the current two-term limit makes him ineligible in 2031, the Observer noted. Masheke's remarks are his own, made in a personal video; the Observer's report carried no response from the party or from Simuuwe.
