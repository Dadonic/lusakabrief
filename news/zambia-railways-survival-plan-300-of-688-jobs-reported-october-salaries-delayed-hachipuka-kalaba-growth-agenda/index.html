Three hundred jobs out of 688, and October's pay late: Daily Revelation reports a survival plan at Zambia Railways, and Kalaba asks what it says about the growth agenda

The state-owned railway's board and management have not answered reports that they proposed laying off 300 of 688 workers, Daily Revelation reports, and a staff memo dated 26 October said October salaries were delayed because operational problems had hit cash flow. Board chairperson Emmanuel Hachipuka has said jobs will have to go for the company to survive. The job-loss figure rests on one newspaper and has not been confirmed by the company.

By Lusaka Brief Business Desk • LUSAKA — 9 November 2026 • 3 min read

Zambia Railways Limited, the state-owned operator, is reported to be planning to shed more than four in ten of its workers. Daily Revelation reports that the company's board and management have not responded to reports that they proposed laying off 300 workers out of 688 as part of a survival plan, and in its Monday 9 November edition the paper carries Citizens First leader Harry Kalaba's criticism of the government's silence on the matter. The figure comes from that one newspaper. No statement from the company, the Industrial Development Corporation or the government confirming or denying it was found for this edition.

What the company's own chairperson has said, according to the paper, points the same way. Emmanuel Hachipuka told Daily Revelation in a recent interview that "some people will have to lose their jobs in order for the company to survive", and the paper has separately reported him as saying the company had been asked to come up with a survival plan in which jobs would be lost. In an editorial in its 19 October edition the paper reported his account that the railway was spending 71 ngwee of every kwacha it earned on wages, against what he called an industry norm of 30 ngwee, and was already struggling to pay staff when its former managing director, Cuthbert Malindi, left.

Late salaries and a row over the board

The cash problem is documented in the company's own words. A memo to staff from the director of human resource and administration, Clyde Muleya, dated 26 October 2026, said that operational challenges had negatively affected cash flow and that October salaries had therefore been delayed, Daily Revelation reported. The same paper has reported that employees have asked, through the state's Industrial Development Corporation, for Mr Hachipuka's board to be dissolved, alleging interference in day-to-day operations and raising questions about appointments and the use of company resources. Mr Hachipuka called the allegations unfair and has said he will wait for the authorities to respond, the paper reported.

Mr Kalaba's argument is political. He said the government's silence over the planned job cuts shows that the UPND administration's description of the President's final term as a growth agenda is empty, Daily Revelation reported, and the paper's headline asks whether the railway is being allowed to decline to the advantage of private operators ahead of the planned repeal of the Railways Act. That is a question, not a finding: no evidence for it is set out in the part of the report open to non-subscribers, and no government response to it has been reported.

The plan the railway had in March

Earlier reporting by other outlets shows how far the company is from where it meant to be. In March, Zambia Railways appointed Pangaea Securities to help it raise US$60 million from development finance institutions and private investors, to sit alongside a €50 million European Union grant for track, signalling and communications, Zambia Monitor reported on 19 March and Railway Gazette International on 1 April. Its 2024–2028 business plan aimed to lift freight from 800,000 tonnes in 2024 to 2.6 million tonnes by 2028, with a first phase costed at US$113 million. Mr Malindi, then managing director, compared the railway to "a stool with three broken legs": rolling stock, track and operational efficiency, all of which he said had to be mended at once.

Whether the US$60 million has been raised, how many jobs the survival plan would actually remove, on what terms, and whether the October salaries have since been paid are not answered in any of the reports read for this edition.

Confirmed vs. alleged

Confirmed: Daily Revelation reports that a company memo dated 26 October said October salaries were delayed, and quotes the board chairperson as saying jobs must be lost for the company to survive. Zambia Monitor and Railway Gazette International reported in March and April that the railway was seeking US$60 million alongside a €50 million EU grant.

Alleged or unverified: The figure of 300 job losses out of 688 is reported by Daily Revelation alone and has not been confirmed by Zambia Railways, the IDC or the government. The suggestion that the company is being allowed to deteriorate to favour private operators is a question raised in a headline, not an established fact. The employees' allegations against the board are disputed by its chairperson.
