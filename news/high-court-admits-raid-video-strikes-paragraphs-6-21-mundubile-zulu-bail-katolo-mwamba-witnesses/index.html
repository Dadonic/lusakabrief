Courts · Treason bail application

The raid video stays, two paragraphs go: the High Court splits the DPP's objection in the Mundubile and Zulu bail case, and a defence lawyer says three of the 18 could walk

The bench of Derrick Mulenga, Davies Chibwili and Ireen Wishimanga ruled on Friday 13 November that the flash disk carrying footage of the raid on Makebi Zulu's home stays on the record of the constitutional bail application by Brian Mundubile, Zulu and 16 others, while striking paragraphs 6 and 21 of their affidavit in reply and keeping 19, 20 and 22. The DPP had wanted all five paragraphs and the video out. Defence lawyer Milner Katolo told News Diggers that Bishop Trevor Mwamba and two others could be released or become State witnesses.

By Lusaka Brief Courts Desk • LUSAKA — 15 November 2026 • 3 min read

The ruling the Lusaka High Court promised for Friday 13 November has gone partly each way. The bench of Justices Derrick Mulenga, Davies Chibwili and Ireen Wishimanga admitted the flash disk containing video footage of the raid at Makebi Zulu's residence into the record of the constitutional bail application brought by Brian Mundubile, Zulu and 16 co-applicants, but struck two of the five paragraphs of their affidavit in reply that Director of Public Prosecutions Gilbert Phiri SC had objected to. The Zambian Observer reported the ruling in two pieces on Friday; News Diggers carried the defence's reading of it on Saturday.

According to the Zambian Observer's account, the State had argued that the affidavit in reply introduced fresh matters it had no chance to answer, and asked the court to expunge paragraphs 6, 19, 20, 21 and 22 together with the disk. The court removed paragraph 6, finding it raised a new issue in breach of the rules on affidavits in reply, and paragraph 21, which it found went beyond the issues raised by the DPP as second respondent. Paragraphs 19 and 20 stayed because they directly answered the State's allegation that the applicants were a flight risk, and paragraph 22 stayed too. The State had separately questioned the foundation laid for the recording, the Observer reported, but the video was admitted. The ruling settles only what is on the record; it does not decide whether the 18 should be released.

What the defence takes from it

News Diggers reported defence lawyer Milner Katolo saying the court had rejected the DPP's application to remove the video of the raid at Zulu's house. Katolo went further, telling the outlet that Bishop Trevor Mwamba and two other accused could be released or turned into State witnesses, because the police had taken ordinary statements from them rather than warn-and-caution statements, the form used when a person is being treated as a suspect. That is the lawyer's argument, not a finding of the court, and the State has not been reported responding to it.

The case so far

The 18 applicants, held at Mukobeko Maximum Security Correctional Facility, have been in custody since 14 August 2026 over alleged treason, sedition and terrorism-related offences, the Zambian Observer reported earlier in the week. They have sued Attorney General Mulilo Kabesha and the DPP, seeking unconditional release or bail on reasonable conditions and arguing that continued detention violates their constitutional rights. On Tuesday 10 November the same bench dismissed a separate State objection that had challenged their route to the court, finding no law or rule barred an aggrieved person from seeking constitutional bail this way; the DPP's objection to the video followed a day later. Mundubile stood for president in August and Zulu was his running mate; all 18 are presumed innocent, and the charges have not been tested at trial.

Confirmed vs. alleged

Confirmed: That the bench struck paragraphs 6 and 21, kept 19, 20 and 22, and admitted the flash disk is reported by the Zambian Observer; News Diggers reports the defence's account that the video application was rejected. Both outlets state that bail itself has not been decided.

Alleged or unverified: Katolo's claim that three of the accused could be released or become witnesses is his own assessment and has not been confirmed by the court or the State. The outlets cited do not give the date on which the bench will rule on bail, nor what the admitted footage shows beyond a raid at Zulu's home.
