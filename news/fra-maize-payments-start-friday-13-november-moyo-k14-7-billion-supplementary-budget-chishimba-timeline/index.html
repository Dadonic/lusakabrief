The maize money starts today: Moyo says farmers owed for 2025/26 deliveries to the FRA will be paid from Friday 13 November, drawing on K14.7 billion of the supplementary budget

Agriculture Minister Moses Moyo said in a statement on Thursday 12 November that payments to farmers who supplied maize to the Food Reserve Agency in the 2025/2026 marketing season and are still waiting would begin on Friday 13 November, following Parliament's approval of a K19.9 billion supplementary budget of which K14.7 billion is for the arrears. News Diggers and the Zambian Observer report the announcement; UPP president Saviour Chishimba says institutions that missed earlier timelines owe farmers an apology.

By Lusaka Brief Business Desk • LUSAKA — 13 November 2026 • 2 min read

Farmers who delivered maize to the Food Reserve Agency during the 2025/2026 crop marketing season and have not yet been paid should start receiving their money on Friday 13 November, the Minister of Agriculture, Moses Moyo, said in a statement on Thursday reported by News Diggers. The payments follow Parliament's approval of a K19.9 billion supplementary budget, of which K14.7 billion is earmarked for settling what the government owes farmers.

According to News Diggers, Moyo thanked farmers for their patience and for their contribution to national food security and agricultural development. The Zambian Observer, which also reported that payments were to begin on Friday, described the announcement as a relief to farmers who had delivered grain and waited.

A date set from the top

The Zambian Observer carried a response from Saviour Chishimba, president of the United Progressive People, who thanked President Hakainde Hichilema for the announcement that payments would start on Friday 13 November. Chishimba's argument, as the outlet reports it, is that when the President gives a timeline the institutions beneath him must meet it, and that those who failed to do so on earlier occasions owe farmers an apology. He urged officials to act on presidential commitments rather than delay them, and closed by asking that every outstanding farmer be paid promptly.

The arithmetic of the supplementary budget is the backdrop. Of the K19.9 billion that Parliament approved, roughly three-quarters is for the maize bill owed to farmers who sold to the FRA. The first payments on Friday are the beginning of that process rather than the end of it: neither report gives a schedule for how quickly the K14.7 billion will be disbursed or how many farmers will be paid in the first tranche.

What to watch

Two things will show whether the promise holds. The first is whether farmers in the districts furthest from Lusaka see money in their accounts in the coming days, rather than a further announcement. The second is whether the Treasury's release of the K14.7 billion is spread over weeks or compressed into the days before the planting season's input bills fall due. Chishimba's intervention, and the Zambian Observer's choice to frame the story around a presidential timeline, suggest the payments will be judged against the calendar as much as the total.

Sources: News Diggers, “Govt to start settling farmers with outstanding payments tomorrow” (12 Nov 2026); The Zambian Observer, “FRA to pay farmers starting tomorrow” (12 Nov 2026).
