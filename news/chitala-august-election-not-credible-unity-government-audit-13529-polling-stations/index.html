'Not credible': Mbita Chitala indicts the August election — an audit of 13,529 polling stations, and a unity government as the cure

Zambia Research Foundation director Dr Mbita Chitala, a co-founder of the MMD, has declared the 13 August general election not credible — citing the ECZ's six-hour blackout, the closure of the courts during the presidential petition window, and a claimed variance of more than 800,000 votes — and proposed an eight-pillar reconciliation process culminating in a government of national unity.

By Chanda Mwape • LUSAKA — 9 October 2026 • 6 min read

The reconciliation debate acquired its heaviest document this week. Dr Mbita Chitala — co-founder of the Movement for Multi-Party Democracy in 1990, former deputy finance minister and director of the Zambia Research Foundation — has published a formal statement of "governance grievances and national concerns" declaring the 13 August general election not credible and warning that the vote has left the country fractured. His remedy is an eight-pillar national reconciliation and constitutional restoration process, modelled in its final pillar on the unity governments once formed in Zimbabwe and South Africa.

The statement is built as a lawyer's brief, sorting its material into established facts, opposition allegations, government explanations and matters requiring independent investigation. Its charges, in his ordering: the Electoral Commission's abrupt six-hour halt to counting and results announcements, with observers sent away; stolen ballot materials in Mandevu and tear gas fired into a totalling centre in Kabwe; a claimed variance of more than 800,000 votes that, he argues, only an independent audit of all 13,529 polling stations can settle; and the Christian Churches Monitoring Group's independent assessment, which he cites as identifying anomalies including presidential votes exceeding parliamentary votes by more than 248,670.

His gravest count is aimed above the ECZ. The closure of all courts on 24 August, during the seven-day window Article 103 allows for a presidential petition, is "the most urgent constitutional crisis" — a shutdown that forced a petition to be lodged electronically through Chief Justice Mumba Malila after physical filing became impossible. The activist Brebner Changala, amplifying the statement, questioned the Chief Justice's decision to administer the presidential oath while a petition was pending, contrasted him with Matthew Ngulube, who resigned on principle, and praised Judge Catherine Makungu for stepping aside. These are allegations and arguments, it must be said plainly, from actors who have disputed the election since August — the courts that remain open have not endorsed them, and the Government's position is that the election was won and sworn in accordance with the Constitution.

What gives the document its weight is not its charge sheet but its author and its timing. Chitala is no opposition functionary; he is one of the men who built the movement that ended one-party rule, and he says he writes as a registered voter inspired by Chipata Bishop George Lungu's pastoral call for reconciliation. His pillars run from political dialogue and a review of political prisoners through electoral fact-finding, security-sector neutrality and constitutional justice — reconciliation, he insists, must follow truth and cannot mean impunity. Published two days before the President opens Parliament, the statement reads as an attempted agenda for today's address. The podium will answer it, or not, by mid-morning.

Confirmed vs. alleged

Confirmed: The statement's publication and authorship; the six-hour ECZ pause, the Mandevu totalling-centre attack and the 24 August courts closure as events that occurred and have been previously reported; the CCMG's published role as an election observer; Chitala's eight-pillar proposal as stated.

Alleged or unverified: The 800,000-vote variance claim and the reading of the CCMG figures — contested and never adjudicated, the presidential petition window having closed without a hearing on the merits; the characterisation of Mutotwe Kafwaya's death as an assassination, which remains a demand for an inquiry, not a finding; the call for the Chief Justice's resignation, which is an opinion held by its authors.
