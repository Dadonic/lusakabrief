US$30.89 billion owed, US$275 million saved: external debt fell 5% after the Eurobond buyback, Musokotwane tells Parliament, as the IMF board weighs a US$1.46 billion programme

Central government debt stood at US$30.89 billion at the end of June 2026, split almost evenly between domestic and external borrowing, the Finance Minister told the National Assembly on Tuesday 10 November. External debt fell from US$16.15 billion in December to US$15.35 billion, mainly because the Treasury bought back about US$1.36 billion of its 2053 Eurobond in May at a discount. A staff-level agreement on a new 36-month IMF programme worth US$1.46 billion was reached on 9 October; the Fund's board is expected to decide in December.

By Lusaka Brief Business Desk • LUSAKA — 12 November 2026 • 2 min read

Zambia's central government debt was US$30.89 billion at the end of June 2026, the Minister of Finance and National Planning, Situmbeko Musokotwane, told Parliament on Tuesday 10 November. Of that, US$15.54 billion was domestic debt and US$15.35 billion external, Zambia Monitor, News Diggers and The Zambian Observer all reported. The external figure is about 5% lower than the US$16.15 billion recorded at the end of December 2025, a fall the minister attributed mainly to a liability management operation early this year linked to a Eurobond buyback, together with slower disbursements on external loans.

The buyback itself was done in May 2026, Zambia Monitor reported: the government repurchased roughly US$1.36 billion of its Bond B, which had been due to mature in 2053, at below the bond's original value. The Treasury puts the saving at an estimated US$275 million and has earmarked it for the Energy Grid Resilience Programme, News Diggers reported. Dr Musokotwane told the House that a Special Report with fuller detail would be laid before Parliament and that further consolidation of the country's bonds is planned under a Benchmark Bond Policy. He said the government remained firmly committed to debt sustainability.

The next programme

The minister's statement also set out where the relationship with the International Monetary Fund stands, Zambia Monitor reported. The previous 38-month Extended Credit Facility, approved on 31 August 2022, was completed on 27 January 2026 with about US$1.7 billion drawn. A staff-level agreement on a successor ECF was reached on 9 October 2026: 36 months, US$1.46 billion, split equally between balance-of-payments support and budget support. That is not final approval; the IMF Executive Board is expected to consider the programme in December 2026.

The remarks came in answer to a supplementary question from the Lukashya MP, Daniel Bwalya of the NRPUP, who asked which African country had seen people's lives improve after an IMF programme, News Diggers reported. Dr Musokotwane's reply was that Zambia itself was an example of a country that had become better off.

itself is an example of a country that has become better off
— Situmbeko Musokotwane, Minister of Finance and National Planning, on Zambia, answering a supplementary question in the National Assembly on Tuesday 10 November, as quoted by News Diggers

The Secretary to the Treasury, Felix Nkulukusa, gave the growth side of the same argument at the 2027 budget symposium on Monday 9 November, News Diggers reported: Zambia can reach an US$80 billion economy by 2031 if it hits the growth targets that begin with the 2027 budget, and domestic revenue is expected to reach 23.6% of GDP by 2029. Dr Musokotwane told the symposium that policies and regulations must change so that the private sector drives export-led growth.

The debt numbers, as reported

  • Total central government debt, end-June 2026 — US$30.89 billion.
  • Domestic — US$15.54 billion. External — US$15.35 billion, down from US$16.15 billion in December 2025.
  • May 2026 buyback — about US$1.36 billion of Bond B (due 2053), estimated saving US$275 million.
  • Successor ECF — staff-level agreement 9 October; 36 months; US$1.46 billion; board decision expected December 2026.
