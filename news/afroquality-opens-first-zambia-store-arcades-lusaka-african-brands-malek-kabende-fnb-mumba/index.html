'Easier to ship from Zambia to London than to Ghana': AfroQuality opens its first Lusaka store at Arcades to sell African brands across African borders

The pan-African retailer, which already has shops in Accra, Kigali, Nairobi and Dakar, has opened at Arcades Shopping Mall with a pitch to Zambian designers: a shelf in other African capitals. The Zambia Association of Fashion Designers' president Faith Kabende and FNB Zambia's Luyando Mumba spoke at the launch, the Times of Zambia reports.

By Lusaka Brief Culture Desk • LUSAKA — 11 November 2026 • 2 min read

AfroQuality, a retailer that stocks fashion, accessories and other products from African brands, has opened its first store in Zambia at Arcades Shopping Mall in Lusaka, the Times of Zambia reported in a feature on Tuesday 10 November. The company already has stores in Accra, Kigali, Nairobi and Dakar, and its stated purpose is to give brands from one African country a route to customers in another.

Estella Malek, the group's Chief Curation, Logistics and Merchandising Officer, told the launch that the company had been set up to help African brands grow beyond their home markets, and described the difficulty of moving goods between African countries: it can be easier, she said, to ship a product from Zambia to London than to Ghana. She also said she wanted to change perceptions of African fashion, which she argued should not be confined to traditional fabrics and prints.

We call ourselves an engine, an engine that has built an ecosystem.
— Estella Malek, AfroQuality Group, at the opening of the Arcades store, as quoted by the Times of Zambia on Tuesday 10 November

The designers' view

Faith Kabende, president of the Zambia Association of Fashion Designers and chief executive of Fay Designs, said the store would help Zambian designers reach buyers in other countries, and argued that young people who favour foreign fashion might come to value local culture through a shop of this kind. Fashion, she said, is not just clothing but identity and culture, the Times reported.

FNB Zambia's Business and Commercial Executive, Luyando Mumba, said the bank supports local businesses with market access and finance, pointed to its trade fairs as a way of opening new markets, and said that backing businesses like AfroQuality could benefit many people because small and medium-sized enterprises are the backbone of the economy.

The opening adds a cross-border retail channel to a Zambian fashion scene that has mostly reached foreign customers through fairs, social media and individual orders. Whether a Lusaka-made garment ends up on a shelf in Nairobi or Accra will depend on the curation and logistics the company says it has built; the Times feature gives no figures for the store's investment or the number of Zambian brands it has taken on.

Sources: Times of Zambia, “AfroQuality brings Africa's fashion to Zambia” (10 Nov 2026).
