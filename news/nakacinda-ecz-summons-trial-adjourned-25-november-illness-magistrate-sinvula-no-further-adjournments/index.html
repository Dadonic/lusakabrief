Out of Chimbokaila and back to court: Nakacinda’s ECZ summons trial is put off to 25 November after his lawyer says he is too ill, and the magistrate calls it the last adjournment

Raphael Nakacinda was due to open his defence on Friday 23 October to a charge of failing to comply with an Electoral Commission of Zambia summons in November 2022. His lawyer Nkula Bota told Magistrate Mbuywana Sinvula that he was unwell and needed medical attention, the Zambian Observer and News Diggers report; the case resumes on 25 November with a warning that no further adjournments will be granted.

By Lusaka Brief Courts Desk • LUSAKA — 25 October 2026 • 2 min read

Raphael Nakacinda's first court date since leaving prison lasted only as long as it took to ask for another one. The former Patriotic Front secretary general appeared before Lusaka Magistrate Mbuywana Sinvula on Friday 23 October to begin his defence to a single count of failing to comply with a lawful summons issued by the Electoral Commission of Zambia. According to the Zambian Observer, his lawyer, Nkula Bota, told the court that his client was unwell and needed medical attention; News Diggers reports the submission as being that he was incapable of standing trial because of illness.

The magistrate adjourned the matter to 25 November 2026 and, the Observer reports, warned that no further adjournments would be granted when the case resumes. Both outlets note that this was Nakacinda's first appearance since his release from Chimbokaila earlier in the week after serving a sentence for defaming President Hakainde Hichilema, which Lusaka Brief reported on 22 October.

The charge

The case goes back to the by-elections of November 2022. Zambia Monitor reported in August that Nakacinda had alleged the UPND was moving vehicles carrying pre-marked ballot papers to districts holding by-elections; the Observer names the districts as Lusangazi, Mkushi and Mwense. The Commission summoned him over the allegation, and the charge, as News Diggers puts it, is that he failed to comply with that summons between 4 and 7 November 2022. Zambia Monitor reported on 11 August that Magistrate Sinvula had found he had a case to answer and that the defence intended to call ten witnesses in addition to Nakacinda's own sworn evidence; the defence had then been set to open on 14 September. Friday's adjournment pushes that opening into late November.

Nakacinda has not yet given his evidence. Failing to comply with a summons is a distinct charge from the allegation that prompted it; the court has made no finding on whether the claim about ballot papers was true or false, and nothing in the reports suggests it is being asked to.

Confirmed vs. alleged

Confirmed: The adjournment to 25 November and the magistrate's warning (Zambian Observer); the ground of illness (Zambian Observer, News Diggers); the charge of failing to comply with an ECZ summons and its origin in the November 2022 allegation (News Diggers, Zambia Monitor); the earlier case-to-answer ruling and the ten planned defence witnesses (Zambia Monitor, 11 August).

Alleged or unverified: The nature of the illness, which neither report describes. Whether the defence will call all ten witnesses when the case resumes.

Sources: Zambian Observer, “Nakacinda Case Adjourned to November 25 over Illness” (23 Oct 2026); News Diggers, “Nakacinda fails to open defence due to illness” (24 Oct 2026); Zambia Monitor, “Nakacinda to call 10 witnesses in defence over summons charge” (11 Aug 2026).
