'How can we be unpopular?': in Choma, Hichilema points to 14 by-election wins, promises a dual carriageway to the south and says FRA money will arrive within 10 to 15 days

Addressing a public engagement in Choma on Friday 13 November, President Hichilema dismissed talk on social media that the UPND is losing support, citing its sweep of all 14 seats contested in the by-elections of 5 November. He promised a dual carriageway from Lusaka into Southern Province on the Lusaka–Ndola model, said farmers owed by the Food Reserve Agency would be paid within 10 to 15 days, and warned teachers and nurses who refuse rural postings that they risk their jobs. The Times of Zambia and News Diggers reported the event.

By Lusaka Brief Politics Desk • CHOMA — 15 November 2026 • 2 min read

President Hakainde Hichilema used a public engagement in Choma on Friday 13 November to answer the claim, which he said circulates on TikTok, that his party is losing popularity. News Diggers reported him asking how the United Party for National Development could be unpopular when it had won all 14 seats contested in the by-elections of 5 November. The Times of Zambia, which said thousands of residents and supporters attended, reported him describing those wins, spread across eight provinces, as a sign of public confidence, and thanking Zambians for renewing his mandate in the August general election.

The Times reported that he said his second term would concentrate on job creation through the Grow Zambia Agenda. News Diggers reported a specific promise for Southern Province: a dual carriageway linking it to Lusaka, built on the model of the Lusaka–Ndola dual carriageway.

Maize money and dams

On the maize farmers still waiting to be paid by the Food Reserve Agency, the Times of Zambia reported the President saying that outstanding payments would be made within 10 to 15 days and that the government had secured the money to clear every farmer owed across the country. He urged farmers not to sell all their maize and to keep some for the household. The paper also reported him announcing a programme of strategic dams across the country to provide water for people and livestock as a response to climate change, and saying Zambia would overcome the effects of the El Niño phenomenon.

The President's words, as the Times reported them, describe when farmers can expect to have been paid rather than when the first transfers go out; neither outlet reported how much is outstanding or how many farmers are still waiting.

A warning to public workers

Both outlets reported a pointed message to public servants. News Diggers said the President warned teachers and nurses who have refused to report to rural duty stations that they risk losing their jobs. The Times of Zambia reported him calling on teachers, nurses and agricultural officers to commit to their work, cautioning nurses against spending working hours on social media, and urging citizens to report officers who fail to deliver services. Neither outlet's visible text names the rural postings he had in mind or says how many workers have declined them.

News Diggers' account quotes the President beginning a sentence with the by-elections: "On the 5th of November, there were by-elections in eight...", the visible text ending there. The full speech has not been published by either outlet.

Sources: News Diggers, “UPND is still popular, says HH as he promises S/Province dual carriageway” (14 Nov 2026); Times of Zambia, “HH assures farmers” (14 Nov 2026).
