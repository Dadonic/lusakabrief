Politics · Term limits Developing

Reasons and another date, or an appeal to the minister: the anti-third-term consortium invokes a 2014 ruling against the police advice, as a priest, an analyst and Lubinda weigh in

The civil society groups told not to march on 25 November say they will test the police advice against the 2014 LAZ v Attorney General ruling, which requires the police to give reasons and propose another date, and will appeal to the Home Affairs Minister if no date is offered by Thursday 19 November, former LAZ president Linda Kasonde said in a statement carried by the Zambian Observer. George Chulumanda called the advice 'dictatorship in uniform'; analyst Neo Simutanyi told News Diggers the UPND should groom a successor.

By Lusaka Brief Politics Desk • LUSAKA — 19 November 2026 • 4 min read

A day after the Zambia Police Service advised both consortiums planning processions on Wednesday 25 November not to proceed, the groups defending the two-term limit have set out how they intend to answer. In a statement reported by the Zambian Observer on Wednesday 18 November, citing TV Yatu, former Law Association of Zambia president Linda Kasonde said the advice, which cited intelligence and the national security situation, amounted to a restriction of constitutional freedoms and continued a pattern of limiting public gatherings. The organisers, she said, would challenge it on legal grounds, relying on the 2014 case of LAZ v The Attorney General, in which the courts held that police who consider a demonstration unsafe must give reasons and propose an alternative date.

The statement sets a short clock. If the police do not offer an alternative date by Thursday 19 November, the organisers will appeal to the Minister of Home Affairs and Internal Security, the Observer reported. The procession was notified to the police on 4 November by a consortium including Chapter One Foundation, the Zambia Council for Social Development and Transparency International Zambia, whose joint statement that day was signed by Josiah Kalala, Leah Mitaba and Maurice Nyambe, according to the Rio Times.

The minister's answer

The minister to whom any appeal would go has already given his view. Home Affairs Minister Clement Andeleki told News Diggers he did not expect anyone to take to the streets on 25 November, for or against the limit, because President Hakainde Hichilema was not seeking a third term and was focused on development in his new five-year mandate. He urged civil society and concerned citizens to raise their concerns through established procedures rather than on the street. State House has said the campaign to remove the limit has nothing to do with the head of state, communications official Clayson Hamasaka having said this month that it began among citizens satisfied with the President's leadership, the Rio Times reported.

That answer did not satisfy George Chulumanda. The former minister, in remarks carried by the Zambian Observer on Wednesday, described the police decision as "this is dictatorship in uniform", accused the government of restricting democratic freedoms, called for the release of Brian Mundubile and Makebi Zulu, and said Zambians must defend the Constitution through peaceful action, not violence.

A priest in Mansa, a professor in Ndola

The churches continued to speak. Fr Andrew Chewe Mukosa, parish priest of St Francis Kapesha Catholic Church in Mansa, said abolishing presidential term limits would be wrong and must not be allowed, that limits give different leaders a chance and set a legal boundary on how long a president rules, and that civic leaders should be spending their energy on agriculture, energy and growth instead, the Zambian Observer reported. The Observer also republished a letter of 5 November from the Evangelical Fellowship of Zambia's executive director, Rev Allan Kasungami, calling for an immediate end to the debate and describing the two-term rule as a cornerstone that should not be altered for political expediency, a position the EFZ traces to the 2001 Oasis Declaration against Frederick Chiluba's third term. On Radio Christian Voice, the Council of Churches in Zambia's general secretary, Rev Canon James Phiri, said the current limit was appropriate.

You should groom a leader to take over from him. You should groom a leader to implement his programmes.
— Dr Neo Simutanyi, political analyst, in an interview with News Diggers published on 18 November

From Ndola, political analyst Dr Neo Simutanyi offered the ruling party a different route out of the argument. In an interview with News Diggers he advised the UPND to start grooming President Hichilema's successor, warning that tying the Grow Zambia Agenda to one person was a risk for the programme itself.

Lubinda returns

News Diggers also reported the return to public politics of Given Lubinda, the former vice-president of the Patriotic Front. Describing himself as still a political animal, Lubinda said he was back to mobilising the opposition, that several party leaders had offered him a presidential candidacy or a running-mate slot before the August poll and that he had declined because he believed the opposition needed to unite, and that he had predicted in 2022 that a divided opposition would lose in 2026. He warned the UPND that pushing the term-limits debate could harm the party. The Rio Times noted that Lubinda has called for a referendum on the question.

Citizens petitioned the National Assembly on 27 October to abolish the limit, and no date for the House to consider the petition has been confirmed, the Rio Times reported. The police have not published the intelligence behind their advice, which does not name either consortium, nor set out any consequence for marching regardless.
