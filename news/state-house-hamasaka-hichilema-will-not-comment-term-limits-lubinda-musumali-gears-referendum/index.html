'So why should he be dragged into it?': State House says Hichilema will not comment on term limits, as Lubinda demands he rule out benefiting and Musumali calls silence endorsement

Two days after the Justice Minister left three term-limit petitions to Parliament, State House spoke for itself on Sunday 15 November. Chief Communications Specialist Clayson Hamasaka said the debate belongs to citizens, that the President is focused on development, and that those demanding a comment would cry foul if he gave one. Former PF vice-president Given Lubinda has challenged Hichilema to distance himself from a third term; Socialist Party general secretary Cosmas Musumali calls standing aside indirect endorsement; GEARS wants a referendum.

By Lusaka Brief Politics Desk • LUSAKA — 16 November 2026 • 4 min read

The argument over Zambia's two-term presidential limit has now drawn a statement from State House itself, and it is a refusal to take part. In a written statement issued in Lusaka on Sunday 15 November, Clayson Hamasaka, the Chief Government Communications Specialist at State House, said President Hakainde Hichilema would not comment on the debate about removing Article 106(3) from the Constitution, because his attention is on development and on reducing poverty. The statement was reported by the Zambia Monitor and carried in full by the Zambian Observer, and News Diggers reported it on Monday 16 November alongside the opposition reaction.

Hamasaka's central point was that the campaign did not start in government. "This debate is coming from the citizens," he said in the statement, adding: "So why should he be dragged into it?" He asked what the President's critics expected him to say, and argued that if Hichilema spoke they would accuse him of restricting free expression, while the Zambian Observer's text of the statement has him saying that it would be dictatorial for a head of state to curb a constitutionally guaranteed freedom. In the Observer's account he described the attempts to pull the President into the row as unfair and as political mischief, and said Hichilema would continue to uphold the Constitution and the rule of law.

The statement then turned to the government's programme. Hamasaka said the President was concentrating on the Grow Zambia Agenda and its core targets, and named El Niño, food security and energy security as the immediate priorities, urging Zambians to unite behind the agenda rather than the constitutional argument.

What the critics want to hear

The statement answers a specific demand. News Diggers reported on Monday that Given Lubinda, the former vice-president of the Patriotic Front and a former justice minister, had challenged Hichilema to state his position on the removal of term limits and to assure Zambians that he would not be the beneficiary of any change. In an interview on Friday 13 November, according to the paper, Lubinda said that if the limit were ever removed the change should take effect only after the 2031 elections and should be put to a national referendum. The Zambian Observer, in a report on Saturday 14 November of a Facebook post by Lubinda, quoted him arguing that a parliamentary majority is not the same as the will of the people: "The will of the people must be demonstrated, not merely declared," he wrote, and he pointed to Frederick Chiluba's rejection of a third-term campaign in 2001 as the precedent the President should follow. Opposition leaders and civil society groups have been asking Hichilema for his position.

Dr Cosmas Musumali, the general secretary of the Socialist Party, went further. Responding to a News Diggers press query on Saturday 14 November, he said that by declaring it would not interfere with the petitions the government had indirectly endorsed them, and he called the claim of non-interference "a textbook exercise in political deflection". In the same report, Luckson Lungu, the board chairperson of the GEARS Initiative, said the removal of presidential term limits should be subject to a referendum rather than left to Parliament alone.

The exchange follows the press briefing on Friday 13 November at which Justice Minister Jack Mwiimbu said three petitions asking the National Assembly to remove Article 106(3), the two-term limit, had been submitted by members of the public, political leaders and civil society organisations, that the government would not interfere, and that the Assembly was the appropriate institution to consider them, as the Times of Zambia and News Diggers reported. The Zambian Observer's account of Sunday's statement recalled that the petitions have been promoted by some UPND supporters, including nominated members of Parliament, who cite the President's record, while opposition parties, church bodies and civil society groups have warned that removing the limit could weaken Zambia's democracy.

A different proposal

Not every intervention at the weekend was for or against the petitions as drafted. The Zambian Observer on Sunday 15 November carried remarks by the economist Dr Lubinda Haabazoka suggesting that Zambia should consider cutting the presidential term from five years to four, rather than discussing a longer one, and testing whether the ruling party would accept that alternative. Zambia has had a five-year presidential term since independence in 1964.

Who has said what

  • Friday 13 November — Justice Minister Jack Mwiimbu: three petitions; government will not interfere; Parliament decides.
  • Friday 13 and Saturday 14 November — Given Lubinda (PF): the President should state his position and distance himself from a third term; any change after 2031 and by referendum.
  • Saturday 14 November — Cosmas Musumali (Socialist Party): non-interference is indirect endorsement. Luckson Lungu (GEARS): put it to a referendum.
  • Sunday 15 November — Clayson Hamasaka (State House): the debate is the citizens'; the President will not comment.

Confirmed vs. alleged

Confirmed: State House issued a statement on Sunday 15 November, in the name of Clayson Hamasaka, saying the President will not comment on the term-limit debate; the Zambia Monitor, the Zambian Observer and News Diggers all reported it. Lubinda's challenge was reported by News Diggers and the Zambian Observer; Musumali's response and the GEARS call for a referendum by News Diggers. Mwiimbu's Friday briefing, and the three petitions, were reported by the Times of Zambia and News Diggers.

Alleged or unverified: Whether President Hichilema would sign an amendment if Parliament passed one is unknown: State House has declined to say, and the reports of the Justice Minister's Friday briefing do not record his personal position. How many signatures the three petitions carry, and when the National Assembly will consider them, has not been reported.
