The eve of the must-win: a recovery session, a quiet stadium and a Tuesday night that could define the qualifying campaign

Twenty-four hours before Togo arrive at Levy Mwanawasa Stadium, Zambia's squad is managing its wounds from Algeria and the federation is still selling tickets. The table is not complicated: the visitors are level at the top, the hosts are bottom, and November's fixtures get very dark if Tuesday goes wrong.

By Mwelwa Phiri • NDOLA — 28 September 2026 • 4 min read

There was no media theatre in Ndola on Sunday — no bold declarations, no fresh call-ups, just a recovery session for the legs that came back from Tizi Ouzou and the quiet of a stadium waiting to find out what it is hosting. Tuesday night is either a restart or a reckoning. Zambia, bottom of Group I with no points after Friday's 3-1 defeat in Algeria, host Togo — who beat Burundi 1-0 in Lomé to sit level with the Algerians at the top — at Levy Mwanawasa Stadium at 18:00 CAT.

The squad bulletin remains interim coach George Lwandamina's daily puzzle. Kings Kangwa is serving the four-match CAF ban that covers this entire campaign window. Gamphani Lungu, who travelled with a hamstring strain and was given ten days to recover, is expected to be available; the variable is how many of Friday's walking wounded join him on the teamsheet. The attacking reference points from Algeria are intact — Fashion Sakala's 57th-minute equaliser was the captain's answer to a difficult week, and Patson Daka's half-time introduction sharpened the front line — but the three goals conceded in Kabylie were the kind Lwandamina described as mistakes punished, and his midweek selections will be read for what they say about trust in his defence.

The federation's job is the stands. Tickets remained on sale through the weekend on the TechPay platform and with agents in Ndola, with FAZ's Copperbelt structures — chairperson Patrick Ndhlovu has been rallying support under a "Let's fill up Levy" push — working to turn a qualifying match into the home advantage the table says Zambia need. The statistic following this team is nine matches without a win. The arithmetic beneath it is colder: a tenth on Tuesday would leave Zambia needing results in November's double-header against Burundi and beyond, with the group's leaders already out of sight.

It is worth remembering what Friday actually was — a young side, rebuilt almost on the fly, level with Algeria approaching the hour and beaten by the details. "We can actually change the tables," Lwandamina said on Saturday, and he has spent the days since saying it quietly, to his players, in training. Tuesday is when he finds out who was listening.
