Day one at K31.46: the commuter arithmetic of a 24-per-cent jump, a kwacha at K19.61 — and December's VAT question

The first full day of the new pump prices dawned on the ranks and the filling stations with fares yet to move. But the arithmetic has moved: at long-distance consumption rates, the diesel line alone adds about K820 to a one-way Lusaka–Ndola run — and the VAT reinstatement dated for 1 December is still in the post.

By Natasha Banda • LUSAKA — 2 October 2026 • 4 min read

The queues at the ranks this morning looked like any Friday's, which is precisely the story: the price changed at midnight on Wednesday, and the fares had not followed by sunrise on Friday. Transporters were still counting. What they are counting is no longer in dispute — petrol at K31.46 a litre, diesel at K33.27, up by almost a quarter in one review after the excise-duty waiver died at midnight — and what they will do with it is now the only question that matters to the commuter.

The distance arithmetic is brutal in its simplicity. At the 2.5 kilometres per litre that long-haul operators quote for a loaded bus or truck, the K6.41 diesel differential adds about K820 to a one-way Lusaka–Ndola run of 320 kilometres, about K920 to the 359-kilometre Lusaka–Kitwe leg, and more than K4,100 to the 1,623 kilometres to Johannesburg. Those are this newspaper's calculations from the ERB's published differentials, not an announced fare schedule — but they are the numbers sitting in every operator's spreadsheet this morning, and the reason nobody at the rank believes the quiet will last the weekend.

Two numbers in the background are pulling against each other. The kwacha traded at a Bank of Zambia mid-rate of K19.61 to the dollar on Thursday — firmer than the K19.76 the ERB used in its price build-up, a small mercy that trims the import side of the next review if it holds. And pulling the other way is the calendar: the zero-rating of VAT on fuel expires on 1 December, meaning the October jump is, on the Treasury's current course, only the first of the season's two adjustments. The ERB's smoothing mechanism now carries the full load alone for eight weeks.

The city offered its own footnote to the morning. The town-bound carriageway of the Great East Road reopens today, the Lusaka City Council has announced — a small mercy for the commuter stream, and a reminder that the cost story and the congestion story are the same story told at different speeds. The waiver era is over; the era of paying full freight has begun with a Friday.
