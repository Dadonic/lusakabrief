Health · Cholera

Four deaths in a day and 82 new suspected cases: the cholera toll reaches 75 as the count passes 3,250, and Mpongwe opens a treatment centre with 26 patients

Zambia recorded 82 new suspected cholera cases and four deaths in 24 hours, News Diggers reported on Thursday 19 November, citing the Zambia National Public Health Institute: 3,258 suspected cases since the outbreak was declared on 28 July, 464 of them confirmed, 75 deaths, 98 patients in hospital and 3,085 discharged. Three days earlier Zambia Monitor had reported 3,099 cases, 68 deaths and 27 districts affected across seven provinces. On the Copperbelt, Mpongwe's district health director said a dedicated centre was treating 26 patients.

By Lusaka Brief Health Desk • LUSAKA — 20 November 2026 • 2 min read

The cholera outbreak declared on 28 July has entered its most lethal stretch in weeks. In the 24 hours to Thursday 19 November, the country recorded 82 new suspected cases and four deaths, News Diggers reported that morning, citing the Zambia National Public Health Institute (ZNPHI). Eighteen of the new cases have since been confirmed by laboratory. The cumulative count now stands at 3,258 suspected cases, of which 464 are confirmed; 75 people have died; 98 patients are in hospital; and 3,085 have recovered and been discharged. The figures beyond the opening paragraphs of the Diggers report sit behind that outlet's subscription.

The trend is clear when set against Zambia Monitor's report of Monday 16 November, based on the ZNPHI update of that day: 3,099 cumulative cases, 437 confirmed, 68 deaths, 101 patients under treatment and 2,930 discharged, with 74 new cases and two deaths in the preceding 24 hours. Between the two updates, three days apart, the case count rose by 159 and the death toll by seven. A week earlier still, on 12 November, Diggers had reported 2,658 suspected cases and 56 deaths.

Where the cases are

Zambia Monitor's 16 November report listed 27 affected districts across seven provinces. Lusaka District accounted for 1,959 of the 3,099 cases, followed by Chibombo with 371, Chongwe 158, Chilanga 130, Mpulungu 90, Chiengi and Mwansabombwe 86 each, and Mpongwe 61. Smaller numbers were recorded in Nsama, Nchelenge, Mwense, Mumbwa, Monze, Kaputa, Pemba, Chisamba, Kafue, Kitwe, Shibuyunji, Mbala, Sinazongwe, Solwezi, Chikankata, Livingstone, Kalomo, Mazabuka and Ndola, the last three with a single case each. ZNPHI's advice, as the report carried it, was that anyone with watery diarrhoea or vomiting should go to the nearest health facility immediately, and that households should wash hands with soap, drink boiled or treated water, cook food thoroughly and use clean toilets.

On the Copperbelt, the picture in Mpongwe has changed within a week. On 12 November, Diggers reported that the provincial health director, Dr Charles Mwinuna, had refuted reports of two suspected cholera deaths in the district and said the province had recorded only three cases. By Thursday, the same outlet was quoting Mpongwe's district health director, Dr Evaristo Kunka, as saying the district had admitted 26 cholera patients at a dedicated treatment centre. "We have created a cholera centre and we have about 26 patients...", he said, in the extract available to non-subscribers.

How the outbreak has grown

When Health Minister Professor Roma Chilengi gave Parliament a ministerial statement on Thursday 29 October, Zambia Monitor reported, the outbreak stood at 1,622 cases, 228 confirmed, and 30 deaths across 20 districts in six provinces; 16 of those deaths had occurred in communities before the patients reached a health facility. The minister told MPs that 709,018 people had received oral cholera vaccine, 91 per cent of the target population, out of 782,147 doses approved by the International Coordinating Group, and that the Treasury had released K13 million for emergency water supply. In the three weeks since, the case count has doubled and the death toll has risen two and a half times. No updated vaccination figure appears in this week's reports.
