A 180-megawatt plant for a province on a 1972 line: Stanbic and the DBSA are mandated to raise the money for Ngonye Falls, with construction pencilled in for 2027

Western Power Company has signed a mandate with Stanbic Bank Zambia and the Development Bank of Southern Africa to structure and arrange the debt for its run-of-river hydropower project on the Zambezi, Zambia Monitor reported on Monday 9 November. The developer calls it the last step before financial close. The outlets disagree on the project's size: the Monitor puts it at US$600 million, International Water Power at US$800 million. Western Province currently depends on a 66kV line built in 1972.

By Lusaka Brief Business Desk • LIVINGSTONE — 10 November 2026 • 2 min read

The Ngonye Falls hydropower project, planned for years on the Zambezi in Western Province, has its lenders' arrangers. Western Power Company (WPC) signed a mandate agreement with Stanbic Bank Zambia and the Development Bank of Southern Africa (DBSA) at the Zim-Zam Energy Summit in Livingstone, making the two institutions responsible for structuring and raising the debt for the scheme, Zambia Monitor reported on Monday 9 November, citing a statement issued that day. International Water Power, which reported the signing on 4 November, listed three co-mandated lead arrangers: Standard Bank South Africa, Stanbic Bank Zambia and the DBSA.

The plant is a 180-megawatt run-of-river scheme. According to the Monitor, the financing will cover both the power station and a 110-kilometre, 220-kilovolt transmission line to connect it to the national grid, in a province that currently relies on a 66kV line built in 1972. The two reports differ on the overall cost: Zambia Monitor describes a US$600 million project, International Water Power a US$800 million development. Neither explains the gap, which may reflect whether the transmission line and development costs are counted.

The Ngonye Falls Hydropower Project is an important investment in Zambia's energy future.
— Sherill Byrne, Head of Project Finance at Standard Bank, in the statement reported by Zambia Monitor on Monday 9 November

Who is behind it and when it would run

The project is being developed jointly by African Power Projects, the Private Infrastructure Development Group and Lunzua Power, the Monitor said; WPC's managing director, Tom Younger, also thanked the Barotse Royal Establishment and PIDG's InfraCo arm for their backing, International Water Power reported. Construction is expected to begin in 2027 once financial close and the remaining development work are complete, and the Monitor said the build should take just under four years, which would put commissioning around 2031. Mr Younger said WPC looked forward to working with the arrangers to "conclude the financing process in the coming months".

WPC says the plant will supply mining companies, industry, commercial customers and households, create more than 100 permanent jobs and improve the reliability of supply across Western Province, where the 1972 line has long been a constraint. International Water Power noted that the scheme holds Africa's first Gold certification under the Hydropower Sustainability Standard. Ms Byrne said hydropower offered renewable baseload power and that partnerships between developers, commercial lenders, development finance institutions and local communities were essential to meeting the country's long-term energy needs.

Ngonye Falls at a glance

  • Capacity — 180MW, run-of-river, on the Zambezi in Western Province.
  • Cost — US$600 million (Zambia Monitor) or US$800 million (International Water Power).
  • Grid link — a 110km, 220kV line, replacing dependence on a 66kV line built in 1972.
  • Arrangers — Stanbic Bank Zambia and the DBSA, with Standard Bank South Africa also named by International Water Power.
  • Timeline — financial close in the coming months, construction from 2027, just under four years to build.

Sources: Zambia Monitor, “Stanbic, DBSA arrange financing for $600 million Ngonye hydropower project” (9 Nov 2026); International Water Power, “Ngonye Falls hydropower project appoints financing arrangers” (4 Nov 2026).
