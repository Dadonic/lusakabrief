Friday at Parliament: Hichilema opens the 14th National Assembly — and the country listens for the second term's real agenda

President Hichilema will officially open the First Session of the 14th National Assembly on Friday, setting out the Government's policy direction and priorities for 2026–2027. It is his first address to a House his party dominates — with a budget framework, a drought forecast and a restive opposition all waiting in the foyer.

By Chanda Mwape • LUSAKA — 7 October 2026 • 5 min read

The trumpets have a date. President Hakainde Hichilema will officially open the First Session of the 14th National Assembly on Friday, 9 October, at Parliament Buildings in Lusaka, delivering the address that sets out the Government's policy direction and priorities for 2026–2027. The timetable was confirmed by Clerk of the National Assembly Loveness Mayaka, with members then due to consider the policy pronouncements in the speech.

It will be a different House from the one he last addressed. The 13 August election returned a National Assembly in which the ruling UPND holds a commanding position, presiding officers were elected on 2 October — Nelly Mutti returned as Speaker unopposed — and the first members chosen under the new Mixed Member Proportional Representation system took their seats in September. Friday's address is therefore the first time the re-elected President speaks to a legislature assembled entirely on his second-term mandate.

The content has a week of context to answer. The Treasury has just put a K271.9 billion spending framework for 2027 before public hearings, conceding that growth this year will reach 5.3 percent against the 6.4 percent projected a year ago; a "supersized" El Niño forecast hangs over the planting season; and the Grow Zambia Agenda's production targets — three million tonnes of copper, ten million tonnes of maize — sit far above last year's 890,346 tonnes of copper reality. Add the reconciliation question pressed by the Catholic bishops and a growing list of opposition figures, and the address has more than one audience: the House in front of him, and a country keeping score outside it.

The formalities will be ceremonial; the reading will be political. Every promise in Friday's speech will be measured against the budget that follows it, and every omission will be catalogued by an opposition that currently conducts much of its politics through court filings. The 14th Assembly opens with the rituals of continuity. What the President says from its podium will define whether the second term begins as a fresh mandate — or a continuation under new management.
