'We don't arrest on rumours': the ACC says it is investigating seven Auditor General queries and six FIC referrals, as suspected illicit flows fall to US$1.8 billion

The Anti-Corruption Commission's Director General, Daphne Pauline Soko Chabu, says the Commission is investigating seven complaints from the 2025 Auditor General's report and six cases referred by the Financial Intelligence Centre, one of which, over K2 million meant for school feeding, has already produced five arrests. She also noted that suspected illicit financial flows fell from US$3.5 billion in 2024 to US$1.8 billion in 2025, and that an audit query is not in itself proof of corruption.

By Lusaka Brief Politics Desk • LUSAKA — 6 November 2026 • 2 min read

The Anti-Corruption Commission has set out what it is doing with the two documents that dominated last week's conversation about public money, the 2025 Auditor General's report and the Financial Intelligence Centre's 2025 Trends Report. In a statement issued in Lusaka on Wednesday 4 November, reported by Zambia Monitor that afternoon and described by News Diggers on Thursday as a press briefing, the Director General, Daphne Pauline Soko Chabu, said the Commission is investigating seven corruption-related complaints arising from the audit report and six cases referred to it by the FIC in 2025.

Of the six FIC referrals, one has been concluded. According to Zambia Monitor, it involved more than K2 million intended for the school feeding programme and led to the arrest of five people, including the Chama District Board Secretary and bank officials; the other five remain under investigation. News Diggers put the same case at over K2 million and five arrests. Lusaka Brief has not seen the charge sheets and does not name the accused.

The trend the Commission welcomed

The Director General drew attention to two falling numbers. Zambia Monitor reported her saying that unresolved audit findings dropped from 33 in 2024 to 27 at the end of the 2025 audits, which she called "an encouraging development", and that suspected illicit financial flows fell from US$3.5 billion in 2024 to US$1.8 billion in 2025. News Diggers reported that she nonetheless regarded US$1.8 billion as still a great deal of money.

A query is not a charge

Much of the statement, as both outlets reported it, was about process. She said audit queries and irregularities do not automatically amount to corruption, though they can signal weaknesses that lead to abuse of office or misappropriation, and that being named in an audit or FIC report does not make a person guilty. News Diggers summarised her position in its headline: the Commission does not arrest on rumours, but "first conducts investigations and only takes action after gathering evidence". Zambia Monitor quoted her saying that "the Commission is required to conduct thorough investigations, establish the facts and gather sufficient evidence" before acting, and that earlier FIC-linked investigations have already led to arrests and prosecutions.

The statement follows the FIC's own account, carried in Lusaka Brief this week, of a K1 billion mineral smuggling syndicate and a company that functioned as a payment channel for K222 million and US$5.7 million in three months. The Commission did not say whether either of those case studies is among the six referrals.

Sources: Zambia Monitor, “Anti-corruption agency probes queries from Auditor General's report” (4 Nov 2026); News Diggers, “We don't arrest on rumours, says ACC as it probes 13 AG, FIC cases” (5 Nov 2026).
