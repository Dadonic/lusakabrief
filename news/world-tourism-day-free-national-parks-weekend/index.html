The gates are open: Zambia's national parks are free to enter this weekend — and today is the last day

To mark World Tourism Day, the Ministry of Tourism waived entry fees at all 20 national parks on 26 and 27 September for citizens and school groups — a two-day invitation to see the estate the country keeps selling to the world.

By Lusaka Brief Culture Desk • LUSAKA — 27 September 2026 • 3 min read

For one weekend a year, the maths of a Zambian safari changes. The Ministry of Tourism opened all 20 of the country's national parks to free entry on Saturday and Sunday — citizens and educational tours alike — as Zambia's nod to World Tourism Day, marked globally on 27 September.

The waiver covers the headliners and the overlooked in equal measure: South Luangwa's leopard country, the Kafue's vastness, the Mosi-oa-Tunya gate above the Falls, and the quieter circuit — North Luangwa, the Bangweulu wetlands' neighbours, the western floodplain parks — that domestic tourism campaigns spend the rest of the year trying to put on family itineraries. Park authorities advised visitors to carry identification and to expect standard rules at the gates even where the fee is waived.

The gesture is small and the strategy behind it is not. Tourism is one of the sectors the government names when it talks about diversifying an economy still priced in copper, and the domestic market is the industry's shock-absorber: the lodges and camps that survive lean international seasons do so on Zambian weekenders. Two free days will not reorder the sector. But a child who sees a giraffe this Sunday is the constituency that sustains it in twenty years — and the cheapest marketing the ministry will buy all year is an open gate.

Sources: Ministry of Tourism announcement on World Tourism Day free entry, 26–27 September 2026; ZNBC / Daily Times notices (26 Sep 2026).
