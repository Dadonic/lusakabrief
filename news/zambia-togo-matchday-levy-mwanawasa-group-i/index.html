Six o'clock in Ndola: a winless streak, a quiet coach's promise and the night that decides what this qualifying campaign is

Zambia host Togo at Levy Mwanawasa Stadium at 18:00 CAT — bottom of Group I against joint-top, nine matches without a win, and a coach who says the table can still be changed. By 20:00, the campaign has either restarted or gone very quiet.

By Mwelwa Phiri • NDOLA — 29 September 2026 • 4 min read

The tickets sold through the weekend, the team trained behind its usual curtains, and the coach kept his powder dry. Tonight at 18:00 CAT, in front of what the Football Association of Zambia hopes is a full Levy Mwanawasa Stadium, Zambia play Togo in a fixture the table has already turned into a small final: the hosts bottom of Group I with no points, the visitors joint-top with three after beating Burundi in Lomé, and Algeria — Zambia's conquerors on Friday — playing away in Bujumbura in the evening's other match.

George Lwandamina's public line has not shifted since Tizi Ouzou. "Definitely, we need to win our home games if we are to qualify," he said after Friday's 3-1 defeat. "Togo won't have it easy." The private arithmetic is colder. A tenth consecutive match without a win tonight would leave Zambia chasing the group's leaders into November's double-header with Burundi, with the margin for error already spent in Kabylie — where a young side levelled through captain Fashion Sakala on 57 minutes and then conceded twice in five, to goals Lwandamina called mistakes punished.

The selection questions are the same ones that travelled home from Algeria. Kings Kangwa is absent for the whole window under his CAF ban. Gamphani Lungu, who carried a hamstring strain into the camp, was expected to be fit; the real variable is how many of Friday's knocks cleared in time, and whether Patson Daka — a half-time introduction in Tizi Ouzou — starts beside Sakala rather than behind him. History offers a warning rather than a comfort: the recorded senior meetings between these sides run eight, with Zambia winning three and Togo five, and not a single draw. Someone wins tonight. The streak says it is overdue being Zambia; the table says it has to be.

There is a wider mood at stake, and it would be dishonest to pretend otherwise. The country's week is tense — an archbishop in the courts, a tax decision pending at midnight tomorrow, pavements being cleared in the capital. Football does not fix any of it. But a full stadium in Ndola on a Tuesday night, singing at a team that has given them little to sing about for a year, is its own kind of civic argument. Lwandamina has spent four days telling his players, quietly, that tables can be changed. At 18:00 he finds out whether they believe him.
