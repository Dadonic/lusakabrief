'A difference between a third term and a term limit': the UPND answers Khama's 'last move' jibe, Ezra Tembo's NRPUP backs the status quo, AIPAC says a petition cannot do it alone

UPND media director Mark Simuuwe said on Tuesday that the proposal before Parliament would remove the ceiling on presidential terms rather than add a third, that voters should decide how long a leader stays, and that former Botswana president Ian Khama had misunderstood it. Khama had said at the weekend that Hichilema was making his last move to consolidate power without time limit. Leader of the Opposition Ezra Tembo said his party backs the current limit, and the Acton Institute said Article 79 cannot be sidestepped by a petition.

By Lusaka Brief Politics Desk • LUSAKA — 18 November 2026 • 3 min read

The argument over Article 106(3) of the Constitution, which bars anyone who has twice held the office of President from standing again, widened on Tuesday 17 November to take in the ruling party's media director, the Leader of the Opposition, a policy institute and one of the country's best-known civic voices.

Mark Simuuwe, the UPND's media director, drew a distinction that the party has not made so plainly before. According to the Zambian Observer, he said the proposal being petitioned for would not create a third term but would remove the limit entirely, leaving it to voters to decide how long a leader remains in office. He added that President Hakainde Hichilema had issued no formal statement seeking removal, and accused former Botswana president Ian Khama of misunderstanding the proposal, saying it should be debated on its merits.

What Khama said

Khama's latest remarks were reported by the Zambian Observer on Monday 16 November, in reaction to Justice Minister Jack Mwiimbu's statement that the government would not interfere with the petitions. Khama said Zambia's leaders were setting up their move to remove presidential term limits, that the President had bloated Parliament with his party so the change would pass without doubt, and, in the phrase that drew the UPND response, that the country's dictator was making his last move to consolidate power without time limit. The paper did not say on which platform he spoke. The paper noted it was the second time he had publicly called Hichilema a dictator; State House has responded to him before, and News Diggers' editorial on Tuesday argued that the right answer to him is restraint rather than a reply in kind.

Our Dictator is making his last move to consolidate power without time limit.
— Ian Khama, former President of Botswana, as quoted by the Zambian Observer on 16 November

The opposition and the institute

Ezra Tembo, president of the National Reconciliation Party for Unity and Prosperity and Leader of the Opposition in Parliament, said in a press statement on Monday 16 November that his party supports retaining the present five-year, two-term arrangement, that constitutional matters should be handled with transparency and respect for democratic principles, and that the party would not engage in what he called speculative debates on behalf of an Executive that has not stated its position, the Zambian Observer reported.

The Acton Institute for Policy Analysis Centre took a procedural line. Its executive director, Solomon Ngoma, said in a statement in Lusaka on Monday that citizens, youth movements, civil society and parties all have a right to petition Parliament, but that a petition to the Speaker cannot by itself change the term limit: any amendment must follow Article 79 of the Constitution and the Constitutional Court's guidance, and where a referendum is required it cannot be replaced by a petition, a parliamentary vote or a press statement. He said reforms must be preceded by broad, people-driven consultation, and urged the government to concentrate on growth, jobs and living conditions, Zambia Monitor reported.

No leader lives forever. Leadership is temporary, but constitutions are meant to endure and serve future generations.
— Solomon Ngoma, executive director, Acton Institute for Policy Analysis Centre, in a statement reported by Zambia Monitor on 17 November

From the civic side, the activist Laura Miti wrote in a piece carried by the Zambian Observer on Tuesday that the President should say plainly whether he supports the campaign rather than, in her words, going round corners, and argued that his defence of Bill 7, which enlarged Parliament, was the first step towards it. Her view that the bill's purpose was to secure the numbers for a third term is her own; the government has maintained that the petitions are a citizens' initiative with nothing to do with the head of State, and the UPND's position, as set out by Simuuwe, is that no such proposal has come from the President.

The Law Association of Zambia's warning on Tuesday that it will litigate to defend the limit, and the police advice against both marches planned for 25 November, are reported separately in this edition.
