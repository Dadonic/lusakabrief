Opinion

The clause that died at the table

Whatever Thursday's ceremony looks like, its most important feature is what will not be in the document. A middle-income country was told that health money walked with mining access and patient specimens, said no — twice — and is signing anyway, on terms its own minister can defend in public. That is not anti-Americanism; it is negotiation. The lesson travels beyond this pact: sovereignty is not a speech, it is a bill of particulars, itemised clause by clause. Two cautions. The signed text must be published, in full, so that 'no mention of critical minerals' becomes a fact rather than a quote. And the US$2.1 billion Zambia has pledged from its own side must be budgeted honestly, not assumed. A win at the table is only a win if the clinics can find the money afterwards.

Opinion

A budget that confesses must also connect

Give the Treasury this much: the 2027 framework does not hide its miss. Growth of 5.3 percent against the 6.4 percent promised, copper at 890,346 tonnes against a million targeted — the confession is printed in the same document as the remedy. But the remedy is a ladder: 1.08 million tonnes in 2027, 1.4 million by 2029, leaning on mine expansions that need power, water and rail slots the same corridor meetings are still negotiating. And the state plans to borrow K44.9 billion while it waits — up nearly a third in one year, mostly from the same domestic market its private sector borrows from. The hearings now touring the country should ask one question in every hall: what exactly switches on first — the mines, the power, or the debt?

Opinion

The witness and the twenty-two spare papers

Strip the drama from the Ndola Central petition and a quieter scandal remains: a teacher of nineteen years, appointed on 12 August, trained 'shortly before' the election, and left unable to reconcile 950 ballot papers against 928 voters in her own stream. Elections are not run by commissions in Lusaka; they are run by thousands of ordinary people at folding tables, and their training is the democracy. Twenty-two spare ballot papers at one stream is precisely the kind of discrepancy a petition exists to test — and precisely the kind a competent reconciliation form should have answered on the night. Whatever the court decides about Chibolele, the ECZ's homework is already marked: train the presiding officers as if a courtroom will one day ask them to count. It will.
