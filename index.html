'Not to proceed': police cite intelligence in telling two consortiums to call off their 25 November term-limit marches, as Andeleki says there is nothing to protest

Economy · Mining

'A commitment, not a wish list': ZCCM-IH plans US$2.7 billion over ten years to become a US$7 billion company, with 3,022 MW of power, more of Mingomba and US$704 million of debt

At its Shareholder Day in Lusaka on Monday 16 November, the State mining investor unveiled a 2026–2035 plan to lift its value from about US$1.2 billion to US$7 billion and annual revenue from US$211 million to US$738 million. It intends to raise its Mingomba stake from 20 to 25 per cent, build a 3,022 MW generation portfolio, open gold and manganese buying centres and ring-fence exploration money. Funding is planned, not secured: US$704 million of debt, US$300 million of equity and US$1–1.7 billion from partners, after a K4.92 billion group loss in 2025.

Health · Cholera

'Commodity sovereignty is not an option': ground is broken on the ZAMVAX cholera vaccine plant in Lusaka South, as the outbreak it is meant to answer reaches 3,099 cases

Health Minister Roma Chilengi and China's Wang Sheng broke ground on Tuesday 17 November at the Lusaka South Multi-Facility Economic Zone for a plant that will fill, finish and pack up to 20 million oral cholera vaccine doses a year from imported bulk antigen. ZAMVAX is 60 per cent owned by the Industrial Development Corporation and 40 per cent by China's Jijia; phase one was costed at US$24.13 million within a US$213 million project signed on 2 November. The ZNPHI's 16 November update put the outbreak at 3,099 cases and 68 deaths.

Opinion & Analysis

Analysis

Advice is not a ban, and that is the point

The police statement of 17 November is carefully worded. It does not prohibit the 25 November processions; it advises the two consortiums not to proceed, citing intelligence it does not describe and a security situation it does not define. The organisers notified the police rather than asking for permission, so the question before them is practical as much as legal: do they march into a situation the police have said they cannot secure, and what happens if they do? The civil society groups have the stronger grievance, having been told first by a minister and now by the police to stay home while a youth procession reached Parliament earlier this month; the youth consortium, for its part, has now been given the same advice. The government's position, restated by the Home Affairs Minister, is that there is nothing to march about because the President is not seeking a third term. Its critics reply that the petitions exist regardless. Watch for a formal response from either consortium, any application to the High Court before the 25th, and whether the National Assembly fixes a date to consider the petitions. The street and the chamber are now on different clocks.

Analysis

ZCCM-IH's plan is a prospectus, and prospectuses need buyers

The 2026–2035 strategy is the most detailed public statement of intent ZCCM-IH has made in years, and its numbers should be read with its own caveat attached: the funding is planned, not secured. Of US$2.7 billion, about US$700 million is to be borrowed, US$300 million raised as equity and US$1 billion to US$1.7 billion found from partners and development lenders. The company's recurring income is real and growing, with investment income up 40 per cent in 2025 on the Kansanshi royalty and power dividends, but the group still booked a K4.92 billion loss once the previous year's Mopani gain fell away and the Trafigura provision was taken. The strategic logic is coherent: swap uncertain dividends for royalty streams, use those streams to fund exploration and power, and buy more of Mingomba before it produces. The risks are equally plain. Mingomba will not be in production by the 2031 copper deadline, royalty income tracks the copper price, and partner money arrives only if the partners like the terms. What to watch: the first borrowing, the terms of any new royalty deal, and whether the London listing is revived.

Analysis

Five points, third place and a captain gone

Two draws against the bottom side in Group I have cost Zambia more than the points they dropped. They began the window second; they end it third on five points, behind a Togo side that has just beaten Algeria 3–0. The reports available do not set out the qualification rules for the 2027 tournament or the dates of the last two rounds, so this desk will not say what Zambia need; what it can say is that the margin for error is gone and that the next fixtures are against the two teams above them. Two other things changed on Tuesday. The coach has lost his captain, with Fashion Sakala retiring hours after the match, and Mathews Banda to an injury whose extent is not yet reported. The coach has spoken of a building process; the table has stopped waiting for it. Watch for the fixture dates, for the appointment of a new captain, and for whether the Football Association addresses Sakala's departure publicly, which neither report on Tuesday night recorded it doing.
