Economy · Prices

Inflation edges up to 6.3 per cent as diesel jumps 24 per cent in a month, and September's trade balance swings to a US$186 million deficit on weaker copper exports

ZamStats' October bulletin, released in Lusaka on Thursday 29 October, puts annual inflation at 6.3 per cent, up from 6.1, with food inflation easing to 5.7 per cent and non-food rising to 7.4. The price table explains why: diesel up 23.8 per cent and petrol up 24.4 per cent in a month, while a 25 kg bag of breakfast meal is a tenth cheaper than a year ago. Exports fell 8.1 per cent in September and imports rose 5.4 per cent, turning August's US$48 million surplus into a US$186.4 million deficit as refined copper earnings dropped 15 per cent.

Opinion & Analysis

Analysis

What 5 November will actually decide, and what it will not

The bail hearing has now consumed four sittings without a word of argument, and the court's reported warning that it will rule on the papers if either side is not ready changes the incentives. The State's affidavit is already filed; the applicants' reply is already filed. On 5 November the judges can decide on documents alone, which means neither side can buy more time by not being ready. The question before them is narrow: whether detention of about ten weeks without a trial date, on charges that carry life imprisonment, violates the applicants' constitutional rights enough to require release or bail. The State's case is that treason and terrorism investigations take time and that two months is short by the standards of the cases it cites; the applicants' case is that investigating after charging is not a lawful reason to hold anyone. Whatever the court decides, it will not decide guilt. A grant of bail does not weaken the charges; a refusal does not strengthen them. The dates for the substantive petition, which the court says will follow the bail ruling, are where the case itself begins. What to watch on the day is whether the State opposes a ruling on the papers, and whether the court sets those dates.

Analysis

Three seats, no votes: what unopposed means in Mandevu, Lumezi and Mbala

If the ECZ confirms the withdrawals, the November by-elections will have produced two MPs and a mayor without a ballot being cast. The two accounts of why are not reconcilable from the reporting so far. The UPND's position, stated before the Mandevu letters were written, is that opposition parties cannot fund campaigns and that candidates leave when the support does not come; one of the three Mandevu withdrawals is reported to cite exactly that. The Tonse Alliance's position is that its candidate was threatened from nomination day, and his withdrawal letter, as News Diggers reports it, says the threats reached his family. The police promised an investigation on 23 October and have reported nothing since. Both things can be true of different candidates, and neither has been tested. What is testable is procedure. The ECZ has not yet declared Mandevu, and how it does so matters: the Mbala and Lumezi declarations followed the same route, and the Commission's conduct of the August count is the reason Mandevu is voting again at all. The other open question is Mapatizya, where a court has reportedly just found the Commission's post-voting conduct wanting in a different constituency. A by-election there would be the next test of whether contests in Zambia are still decided at the ballot box.

Analysis

A fuel-driven 6.3 per cent lands on a budget that is still being written

The October numbers are not alarming on their own: 6.3 per cent is inside the Bank of Zambia's target band and food inflation is still falling. The composition is the point. Non-food inflation jumped from 6.6 to 7.4 per cent in a month because diesel and petrol rose by roughly a quarter after October's fuel price increases, and transport inflation nearly doubled. That is a one-off step up in the price level, but it is also exactly the kind of pass-through that a weaker kwacha, now around K20.30 to the dollar, feeds. The September trade deficit, driven by a 15 per cent drop in refined copper earnings, shows the other channel. Two decisions sit in the next four weeks. The budget on 6 November will say how much of the K19.9 billion supplementary spending and next year's deficit is to be borrowed at home, which economists quoted this week argue pushes up interest rates. The Monetary Policy Committee on 25 and 26 November will decide whether September's 250-basis-point cut still fits a currency at a one-year low and non-food prices accelerating. The case for holding is strong on today's data; the case for reversing is not yet made. November's figures, with a full month of the new fuel prices, will be the ones that matter.
