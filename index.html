Fourteen disappearances and 71 arrests: Human Rights Watch calls for an end to the post-election crackdown, as State House answers Ian Khama and the treason bail ruling nears

In a report released on Sunday 1 November, Human Rights Watch says Zambian police and soldiers forcibly disappeared, detained, beat and threatened opposition members, lawyers, journalists and critics around the 13 August election, and that Mutotwe Kafwaya's killing has still not been credibly explained. The 'wandering zone': the Bank of Zambia says a security thread that sits in a slightly different place does not make a banknote counterfeit, and lists the features to check

In a public notice on Thursday 29 October the central bank explained that the thread is allowed to move within a defined band during production, so that its extra thickness does not build a ridge in a stack of notes. The eagle image can shift slightly too. The bank's assistant director for communications, Besnat Mwanza, urged people to use several features, the watermark, the see-through register, raised print and colour-shifting ink, rather than the thread alone, and pointed to its free Zambian Banknotes app as a guide, not an authenticator.

Analysis

What a Human Rights Watch report does and does not change

The report published on Sunday adds little that was not already reported somewhere in Zambia between August and October. Its force is in the compilation: 14, 61, 71 and 18 are numbers that had not been set down together, and they will now be quoted by anyone who wants to describe the post-election period in a sentence. Three things follow. First, the government's position is on the record and consistent: opposition membership is no shield, suspects are not political detainees, and the courts are open. The test of that last claim is Thursday, when the High Court rules on constitutional bail for 18 treason accused after four adjournments. Second, Zambia is due to sit on the UN Human Rights Council for the 2027 to 2029 term, and the report is addressed as much to the governments that elected it as to Lusaka. Third, the Khama exchange shows how the argument is now conducted, by former presidents and spokesmen on social media rather than through the regional channels both sides invoke. Still unknown: the total number detained since August, which no official body has published, and what has been done to investigate Mutotwe Kafwaya's death.

Analysis

Why a 300-year-old writ is doing the work in the Jay Jay Banda case

Habeas corpus is the oldest instrument a court has for one question: by what authority is this person held? It does not decide guilt and it does not grant bail. It orders the custodian to produce the detainee and explain the legal basis for detention, and it is the remedy a family reaches for when it does not know where someone is. That is the situation Grace Nampokolwe Banda describes, and it is the remedy Mutotwe Kafwaya's wife used in August to learn that her husband was dead. The State's answer is already partly on the record: Banda faces attempted murder and aggravated robbery charges, escaped lawful custody in 2024, and will be brought before a competent court. Both positions can be true at once. A man may be lawfully wanted and still be unlawfully held if no court has been shown a warrant or a detention order, which is what the writ is designed to find out. What to watch is the paperwork rather than the politics: whether the State produces a charge sheet and a date, and how quickly the High Court lists the application. Constitutional limits on detention before charge are, in the end, enforced one writ at a time.

Analysis

Three wins, two places and the arithmetic of the Elite 16

Munali Suns have done the hard part of a six-team group in three days: beaten the two sides most likely to finish second, one by two points and the other by forty. The arithmetic is not finished. Two places in the Elite 16 go to each first-round group, and with two games left Beira, the Dolphins, Bravehearts and Harare Kings can all still reach four wins, so the Suns are not yet through on paper. In practice, a win over Harare Kings on Monday afternoon would leave them needing nothing from the Panthers game, and they hold the head-to-head over Beira and the Dolphins if it comes to a tie. The group has shown a team with two gears: the composure to come back from ten down against Beira, and the depth to run away from a good Dolphins side the next day. Douglas Kandulu's 35 points on Sunday followed 21 points, 13 rebounds and five blocks on Saturday. The reward for a top-two finish is Nairobi from 23 November, where the top two of the East Division's Elite 16 go to the Basketball Africa League. The thing to watch on Monday is fatigue: the Suns play their fourth group game of the week at 16:00.
