Redrawing the ministries before naming the ministers: Hichilema asks Parliament to approve the creation, merger and dissolution of ministries, with Nalumango to move the motion

State House said on Monday 26 October that President Hakainde Hichilema has written to the National Assembly under Article 92(2)(d) of the Constitution seeking approval for a realignment of government ministries, which it says will put the machinery of government behind the Grow Zambia Agenda. K52.6 million of inputs to inactive and double-dipping farmers: an agriculture foundation demands a FISP investigation after the Auditor-General's findings

Opinion & Analysis

Analysis

Structure before names: what the ministries motion does and does not settle

Article 92(2)(d) makes Parliament the gatekeeper for the shape of government, and the President has chosen to go through that gate before filling the offices behind it. That answers one criticism of 2021, when new ministries were created first and approved afterwards, and it offers an explanation for a Cabinet of four that no official has yet given aloud: that ministers are not being named for ministries that may not exist in a month. It does not answer the question Neo Simutanyi and others are asking, which is why the sequence has taken eleven weeks, nor the one opposition MPs will put in the debate, which is what the new structure costs. The strongest case for the government is that a second-term programme built around specific production targets needs ministries drawn to match them. The strongest case against is that ministries can be redrawn in a week and ministers appointed in a day, and the country has had neither. What to watch: the list of ministries, the date of the debate relative to Friday's budget, and how quickly the names follow the structure.

Analysis

The audit figures arrive just as the next season's inputs go out

Three separate findings from the Auditor-General's latest cycle now concern FISP: K17.76 million of inputs to inactive farmers, K34.92 million to beneficiaries already on other programmes, and K19.6 million to farmers not on the national register, concentrated in Isoka and Mpika. Together they describe a verification problem rather than a theft problem, which is why ACAF's question about whether the failures sit in the e-voucher or the direct supply channel matters: the two systems have different controls and different points of failure. The ministry has not yet responded to either report. Against that is a programme that is, by the ministry's own figures, two-thirds redeemed for 2026/27 and ahead of last year's pace, and a Finance Ministry that has just asked for K19.9 billion more largely to pay for the maize that last year's inputs produced. The budget on Friday will show whether FISP is funded at this year's cost or last year's plan. What is not known is whether the register has been cleaned between the audited season and this one.

Analysis

Wednesday's hearing turns on a timing argument the court has not yet reached

The bail application by the 18 treason accused has been adjourned more than once without a ruling on its substance, and Wednesday's sitting is the first at which the merits can be heard with the parties the court has now settled on. The accused's argument, as reported, is a narrow one: that a State which is still investigating after charging has conceded it cannot yet try them, and that detention cannot be justified by an investigation with no end date. The State's position at the last sitting, as reported, was about who had properly been sued, not about delay, and its answer on the merits has not yet been published. Both positions are arguable, and the panel may decide procedure before it decides liberty. What to watch: whether all 18 are produced as directed, whether the DPP files a response, and whether the court rules on Wednesday or reserves. None of it bears on guilt; the charges have not been tested and every one of the accused is presumed innocent.
