Economy · Lead story

Petrol K33.06, diesel K34.45: the ERB raises every fuel price for November on a weaker kwacha and Gulf tensions, a day after Nalumango ruled out using reserves at the pump

A month after the K6 jump that followed the end of the tax relief, the Energy Regulation Board has added K1.60 to petrol and K1.18 to diesel from midnight on Saturday 31 October. Kerosene goes to K32.08 and Jet A-1 to K34.51. The board says international prices rose for petrol and kerosene and the kwacha lost more than 3 per cent; diesel went up even though its international price fell. On Friday Mutale Nalumango told Parliament the country's US$6.5 billion in reserves would not be used for fuel.

No ballot in Mandevu: the ECZ will declare Adrian Banda MP on 5 November, Tonse asks for a rerun with a new candidate, and Nalumango says a retreating opposition could gift 2031

Economy · Prices

Inflation edges up to 6.3 per cent as diesel jumps 24 per cent in a month, and September's trade balance swings to a US$186 million deficit on weaker copper exports

ZamStats' October bulletin, released in Lusaka on Thursday 29 October, puts annual inflation at 6.3 per cent, up from 6.1, with food inflation easing to 5.7 per cent and non-food rising to 7.4. The price table explains why: diesel up 23.8 per cent and petrol up 24.4 per cent in a month, while a 25 kg bag of breakfast meal is a tenth cheaper than a year ago. Exports fell 8.1 per cent in September and imports rose 5.4 per cent, turning August's US$48 million surplus into a US$186.4 million deficit as refined copper earnings dropped 15 per cent.

Economy · Energy

US$31 billion to 2050 and a US$23.1 billion hole for ZESCO: the Energy Ministry's financing strategy for the power plan, as the Permanent Secretary puts capacity at 4,722 MW

The Ministry of Energy's financing strategy for the Integrated Resource Plan, presented at a Centre for Trade Policy and Development meeting in Lusaka and reported by Zambia Monitor, says the plan needs about US$31 billion by 2050 and US$11.6 billion by 2030, roughly US$1.07 billion a year against concessional flows averaging US$231 million. Under an open-access market, ZESCO could face a gap of up to US$23.1 billion between 2033 and 2050. Separately, Permanent Secretary Arnold Simwaba put installed capacity at 4,722 MW, up from 3,777 MW in 2024.

1,622 cases and 30 deaths, more than half of them before reaching a clinic: Chilengi tells Parliament the outbreak has spread to 20 districts as 709,018 people are vaccinated

In a ministerial statement to the National Assembly on Thursday 29 October, Health Minister Roma Chilengi said Zambia had recorded 1,622 cholera cases and 30 deaths since 28 July, 16 of the deaths in communities before patients reached a health facility. Opinion & Analysis

Analysis

Why diesel rose when oil fell, and what 1 December adds

The November review is small next to October's, but it carries two signals. The first is in the diesel line. The board's own benchmark price for diesel fell by about US$6 a barrel over the review period, yet the pump price went up by K1.18. The only other variable the board named was the kwacha, which it said lost more than 3 per cent. When a currency move alone can lift the price of the fuel that moves maize, cement and buses, the exchange rate becomes the monthly fuel policy whether anyone intends it or not. The second signal is a date. The ERB's October statement recorded that the zero-rating of VAT on fuel ends on 1 December. Unless the government extends it, the December review will have to price in a tax change as well as oil and the kwacha, and October showed what a tax change looks like at the pump. The Vice-President's answer on Friday was that reserves are not for this and the budget is; the 2027 budget is due on 6 November. What to watch is whether it says anything about the VAT date, and whether the kwacha's slide continues into the next review period.

Analysis

What a walkover settles, and what it leaves open

Mandevu will have a member of Parliament on Thursday without a single vote being counted, and the regulations the commission cited give it no choice: with one validly nominated candidate, there is nothing to poll. That is the settled part. The rest is contested, and each side's case should be stated at its strongest. The government's position, in the Vice-President's words, is that nobody forced three candidates to withdraw after nominating, that a party which retreats from a contest cannot blame its opponent for the result, and that discipline is the opposition's problem to solve. The opposition's position, in Tonse's request and in the allegations that preceded the withdrawals, is that a contest from which candidates say they were frightened out is not a contest, and that an unopposed return reached that way says little about Mandevu's preferences. What is missing is the material that would let a reader decide: the commission's notice gives no reasons for the withdrawals, says nothing about complaints, and no investigation of the alleged threats has been reported. Harry Kalaba's intervention shows the argument is now inside the opposition as well as between it and the UPND. Watch whether the commission answers Tonse, and whether any of the three withdrawn candidates says more.

Analysis

A suspended ban is a rule with a trigger, and the trigger is the crowd

FAZ has done something more consequential than fine a club. By ordering five closed-door matches 300 kilometres from Kitwe and then suspending them, the disciplinary committee has given Nkana a sanction that its supporters, not its officials, control. The club understood this immediately: its statement is addressed to the stands. The precedent reaches beyond Nkana. The ruling establishes that a chant can cost a Zambian club six figures and its home ground, and it does so without naming the chant, which leaves the next committee to decide what counts as political. Supporters of every club will now be reading that line. There is a fairness question that the reporting has not answered: the same ministry statement that cited Nkana also cited the Zambia–Togo qualifier in Ndola, and no sanction over that match has been reported, so the rule has so far been applied to a club and not to a national-team crowd. What to watch is Nkana's next home fixture, whether the chant travels to other grounds, and whether FAZ publishes the ruling in full so that clubs know where the line is before they cross it.
