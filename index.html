A cell for a night, a courtroom for a year

The State has now shown it can detain an archbishop overnight and release him on schedule — a demonstration of power wrapped, correctly, in procedure. But procedure is the easy half. From the Section 319 charge to the whispered testimony about a farm in Lumezi, Archbishop Banda's name is being litigated in courtrooms he stands in and ones he doesn't. The prosecution must now be as serious as the arrest was. Anything less, and Tuesday's smile at the gate becomes the photograph that outlasts the case.

Opinion

Paper is not pipeline

US$2.14 billion in memoranda makes a fine headline, and Abu Dhabi a fine photograph. But an MoU is a doorway, not a building — no site, no timeline, no financing structure has been disclosed for the Medical City that anchored Tuesday's package. This government has a shelf of impressive signatures already. The measure of the UAE visit is not the pens but the contractors: publish the term sheets, name the sites, and let the country audit the difference between what was promised and what arrives.

Opinion

The lights went out; Zambia didn't

For twenty minutes in Ndola, the floodlights failed and the country's footballing self-doubt was alone on the pitch with it. Then the game resumed, and Zambia won — early goal, late goal, first victory of the campaign, level with Togo on three points. Nothing about November's double-header with Burundi is easy, and one win repairs neither the midfield nor the mood of a year without victories. But a team that keeps playing through a blackout is a team worth a full stadium. On Tuesday, it had one.
