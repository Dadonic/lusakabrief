Opinion

The bus and the two clocks

Today in Solwezi a magistrate will do the most ordinary thing in criminal justice — ask two men how they plead — and the country will watch as if the republic hangs on it. It does not, but something else does: the gap between the two clocks in this affair. The sedition case moves, however haltingly, through a courtroom. The treason case, the one that keeps eighteen people in cells and removes bail from the courts' own hands, moves at the speed of an investigation nobody may inspect. A plea is a promise that evidence exists. The State should keep that promise in Solwezi today — and then explain, with dates, when the graver charge intends to meet a judge.

Opinion

Corridors are built twice

Every trade corridor is built twice: once in ballast and rail, and once in the patience of the institutions that must coordinate across three borders, four ministries and a dozen lenders. The Lobito meeting in Lusaka this week is the second kind of construction. Zambia's copper does not need the Atlantic on a map; it needs a financed extension into North-Western Province, a border post that clears in hours, and a tariff regime stable enough to price a shipment against. The World Bank's guarantees can de-risk the steel. Only the three governments can de-risk the politics. Bring back a timetable, not a communiqué.

Opinion

The unanswered why

Article 270 gives a president the power to remove; it does not relieve him of the duty to explain. Charity Munganga was dismissed on Sunday with the constitutional citation and a courteous thank-you — and nothing else. Perhaps there is a reason the public cannot yet be told. But a police service emerging from a contested election, facing honest questions about political policing, cannot be commanded through silence. Each unexplained removal teaches officers the same lesson: that survival is about the favour of the appointing authority, not the constitution of the Republic. If further changes are coming, let them come with reasons attached.

Mwelwa Phiri
