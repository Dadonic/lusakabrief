Opinion

The pulpit moves first

The churches have done in one Wednesday what the opposition has failed to do in six weeks: put the credibility of the 2026 election on the national table with institutional weight behind it. The bishops named no cases and the CCZ named one — Mutotwe Kafwaya — and that discipline is the statements' strength. Lusaka can dismiss politicians as bad losers; dismissing Kapingila House and the UNZA Chapel is a different calculation. The government should take the dialogue before it is forced to take the verdict.

Opinion

The accelerator and the brake

On a single Wednesday the central bank cut 250 basis points because inflation is beaten, and the energy regulator raised fuel by a quarter because the instrument that helped beat it had expired. Both decisions are defensible; together they are a wager that September's 6.1 per cent survives October's K31.46. The Treasury says VAT follows on 1 December. If the pump passes into fares and food before the cut passes into credit, the bank will have spent its biggest easing in a decade buying the Treasury one quiet month.

Opinion

A fifth of a second of darkness

ZESCO's explanation is precise, honest — and the real indictment. A national stadium went black for eighteen minutes because the grid hiccupped for 0.2 seconds and the floodlights could not forgive it. That is not bad luck; it is design debt, the same kind the Attorney-General disclosed this week when the courts, it turns out, can be closed by the security services and merely announced by the Judiciary. Systems fail at their weakest joint. The work is finding the joints before the country watches them fail.
