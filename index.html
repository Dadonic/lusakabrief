Opinion

The map is now the case

Forty-six days after the arrests, the sedition case has produced two adjournments, one road transfer and now a geography lesson. The defence's jurisdiction objection is not a trick — venue is a real question in criminal law, and a Solwezi court trying a Lusaka posting would be a genuine curiosity. But the State chose this venue, flew the country's chief prosecutor to it, and then asked for more time to explain the choice. On 13 October a magistrate will draw the map. After that, there are no more procedural rooms left to hide in: either the evidence meets a plea, or the country is entitled to ask whether venue was ever the point — or the delay was.

Opinion

US$28 billion is a diagnosis, not a delivery

Anna Bjerde's conveyor-belt warning deserves to be framed in every ministry along the corridor. A railway that only moves other countries' copper past your towns is not development; it is scenery. The IFC's 118 anchor investments are a diagnosis of what could exist — the power lines, warehouses, secondary cities and skills — not a delivery note. Zambia's task in this meeting is unglamorous: land titles, border hours, megawatts and a financed route to the Angolan border. If the communiqué today has numbers for those, the corridor has begun. If it has only adjectives, we have been here before.

Opinion

Katondo Street is the exam

Phase One of Operation Clear Path proved the council can clear a pavement. Phase Two must prove it can rehouse a livelihood. Katondo Street is the hardest test: phone traders, wheelbarrow sellers, car dealers on the kerb — and 250,000 to 350,000 informal traders citywide against roughly 11,000 designated stands, on the architects' count. Enforcement without absorption is just relocation of disorder. The cholera declaration gives the sweep its urgency; the market register must give it its mercy. Clear the street, yes — then publish, weekly, how many traders were actually placed. The broom has done its part. The ledger must now do its own.
