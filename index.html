Grow Zambia day: Hichilema opens the 14th Assembly with a five-year agenda, a unity appeal — and a fuel-tax decision that lands this month

President Hakainde Hichilema ceremonially opened the First Session of the 14th National Assembly yesterday, telling a transformed chamber that the electorate's verdict was 'unequivocal: continuity, unity, progress and hard and smart work'. The address set out the Grow Zambia agenda for agriculture, mining and energy, restored the excise duty on fuel from this month, and ordered an electric-only fleet for government — while saying nothing, in its circulated text, about the third-term question that had crowded the foyer all week.

Economy · Fuel and energy

The tax comes back to the pump: excise duty returns this month — with an ERB pricing review, an ethanol blend plan, and an electric-only rule for the government fleet

President Hichilema told the 14th Assembly that the excise duty on fuel, suspended to shield consumers from global price shocks, is restored from October — the revenue earmarked for free education, CDF, school feeding and social cash transfers. The package pairs the tax with a review of the ERB's monthly pricing mechanism, a firmer TAZAMA open-access regime, a local ethanol blending strategy, and a directive that government institutions will now buy only electric vehicles.

Opinion

The sentence that stayed in the drawer

Somewhere in the drafting of Friday's address there must have been a version of the sentence. It is not a hard sentence to write: the Constitution says two terms, I have been elected twice, and that is the end of it. Article 106 does the heavy lifting; the President would only have had to agree with it out loud. Instead the 14th Assembly opened on a speech that reached for unity, continuity and hard work — all true, all useful — and stepped around the one question the foyer had spent the week asking. State House says there is no third-term plan, and there is no evidence of one. But silence from the podium is not the same as an answer from it, and a debate fed by backbenchers will now be fed by the absence too. The drawer at State House still holds the sentence. It will keep until Monday — after that, it starts costing interest.

Opinion

The tax that came home

Every tax holiday ends the same way: with a speech about the things the revenue will buy. Friday's was better than most, because the shopping list is real — free education, the CDF, school feeding, social cash transfers are exactly the programmes a fuel excise should defend, and the IMF has been telling Lusaka for months that the waiver was leaking money. The honest part of the announcement is the ERB review tucked beside it. You do not review your pricing mechanism unless you know the restored tax is about to meet a restless pump price, and you do not promise an electric government fleet unless you want the story to be about the future rather than the litre. The arithmetic will be judged where it always is — in the minibus fare and the cooking-oil shelf — but the principle is sound: holidays are for emergencies, and a treasury that never ends them ends up with nothing to defend the programmes with.

Opinion

Publish the whole compact

The health pact has now been priced three times in public: US$3.6 billion on the way in, US$2.49 billion by Washington at signature, and 'more than US$1.5 billion' in the actual text of intent. Each number was announced by someone who benefits from it. What we learned this week about the document itself is genuinely reassuring — no compulsory specimen sharing, co-financing down from US$243 million to US$154 million, minerals formally decoupled, half a billion dollars added — and it is reassurance at second hand, from a minister summarising a paper the public has not seen. Fred M'membe's politics may be total, but his demand is surgical: publish the complete, unedited MoU. A government that won the negotiation, as this one plausibly did, has nothing to fear from its own victory lap being taken in daylight. The clinics were burned by ZAMMSA opacity; the cure is the document, in full, online, this month.
