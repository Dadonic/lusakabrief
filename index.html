'Overwhelmingly biased': the EU observers' final report hands Zambia 13 recommendations, and the government replies that observers cannot see what the police can

The EU Election Observation Mission published its final report on the 13 August general elections on Tuesday 3 November: 13 recommendations, eight of them priority. It found a competitive choice and efficient polling staff, but late legal changes, police bias, cadreism and incumbency advantage that distorted the playing field, a state broadcaster that gave the ruling party over 90 per cent of political airtime, and a six-hour halt in counting and court closures that curtailed transparency. Information Minister Elvis Nkandu replied the same day.

A day before the declaration, Kasonde Mwenda asks the High Court to halt the Mandevu process, arguing the Commission had no power to scrap the August poll and order a new one

Economic Freedom Fighters president Kasonde Mwenda has applied for leave to seek judicial review of the Electoral Commission's handling of Mandevu, Zambia Monitor and the Zambian Observer reported on Tuesday 3 November. Economy · Central bank

Oversubscribed by K3.5 billion: the Bank of Zambia formally closes the K5 billion drought and load-shedding facility, and says borrowers must still get the benefit of any rate cut

The Bank of Zambia has closed its Stability and Resilience Facility, the K5 billion line opened in December 2024 for businesses hit by the 2023/24 drought and the power shortages that followed, Zambia Monitor reported on Tuesday 3 November. Applications reached K8.5 billion against the K5 billion available; the access window, extended once from 31 December 2025, closed on 31 March 2026, by which date the bank's own journal had recorded full utilisation. The bank says the facility largely met its objectives and that it will keep monitoring the loans.

Economy · Customs

A spirit-like smell at Kafue: the ZRA stops three trucks carrying 119,500 litres of ethanol declared at Chirundu as paraffin, and says the false entry would have cost K5.8 million

Officers of the Zambia Revenue Authority's Mobile Enforcement Unit stopped three super-link trucks at the Kafue checkpoint carrying 478 drums of 250 litres each that had been entered at the Chirundu One Stop Border Post as paraffin, News Diggers and Zambia Monitor reported on Tuesday 3 November. An inspection found a smell inconsistent with paraffin; laboratory tests at the Makeni Enforcement Centre confirmed ethanol. The authority puts the revenue at stake at about K5.8 million and warns that false declarations carry seizure and prosecution.

Economy · Lead story

One notch up, three days before the budget: Moody's lifts Zambia from Caa2 to Caa1 with a positive outlook, and the Treasury says the rating is a means, not an end

Moody's Ratings raised Zambia's sovereign credit rating to Caa1 on Friday 30 October and kept the outlook positive, the third agency to move the country up since the 2020 default. The Ministry of Finance and National Planning welcomed the decision on Monday 2 November, crediting debt restructuring, fiscal consolidation and a steadier economy, and warning that the rating must be turned into investment and jobs. Situmbeko Musokotwane presents the 2027 budget on Friday 6 November, a week later than first planned.

Health · Vaccines

Sixty-forty and US$213 million over two phases: the IDC and a Beijing firm sign the ZAMVAX joint venture, starting with 20 million cholera doses a year filled in Lusaka South

The Industrial Development Corporation and Jijia (Beijing) International Medical Technology signed a joint venture agreement on Monday 2 November to develop ZAMVAX Limited, a vaccine maker to be built in the Lusaka South Multi-Facility Economic Zone, with the IDC holding 60 per cent. Phase one, an oral cholera vaccine fill-and-finish line costing about US$24.13 million and built for up to 20 million doses a year, uses imported bulk antigen; a second phase of about US$189 million would add local antigen production.

Health · Cholera

1,622 cases and 30 deaths, more than half of them before reaching a clinic: Chilengi tells Parliament the outbreak has spread to 20 districts as 709,018 people are vaccinated

In a ministerial statement to the National Assembly on Thursday 29 October, Health Minister Roma Chilengi said Zambia had recorded 1,622 cholera cases and 30 deaths since 28 July, 16 of the deaths in communities before patients reached a health facility. The outbreak covers 20 districts in six provinces, Mumbwa and Chisamba the latest; Lusaka Province accounts for 1,090 cases. The Treasury has released K13 million for emergency water supply, and 709,018 people, 91 per cent of the target, have been vaccinated in Lusaka, Chibombo and Chongwe.

Analysis

Thirteen recommendations and a track record of one: what the EU report can and cannot do

The EU mission's final report has no legal force, and both sides know it. Its weight comes from the detail: airtime measured to a decimal point, a six-hour suspension of counting that the mission says was never adequately explained, courts closed for two of the seven petition days. The government's reply does not dispute those facts so much as their framing. It says election-year amendments have precedents going back to Kaunda, that ZNBC's commercial airtime is open to all and its duty to report government is statutory, and that observers cannot see what investigators can. Those are arguments about context, and its complaint that it was not asked for that context is its strongest card. The weakest point in the government's position is the one the report puts in a single line: of 22 recommendations made in 2021, one was implemented. The political parties law the Constitutional Court ordered is still not enacted. What to watch is not a reply to Brussels but three domestic tests: whether the Electoral Commission publishes polling-station results, whether the budget session finds time for the parties and political finance bills, and whether the cyber laws are touched at all.

Analysis

Thursday: a declaration under challenge, a bail ruling due, and six wards that will vote

Thursday 5 November was meant to be by-election day. It has become something more complicated. In Mandevu the Electoral Commission intends to declare Adrian Banda elected without a vote, and Kasonde Mwenda has asked the High Court to stop it, not because of the withdrawals but because, he argues, the Commission had no power to cancel the August election in the first place. His application was stamped on Monday; nothing reported so far says a judge has granted leave, let alone a stay, and unless that changes the declaration goes ahead. At the High Court, meanwhile, the constitutional bail application of Brian Mundubile, Makebi Zulu and 16 others returns at nine o'clock, after a court that has said it will allow no further adjournment. And in six wards in five districts, people will queue and vote. News Diggers' editorial on Wednesday argues that lawful walkovers still need explaining and wants the Anti-Corruption Commission to look at how they came about. The unresolved question is institutional: who, if anyone, examines a withdrawal once the Commission has accepted the letter.

Analysis

Two days to the budget: the drought money is spent, and the reserves argument is back

The Bank of Zambia's closure of its K5 billion Stability and Resilience Facility is a tidy end to the drought emergency: oversubscribed by K3.5 billion, fully drawn by March, concessional terms that still ratchet down with the policy rate. It is also a reminder that the easy liquidity is gone just as the next shock arrives through the fuel pump. Gertrude Imenda, a nominated UPND MP, told News Diggers it would be perfectly in order to use some of the country's US$6.5 billion in reserves to steady the kwacha and cushion fuel; Vice-President Nalumango had told Parliament on Friday that the government has no such intention. Friday's budget is where that argument gets settled, in the exchange-rate assumption, the fuel levies and the size of the deficit. One more item belongs on the watch list: Zambia Monitor, citing Access Bank commentary, reports that the Africa Finance Corporation is asking Zambia and Angola for initial viability-gap funding for the Lobito railway after expected commitments from international partners did not materialise. If that is confirmed, the US$3 to 5 billion line Lusaka has been counting on will need a line of its own in someone's budget.
