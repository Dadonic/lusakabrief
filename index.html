Opinion

The gavel before everything

The first act of a 266-member Parliament is not a law but a choice, and the Standing Orders are right to make it so: no Speaker, no business. The expanded assembly was sold as a bigger table for a bigger democracy; today it must first prove it can organise its own chair. Nelly Mutti's fate, the opposition's leadership contest, and three empty seats awaiting November's by-elections all say the same thing — the Fourteenth Assembly begins as a work in progress. The country should watch who it becomes, starting with who holds the gavel this morning.

Opinion

US$2.1 billion meets K31.46

The President returned from Abu Dhabi quoting billions while his capital city queued under the first full day of the new pump prices. Both numbers are real, and the government's credibility now lives in the space between them. Commitments are not disbursements, a health city is not a clinic, and an ADNOC supply conversation is not a litre of diesel. The administration knows this — it is why the President kept his figure conservative. The follow-through files, not the forum photos, will decide whether Abu Dhabi was investment or theatre.

Opinion

A week for verification

The news this week kept offering the same lesson in different costumes. In Marondera, a family and a nation wait for DNA before mourning can be official. In 9 Miles and Meanwood, 739 bags looked like fertiliser until somebody tested the claim. In Lusaka's clinics, 0.98 per cent facility survival says the system works — if the patient reaches it. Verification is unglamorous, slow and absolutely everything. The counterfeiters, the rumour-mills and the careless all bet on our impatience. This week, impatience lost.
