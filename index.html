Opinion

The gavel, earned twice

There are two ways to win the chair: unopposed, and by ballot. Dr Mutti's unopposed return is the House's compliment to itself — a first woman Speaker confirmed without a whisper of a rival. Nshamba Muzungu's 166-to-58 victory is the other kind of legitimacy: argued, counted, rejected ballots and all. The Fourteenth Assembly's first day was procedure, but procedure is the point. A parliament that cannot elect its own officers elects nothing else. This one can — and on Friday the President hands it the programme that will test what the election was for.

Opinion

Price it in seasons

The monthly fuel review was built to pass the world's volatility through to the pump, honestly and often. The President now wants the opposite: honesty less often. There is a real case — businesses can price a three-month window and cannot price a monthly coin-toss — but a fixed window is a subsidy in disguise whenever world prices run ahead of it, and December's VAT return will test how much stillness the budget can afford. ADNOC's long-term table is attractive precisely because it is long. The question is who pays when the calendar and the market disagree.

Opinion

Forty-two days

Whatever the treason case eventually proves, the petition listed for 26 October asks a narrower and more fundamental question: what may the State do to a person in the first six weeks of a charge? The Constitution answers in articles, not slogans — liberty, named reasons, a competent court, counsel and family. The alliance's claims of mistreatment are untested; the State's defence is unheard. That is what the fortnight before three judges is for. A country is measured by how it treats the people it accuses of the worst things. Watch the courtroom, not the chorus.

MP
Mwelwa Phiri
Investigations editor
