Economy · Lead story

‘Preserve this year’s stocks’: 1.25 million Zambians face hunger in the lean season as the Government activates its El Niño plan — but the Vice-President says the lights stay on

Two ministerial statements to Parliament on Friday 23 October set out what the 2026/27 El Niño season could mean. Vice-President Mutale Nalumango said about 1.25 million people in 93 districts could face acute food insecurity between August 2026 and March 2027 and that K31.7 million has already gone to the Disaster Risk Management Division; Green Economy Minister Gilbert Siame listed the ten driest districts and announced a US$3.6 million grant for weather stations. Asked about electricity, Nalumango said there would be no load-shedding.

Opinion & Analysis

Analysis

The 2023/24 drought is the yardstick for this El Niño, and the plan shows it

Friday's two statements are best read against the 2023/24 drought, which the Vice-President herself invoked when she asked MPs to have their constituents preserve this year's stocks. This time the warnings have come before the season rather than during it, the needs assessment has put a number on the exposure — 1.25 million people in 93 districts — and some relief money has reached the provinces before the lean months begin. That is the case for saying lessons were learned. The case for caution is in what was not said. K31.7 million is a modest sum against the population named, and neither statement, as reported, gave the size or timing of the main lean-season package of food or cash. The Vice-President's assurance on electricity speaks to the present; the Zambezi River Authority has already cut Kariba's 2027 generation allocation from 36 to 23 billion cubic metres, and a dry season would make that cut bite harder. What to watch: whether the Food Reserve Agency's large maize stock is released into southern markets, whether the Meteorological Services Bill reaches the House before the rains, and the first Zambia Meteorological Department bulletins on when the onset arrives in Southern and Western provinces.

Analysis

Mandevu is a small by-election carrying a large test

One Lusaka constituency, eleven more days of campaigning and a single seat will not change the balance of the National Assembly. But both sides have already told voters how to read it. The Tonse Alliance's complaint of an attack in Raphael Chota ward, if the police confirm it, would be the first recorded violence of the new term; the UPND's earlier reply — name the attackers or withdraw — sets up a dispute about evidence that the police investigation can settle. The Inspector General's three-zone plan is unusually specific for a by-election, and that is to the police's credit; it also gives observers a checklist. Arguments on each side deserve a hearing: the opposition says intimidation is being used against a campaign already struggling for resources, the ruling party says the allegations are unproven and politically convenient. What is unknown is who the attackers were and whether the abduction reported by the alliance happened as described. What to watch: arrests or their absence before 5 November, and whether the Electoral Commission's campaign timetable is enforced evenly.

Analysis

What a group stage gives Red Arrows that a cup run never has

Red Arrows have reached a CAF group stage for the first time, and the significance is less the scoreline in Durban than the calendar that follows it. A group stage means a run of fixtures against clubs from across the continent from November into the new year, the income CAF attaches to that phase, and the kind of regular continental football that Zambian clubs have rarely managed to sustain. It also means strain: a transitional domestic season of 18 league matches with a final set for 31 January, and travel budgets that a Zambia Air Force club will have to meet. The assistant coach's promise to attack in Durban was not quite kept — Arrows defended for most of the second half — but the discipline that saw them through a shoot-out is exactly what group football rewards. What is unknown is the draw, the opponents and where Arrows will host. What to watch: the date CAF sets for the group draw, how Arrows handle a midweek-and-weekend rhythm, and whether the domestic fixture list makes room for them.
